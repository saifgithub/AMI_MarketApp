# VIX term structure: contango vs backwardation

<Lesson id="062_volatility_regimes"/> used spot VIX as the headline volatility-regime gauge. This lesson goes one layer deeper: the *term structure* of VIX futures (1-month vs 3-month vs 6-month) often gives a cleaner read on regime state than spot VIX alone. After this lesson you will be able to read the VIX1M/VIX3M ratio, classify the regime as contango (calm) or backwardation (stress), name the strategies that fit each, and state the regime-change threshold.

## How this plays out in real markets

VIX term structure is captured by the relationship between VIX (1-month, here we use the standard 30-day spot VIX) and VIX3M (the 3-month constant-maturity volatility index, ticker VIX3M).

Contango (calm regime): VIX3M > VIX (the longer tenor trades higher because the market expects mean reversion of vol back toward the long-run average). The VIX1M/VIX3M ratio sits under 0.95 the majority of the time. Through 2024, the ratio averaged ~0.87 — a deeply calm reading consistent with the SPX uptrend.

Backwardation (stress regime): VIX > VIX3M (near-term vol elevated above longer-dated). VIX1M/VIX3M ratio above 1.0. Real cases:

- 5 August 2024 yen-carry unwind: VIX closed at 38.57, VIX3M at 27.85 — ratio 1.385, deep backwardation. SPX 5-day return that week was -3.2%.
- 13–16 March 2020 COVID crash: VIX1M/VIX3M ratio peaked above 1.4 with VIX intraday at 85.47 (18 March 2020).
- 2 February 2018 volmageddon week: VIX1M/VIX3M ratio crossed 1.0 on 5 February 2018 — the day SPX fell 4.1%.
- 24 December 2018 Fed-fear low: VIX1M/VIX3M ratio hit ~1.15; SPX's closing low printed that same session.

Detection rule: VIX1M/VIX3M ratio < 0.95 = contango (calm regime, trend-following permitted at full mandate sizing); 0.95–1.0 = transition (compress sizes by ~25%); > 1.0 = backwardation (stress regime, defensive playbook). A flip from sustained contango to backwardation that holds for 3+ trading sessions is a regime-stress confirmation.

Invalidation: backwardation reverts to contango (ratio falls back under 0.95) for 5 consecutive sessions — the stress event has passed.

## The trap

The VIX term-structure trap is using spot VIX in isolation. A trader reads "VIX is 22, normal range" and adds risk — but if VIX3M is 19, the ratio is 1.16 and the curve is in backwardation despite an unremarkable VIX print. The market structure is telling you something the headline number isn't.

The correct model: in contango, the structure pays you (and pays VIX-short ETPs over time) and trend-following works. In backwardation, the structure punishes you, single-name idiosyncratic risk dominates, and the calendar-style trades that printed in calm get repriced. The ratio is the regime classifier; the absolute VIX level is secondary.

## Takeaway

The VIX term-structure ratio classifies the regime more cleanly than spot VIX. Contango earns full sizing; backwardation compresses it; the flip is the regime-stress signal, not the absolute number.
