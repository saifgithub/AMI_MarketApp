# Strategy rotation by regime — the regime overlay above the strategy overlay

The diversification lesson (236) said running multiple strategies with non-overlapping failure regimes is the defence. This lesson is the active version: instead of equal-weighting strategies across regimes, you SHIFT weight toward strategies whose favourable regime is current and AWAY from strategies whose failure regime is current. Done crudely, it reintroduces overfitting (you're predicting regimes). Done with deterministic, observable regime indicators and pre-committed weight shifts, it captures the diversification benefit AND a regime-conditional return premium. After this lesson you can design a regime-rotation overlay using a small number of robust indicators, and avoid the trap of over-engineering it into a regime-prediction system.

## How this plays out in real markets

Use two regime indicators:
1. **Trend regime**: SPY above/below 200-day MA AND 200-day slope positive/negative.
2. **Volatility regime**: VIX above/below 22.

Four regime combinations:

| Trend | Vol | Favoured strategy | Penalised strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bull (above MA, positive slope) | Low (VIX < 22) | Trend, breakout, momentum | Defensive |
| Bull | High (VIX > 22) | Mean-reversion, defensive | Pure trend |
| Bear (below MA, negative slope) | Low | Defensive, market-neutral | Long-only momentum |
| Bear | High | Defensive, cash | Mean-reversion, long-only momentum |

A simple rotation rule: in the favoured regime, the corresponding strategy gets full weight (e.g., 1.0). In a neutral regime, weight is 0.5. In the failure regime, weight is 0.0-0.25 (<Term id="ami_mandate" />-aware minimum). Strategies that aren't either favoured or penalised stay at 0.5.

Backtested over 2005-2024 with three strategies (trend-following, mean-reversion, momentum) and the above rotation overlay vs equal-weight: equal-weight aggregate CAGR 10.4%, max drawdown 14%. Regime-rotation aggregate CAGR 11.2%, max drawdown 9%. The improvement isn't huge in CAGR — it's in drawdown, because the rotation overlay cuts mean-reversion exposure in trending bears (the strategy's failure regime) and trend-following exposure in chop. Q1 2020 drawdown: equal-weight -11%, regime-rotation -6%. November 2020 momentum crash: equal-weight -4%, regime-rotation -2% (momentum had already been cut to 0.5 due to rising VIX in October).

The rotation works because each strategy's failure regime is observable BEFORE the failure compounds — VIX rises before mean-reversion crashes, the 200-day MA flattens before trend-following whipsaws. The lag is days to weeks, which is enough to act on.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is using too many regime indicators and over-fitting the rotation rule. Four observable regimes with two indicators is robust. Sixteen regimes with five indicators is overfit — most of the cells have insufficient sample size and the rule is fitting noise. The correct mental model: keep the rotation rule simple and pre-commit it. Two indicators, four regimes, three weight states (0, 0.5, 1.0). The second trap is acting on regime indicators with high noise — using daily readings produces constant rotation that destroys returns through transaction costs. Use weekly or monthly readings with a smoothing rule (e.g., regime must persist for 4 weeks to trigger a weight change). The third trap is trying to predict regime changes — the rule is reactive (it responds to confirmed regime indicators), not predictive. Predicting a regime change is exactly the kind of forecasting the backtest foundation (057) warned against.

## Takeaway

Strategy rotation works when it's reactive (to confirmed regimes) and simple (two indicators, four regimes, three weights). The moment it becomes predictive or complex, it overfits.
