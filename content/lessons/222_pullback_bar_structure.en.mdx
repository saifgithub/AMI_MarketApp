# Pullback bar structure — 3-bar and 5-bar patterns

The pullback foundation (054) defined a valid pullback as a dip that closes back above support. The 3-bar and 5-bar pullback patterns refine THAT by counting the down-bars themselves. A 3-bar pullback is the classic: three consecutive down-closes inside an uptrend, followed by a bullish reversal bar. A 5-bar pullback extends to five consecutive lower-highs (the bars don't have to all close red), creating a more meaningful shake-out before the entry. Both have measurable historical edge in trending markets; both fail in regime transitions. After this lesson you can name exactly when bar-count rules apply and how to convert the pattern into a precise entry, stop, and invalidation.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at AAPL in May 2024. AAPL was in a clear uptrend from a March low of $169 to a May 23 high of $192.59. From May 23 through May 30, AAPL printed five consecutive bars with lower highs: $192.59, $191.06, $189.99, $190.29, $189.99 (close $187.93 on May 30) — a textbook 5-bar pullback with declining momentum and contracting daily ranges. On May 31, AAPL closed at $192.25 — a bullish reversal bar that closed above the highs of the prior two pullback bars AND above the pullback's high of day 1. Entry on the May 31 close at $192.25, stop under the pullback's swing low at $186.69 (low of May 30). Risk per share: $5.56. AAPL ran to $220 by mid-June — over 5R on the trade. The 3-bar variant would have triggered slightly earlier on May 29 or 30, with a worse stop and roughly the same target.

Entry criteria for 3-bar pullback: established uptrend (50-day MA up, price above 200-day MA), three consecutive lower-close bars after a swing high, then a bullish reversal bar that closes above the prior bar's high. Entry: close of the reversal bar. Stop: below the lowest low of the three pullback bars. For 5-bar variant: five consecutive lower-HIGH bars (not necessarily all red), reversal bar closes above the high of the prior two bars. Stop: below the 5-bar pullback's lowest low. Individual-trade invalidation: a daily close below the pullback's swing low — the pattern failed; exit. The strategy FAILS in two regimes: (1) gappy news-driven tapes where bars don't follow micro-structure (a single 8% gap-down on guidance breaks any 3-bar count); (2) sustained downtrends inside larger uptrends, where 3-bar counts repeatedly trigger and stop you out as the deeper pullback completes.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is mechanical bar-counting without the trend filter. You see three red bars in a downtrending stock, you call it a "3-bar pullback", you buy the reversal bar, and you've just bought into a bear-market rally. The correct mental model: bar-count patterns are valid ONLY inside a confirmed uptrend on the same timeframe. Outside that context the pattern means nothing — three down bars in a downtrend are normal continuation, not a setup. The other trap: counting too loosely. A "3-bar" pullback with an inside bar in the middle is not the same setup; tighten the rule to "three consecutive lower closes" and don't fudge the count when one bar nearly qualifies but doesn't.

## Takeaway

3-bar and 5-bar pullbacks only have edge inside confirmed uptrends. Count strictly, enter on the reversal bar, stop below the pullback low minus an ATR noise buffer. No fudging the count.
