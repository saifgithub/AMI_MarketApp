# MACD — zero-line cross vs signal-line cross

Lesson 028 introduced MACD's two crosses but ran them together. They are not the same event and they don't have the same weight. The signal-line cross (MACD line crossing its EMA(9) signal line) is a fast, frequent momentum-turn read — it fires roughly every two to four weeks on a typical daily chart, often inside a broader trend that doesn't change. The zero-line cross (MACD line crossing zero) is a slower, structural read — it fires when the underlying 12-EMA crosses the 26-EMA, which is the regime change itself. One is timing; the other is regime. Knowing which is which decides whether you take the cross, scale into it, or wait for confirmation.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at AAPL through Q3 2025. From mid-July to mid-September, AAPL traded sideways between 218 and 232. In that 50-session window, MACD's signal-line cross fired six times — three bullish, three bearish. None of the six produced a sustained move; each was reversed within 7–10 sessions. Trading every signal-line cross in that range generated 6 small losses averaging -0.7%, total drawdown about 4%.

Then on September 24, AAPL broke 232 and on October 7 the MACD line itself crossed above zero at 235 — the first zero-line cross since June. From that point through November 18, AAPL ran to 256 in a clean trend. The signal-line cross at -0.4 on September 16 had been the earliest entry; the zero-line cross on October 7 was the confirmed-regime entry.

Falsifiable claim: in a typical 12-month sample, signal-line crosses fire roughly 4× more frequently than zero-line crosses and have materially lower follow-through. The zero-line cross identifies a regime change that lasts on average 30–80 sessions; the signal-line cross identifies a momentum turn that may resolve in 5–15 sessions or get reversed entirely. Invalidation is mechanical — a zero-line cross fails if MACD crosses back through zero within 10 sessions; a signal-line cross fails when MACD re-crosses its signal line.

On Bursa Malaysia: TENAGA in 2025 had nine signal-line crosses inside a six-month sideways range with no meaningful follow-through; the one zero-line cross in October — when the regime actually flipped — caught a six-week trend from RM12.50 to RM13.40.

## The trap

The trap is treating the two crosses as interchangeable. A trader sees "MACD bullish cross" in chart commentary and assumes it's the cross that matters — usually it's the signal-line cross, which in a sideways market is a near-coin-flip. The same trader misses the zero-line cross because the chart doesn't shout about it the same way.

The correct mental model: the signal-line cross is timing within a regime; the zero-line cross is the regime itself. Use the signal-line cross to scale in earlier when you already have evidence the trend is forming. Use the zero-line cross as confirmation that the regime has actually changed. In a clear trend, both crosses fire close together and the difference doesn't matter. In a sideways market, the signal-line cross fires repeatedly with no zero-line confirmation — that's the signature of a noise environment.

## Takeaway

MACD has two crosses with very different weights — the signal-line cross is timing within a regime, the zero-line cross is the regime itself; don't trade the first without evidence the second is forming.
