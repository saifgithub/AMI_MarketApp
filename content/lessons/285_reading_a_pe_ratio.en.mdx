# Reading a P/E Ratio

The price-to-earnings ratio is the single most quoted number in stocks. Knowing what it actually says — and what it doesn't — is the first real tool in your fundamentals kit.

## The formula

```
P/E = Price per share ÷ Earnings per share (EPS, trailing 12 months)
```

That's it. NVDA at $152 with $4 of trailing earnings per share has a P/E of 38.

## What it actually means

A P/E of 38 means the market is currently paying $38 for every $1 of *this year's* profit. If earnings stayed flat forever and the company paid 100% of profits out as dividends, you'd recoup your investment in 38 years.

Nobody actually thinks earnings stay flat. The P/E is the market's way of pricing in **expected growth** — a high P/E says "I think this company will earn a lot more in the future." A low P/E says "I think growth has slowed or the business is risky."

## Reference points (US large-caps, 2026 vintage)

| P/E range | What the market is saying |
|---|---|
| Under 10 | Deep cyclical, declining business, or out-of-favour sector |
| 10–15 | Mature, stable, mid-growth |
| 15–25 | Roughly the long-run S&P average |
| 25–40 | Growth premium — earnings expected to compound fast |
| Over 40 | Hyper-growth or speculative — high expectations baked in |

These aren't absolute. A 40 P/E on a 30%-growth software name is reasonable. A 40 P/E on a stagnant utility is reckless.

## The trap: forward vs trailing P/E

You'll see both:

- **Trailing P/E** uses the last 12 months of *reported* earnings. Real numbers, but backward-looking.
- **Forward P/E** uses analyst *estimates* for the next 12 months. Forward-looking, but only as good as the consensus.

In a slowing economy, forward P/E often looks lower than trailing P/E because analysts haven't cut estimates fast enough yet. Beware: a "cheap" forward P/E based on stale estimates can be a value trap.

## Why your Fundamentals Analyst quotes it

When the Fundamentals Analyst opens a discussion of a name, they typically lead with the P/E vs the sector median:

> *"NVDA trades at 38x trailing, 32x forward. Semiconductor sector median is 22x. So you're paying a 60% growth premium relative to peers."*

That sentence is the P/E doing its job — anchoring the conversation. Whether 60% is too much or fair depends on growth, margins, and the Bull / Bear cases. The P/E doesn't tell you that — it just frames the question.
