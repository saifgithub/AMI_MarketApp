# Momentum trading

Momentum trading buys what has already gone up the most and rotates out when relative strength fades. It is structurally different from trend-following: trend-following waits for an established trend in one name; momentum ranks the entire universe and owns the top decile. The factor has produced one of the most persistent risk premiums in equity research, and it is also responsible for the largest single-day strategy drawdowns on record. After this lesson you can specify a momentum rule that names exactly when you hold and when you rotate, and the regime in which the factor crashes.

## How this plays out in real markets

In 2024 a simple momentum rule was: rank US large-caps by their trailing 6-month return excluding the most recent month, hold the top decile, rebalance monthly. NVDA was in the top decile for nearly the entire year, returning roughly 170% from January through year-end. META, GOOGL, and AMZN cycled in and out at various points. A trader running this rule in early 2024 entered NVDA around $50 (post-split) on the January rebalance and held — never selling on a single down day, only rotating if NVDA dropped out of the top decile at the next monthly rebalance. The rule generated outsized returns precisely because it ignored every "looks expensive" intuition.

Entry criteria: ticker enters the top decile by trailing 6m-1m return at the rebalance date AND volume is liquid enough to size the position within mandate limits. Hold: until the next rebalance. Exit/rotation criteria: ticker drops out of the top decile at rebalance — sell, replace with the new entrant. Individual-trade invalidation: a hard stop at, say, 25% drawdown from cost — this exists not for normal strategy operation but to limit damage during a momentum crash. The strategy FAILS in "momentum crash" regimes — short, violent reversals where high-momentum names get sold and low-momentum names get bought (March 2009, Q1 2016, November 2020 vaccine rotation). On those days a momentum portfolio can lose 15%+ in a week.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is selling momentum winners early because they "feel toppy". You hold NVDA from $50 to $100, it doubles your cost, you take profits, and then watch it run to $140. Or, equally common: you read a critical article on the highest-momentum stock and decide to sell on the qualitative argument. The correct mental model: momentum is a quantitative factor; it runs longer than feels reasonable. The rule sells, not your gut. Discretion at the exit is the single biggest source of underperformance for retail momentum traders. ## Takeaway

Momentum runs longer than feels reasonable, and crashes faster than feels possible. The rule rebalances; you don't. The mandate's drawdown floor is the only override.
