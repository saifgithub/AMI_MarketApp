# How AMI's halal flag actually works

You already know the PM's deterministic compliance check (<Lesson id="271_inside_the_deterministic_compliance_check"/>) is plain Python, not a prompt — same inputs, same answer, every time. The halal Mandate flag rides that same architecture, but here is the part beginners get wrong: it does **not** run the two-stage screen you just learned in <Lesson id="348_the_business_activity_screen"/> and <Lesson id="349_the_three_financial_ratio_screens"/>, and it does not compute a single ratio itself. Instead it checks the ticker against a **sourced** list — the published constituents of an established AAOIFI Sharia index — and returns one of four outcomes. By the end of this lesson you'll be able to state exactly what a halal PASS does and doesn't mean.

## Example

Set the Mandate's halal flag on and Convene the Room. As in <Lesson id="271_inside_the_deterministic_compliance_check"/>, two things happen in sequence: the analyst team debates and produces its Verdict, then — separately, after the debate finishes — the PM's deterministic check runs. For the halal flag, that check looks the ticker up in the constituents of the **S&P 500 Sharia Industry Exclusions Index** — the AAOIFI-standard screen run by S&P Dow Jones and tracked by the SPUS ETF — ingested with the date it was published. Four outcomes:

- The ticker is in that compliant set → **PASS**. (Large names like AAPL, MSFT and NVDA are typically in it.)
- The ticker is in the parent S&P 500 but *not* in the compliant set → **SCREENED OUT**, and the flag blocks the trade. A conventional bank is the classic case.
- The ticker isn't in the S&P 500 at all → **UNKNOWN**: the index rules only on its own members, so AMI has no ruling either way. This is *permitted*, but the trade carries a disclosure that no screen covered it — silence is not a pass.
- The source can't be refreshed, or is stale beyond its window → the flag **PAUSES** and blocks, loudly, rather than trading on a missing or out-of-date list.

None of this computes a debt or income ratio live. It reads someone else's published, dated screen and reports which of the four states applies.

## The trap

The trap is reading a PASS as "AMI ran the three-ratio screen from <Lesson id="348_the_business_activity_screen"/> and <Lesson id="349_the_three_financial_ratio_screens"/> and this company cleared it." It didn't. AMI is not computing that screen here — the ratio math exists in the codebase but stays dormant on this path. A PASS means "this ticker is a current member of a published AAOIFI-screened list, as of a date," nothing more; the list can lag a company's latest filing, and a name can enter or leave it at a rebalance. The subtler trap is treating "not blocked" as "screened and cleared" — an **UNKNOWN** ticker is permitted *precisely because no screen ruled on it*, which is the opposite of cleared. Read the outcome AMI actually returns, not the one you assumed.

## Try it

Toggle the halal flag on in Settings → Mandate, then Convene the Room three times: on a large-cap that is usually compliant (say AAPL), on a conventional bank inside the S&P 500, and on a small-cap outside the S&P 500. Compare the outcome each time — PASS, screened-out-and-blocked, and permitted-but-UNKNOWN — and read the reason the PM cites for each. Keep in mind: AMI's halal flag is a training-loop tool built on a third party's published screen, not a religious ruling or personal compliance advice; take real observance questions to a qualified Islamic-finance scholar.

## Takeaway

AMI's halal flag doesn't compute a Sharia screen — it checks the ticker against a sourced, dated AAOIFI index and returns PASS, screened-out, UNKNOWN, or paused. A PASS is membership on someone else's published list as of a date; an UNKNOWN is permitted but explicitly un-ruled. This is methodology training, not a fatwa — consult a qualified scholar for your own holdings.
