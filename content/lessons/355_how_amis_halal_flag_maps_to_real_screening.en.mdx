# How AMI's halal flag maps to real screening

You already know the PM's deterministic compliance check (lesson 271) is plain Python, not a prompt — same inputs, same PASS/BLOCK answer, every time. The halal Mandate flag runs that exact discipline against the two-stage screen you've now learned in full: business activity (lesson 2) first, then the three financial ratios (lesson 3), each computed the same way `sharia_screen` computes them. When you toggle the halal flag on, you are wiring a real, published screening methodology into the PM's uncoachable floor — not a vibe check the underlying language models are asked to apply. By the end of this lesson you'll be able to trace a ticker through the halal flag exactly as the PM does.

## Example

Set the Mandate's halal flag to on and Convene the Room on a real ticker. Two things happen in sequence, mirroring lesson 271's architecture: first, the analyst team debates and produces its usual training Verdict (Bull case, Bear case, technical read, size, entry, stop). Second — entirely separately, after the debate finishes — the PM's deterministic check runs the ticker's business activity and three ratios through the same screening logic this whole module has covered, and returns PASS or BLOCK. If the ticker is, say, a conventional bank or a company whose debt ratio exceeds 33% of market cap, the Verdict is blocked regardless of how bullish the analyst debate was — exactly the uncoachability lesson 273 described: the halal check isn't a persuadable opinion inside a prompt, it's a comparison against a threshold that runs after the debate, immune to how convincing the Bull Researcher's argument was.

## The trap

Beginners assume the halal flag is a keyword filter — "block anything with 'bank' or 'brewery' in the sector tag" — because that's the mental model a simple content filter would suggest. That undersells it. The correct mental model, now that you've done lessons 2 and 3: the halal flag runs the *actual two-stage screen* — qualitative business-activity exclusion, then the three quantitative ratios — computed the same way a real Sharia-compliance analyst would compute them, not a sector-keyword heuristic. A company can pass a naive "no excluded sector keyword" check and still fail on the ratio screen (lesson 3's exact lesson) — the halal flag's PASS/BLOCK reflects the full screen, not a shortcut version of it.

## Try it

Toggle the halal flag on in Settings → Mandate, then Convene the Room on a ticker you suspect might be borderline on debt. Read the PM's PASS/BLOCK reasoning and check whether it cites business activity, a specific ratio, or both.

## Takeaway

The halal Mandate flag isn't a sector-keyword filter — it's the PM's uncoachable deterministic check running the exact two-stage screen (business activity, then three ratios) this module taught, after the debate finishes.
