# How AMI's halal flag actually works

You already know the PM's deterministic compliance check (<Lesson id="271_inside_the_deterministic_compliance_check"/>) is plain Python, not a prompt — same inputs, same PASS/BLOCK answer, every time. The halal Mandate flag uses that same deterministic-after-the-debate architecture, but its actual check today is simpler than the two-stage screen you just learned in <Lesson id="348_the_business_activity_screen"/> and <Lesson id="349_the_three_financial_ratio_screens"/>: it looks up whether the ticker is a member of a small, fixed, curated set — nothing is computed, no business activity is evaluated, and no ratio is calculated on this path. By the end of this lesson you'll be able to state exactly what the halal flag checks in the app today, and why that's a different thing from the screening methodology this module teaches.

## Example

Set the Mandate's halal flag to on and Convene the Room on a real ticker. Two things happen in sequence, mirroring <Lesson id="271_inside_the_deterministic_compliance_check"/>'s architecture: first, the analyst team debates and produces its usual training Verdict (Bull case, Bear case, technical read, size, entry, stop). Second — entirely separately, after the debate finishes — the PM's deterministic check runs, and for the halal flag that check is a membership test against a fixed seven-ticker list (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, GOOGL, META, TSLA, AMZN). Convene on one of those seven and the flag passes. Convene on anything else — say, a conventional bank, or even a large tech name that isn't on the list — and the flag blocks the trade, regardless of that ticker's actual business activity or balance sheet, and regardless of how bullish the analyst debate was. The block (or pass) is driven purely by list membership, not by any of the business-activity or ratio reasoning <Lesson id="348_the_business_activity_screen"/> and <Lesson id="349_the_three_financial_ratio_screens"/> taught you — that reasoning is real and it's what a genuine Sharia screen runs on, it just isn't what this flag checks.

## The trap

Beginners assume that because the flag sits behind the same uncoachable architecture as the rest of the PM's compliance check, it must be *running* the two-stage screen this module covers — business activity, then the three ratios, computed live per ticker. It doesn't. Today the halal flag is a curated demonstration allowlist: seven large, liquid, widely-recognized tickers chosen so the training loop always has a workable "halal" universe to trade inside, not seven tickers that were run through <Lesson id="348_the_business_activity_screen"/>'s and <Lesson id="349_the_three_financial_ratio_screens"/>'s tests and happened to pass. A real screen exists in AMI's codebase and is exactly what those two lessons describe — but it isn't wired to this flag's enforcement path. Don't infer "the flag ran the screen and this ticker passed" from a PASS; infer "this ticker happens to be one of the seven on the list."

## Try it

Toggle the halal flag on in Settings → Mandate, then Convene the Room on a ticker from the seven-name list (say, AAPL) and separately on one that isn't (say, a conventional bank). Compare the PM's PASS/BLOCK reasoning on each — notice it cites list membership, not a business-activity or ratio finding. Keep in mind: AMI's halal flag is a training-loop demonstration tool today, not a religious judgment or a live compliance screen; real screening and compliance questions should involve a qualified Islamic finance professional.

## Takeaway

The halal Mandate flag runs the PM's uncoachable deterministic check after the debate finishes — same architecture as the rest of Brief Your Agent — but today that check is membership in a fixed, curated set of seven tickers, not the business-activity-and-ratio screen taught earlier in this module. This is methodology training, not a Sharia ruling; consult a qualified scholar for real portfolio decisions.
