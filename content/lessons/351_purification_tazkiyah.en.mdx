# Purification (tazkiyah)

Even a stock that clears both the business-activity screen and the three financial-ratio screens can carry a small sliver of impermissible income inside it — interest earned on the company's own cash holdings, for instance. *Tazkiyah* (purification) is the practice of calculating that sliver's share of a dividend received and donating that exact amount away, rather than keeping it. It is a per-dividend calculation, computed from the company's own disclosed non-compliant-income ratio, never a hand-waved estimate. By the end of this lesson you'll be able to compute a purification amount from real inputs and explain why it applies even to a stock that already passed every screen.

## Example

Take a hypothetical balance sheet shaped like a large, screen-passing tech company — the kind that clears the business and ratio screens comfortably but still holds a cash pile earning some interest. Say its non-compliant (interest) income is **$700M** against total income of **$100,000M**, and you received a dividend of **$960.00** from your holding this quarter. Run this through AMI's `purification_amount` function: (700 / 100,000) × 960.00 = **$6.72 exactly**. That $6.72 — 0.7% of the dividend, matching the company's own impermissible-income share — is the amount purified away, typically donated to charity without expectation of tax benefit or spiritual credit for the donor, since it was never legitimately theirs to keep in the first place. The remaining $953.28 of the dividend is retained as ordinary income. Note the ratio driving this (0.7%, from non-compliant income over total income) is a different figure from the *income ratio* in lesson 3's screen (which compares non-compliant income to total *revenue*, not total income) — related concepts, distinct denominators.

## The trap

Beginners assume that once a stock passes the Sharia screen, its dividends are automatically "clean" and need no further step. That's the exact gap purification exists to close: passing the screen (lesson 3's ratio caps) tells you the company's impermissible income is *small enough to tolerate holding the stock at all* — it does not mean that small sliver of impermissible income disappears. The correct mental model: screening decides whether you may hold the stock; purification decides what fraction of what you're paid from it, you must not keep. Skipping purification because "it already passed the screen" conflates two separate steps that solve two separate problems.

## Takeaway

A screen-passing stock can still owe purification on its dividends — compute the impermissible-income share exactly, and treat it as never legitimately yours to keep.
