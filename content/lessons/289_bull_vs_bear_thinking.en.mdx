# Bull vs Bear Thinking

Every stock has at least two coherent stories: a bull case and a bear case. A good investor can articulate **both** before forming a view. Knowing this discipline is what separates research from rationalisation.

## Why two stories, not one

When you only know the case for buying, you don't actually know whether you should buy. You know what the *bullish* observer sees. You need the bearish observer too — what would have to be true for you to be wrong?

This is why AMI Trade ships a **Bull Researcher** *and* a **Bear Researcher** as separate agents. They're not optional perspectives. They're the structural debate.

## What a good bull case looks like

A serious bull case isn't "I think the stock will go up." It's specific:

1. **Thesis in one sentence.** "Hyperscaler AI capex will drive 30%+ revenue growth for NVDA through 2027."
2. **Evidence backing the thesis.** Capex commentary from the Big 4 hyperscalers. Order backlogs. Margin trends.
3. **What the market is missing.** Why is the bull case mispriced right now? "Consensus models AI capex tapering in 2026; the bull case is it accelerates."
4. **Sizing implication.** Given the thesis and risks, how much should you commit?
5. **Where you'd be wrong.** What evidence would invalidate the thesis? "Hyperscaler capex cut by 20%+ in any single guide."

The last point is non-negotiable. **A bull case without falsifiability is a religion.**

## What a good bear case looks like

The bear isn't a doom-monger. The bear is a precision tool:

1. **Risk in one sentence.** "Multiple compression risk if AI capex disappoints — NVDA at 38x can re-rate to 20x in a quarter."
2. **Quantification.** Don't say "the stock could fall." Say "20-point multiple compression = ~50% downside."
3. **Catalysts to watch.** What specifically would trigger the de-rating? Earnings, hyperscaler guidance cuts, China demand drop.
4. **Position-sizing impact.** A 50% drawdown risk in a 10% position is a 5% portfolio hit. Is that OK?
5. **What would invalidate the bear case.** "If TSMC raises AI-capacity guide again next quarter, the supply-side bear case is dead."

A real Bear Researcher doesn't argue the company is doomed. They argue the *current price* doesn't reflect the *full risk*. That's a much harder, sharper position.

## The Research Manager's job

When the Bull and Bear have both built their cases, the Research Manager synthesises:

- Which case is stronger today, *at the current price*?
- What are the asymmetries? (A 30% upside vs 50% downside is bad asymmetry even if you think the bull case is right.)
- How does the user's mandate constrain sizing?
- What's the *position* — not just the view?

This is the moment a "buy" becomes a "buy 3% with a stop at $148" — opinion turning into action.

## A trap: the asymmetric-strawman bear case

Beware the Bear Researcher who knocks down a strawman — "what if AI is overhyped" — and then declares victory. A real bear case has to engage with the strongest version of the bull case, not its weakest version.

If your Bear Researcher isn't *steel-manning* the bull case before refuting it, push back. (You can brief them to do that — see <Term id="ami_coach_your_agent" />.)
