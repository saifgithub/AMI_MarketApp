# RSI hidden divergence — the continuation signal

Lesson 027 taught regular RSI divergence — price makes a new high, RSI makes a lower high, momentum is weakening, expect reversal. Hidden divergence is its mirror image. In an up-trend, hidden bullish divergence is when price makes a higher low but RSI makes a lower low. That sounds bad on the surface — until you see what it means: price pulled back shallowly, but RSI swung deep. The momentum reset was bigger than the price reset, which means the trend has fresh fuel. Hidden divergence is a trend-continuation signal, not a reversal. Recognising the difference between regular and hidden divergence is the difference between fading the trend and riding it.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at AMZN through Q4 2025. From mid-September to mid-October, AMZN climbed from 178 to 195 with daily RSI(14) peaks at 71, 68, and 72 — a textbook up-trend. The first pullback came October 22: price dipped to 188 (higher than the September swing low at 183), and RSI(14) reached 41 — lower than the September swing low's RSI reading of 47.

That's hidden bullish divergence. Price held its higher-low structure; RSI swung deeper than the prior reset. The deeper RSI reset said momentum was being recharged for the next leg, not exhausted. AMZN bounced to 211 over the next six weeks.

Falsification is mechanical — hidden bullish divergence invalidates if price closes below the higher low that anchored the pattern. If AMZN had closed below 188 within 5–8 sessions, the higher-low structure breaks and the continuation signal cancels.

Contrast that with regular bearish divergence on the same chart later: in February 2026, AMZN printed a new high at 224 but RSI(14) only made 64 (vs 72 at the prior high). Regular divergence at a high = reversal warning. Same indicator, opposite trade.

On Bursa Malaysia: PETRONAS in mid-2024 — price pulled back to a higher low at RM6.80 while RSI reached a deeper low at 38 (vs the prior pullback's RSI of 44). The trend continued to RM7.40 over the next four weeks. Hidden bullish divergence, textbook continuation.

## The trap

The trap is conflating the two divergence types and trading them the wrong way around. Regular divergence (price higher high, RSI lower high) at a market top says reverse. Hidden divergence (price higher low, RSI lower low) inside a trend says continue. They look superficially similar — both involve price and RSI disagreeing — but they signal opposite trades. Beginners see "divergence" and short the trend, exiting strong continuation entries.

The correct mental model: divergence direction depends on what's making the new extreme. If price makes the new extreme and RSI doesn't, that's regular (reversal). If RSI makes the new extreme and price doesn't, that's hidden (continuation). One sentence: "Whichever made the new extreme is the one signalling against the move." That mental shortcut is the entire difference between the two patterns. Use hidden divergence only in confirmed trends — in a sideways market, both readings are noise.

## Takeaway

Hidden RSI divergence (price higher low, RSI lower low) is a continuation signal inside a trend — recognise it as the mirror image of regular divergence, and only act on it when a trend is already in place.
