# Multi-timeframe trend confirmation

The trend-following foundation (052) judged trend on one timeframe. The cleanest setups are confirmed on three — a higher timeframe that defines the dominant direction, a trading timeframe that gives entry context, and a lower timeframe that times the trigger. The principle is simple: don't take a daily-chart long when the weekly is rolling over, and don't pick the exact entry candle on the daily when a 60-minute bar would give it to you with half the risk. After this lesson you can write a three-timeframe rule that names what must align before a trend entry is valid, and what disqualifies it instantly.

## Example

Look at AMZN through February to June 2024. On the weekly chart, the 20-week and 40-week MAs both sloped up from mid-2023 — a clear higher-timeframe uptrend. On the daily chart through February, the 20-day MA also sloped up and price held above the 50-day; trading-timeframe alignment. On May 3, after Q1 earnings, AMZN broke out on the daily from a four-week base between $178 and $189 and closed at $186.21. Zooming into the 60-minute chart, price closed above the prior consolidation high of $189 at 10:30 a.m. EST on May 3 on a strong volume bar — that was the lower-timeframe trigger inside the daily-breakout candle. Entry on the 60-minute close near $189, stop under the 60-minute pullback low at $182. Daily target: the prior range high projection near $200, which printed three sessions later. Risk per share: $7. Reward at first target: $11 — a 1.6R win with a much tighter stop than waiting for an end-of-day daily entry would have given.

Entry criteria: (1) weekly trend up — price above rising 40-week MA, (2) daily trend up — price above rising 50-day MA, (3) lower-timeframe trigger fires inside the daily candle (60-minute close above consolidation high, or 4-hour breakout retest). Stop: under the lower-timeframe pullback low. Individual-trade invalidation: a daily close that nullifies the daily-trend leg (close below 50-day) OR the lower-timeframe trigger fails (price closes back below the trigger level on the next 60-minute bar) — exit. The strategy FAILS when the three timeframes disagree (weekly up, daily down, hourly chopping) — that's a regime transition where you're picking entries inside a confused tape. Skip.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is using lower timeframes for confirmation instead of for timing. You start on the daily, see weakness, drop to the 60-minute looking for "a reason to still be bullish", and find one — because at any moment a lower timeframe is somewhere in a micro-trend. That's confirmation bias dressed as analysis. The correct mental model: higher timeframes set direction, lower timeframes only refine entry. If the daily disagrees with the weekly, you don't take the trade. If the 60-minute disagrees with the daily, you don't take the trade. Lower timeframes can never override higher ones; they can only choose where on the daily candle you fire.

## Try it

<Term id="ami_convene_the_room" /> on a ticker you're considering and explicitly ask: "Walk me through the trend state on weekly, daily, and 60-minute timeframes. State which of the three currently disagree, if any." If all three align, ask the Trader to convert the lowest-timeframe trigger into a precise entry, stop, and invalidation. Log the three-timeframe state in the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> so the next post-mortem can audit whether your alignment check was honest.

## Takeaway

Higher timeframes set direction; lower timeframes refine entry — never the other way around. Three-timeframe alignment is the price of admission; conflict is the signal to skip.
