# Stops around earnings and event risk

This lesson assumes you've completed <Lesson id="015_stop_loss_basics"/> (Stop-loss basics); here we go deeper on a specific failure mode — stops do not protect you across a binary event. Earnings, Fed decisions, court rulings, FDA outcomes, and elections can gap a stock past your stop overnight. The next print is the open the morning after, which may be 15% to 25% away from your stop level. Your loss is whatever the open shows, not what you wrote on the ticket. This lesson teaches the three legitimate responses — size down through events, exit before the event, or accept the gap risk explicitly — and the one illegitimate response: pretending the stop will protect you.

## How this plays out in real markets

You're long NVDA at $480 with a stop at $458. NVDA reports earnings after the close. The chart-based stop is structurally sound for normal trading days. Earnings prints: revenue miss, guidance cut. NVDA halts in the after-hours, reopens pre-market at $412 — $46 below your stop. Your stop becomes a market order at the open and you fill near $410. Per-share loss: $70, not the $22 you planned. The trade lost 3.2% of account, not 1%. The stop did not fail mechanically — it filled exactly as designed; the gap simply skipped past it before any liquidity existed at $458.

Contrast: same NVDA setup, same $480 entry. The Trader flags earnings in 4 trading days. You have three options. Option 1: halve the position before the print so the gap risk is 0.5% account, not 1%. Option 2: exit fully before the close on the earnings day and re-enter the next morning once the gap resolves. Option 3: keep full size and accept the gap risk explicitly in the journal. On Bursa Malaysia the same logic on PETRONAS around quarterly results — a typical earnings gap on a Bursa name is 4–8%, smaller than US tech but still capable of skipping a 2% stop. Falsifiable test: pull any 10 large-cap earnings prints from the last quarter — at least 3 of them gapped through any stop placed within 5% of the prior close.

## The trap

The trap is treating the stop as insurance against everything. A beginner takes a $480 NVDA entry the day before earnings, "stop at $458", and goes to bed thinking max loss is 1% of account. The next morning the position is open 14% lower than the stop and the account is down 4%. The stop did not fail — it had nothing to fill against between $458 and $412. The correct mental model: stops protect against continuous price action, not against gaps. Before any binary event, you must explicitly decide one of three things — size down, exit, or accept the gap risk and journal that decision. Doing nothing is the same as accepting full gap risk without admitting it; that is how a 1% mandate gets converted into a 4% loss the trader didn't see coming.

## Try it

Open the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" />, filter to open positions, and check each ticker's earnings calendar (the Trader can pull it in <Term id="ami_one_on_one" />). For any position with earnings in the next 5 trading days, ask the Trader: "Should I halve, exit, or hold through? What's the typical gap range on this ticker?" Then journal the decision before the event so future-you can see how the choice played out.

## Takeaway

Stops protect against price action, not gaps — before any binary event, pick halve, exit, or accept-the-gap, and journal the choice.
