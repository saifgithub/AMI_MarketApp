# Hidden costs in risk/reward: spread, slippage, fees

This lesson assumes you've completed <Lesson id="016_risk_reward_ratio"/> (Risk/reward ratio); here we go deeper on the costs that erode that ratio between when you write the ticket and when you collect the realised gain. The R:R you compute on paper assumes you fill at the entry price, fill at the target price, and pay nothing in between. In real markets, you cross the spread twice, you slip on stops during fast moves, and on live brokers you pay fees per side. On a 1:2 setup with $50 of risk per share, $5 of total friction is 10% of risk eaten before the trade even resolves. This lesson teaches how to subtract those costs before you accept a setup.

## How this plays out in real markets

A <Term id="simulated_trade" /> on AMZN: entry $185, stop $180, target $200. Paper R:R = 15 ÷ 5 = 3.0. Now layer the friction. Bid–ask spread on AMZN intraday: typically $0.02. You pay half the spread on entry, half on exit — say $0.02 round-trip. Slippage on a market stop in a fast move: 0.1% to 0.3% past your stop level on a normal day, more on news — call it $0.30. Commissions in a live broker (not in AMI's simulation): $0.005 per share each side, so $0.01 round-trip. Total friction: $0.33 per share. Net risk = $5.33, net reward (target filled at $199.98) = $14.98. Realised R:R = 14.98 ÷ 5.33 = 2.81 — close to 3.0, friction roughly trims one-third of an R-multiple off the planned outcome.

Now consider a small-cap. AIRASIA on Bursa: entry RM2.40, stop RM2.30 (risk RM0.10), target RM2.60 (reward RM0.20), paper R:R 2.0. Typical spread RM0.005, slippage on a stop RM0.02, brokerage 0.1% of notional ≈ RM0.0024 per share. Round-trip friction: roughly RM0.027 per share — that is 27% of the planned risk eroded by costs alone. Realised R:R drops to about (0.20 − 0.027) ÷ (0.10 + 0.027) = 0.173 ÷ 0.127 = 1.36. Falsifiable test: take any 20 small-cap trades from your journal and compute planned vs realised R:R — small-cap setups with paper R:R under 2.0 routinely realise sub-1.5 once friction is included.

## The trap

The trap is treating a 1.2:1 R:R setup as positive expectancy. The paper math looks fine — you make $1.20 for every $1 risked — but once you bake in spread + slippage + (in live markets) fees, your realised R:R can drop to 0.9 or lower. The setup is then negative expectancy regardless of win rate. The correct mental model: subtract friction from reward and add friction to risk before computing the ratio. A useful minimum is "paper R:R must be at least 1.5× the threshold you'd accept on net" — so if you want a net 1:2, demand a paper 1:3. This is why the Trader inside AMI defaults to a 1.5 minimum even though the math says 1.0 is the break-even line; the buffer absorbs friction.

## Try it

Open the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> and filter to closed trades. For each, compute paper R:R (planned target ÷ planned stop distance) vs realised R:R (actual exit price ÷ actual entry price). Then open a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Trader and ask "Across my last 20 trades, what's the average gap between paper R:R and realised R:R?" The gap is the friction tax — once you know its size, you set your paper minimum above it.

## Takeaway

Paper R:R is the brochure number; realised R:R is the receipt — subtract friction before you accept the setup, not after the trade closes.
