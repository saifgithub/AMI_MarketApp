# Equal-weight vs vol-adjusted strategy weights — picking the scheme

You now have three weighting schemes for combining strategies: equal-dollar (the default), risk-parity (covered in 240), and regime-rotation (covered in 239). This lesson is the decision framework — when to use each, and how to combine them. The honest answer is that there's no universally best scheme; each has a regime where it dominates and a regime where it underperforms. Equal-dollar wins when strategies have similar vols and expected returns. Risk-parity wins when one strategy is much more volatile than others and mandate cap is tight. Regime-rotation wins when failure-regime indicators are reliably observable. After this lesson you can choose the right scheme for your portfolio AND combine them when sensible (e.g., risk-parity weights within a regime-rotation overlay).

## How this plays out in real markets

Three strategies on US large-caps over 2010-2024:
- Trend-following (vol 11%, CAGR 9.2%)
- Mean-reversion (vol 8%, CAGR 11.4%)
- Momentum (vol 18%, CAGR 10.8%)

Compare four weighting schemes:

| Scheme | CAGR | Vol | Max DD | Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equal-dollar | 10.4% | 12.8% | 14% | 0.81 |
| Risk-parity | 9.3% | 9.2% | 9% | 1.01 |
| Regime-rotation (equal-dollar within regime) | 11.2% | 11.1% | 9% | 1.01 |
| Risk-parity within regime-rotation | 10.6% | 8.4% | 7% | 1.26 |

The last scheme — combining risk-parity for relative weighting with regime-rotation for total exposure — dominates on Sharpe (1.26) and drawdown (7%), trading a small amount of CAGR (10.6% vs 11.2% pure regime-rotation) for substantially lower vol. For a mandate with a tight drawdown cap (e.g., 15% or lower), this is the right choice. For a mandate with a high cap and a CAGR target, pure regime-rotation may be better.

For Bursa-focused traders with fewer strategies and fewer regimes to detect, equal-dollar is often the right starting point — the complexity of risk-parity and regime-rotation overlays needs enough strategy diversity to justify it. With two strategies, the choice is largely between equal-dollar and a simple risk-parity, with regime-rotation likely overfitting on too few strategies.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is picking the scheme that has the highest backtested Sharpe and deploying it without thinking about why. Each scheme has assumptions: equal-dollar assumes similar vol and return profiles, risk-parity assumes stable correlations, regime-rotation assumes the regime indicators are robust and not overfit. The correct mental model: pick the scheme whose assumptions you can defend in your specific situation. The second trap is layering too many overlays — risk-parity within regime-rotation within a volatility-target overlay within a mandate cap. Each overlay adds complexity, transaction costs, and overfitting risk. The third trap: switching schemes when the current one is underperforming. Every scheme has periods where another would have done better; cycling between them is the meta-version of chasing returns and almost always destroys value.

## Takeaway

There is no universally best weighting scheme — pick the one whose assumptions match your portfolio, validate via walk-forward, and don't switch under stress. Complexity helps only when each layer earns its keep.
