# Discipline vs excitement

Discipline is the trader who follows the plan when the plan is boring. Excitement is the trader who deviates when the plan is exciting. After this lesson you will be able to tell the two apart in real time, name the agent on <Term id="ami" />'s team whose job is to refuse to be exciting, and configure the Portfolio Manager's mandate so the <Term id="ami_pm_safety_floor" /> is the deterministic backstop against every emotion the prior six lessons described. The goal is not to feel less. It is to make the feeling irrelevant to the position size.

## How this plays out in real markets

A trader had a written process: 1% per-trade risk, max 5 open positions, 2-hour cooldown after a stop, max 6 trades per week, and a <Term id="ami_mandate" />-enforced max drawdown of 20%. In a hot Q1 he watched his peers compound 35% on TSLA and NVDA momentum trades while his disciplined book returned a quiet 7%. Excitement won the argument; he removed the trade-count cap, raised per-trade risk to 3%, and disabled the cooldown "because the regime is trending". The next month brought a sharp 9-day pullback that should have cost him 3% on his original mandate. It cost 14% — and the PM's safety floor was the only thing that prevented it becoming a 22% account event. Three months later, on a calm Sunday, he restored every setting he had disabled. His written returns post-restoration matched his pre-disabling returns. The only thing the excitement-month had added was the 14% hole.

The falsifiable pattern: you are running on excitement, not discipline, if in the last 14 days you have loosened *any* mandate parameter (per-trade risk, max positions, cooldown, max trades, max drawdown) without a corresponding written change in strategy criteria, and the loosening happened during or immediately after a winning streak or a fear-of-missing-out spike. One change, no documented reason, suspicious timing. That's the signature.

## The trap

The trap is the belief that the mandate is too conservative *for this moment*. Every emotion in this module — greed, fear, revenge, FOMO, overconfidence, boredom — speaks in this exact sentence. The mandate is always too conservative for this moment, because the mandate is calibrated for *every* moment, including the one you have not yet seen. The Portfolio Manager's role is to be the version of you that does not have a stomach. Its safety floor is uncoachable on purpose: the system refuses to let any single emotional moment outvote the trader who wrote the rules. The correct mental model: discipline is not the absence of excitement. Discipline is excitement that does not touch the position size. Feel it, journal it, take the setup the plan permits, decline the one it doesn't. The P&L over a year is overwhelmingly determined by how many bad trades you didn't take, not how many good ones you did.

## Try it

Open Settings → Mandate and review every parameter: `max_per_trade_risk_pct`, `max_open_positions`, `cooldown_after_stop_minutes`, `max_trades_per_week`, `max_drawdown_pct`. For each one, ask: have I changed this in the last 14 days, and is the change documented in the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> with a written criteria reason? If any answer is "changed, no reason", restore the prior value before the next session. Then start a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Portfolio Manager and ask: "Run my mandate against my last 30 trades — where would the safety floor have flagged?"

## Takeaway

Discipline is excitement that does not touch the position size. The Portfolio Manager's safety floor is the version of you that does not have a stomach — let it vote.
