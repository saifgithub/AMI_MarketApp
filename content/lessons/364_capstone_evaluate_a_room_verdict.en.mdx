# Capstone: evaluate one Room verdict end to end

You now hold the whole EVAL toolkit: judging the decision rather than the result, reading a confidence number as a scorable claim, writing the thesis before the outcome exists, arguing the strongest opposing case, assuming failure in advance, separating process error from variance, and tracking your own hit rate. This capstone puts that toolkit to work on one training Verdict as six steps in sequence — read, check, steelman, pre-mortem, journal, decide — and records the reasoning whether you accept or override. Nothing new is introduced here; the skill is making the pieces interact on a single artifact under time pressure.

## Example

Take a hypothetical mid-cap industrial — no real ticker, invented figures, so the numbers are the lesson rather than a market claim. The Room returns: **BUY · size 3% · entry $50 · stop $44 · target $62 · horizon 8 weeks · confidence 0.68 · PM: PASS**.

**Read it.** PASS is a compliance result, not an endorsement — see <Lesson id="277_pass_is_not_buy"/>. Two independent statements arrived together; treat them separately.

**Check the numbers.** The geometry is checkable in ten seconds: risk per share $50 − $44 = **$6** (12% of entry), reward $62 − $50 = **$12**, so **2.0 to 1**. At 3% size, a clean stop-out costs 0.03 × 0.12 = **0.36% of the portfolio**. Then audit the load-bearing claim underneath — if the Bull case rests on one cash-flow figure, that figure is the one to verify, per <Lesson id="276_spotting_hallucinated_numbers"/>.

**Steelman the opposite.** State the sharpest bear case in its strongest form, not a version you can knock over.

**Pre-mortem.** It is eight weeks later and the position stopped out. Write why — before you know.

**Journal it.** Thesis, the 0.68 confidence, the reward-to-risk, and one invalidation condition specific enough to fire on its own: *close below $44, or the cash-flow claim failing verification.*

**Decide.** Accept or override — and record which, and why, in the same entry. An unrecorded override is indistinguishable later from an unrecorded accept.

## The trap

The trap is performing all six steps and then treating the performance itself as the answer. It shows up in one place: the invalidation condition. "If the thesis breaks" cannot fire — nothing observable triggers it, so eight weeks later you will interpret whatever happened as consistent with what you wrote. "Close below $44" fires or it doesn't, and the journal grades you rather than comforting you. The same failure hides inside the override: writing "overrode on gut" records the act but not the reasoning, so the post-mortem has nothing to separate a genuine read from an unearned one. The process is only worth its cost when each step produces something falsifiable.

## The steelman

The strongest case against this sequence: it is expensive, and here it changed nothing — you read, checked, steelmanned, pre-mortemed, journalled, then accepted the 3% BUY exactly as proposed. Six steps, same position; on a fast setup that latency buys no observable difference. The falsifier is measurable from your own record, but not quickly. Splitting logged verdicts into examined and unexamined arms and comparing hit rates needs far more decisions than it feels like it should: at thirty verdicts you have roughly fifteen an arm and an interval wide enough to hide any real effect, so "indistinguishable" there means *not yet resolved*, not *ritual* — the small-sample trap <Lesson id="363_scoring_yourself_over_time"/> exists to name. The reading available sooner is not a rate at all: count how often the pre-mortem named an invalidation that later fired and would otherwise have surprised you. That is observable on a single decision rather than only in aggregate. If, across many decisions, it never names anything that fires, the sequence is ritual and should be cut down. Note the test needs the journal to exist first — skipping the process destroys the evidence that would settle whether skipping it was right.

## Try it

Open the Decision Journal and find your most recent entry. Check one thing: is the invalidation condition specific enough that something in the market could fire it without your interpretation? If not, rewrite it as a price, a level, or a datapoint. Then Convene the Room on a watchlist ticker and run the full sequence once, timing yourself. Remember: the Verdict is a training artifact for you to evaluate, not investment advice.

## Takeaway

Read, check the numbers, steelman, pre-mortem, journal with an invalidation condition that can actually fire, then decide — and record the reasoning whether you accept or override, because the entry you skip is the one your future scoring needs most.
