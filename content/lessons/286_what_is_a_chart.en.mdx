# What Is a Chart?

A stock chart is a picture of one number — the price — repeated over time. Everything technical analysts do is some interpretation of that picture.

## The two main types

### Line chart

Connects closing prices day-by-day. Smooth, easy to read, hides intraday detail. Useful for spotting long-term trends.

### Candlestick chart

Each "candle" is one period (often a day) and shows four numbers: open, high, low, close.

- **Body** — colored. Green if close > open, red if close < open.
- **Wicks** — thin lines above and below. The high and the low.

A long green body with short wicks = strong buying pressure all day. A small body with long wicks = indecision, lots of fighting between buyers and sellers.

Candlesticks are the default for active traders because they pack four data points into one mark.

## Timeframe matters

The same stock looks completely different at different timeframes:

| Timeframe | What you see |
|---|---|
| 1 minute | Pure noise. Most movement is order-flow randomness. |
| 1 hour | Intraday rhythm. Useful for day traders. |
| Daily | The default. Most analysis happens here. |
| Weekly | Trend. Cleaner, slower, fewer false signals. |
| Monthly | The long sweep. Where multi-year stories live. |

A Technical Strategist arguing for a swing trade will lean on the daily and weekly. A long-horizon investor barely looks below monthly.

## What charts can and can't tell you

| Can | Can't |
|---|---|
| Show where price has been | Tell you where it's going |
| Reveal support / resistance levels | Reveal *why* those levels exist |
| Show momentum and trend | Replace fundamentals |
| Time entries and exits | Pick winners on their own |

Technical analysis is **conditional**: *"if the chart breaks above $X with volume, then the odds favour continuation."* Never *"this stock will go up."*

## Volume — the second number on every chart

Below the price you'll see a bar showing **volume** — shares traded that period. Volume is the chart's lie detector.

A big move on huge volume = many participants agreeing. A big move on thin volume = a few people pushing price around. Volume confirms or denies the price action.

Your Technical Strategist will mention volume often:

> *"The break above $150 came on 2× average volume. That's real."*
> *"The pullback to $145 happened on falling volume. Sellers don't have conviction."*
