# Market breadth

Breadth is the answer to a single question: when the index moves, how many stocks moved with it? A bull regime led by 8 mega-caps is structurally different from a bull regime where 70% of NYSE issues are above their 200-day SMA — and the second one survives shocks the first one doesn't. After this lesson you will be able to detect breadth numerically (advance/decline, new-highs vs new-lows, % of stocks above 200-day SMA), name what breadth tells you about regime durability, name the strategies that fail when breadth diverges, and state the invalidation condition for a breadth-confirmed move.

## How this plays out in real markets

Two contrasting cases inside the same SPX bull:

Narrow-breadth advance (Q1 2024): SPX gained 10.2% from 1 January to 31 March 2024 and closed at 5,254 on 28 March 2024. But only ~38% of NYSE issues were above their 200-day SMA at that high, vs 70%+ in healthy prior bull peaks. The advance was carried by 7 mega-cap tech names. McClellan A/D oscillator readings on SPX were repeatedly *lower* on new index highs — a textbook bearish breadth divergence.

Broad-breadth advance (Q4 2024): from late October to early December 2024, SPX rallied from 5,750 to 6,090 while the % of NYSE issues above their 200-day SMA expanded from 58% to 73%, NYSE new 52-week highs exceeded new 52-week lows by an average of 250+ issues per session for 7 straight weeks, and the Russell 2000 (IWM) outperformed SPX by 4.5%. Small caps participating + advance/decline broad = high-quality regime.

Detection rule: a "broad" bull is in force when % of NYSE stocks above their 200-day SMA > 60% AND new 52-week highs exceed new 52-week lows for 5 of the last 7 weeks AND IWM is keeping pace with or outperforming SPY over 50 days. A "narrow" advance (≥3 weeks of new SPX highs while % of issues above 200-day SMA < 50%) is a breadth-divergence signal — durability concern, not a sell signal in isolation.

On Bursa Malaysia: similar principle on the KLCI Advance/Decline line; when KLCI rises but A/D is flat, the rally is concentrated in heavyweights and is structurally thinner.

## The trap

The breadth trap is reading the headline index number and assuming "the market" is doing whatever it shows. In Q1 2024, an investor seeing SPX hit a new high every other week reasonably assumed strength was broad — and concentrated into the most-extended mega-caps as the "obvious" winners. When the August 2024 vol-shock hit, those mega-caps drew down 12–20% while the average S&P 500 stock dropped only 6%. The trader's "diversified" portfolio of 5 mega-caps drew down like a single sector because that's what it actually was.

The correct model: breadth is a regime-quality scoreboard. Narrow advances are not necessarily fatal — many run for months — but they require smaller position sizes, more diversification, and a closer-watched invalidation. Broad advances earn their mandate-permitted size.

## Takeaway

Breadth is the quality grade on the regime: a broad advance earns full mandate-permitted size, a narrow one earns smaller positions and a closer invalidation — same index print, different prescription.
