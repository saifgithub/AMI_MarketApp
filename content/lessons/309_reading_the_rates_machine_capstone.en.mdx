# Capstone: reading the rates machine

You now hold the full M13 toolkit: what a bond actually is, how price and yield move inversely, how duration measures rate sensitivity, what an inverted yield curve says about growth expectations, how credit spreads price default risk, and how the discount rate reaches all the way into equity valuations. This capstone runs a single surprise rate shock through all of it — the kind of one-day event where a bond desk, a credit desk, and an equity desk are all repricing the same fact at once. The skill being tested: seeing one Fed surprise ripple through four different markets via one shared mechanism.

## One scenario, every M13 concept

A 20-year corporate bond, face value $1,000, 4.5% coupon (semiannual), was issued at par — priced at exactly **$1,000.00** when the market's required yield was 4.5%. Then the Fed delivers a surprise: a 100 basis point hike, pushing the market's required yield on this bond to 5.5%. The bond instantly reprices to **$879.61** — a $120.39 drop, 12.0% of its value, on a single rate announcement, with nothing about the issuer's business changing at all. Duration explains why the move is that large: modified duration at issue (4.5% YTM) is **13.1 years**, so the linear estimate predicts roughly 100bp × 13.1 ≈ 13.1% — close to the actual 12.0% drop, with convexity making the real loss a bit smaller than the straight-line estimate, exactly as the duration lesson predicted it would.

Now trace the same shock through the rest of the machine. **The yield curve:** if this hike was the market repricing near-term policy while long-run growth expectations stayed anchored or worsened, the short end can rise faster than the long end — pushing the curve flatter or toward inversion, the market's signal that it now assigns higher odds to a future slowdown. **Credit spreads:** this bond's 12.0% repricing came purely from the risk-free rate moving; a lower-rated issuer's bond takes that same risk-free-rate hit *and* often sees its spread widen on top, because a sudden hike raises the odds that weaker borrowers can't refinance cheaply — so a similar-duration lower-rated bond falls harder than this investment-grade example — though high-yield indices typically carry much shorter duration (~3y vs ~6.5y), so on a pure rate shock the high-yield index can fall less, a compounding effect, not a coincidence. **Equities:** the same 100bp move that just cost this bond 12% is the identical discount-rate input equity valuation runs on — a long-duration growth stock, priced mostly on cash flows a decade out, takes a proportionally larger hit than a mature dividend payer, for the same duration reason the bond just demonstrated numerically. One number — the discount rate — is doing all four jobs at once.

## The steelman

The strongest case against reading this as one clean mechanism: "Real markets don't move this tidily — a Fed surprise also changes growth expectations, currency, and sector-specific factors simultaneously, so attributing the equity and credit moves purely to the discount-rate channel overstates how mechanical it really is." That's a fair challenge, and it's the same discipline the rates-and-equities lesson raised: rates are one input, not the only one. The falsifier: isolate names and bonds where near-term fundamentals and credit quality are genuinely unchanged around the announcement. If their prices still move by roughly the duration-implied amount, that isolates the discount-rate channel cleanly. Where guidance or credit quality also shift in the same window, the moves are compounding, not contradicting — the duration/discount-rate mechanism is still present, just riding alongside other forces you have to separate out, exactly as this capstone's credit-spread section showed for the lower-rated issuer.

## Takeaway

One rate shock, one discount rate, four markets: the bond's duration-scaled price drop, the yield curve's shape, the credit spread's default-risk premium, and the equity discount rate are all the same mechanism wearing different clothes — learn to see the single thread instead of four separate stories.
