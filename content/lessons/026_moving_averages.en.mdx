# Moving averages

A moving average (MA) is the average closing price of the last N bars, recalculated every bar. It smooths noise so you can see the trend underneath the wiggles. A 50-day simple moving average (SMA50) takes the last 50 daily closes and divides by 50; tomorrow, the oldest close drops off and today's close joins. The exponential moving average (EMA) weights recent bars more, so it turns faster. MAs do not predict — they describe where the average buyer has been. That single fact decides how you use them.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at MSFT through Q3 2025. From July through September, the daily close stayed above the rising SMA50, which climbed from around 445 to 465. Every shallow dip touched the SMA50 and bounced — the average buyer of the last 50 days kept stepping in. On October 9, MSFT closed at 458, sliced through the SMA50 at 463, and the SMA50 itself rolled over and began declining by October 22. Price then spent six weeks below it, oscillating between 440 and 455.

The signal is mechanical: as long as price holds above a rising SMA50, the medium-term trend is up. The falsification condition is equally mechanical — two consecutive daily closes below a flattening or declining SMA50 means the up-trend setup has invalidated. You don't argue with the line; you note what side of it price closed on.

On Bursa Malaysia: MAYBANK held its SMA50 around RM10.20 for most of Q4 2025, every pullback bought; when price closed at RM9.94 on December 18 — below a now-flat SMA50 — the same buyers stopped showing up.

## The trap

Beginners treat moving averages as predictions. "The 50-day is at 463, so MSFT will bounce there." That is not what an MA says. An MA is the average price of the last N bars — it is descriptive, not prescriptive. Price respects it only when participants who bought near that average are still willing to defend their cost. When sentiment shifts, those same buyers sell into rallies back to the MA, and the line becomes resistance instead of support.

The correct mental model: the MA is a context line that tells you which side of "average" the current price is on. Trade with the slope, not the level. A rising MA with price above it is a tailwind. A declining MA with price below is a headwind. That's the whole signal — the rest is noise.

## Takeaway

A moving average describes where the average buyer has been; trade with its slope, not its level — and define your invalidation in closes, not feelings.
