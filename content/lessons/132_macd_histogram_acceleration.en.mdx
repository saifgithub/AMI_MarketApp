# MACD histogram — measuring momentum acceleration

<Lesson id="028_macd_trend_and_momentum"/> named the MACD histogram as the difference between the MACD line and the signal line. That description is correct but undersells what the histogram actually is — the rate of change of momentum, the second derivative of price. The MACD line itself measures the speed of trend; the histogram measures whether that speed is accelerating or decelerating. Histogram bars getting taller (in either direction) means momentum is accelerating away from the signal line. Bars getting shorter means momentum is decelerating, even if MACD itself hasn't crossed back. That single insight — that the histogram peaks before the MACD line does — is what makes it the earliest of the MACD readings.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at META through November 2025. On November 4, MACD line crossed above signal at -1.2, histogram printed +0.3 (the first positive bar after weeks of negative). On November 11, histogram was +1.8 — six times taller than the cross bar. On November 18, histogram peaked at +2.4. From November 19 onward, the bars started shrinking — +2.1, +1.7, +1.3, +0.9 — even though MACD line was still climbing and well above signal.

The histogram peak on November 18 said momentum was decelerating. META's price kept rising for another nine sessions to a high of 612 on December 1, but the velocity of the rise had collapsed. On December 2, histogram crossed back to negative and the MACD line crossed below signal two sessions later. The histogram peak had warned of the momentum exhaustion roughly two weeks before the MACD signal-line cross fired.

Falsifiable claim: across a typical sample of trends, MACD histogram peaks lead MACD signal-line crosses by 5–15 sessions, providing earlier (but lower-confidence) warning of trend exhaustion. Invalidation is mechanical — if the histogram makes a new high within 10 sessions of the apparent peak, the deceleration warning cancels and the trend's momentum is re-accelerating.

On Bursa Malaysia: CIMB in 2024 — histogram peaked at +0.18 on May 12, MACD signal-line cross didn't fire until June 3 (15 sessions later); price topped on May 28, in between the two signals. The histogram led both.

## The trap

The trap is using histogram peaks as a stand-alone reversal trigger. The histogram can shrink for a few bars and then re-accelerate — false peaks are common, especially in choppy trends. A beginner exits a winning trend trade on the first histogram down-bar and misses the next 8% of the move. The opposite trap: confirming the peak by waiting for the MACD signal-line cross, by which point the trend has typically already lost 30–50% of its remaining move.

The correct mental model: the histogram is an early-warning instrument, not a trigger. Treat a sustained histogram contraction (3+ consecutive shrinking bars after a clear peak) as a signal to tighten stops, scale out partial position, or stop adding — not as a full exit. The MACD signal-line cross remains the trigger for closing the position fully. Histogram for risk management; cross for entry/exit.

## Takeaway

The MACD histogram measures momentum acceleration, not direction — its peak leads the MACD-line cross by 5–15 sessions on average, so use it for tightening stops and scaling out, not for the full exit.
