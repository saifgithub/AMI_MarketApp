# Margin trends: reading the direction

Lesson 033 ended on the line that matters most: direction beats level. This lesson teaches the technique. A margin's *trajectory* across 6–8 quarters is more predictive than any single-quarter level — because trends self-correct slowly. Expanding margins suggest pricing power and scale; compressing margins suggest the opposite; flat margins suggest a stable equilibrium. The most valuable reading skill is recognising an *inflection* — the quarter where a multi-year trend changes direction. By the end of this lesson you should be able to spot inflections, compute the rate of margin change, and know the specific pattern that historically precedes EPS surprises (positive and negative).

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at TSLA's automotive gross margin (excluding regulatory credits) across 2022–2024. Roughly: Q1 2022 ~30%, Q4 2022 ~25%, Q4 2023 ~17%, Q4 2024 ~13–14%. That's an eight-quarter compression of ~16 percentage points — the steepest sustained margin decline at a mega-cap in recent memory. Each quarter's print individually felt manageable; the trend, viewed in a single chart, was the diagnostic the headlines didn't deliver in real time. The bull thesis kept evolving (volume offsets, software margins, robotaxi optionality) but the line on the chart kept going in one direction.

Now the inverse. Look at META's operating margin in 2022–2024. Roughly: Q4 2022 ~20%, mid-2023 ~29%, Q4 2024 ~48% for the most recent print on the Family of Apps segment. That's a +25-point expansion over two years on the back of headcount discipline and AI-driven ad pricing — one of the cleanest expanding-margin stories of the cycle. The stock tripled across the period; the operating margin chart told the story before the price did, for anyone willing to read it.

The diagnostic ratio: compute *margin delta per quarter* over the trailing eight quarters. A consistent +50bps/qtr or –50bps/qtr trend is a structural move. A one-off ±200bps with revert in the next quarter is noise. The most reliable inflection signal is two consecutive quarters reversing the trailing four-quarter trend with no obvious one-off cause.

On Bursa Malaysia: TOPGLOV's gross margin journey from the 2020–2021 cycle peak (mid-50s%) to 2023–2024 troughs (single digits) is a textbook commodity-cycle margin reversion. Each quarter felt like "the bottom is in" — the trend continued for eight consecutive prints.

The falsifiable claim: a margin trend of ≥+50bps/qtr sustained for 4+ quarters statistically predicts a positive EPS surprise in the next print roughly two-thirds of the time. The mirror pattern (≥-50bps/qtr for 4+ quarters) predicts negative surprises at a similar rate. Trends self-reinforce in the short term.

## The trap

The trap is treating each quarter as an independent event. A beginner sees Q3 margins down 80bps and the company explains "transitory input costs". They take the explanation and move on. Then Q4 comes in down another 70bps with a new explanation. Then Q1 the next year down 60bps with a third. The correct mental model: a margin trend is the *aggregate* of every "transitory" excuse the company has offered. Stack the deltas. If the cumulative move over four quarters is –250bps and four different explanations have been given, the underlying issue is structural, not transitory. Conversely, four quarters of "small beats" on gross margin aren't noise — they're the same trend in reverse. Track the running total, not the single-quarter delta.

## Takeaway

A margin trend is the cumulative truth that single-quarter explanations try to hide. Stack the deltas, ignore the excuses, and let the chart of the margin line speak.
