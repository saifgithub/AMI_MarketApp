# Fake brokers and clone entities

A clone-broker scam impersonates a legitimately licensed firm — same name, almost-identical logo, a domain one character different. The reader thinks they are wiring money to a regulated brokerage; the money lands in a personal or shell account the operator controls. This lesson trains the simple, falsifiable check that catches almost every clone: verify the entity name, registration number, *and* contact details against the regulator's public register before you transfer a single ringgit or dollar.

## How this plays out in real markets

The UK's FCA maintains a public Warning List specifically for clone firms — fca.org.uk/scamsmart — and adds dozens per month. The typical clone takes a real, licensed UK or US wealth-management firm, lifts its name verbatim or shifts one letter ("Goldnan Sachs", "JP-Morgan-Capital"), copies the legitimate firm's About page wholesale, then publishes a phone number and bank-account details that route to the scammer. Victims who later call the *real* firm's listed phone number discover the firm has never heard of them.

SC Malaysia runs the same model under "Investor Alert List" and BNM under "Financial Consumer Alert". A recurring local variant: a Telegram account claiming to be a "Senior Dealer" at a major Malaysian investment bank, asking for an "onboarding deposit" into a personal Maybank or CIMB account. Real brokers never accept client deposits into individual personal accounts; client money sits in a trust account in the firm's name.

ASIC publishes near-weekly imposter alerts. Many overseas clones target SEA expat communities, mixing English-language pitches with WhatsApp local-language hooks.

Signature traits:

- Domain off by one character, hyphen, or TLD (.org instead of .com).
- Phone or WhatsApp number doesn't match the legitimate firm's published number.
- Bank account for transfers is in an individual's name, not the firm's name.
- "Onboarding manager" who refuses video calls or only video-calls with avatar on.
- No working entry on the regulator's register when you look up the registration number they gave you.

## The trap

The mistake is checking the *website* and stopping there. A clone site can look indistinguishable from the real one — same colour palette, same About text, same staff bios. The reader confirms "yes, this firm is licensed" without realising the licence belongs to the *real* firm, not the clone they are talking to. The fix is to never use a number, email, or bank detail provided by the firm itself. Look up the firm independently on the regulator's register (FCA, SEC, FINRA, SC Malaysia), call the number listed there, and confirm the person who contacted you actually works at the firm. If the firm exists but the staff member doesn't, you have caught a clone.

## Takeaway

Always look up the firm using a number from the *regulator's* register, never a number the firm gave you. Real brokers never take client deposits into personal bank accounts.
