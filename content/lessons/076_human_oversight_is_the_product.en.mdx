# Human oversight is the product

The <Term id="ceo_framing" />-of-<Term id="ami" /> framing is not a metaphor. Inside the <Term id="training_simulator" />, you make every call. You set the <Term id="ami_mandate" />. You decide whether to convene the Room. You decide whether to accept the simulated Verdict, modify it, or reject it. The discipline being trained is the *evaluation* — the practice of running an analyst team and judging its work, not the collection of recommendations to take anywhere. AMI does not execute, will not execute, and is not licensed to give investment advice. By the end of this lesson you will see why the human-in-the-loop is not a safety feature bolted on; it is the entire product.

## How this plays out in real markets

You convene the Room on JPM. The <Term id="ami_verdict" /> comes back:

> **BUY JPM · size 4% · entry $218.40 · stop $208 (-4.8%) · target $238 (+9.0%) · horizon 6–8 weeks · confidence 0.66**
>
> *PM compliance: PASS. Within max_position_pct 5, within max_drawdown_pct 20, no exclusions triggered.*

Three things happen next, and all three are yours:

1. **Read the dissent.** The Bear Researcher's case might be that rate-cut expectations are already priced in. You weigh that against the Bull case. AMI does not weigh it for you — weighing it *is* the training.
2. **Decide what the scenario taught you.** "PASS" from the PM means the simulated trade fits your Mandate — not that AMI is advising you to do anything. You can reject a passing Verdict. You can accept it at smaller size. You can wait. The button labelled "Accept Verdict" records your decision in the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> so you can study your own judgment later — it does not place a trade anywhere, in this app or any other.
3. **Carry the lesson, not a position.** No drawdown ever happens in AMI — there is nothing to press, no broker, no order. Nothing leaves the simulator. The training value of the scenario is the question it forces: *did your evaluation hold up under scrutiny?* That is the consequence the simulator is built to deliver, and the only one it can.

Compare to a system that auto-executes: there, "human oversight" is a confirm dialog and a regret form. Here, oversight is the only thing that exists. The 12 agents argue, the PM compliance-checks, and then the system *stops* — and waits for you to evaluate. That pause is the product.

## The trap

The trap is the language of automation seducing you into outsourcing the decision. "AMI's simulated Verdict was BUY", "PM stamped PASS", "the Room's confidence was 0.7" — all true, all training artifacts. None of them mean AMI is recommending you do anything outside the app. AMI is not licensed to give investment advice and does not pretend to. The mental model to keep: AMI is a simulated analyst desk and a simulated compliance officer combined, built to train your judgment; nothing in the app reaches your real account, ever. A real-world CIO reads the analyst report and decides. They don't blame the analyst when a position turns. They evaluated the work and made a call. The training here is precisely that evaluation skill — and the Decision Journal is built to capture *your* reasoning, not AMI's, for exactly this reason.

## Try it

Open the **Mandate editor** and change one field — say, `max_position_pct` from 5 to 3. Then convene the Room on a ticker you've recently considered. Watch the PM's recommended size move accordingly. Then *reject* the Verdict in the Decision Journal and write one sentence of why. You've just exercised the only authority that actually moves anything in this app — yours.

## Takeaway

There is no execution surface in AMI — no button, no broker, no order. No drawdown ever happens in the simulator. The training value is the discipline of evaluating: did your reading of the Bull case, the Bear case, the Risk Debator spread, and the PM check hold up under scrutiny? AMI is teaching you to evaluate analyst output, not giving you advice to follow.
