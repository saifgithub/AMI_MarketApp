# Late-bull warning signs: narrowing breadth and sentiment extremes

Lesson 059 defined a bull regime numerically: SPX above the 200-day SMA, 20-week rising, breadth expanding. This lesson teaches the next layer — the warning signs that print *before* the regime breaks, so you can compress sizing and shorten invalidation distances while still long. After this lesson you will be able to detect a late-bull state from three breadth divergences and two sentiment extremes, name what each invalidates (and does not), and act inside the regime rather than fighting it.

## How this plays out in real markets

The classic late-bull case is the SPX run into early 2022. SPX printed a closing high of 4,796 on 3 January 2022 — still above the 200-day SMA, technically still a bull regime by the 059 test. Underneath:

- % of NYSE issues above their 200-day SMA had fallen from 78% in May 2021 to 49% by 31 December 2021 — index up, participation down.
- NYSE new 52-week highs averaged 110 per session in November 2021 but only 65 per session in December 2021 — fewer leaders pulling the average.
- AAII bull-bear spread hit +30 on 24 November 2021, in the 90th percentile of readings since 2010.
- CNN Fear & Greed Index closed at 81 ("Extreme Greed") on multiple sessions in late December 2021.
- Russell 2000 (IWM) peaked on 8 November 2021 at 244.46 and was already 12% off that high while SPX was making fresh highs on 3 January 2022 — small caps had topped two months early.

A trader using these five readings would have started compressing in late December 2021 — six to ten weeks before the 21 February 2022 break of the 200-day SMA, capturing most of the bull while sidestepping the bulk of the 25% drawdown that followed.

## The trap

The late-bull trap is the inverse of the bull-trap: selling early *because* you heard "breadth is narrowing". A reader sells in October 2021 when % above 200-day SMA first drops below 60% — and watches SPX rally another 6% into year-end while their cash earns nothing. The mental error is treating a warning sign as an invalidation. They are different things. A warning sign tightens your mandate (smaller positions, closer stops, cap concentration); an invalidation flips the regime (close below 200-day on weekly basis; death cross).

The correct model: warning signs compound. One reading flashing is noise. Three of five readings in the late-bull cluster is a credible regime-deterioration signal — and the response is to dial down, not to exit.

## Takeaway
