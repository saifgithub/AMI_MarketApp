# The Research Manager: synthesis, not opinion

When the Bull and the Bear have both built honest cases, somebody has to **decide**. That somebody is the Research Manager. Their job is not to pick a side — it is to convert a debate into a position.

## What the RM does NOT do

- Cast a tie-breaking vote.
- Average the bull and bear cases into a wishy-washy "mildly positive."
- Defer to whoever spoke last.
- Add their own personal opinion to the pile.

If the RM is just a third opinion in the room, they have failed. There are already four Analysts upstream. A fifth opinion is noise.

## What the RM actually does

The RM does **four** things that no other agent does:

1. **Score the asymmetry.** A 30% upside vs a 50% downside is bad asymmetry, even if you lean bullish. The RM is the only agent whose job is to *measure both sides on the same axis*.
2. **Translate view into size.** "We're cautiously bullish" is not a position. "Buy 3% with a stop at $148, target $192, horizon 6 months" is a position. The RM is the bridge from opinion to action.
3. **Reconcile the mandate.** The user's mandate sets risk_score, max_drawdown, single-name cap, halal screen. The RM is the first place those constraints touch the trade. (The PM enforces them at the gate; the RM internalises them upstream.)
4. **Flag debate quality.** If the Bear is strawmanning, the RM calls it. If the Bull's evidence is just narrative, the RM calls it. The RM is the room's quality bar.

## Asymmetry, in numbers

Think of every trade as a bet with four numbers:

- **Probability of bull case** — what the Bull thinks; usually 50-70%.
- **Magnitude of upside** — typically 20-40% over the horizon.
- **Probability of bear case** — what the Bear thinks; usually 30-50%.
- **Magnitude of downside** — typically 15-35%.

Multiply: `EV = p_up × upside − p_down × downside`. If EV is positive *and* the downside fits the mandate, it's a trade. If EV is positive but the downside exceeds your single-name cap × max_drawdown budget, it isn't.

The RM is the agent who actually runs that arithmetic. The Bull and Bear argue qualitatively. The RM is *quantitative*.

## A worked example

Bull on NVDA: 60% chance of +30% in 9 months (hyperscaler capex re-accelerates).
Bear on NVDA: 40% chance of -25% in 9 months (multiple compression on any capex guide cut).

EV = 0.6 × 30 − 0.4 × 25 = 18 − 10 = **+8% expected return**.

That sounds tradeable. But check the downside against the mandate:

- User max_drawdown is 20%. A 25% drawdown in a 10% position = 2.5% portfolio hit. OK.
- User single-name cap is 8%. So the RM caps position at 8%, not 10%.
- Stop is set 1 ATR below the bear's invalidation level — say $148.

The output: **BUY 8% NVDA, stop $148, target $192, horizon 9 months.** The Bull and Bear didn't say that. The RM did.

## When the RM should refuse

A real Research Manager will sometimes return "no trade." Specifically:

- EV is negative or barely positive — the asymmetry doesn't justify the cost.
- Downside fits no rational stop — the bear case is "if AI bubble pops, -60%" with no clean invalidation level.
- <Term id="ami_mandate" /> conflicts — the user is long-only and the only edge is on the short side.
- Debate quality was poor — neither side built a falsifiable case.

"No trade" is a real answer. The PM downstream will respect it. (And if the RM is forced into a trade they don't believe in, the <Term id="ami_pm_safety_floor" /> will catch it anyway — that's the redundancy.)
