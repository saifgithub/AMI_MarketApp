# Doji: the indecision candle

This assumes you've completed 020 (anatomy of a bar). Here we go deeper on one specific shape: the doji — a candle where open and close finish at, or within a few cents of, the same price. The body is a thin line; the wicks tell the story. A doji means the session opened, fought up, fought down, and finished where it started. Buyers and sellers cancelled out. By the end of this lesson you should be able to spot a doji on any chart, distinguish a meaningful one from a meaningless one, and know what context turns it into a signal.

## Example

Look at NVDA on the daily chart on 4 March 2026. NVDA had run from $108 on 14 February to $124.80 on 3 March — an 15% rally in roughly two weeks. On 4 March the candle opened at $124.55, traded as high as $127.10, dropped as low as $122.30, and closed at $124.60 — a body of $0.05 inside a $4.80 intraday range. That's a textbook doji.

Crucially, it printed at a level: $127.10 was the same area as the late-January swing high near $127.40 that NVDA had failed at the first time. So the doji wasn't random — it sat at known resistance, after a 15% extended run, on volume 1.4x the 20-day average. The next session (5 March) opened at $123.80 and closed at $119.40 — a 4.2% drop confirming the indecision had resolved bearishly.

Compare that to a doji on AAPL on 22 January 2026, mid-range, with no level nearby, after three sideways weeks. That doji preceded another two sideways weeks. Same shape, completely different signal value.

The pattern invalidates as a reversal signal if the next session closes back above the doji's high — that voids the rejection read.

## The trap

The trap is treating every doji as a reversal. A doji in the middle of a sideways chop is just noise — the market was already undecided, and one more undecided candle tells you nothing new. The doji only carries information when it appears (a) at the end of an extended directional move, (b) at a known support or resistance level, and (c) ideally on above-average volume. Outside those conditions, it's a coin flip wearing a fancy name. The correct mental model: a doji raises a question, not an answer. Context — trend, level, volume — converts the question into a signal. No context, no signal.

## Try it

Open a chart of any ticker that's had a strong move in the last quarter. Scroll through the daily candles and find any doji. For each one, ask in <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Market Analyst: "Was this doji at a level, after an extended move, on elevated volume? What happened in the next two sessions?" Tag the ones that mattered and the ones that didn't. Save the pattern to your <Term id="ami_decision_journal" />.

## Takeaway

A doji is a question the market asks itself — only context (trend, level, volume) turns the question into an answer worth trading.
