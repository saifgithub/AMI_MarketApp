# Sideways / range markets

A sideways regime is the one most traders bleed money in. Price oscillates inside a band; the 200-day SMA flattens; trend strategies whip-saw; breakouts fail; pullbacks don't reach the moving average because there is no moving average to reach. After this lesson you will be able to detect a range regime in numbers, name the two strategies that fit it, name the trend strategies that will quietly drain you in it, and state the condition that confirms a regime break out of the range.

## How this plays out in real markets

The first half of 2023 in SPX is a clean range case. After bouncing off 3,808 on 13 March 2023, SPX oscillated between roughly 3,950 (support) and 4,200 (resistance) for about three months until breaking above 4,200 on 1 June 2023. The 200-day SMA was nearly flat across that window — slope under 5 points per week. The 20-day Average True Range hovered around 45 points (≈1.1% of price), down from 75+ in the 2022 bear.

Detection rule for a range regime: SPX 200-day SMA slope is between -0.05% and +0.05% per week (essentially flat) AND price has tested each of two horizontal levels at least twice within the last 40 trading days AND the gap between those levels is under 8% of price. All three were true for SPX in March–May 2023.

A cleaner sector range: XLF (financials) traded between 31.50 and 35.00 from October 2023 to early March 2024, four months of oscillation with a flat 200-day, before breaking above 35 on the 4 March 2024 close. On Bursa Malaysia: KLCI ranged between 1,415 and 1,495 for most of the first half of 2023 — almost six months of flat 200-day before a regime change in late 2023.

## The trap

The range-market trap is using trend-following indicators in a range. The moving-average crossover that printed money in 2024 prints 6 false signals in 6 months when the 200-day is flat. Each whipsaw is small (1–2%), but six of them compounds into a 6–10% drawdown on a strategy that "should be working". The trader, anchored to the bull-regime memory, doubles size on the next signal to "make it back" — and the next whipsaw lands harder.

The correct model: in a range regime, the edge is mean-reversion to the band middle, not breakout-following. You fade extremes, take small targets, and accept that the regime ends with the eventual breakout — at which point the playbook flips back to trend.

## Takeaway

In a flat 200-day, two-sided range, fade the edges and target the middle; the trend strategies that worked last regime will whipsaw you until the band breaks on a weekly close.
