# Pullback to moving average vs to support/resistance

The pullback foundation (054) used the 50-day MA as the support level. But not all pullbacks end at the same kind of level — some terminate at a rising moving average (dynamic support), others at a prior breakout level or horizontal swing low (static S/R), and the two have different risk profiles. Dynamic-MA pullbacks give you a moving target that ratchets with price; static-S/R pullbacks give you a fixed level that's been tested historically. After this lesson you can name which level type the current pullback is heading toward and choose the stop placement that fits.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at two MSFT pullbacks side by side from 2024. (1) Pullback to a rising MA — early September 2024: MSFT had run from $400 in January to a high of $467 in early July, then pulled back in August toward the rising 50-day MA at $420. On September 6, MSFT closed at $401.70 — tagging just under the 50-day in an intraday spike but closing back near $410, with the 50-day now at $419 acting as dynamic resistance/support. (2) Pullback to a prior breakout level — May 2024: MSFT had broken out from a base near $430 in March, then pulled back through April and tagged $397.58 on April 19 — but the cleaner support level was the prior breakout zone around $410, which had been resistance, became support after the breakout, and held on a daily-close basis for the subsequent retest. The dynamic-MA trade had a stop under the 50-day swing low at $397 (risk ~$13/share from $410 entry). The static-S/R trade had a stop under the prior breakout zone at $405 (risk ~$5/share from $410 entry). Same instrument, same direction, very different position-sizing math.

Entry criteria for MA pullback: established uptrend (50-day sloping up), price pulls back and closes within 1-2% of the rising MA, then closes back ABOVE it with confirmation. Stop: pullback swing low. For static-S/R pullback: prior breakout level (or prior swing high, prior cluster of touches), price pulls back to and respects the level on a daily-close basis, then prints a confirmation candle. Stop: below the static level by the average pullback's noise band (~0.5x daily ATR). Individual-trade invalidation in either case: a daily close significantly below the support level — the support failed, the pullback became a breakdown. The strategies FAIL when the underlying trend is already weakening on the higher timeframe — pullbacks late in a trend frequently break support, especially when broad market breadth has narrowed.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is treating all pullback supports as equivalent and using one default stop distance for all of them. You enter a pullback-to-MA trade with the same 2% stop you'd use on a pullback-to-prior-breakout-zone, and you either get stopped out at the noise (MA support can wick through by 1-3%) or you over-risk (static S/R rarely needs a 2% buffer below it). The correct mental model: stop placement is a function of the support TYPE. Dynamic-MA supports breathe — give them ~1x ATR below the MA. Static S/R levels are fixed — give them ~0.5x ATR below the level. The other trap: stacking the two and thinking the pullback is doubly-supported when MA and prior S/R coincide. That's a real edge, but only if both are confirmed by a close-back candle.

## Takeaway

Pullback supports are not interchangeable. Dynamic MAs breathe and need ~1x ATR below; static S/R is fixed and needs ~0.5x ATR. Stop distance flows from support type, not from a default percentage.
