# ROE inflation via buybacks — the equity-shrinking trap

<Lesson id="041_return_on_equity"/> flagged that "aggressive share buybacks can inflate ROE without any underlying improvement in business quality." This lesson nails down the mechanics and arms the reader against the highest-profile failure mode in modern equity analysis. Buybacks shrink the equity denominator. ROE = net income / equity. If the numerator stays flat and the denominator shrinks 20%, ROE rises 25% even though nothing about the business changed. The same arithmetic distorts P/B (<Lesson id="040_price_to_book"/>) and DuPont (<Lesson id="184_dupont_decomposition_deep"/>). By the end you should be able to detect buyback-driven ROE inflation, name the falsifiable test, and recognise the small subset of cases where buybacks reflect true capital discipline.

## How this plays out in real markets

AAPL is the canonical case. Trailing ROE around 150%. Net income $97B; average equity $65B. But over the last decade Apple has spent more than $700B on buybacks, reducing share count by roughly 40% and shrinking equity from $134B in 2017 to $65B today. Net income grew over the same period, but equity shrunk faster — pushing ROE from a "normal" 35–40% range to an arithmetic-absurd 150%. The underlying business compounds at roughly 15–18% per year in revenue-and-earnings terms, but the ROE makes it look like an alien-grade business.

Now a darker example. Consider an industrial that bought back 25% of its shares over five years using debt. Net income flat over five years at $1B; equity dropped from $5B to $3B (buybacks plus dividends drained retained earnings); debt rose by $1.5B. ROE moved from 20% to 33%. Headline reads as "fantastic improvement in capital efficiency." DuPont decomposition (<Lesson id="184_dupont_decomposition_deep"/>) reveals: margin flat, turnover flat, equity multiplier up sharply. The "improvement" is leverage masquerading as operating excellence. When the next downturn arrives, the higher fixed financing costs eat the same operating margin, the leveraged ROE collapses, and the company is left with debt service and a fragile capital structure.

The defensive case: when a business genuinely earns more than its cost of capital and has more cash than productive reinvestment opportunities, buying back stock *at a fair or low price* is correct capital allocation. AAPL's buyback is largely cash-funded and at sustained returns above 20% on the operating business — it's defensible. The same buyback funded by debt at a stretched valuation is value-destructive.

Falsifiable claim: a buyback is value-creative only when (a) the company is repurchasing at a price below the intrinsic value implied by its sustainable ROE, AND (b) the buyback is funded from free cash flow, not from incremental debt that materially raises the equity multiplier. On Bursa, GENTING-related counters have run substantial buyback programs during gaming-cycle troughs at low valuations — defensible. Buybacks funded by debt at peak valuations (some US large-cap industrials in 2017–2019) destroyed value when the cycle turned.

## The trap

The trap is celebrating ROE improvement that is mechanically the result of equity-denominator shrinkage. The signature: revenue flat, margin flat, asset turnover flat, but ROE up; equity line on the balance sheet shrinking over multiple years; share count down. If you see those four signals together, the ROE "improvement" is bookkeeping, not business. The correct mental model: every reported ROE should be paired with a *return on assets* (ROA = net income / total assets) check. ROA strips out capital structure entirely. If ROE rises while ROA stays flat, the gain is leverage or denominator shrinkage. If ROE and ROA both rise together, the business is genuinely improving. What invalidates the "buyback is bad" reading: a buyback paid for from genuine free cash flow at a valuation below intrinsic value, with debt-to-equity stable or falling, is correct capital allocation. The trap is the *unconditional* belief that buybacks signal quality — the conditional version is the right framing.

## Takeaway

Buybacks mechanically inflate ROE by shrinking equity; pair ROE with ROA and ROIC to see whether the improvement is earned or borrowed. Cash-funded buybacks above cost of capital are good; debt-funded buybacks on flat operations are leverage in disguise.
