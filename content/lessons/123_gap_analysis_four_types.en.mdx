# Gap analysis: four types of gaps

This assumes you've completed <Lesson id="020_candlesticks_anatomy_of_a_bar"/> (anatomy of a bar) and <Lesson id="024_breakouts"/> (breakouts). Here we go deeper on what to do when the chart shows a gap — a price discontinuity where one session's open prints clear of the prior session's high or low, leaving a visible empty space between the two candles. Not all gaps mean the same thing. There are four common types — common, breakaway, runaway (continuation), and exhaustion — and reading the wrong one badly can be the difference between catching a 15% trend and getting trapped at the top. By the end of this lesson you should be able to classify a gap by location and volume, know which fill, which don't, and what each implies for position management.

## Example

Walk through real gaps on NVDA in early 2026.

**Common gap.** 22 January 2026: NVDA closed Tuesday at $138.40, opened Wednesday at $137.90 — a $0.50 gap down in the middle of a sideways consolidation, on average volume, with no news catalyst. Filled within two sessions; closed Friday at $138.80. *Where it formed: in a range, low context. What to do: ignore — common gaps fill, they're noise.*

**Breakaway gap.** 6 February 2026: after consolidating in $125–$132 for three weeks, NVDA closed Thursday at $132.10 and opened Friday at $137.40 — a $5.30 gap above the entire consolidation, on 2.4x average volume, on a positive earnings beat. Did not fill. NVDA ran to $148 over the next four weeks. *Where it formed: out of a clean consolidation. What to do: treat like a high-conviction breakout, see <Lesson id="024_breakouts"/>.*

**Runaway gap (continuation).** 19 February 2026: NVDA had been trending up. Closed Tuesday at $142.10, opened Wednesday at $145.80 — a $3.70 gap mid-trend on 1.8x volume. Continued higher to $148 by week's end. *Where it formed: mid-uptrend. What to do: it's a trend-acceleration signal; existing longs hold, new entries chase carefully.*

**Exhaustion gap.** 4 March 2026 (the day before the doji from <Lesson id="112_doji_the_indecision_candle"/>): after a six-week rally from $108 to $148, NVDA closed Monday at $146.80 and opened Tuesday at $150.20 — a $3.40 gap up on 2.1x volume, but it printed above the prior November high and at the extreme of the move. The gap filled within three sessions and then some — NVDA was at $138 by mid-March. *Where it formed: at the end of an extended trend. What to do: tighten stops; exhaustion gaps often mark the high.*

Falsification rule for each: a common gap is confirmed common once filled — until it fills, you can't be sure it wasn't a breakaway. A breakaway gap invalidates if it fills (daily close back inside the gap range). An exhaustion gap is confirmed exhaustion only after it fills — until it does, it can still be a runaway in disguise. *Type is partly retrospective, which is why context (location, volume) matters so much for the prospective read.*

## The trap

The trap is reflexively shorting any gap up (or buying any gap down) on the premise that "gaps always fill". They don't always fill. Common gaps usually do. Breakaway gaps often don't. Runaway gaps may not fill for months. Exhaustion gaps fill quickly. Trading "the gap will fill" without classifying which kind of gap you're looking at is how short sellers blow up on breakaway gaps that triple-digit-percent without ever filling. The correct mental model: location and volume classify the gap. A gap out of a long consolidation on high volume is probably a breakaway and you do not short it. A gap inside a sideways range on average volume is probably common and the fill thesis is reasonable. A gap at the extreme of a long extended trend on high volume is probably an exhaustion gap and longs should tighten, not chase. Read where the gap is before you decide what to do about it.

## Takeaway

Gaps come in four kinds — common (fills, ignore), breakaway (don't short it), runaway (trend acceleration), exhaustion (likely top, tighten) — and the type is determined by location and volume, not by the gap's size alone.
