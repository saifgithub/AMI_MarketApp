# Fibonacci-zone pullback entries

The pullback foundation (054) used a single support — the 50-day MA. Fibonacci retracement zones give you a probabilistic depth-grid instead: a pullback inside an uptrend most commonly terminates in the 38.2%-to-61.8% retracement zone of the prior leg. That zone is not magic — there's no metaphysics in 0.618 — but the ratios cluster with where institutional algorithms and pattern-trained traders place size, and that clustering is what makes the zone useful. After this lesson you can plot a Fibonacci pullback zone, decide which fib level is the entry trigger versus the invalidation, and size the trade off the zone's edges rather than off a single line.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at NVDA from April through August 2024. The leg up ran from a swing low of $76.60 on April 22 to a swing high of $140.76 on June 20 — a $64.16 advance. The Fibonacci pullback zone projected: 38.2% = $116.27, 50% = $108.68, 61.8% = $101.10. NVDA pulled back through July and tagged $98.91 on August 5 — a brief overshoot of the 61.8% level on a panic-volatility day — but closed back at $100.45, and within three sessions reclaimed $108 (the 50% level). A Fibonacci pullback entry triggered on the close above $108.68 (the 50% level) with the stop placed below the 61.8% level adjusted for noise — say $98 (just below the overshoot low). Risk per share: $10.68. NVDA ran to $128 by mid-August — first target near the 23.6% retracement at $125.66 satisfied at ~1.6R; a runner held with a trailing stop captured the move to $140+ by late August.

Entry criteria: (1) defined prior swing leg (low to high) with at least 5%+ extension, (2) pullback enters the 38.2%-to-61.8% zone, (3) confirmation candle closes back above the 50% level (or a tighter chosen fib trigger like 38.2%). Stop: a daily close below the 61.8% level adjusted for ATR noise (~0.5x ATR below 61.8%). Targets: 23.6% retracement of the leg, then the prior swing high; or a measured-move projection. Individual-trade invalidation: a daily close below 78.6% retracement — at that point the pullback has consumed too much of the prior leg to qualify as a continuation pullback; the structure has broken. The strategy FAILS in trends with steep retracements (typical of high-beta names in volatility regime changes) — a "normal" 61.8% pullback in a stable trend looks identical to the first leg-down of a new downtrend.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is treating each fib level as a static entry trigger and trying to "place an order at 50%". You set a limit order at 50%, price overshoots to 61.8% and bounces past your fill, and you either chase or miss. Or worse, your order fills at 50% on the way through, you have no confirmation that support held, and you ride it down to a deeper retracement. The correct mental model: the fib ZONE between 38.2% and 61.8% is the area where you wait for a confirmation close, not where you place a passive order. Within the zone, the depth of the actual pullback tells you about strength — shallower (38.2%) suggests stronger continuation; deeper (61.8%) suggests weaker. Adjust size accordingly: half size on a 61.8% touch, full size on a 38.2% hold.

## Takeaway

The fib zone is a depth grid, not a set of magic numbers. Wait for a confirmation close back above the deepest tested level; let depth of pullback adjust your conviction and size.
