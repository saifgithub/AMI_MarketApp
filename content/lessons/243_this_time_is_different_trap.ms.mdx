# The "this time is different" trap in late bull

<Lesson id="242_late_bull_warning_signs"/> listed the late-bull warning cluster. This lesson tackles the narrative that disables it. Every late-bull regime arrives with a story that says the old rules no longer apply — new technology, new monetary regime, new productivity paradigm. The story is sometimes partly true. It is almost never sufficient to invalidate the numeric warning cluster. After this lesson you will be able to spot the "this time is different" frame, separate the kernel of truth from the rationalisation it gets wrapped in, and run a structured rebuttal before increasing exposure.

## How this plays out in real markets

Three documented "this time is different" episodes, each with a real partial truth, each ending with the regime asserting itself:

Dot-com 2000: the kernel of truth was real — the internet changed business. The rationalisation that broke: Nasdaq Composite trailing P/E reached ~200 in March 2000 (vs long-run ~25), CSCO traded at 130x earnings on 27 March 2000. The Nasdaq Composite peaked at 5,048 on 10 March 2000 and bottomed at 1,114 on 9 October 2002 — a 78% drawdown. The narrative was right about the technology and wrong about the prices.

2007–2008 housing: the kernel was that mortgage-backed structuring genuinely was newer than the prior cycle's tools. The rationalisation: "national housing prices don't fall". SPX peaked at 1,565 on 9 October 2007 and bottomed at 676 on 9 March 2009 — a 56.8% closing drawdown.

2021 "low rates forever / digital revolution": the kernel was that pandemic-era policy genuinely was unprecedented and tech adoption did accelerate. The rationalisation: SPX forward P/E reached 22.5 in late 2021 (vs 16 long-run average), ARKK peaked on 12 February 2021 at 159.70 and bottomed at 30.74 on 28 December 2022 — an 80.7% drawdown in a basket of "future of everything" names.

In all three, the warning cluster from <Lesson id="242_late_bull_warning_signs"/> was firing before the break. The narrative explained why it could be ignored. The numbers won.

## The trap

The trap is letting the narrative substitute for the numeric framework. The reader nodding along to "AI will keep multiples elevated" stops checking AAII spread, % above 200-day SMA, and forward P/E percentile because the story is doing the analytical work. The narrative is not wrong as a story; it is wrong as a permission slip to ignore the warning cluster.

The correct model: every "this time is different" claim gets a structured rebuttal. Name the kernel of truth (what *is* genuinely new). Name what the kernel does NOT change (gravity, mean-reversion of margins, regime mechanics). Then check whether the warning cluster from 242 is firing. If it is, the narrative is not a permission slip — it is a stress test you owe yourself.

## Takeaway

Every late-bull narrative has a kernel of truth; none of them are permission slips. Run the structured rebuttal — kernel, what it doesn't change, current warning cluster — before letting a story raise your exposure.
