# Rate-sensitive sectors in hiking and cutting cycles

Lesson 063 introduced sector rotation as a regime signal. This lesson teaches the specific rotation pattern around interest-rate cycles — the single most reliable macro-driven rotation in US equities. After this lesson you will be able to identify the rate-sensitivity cohort, name the expected leadership map under hiking and cutting cycles, name the common heuristic that is wrong in real cycles, and state the invalidation that says "this cycle is rotating differently".

## How this plays out in real markets

The 2022 hiking cycle: the Fed funds rate moved from 0.25% in March 2022 to 4.50% by December 2022 — the fastest pace since 1980. 10-year yield ran from 1.78% on 31 December 2021 to 4.25% on 24 October 2022. Sector performance from 1 January to 31 December 2022:

- XLE (energy): +57.7% — commodity tailwind, low-duration earnings.
- XLU (utilities): -0.8% — yield substitute lost relative appeal as bond yields rose.
- XLK (tech): -28.2% — long-duration cash flows penalised by higher discount rate.
- XLRE (real estate): -28.0% — directly rate-sensitive cash flows.
- XLF (financials): -10.5% — net-interest-margin tailwind partly offset by deal-flow contraction.

The 2024 cutting cycle pivot: Fed cut 50bp on 18 September 2024, then 25bp on 7 November 2024 and 25bp on 18 December 2024 — 100bp total. 10-year yield FELL from 4.36% on 1 July 2024 to 3.63% on 16 September 2024 (anticipation), then ROSE back to 4.57% by 31 December 2024 (long-end re-pricing on growth + sticky inflation). Sector performance 1 July to 31 December 2024:

- XLU: +18.4% — bond-substitute rally on falling front-end rates.
- XLF: +14.8% — strong despite the cut, on yield-curve steepening.
- XLK: +6.2% — softer than expected because the long-end didn't follow the short-end.
- XLRE: +9.7% — solid but constrained by long-end stickiness.

Detection rule: under a hiking cycle (Fed funds rising AND 10-year yield rising AND yield-curve flattening), expect XLE / XLF (early) and value-style leadership; XLK / XLRE / XLU lag. Under a cutting cycle, the canonical pattern is XLU / XLRE / XLK rotation in — but this fires *only if the long end falls with the short end*. When the long end doesn't follow (as in late 2024), the rate-sensitive rally is muted and financials with curve-steepening exposure can paradoxically lead.

## The trap

The rate-cycle trap is using a textbook heuristic ("Fed cuts → tech rallies") without checking the actual yield-curve mechanics. A trader pre-positions heavily into XLK in mid-2024 ahead of the September cut, expecting a duration-led tech rally — and watches the long end re-rate higher, neutralising the cut, while XLF and XLU lead instead. The mental error is treating the Fed's policy rate as the only rate that matters.

The correct model: rate-sensitive sector rotation depends on *which* part of the curve moves. Front-end cuts with falling long end = full duration trade (XLK, XLRE, XLU lead). Front-end cuts with rising long end = bear-steepener (XLF, value, defensives lead, growth disappoints). Watch the 10-year yield and the 2s10s curve, not the Fed alone.
