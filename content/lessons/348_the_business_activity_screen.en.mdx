# The business-activity screen

Before any ratio gets checked, Sharia screening asks a qualitative question first: what does this company actually *do*? A business whose core revenue comes from alcohol, tobacco, pork or non-halal food, gambling, conventional (interest-based) banking and insurance, weapons, or adult entertainment is excluded outright — no ratio can rescue a company whose core business is one of these. This is the first of the two-stage screen: business activity, then (lesson 3) the financial ratios. By the end of this lesson you'll be able to apply the business-activity screen to a real ticker and explain why it comes before the ratios, not after.

## Example

Run the qualitative screen against a short real-ticker list. **Diageo** (alcohol, London-listed but illustrative) and **Philip Morris International (PM)** (tobacco) fail outright — their core revenue is a prohibited product, so the screen stops there; their balance-sheet ratios are irrelevant. **Genting Berhad** and **Genting Malaysia** (Bursa: GENTING, GENM) fail because gaming/casino revenue is their core business — a name every Malaysian investor recognizes, and a textbook example every Bursa SAC screening report uses. Conventional banks like **Maybank** or **CIMB** fail on business activity too — not because banking is inherently wrong, but because a *conventional* bank's core revenue is interest income (riba) by construction; this is why Islamic banks are structured as separate legal entities with murabahah/ijarah-based products rather than a screened subset of a conventional bank. By contrast, **NVDA** (semiconductors), **AAPL** (consumer electronics), and **TENAGA** (Malaysian utility) pass the business-activity stage — their core revenue isn't in an excluded sector — and move on to the ratio screen in the next lesson.

## The trap

Beginners think the business screen is the whole test — "it's not a casino or a brewery, so it's halal." That's only stage one. A semiconductor company with a clean business line can still fail the *financial-ratio* screen in lesson 3 if its debt or interest-bearing cash pile is too large a share of its market cap. The correct mental model: business activity and financial ratios are two independent gates in sequence, and a company must clear *both* — passing the first tells you nothing about the second. The reverse trap also exists: don't assume a company is automatically excluded just because it holds *some* interest-bearing debt — nearly every large company does, which is exactly why the ratio screen (not a zero-tolerance rule) exists to draw the line.

## Try it

In your Mandate settings, toggle the halal flag on and add a mixed watchlist (a bank, a consumer-tech name, a Bursa gaming stock). Ask the Fundamentals Analyst which names the business-activity screen alone would exclude, before any ratio is even computed.

## Takeaway

The business-activity screen runs first and is categorical — a business in an excluded sector fails regardless of its balance sheet, and passing it only earns a company a shot at the ratio screen next.

*Where this comes from: Bursa Malaysia SAC's published sector-exclusion methodology; the Dow Jones Islamic Market Index's business-activity screening criteria.*
