# Market manipulation: pumps, spoofs, and wash trades

Manipulation is manufacturing a signal that other people trade on. The three classic shapes: **pump-and-dump** manufactures a narrative (coordinated hype inflates price so organisers can sell into it), **spoofing** manufactures supply or demand (large orders placed with no intention to fill, cancelled once the market reacts), and **wash trading** manufactures volume (trading with yourself so activity looks like interest). You met pump groups from the victim's side in the scam-protection module; this lesson shows you the machinery from the market's side, so you can spot all three in a tape.

## How this plays out in real markets

Navinder Sarao, trading from his parents' house in London, spoofed the S&P 500 E-mini futures for years — layering large sell orders he intended to cancel, creating phantom supply that nudged prices down so his real orders could profit. US authorities said he admitted about $12.8 million in gains in his US guilty plea, and his activity contributed to conditions on May 6, 2010, when the Dow fell 998.5 points intraday — the Flash Crash — before recovering within minutes. One trader, fake orders, measurable market-wide damage.

On Bursa Malaysia, the recurring version is the penny-counter pump: a Telegram group "discovers" an illiquid stock, coordinated buying triples the volume and lifts the price 40-80% in days, screenshots of gains recruit the next wave, and the organisers — who accumulated first — sell into the demand they manufactured. The chart shows a spike and collapse; the mechanism is the same phantom-signal trick as spoofing, done with narrative instead of orders.

## The trap

Beginners treat volume as proof of genuine interest: "look how much is trading — someone knows something." It feels right because in an honest market, volume *is* information. Manipulated volume is engineered precisely to exploit that instinct — a wash-traded token or a pumped penny stock produces the exact tape a real accumulation would. The correct mental model: **ask what would falsify the tape.** Does the bid depth persist when it's actually hit, or vanish? Did volume arrive with a verifiable catalyst — a filing, a contract, an earnings release — or only with anonymous conviction? A signal you can't trace to a source is a signal someone may have built for you.

## Try it

Convene the Room on a small-cap from your watchlist and read the Technical Strategist's volume commentary closely. Does it distinguish *how much* is trading from *why* it's trading — does any cited volume trace to a verifiable catalyst? Practising that one question on a simulated verdict builds the reflex that protects you on a manipulated tape.

## Takeaway

Manipulation manufactures the exact signals you were taught to trust — volume, depth, momentum. Before you weight a signal, trace its source; a tape without a catalyst is a claim, not a fact.
