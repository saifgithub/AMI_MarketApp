# Moving average ribbon — trend regime at a glance

Lesson 026 used one or two moving averages at a time. A ribbon stacks several — typically six to eight EMAs in graduated lengths like 8, 13, 21, 34, 55, 89 — so you see the entire term structure of trend at once. When all the lines fan out cleanly with the shortest on top and the longest on the bottom, every timeframe is in agreement and the trend is strong. When the lines compress into a tight band, the trend has lost differentiation and the market is consolidating. When they cross-weave, the trend has broken down. The ribbon turns "is there a trend?" into a visual three-state answer — fanned, compressed, or tangled.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at MSFT through September 2025. Through the first three weeks of September, the EMA8 sat around 522, EMA21 at 514, EMA55 at 498, EMA89 at 486 — a clean fan with roughly $36 of spread between the fastest and slowest line. Price held above all six MAs. That's a fully-fanned ribbon, the strongest regime.

On October 15, MSFT broke 510 and the EMA8 cut down through EMA21 and EMA34. The ribbon compressed: the spread between fastest and slowest dropped to $11 by October 22. Compression precedes regime change. By November 5, EMAs were braided together — EMA21 above EMA8, EMA34 above EMA55 in places — a tangled state that lasted nine sessions. MSFT chopped between 478 and 495 the whole time. On November 18, MSFT closed at 470, the ribbon re-fanned in the opposite order (longest on top, shortest on bottom), and the new down-regime ran to 452 by December.

Falsifiable claim: a fully-fanned ribbon with rising slope produces materially fewer false signals than the same trend read from a single MA. Invalidation is mechanical — when the ribbon compresses to less than half its average spread for 10+ sessions, the prevailing regime is at risk of ending; when the ribbon braids (any of the fast/slow ordering reverses), the regime has ended by definition.

On Bursa Malaysia: MAYBANK in 2025 had a textbook fanned ribbon from January through March, compressed through April, then re-fanned in the opposite direction in May as it rolled over from RM10.50 to RM9.80.

## The trap

The trap is reading one MA on a chart and missing the term-structure context. A stock can be above its SMA50 (looks bullish) while the EMA8 is below the EMA21 (short-term momentum has already turned). One MA gives you a yes/no; the ribbon gives you the entire shape. Beginners enter long when one MA looks favourable and ignore that the rest of the term structure is already braiding against them.

The correct mental model: the ribbon is a regime classifier. Fanned and aligned = trade with the trend; compressed = stand aside or position for the breakout; tangled = no trend, no trade unless you're a mean-reversion specialist. The ribbon doesn't tell you what to do — it tells you whether your strategy is in season. Trend-following strategies need a fanned ribbon to work; mean-reversion strategies need a tangled one.

## Takeaway

A moving-average ribbon turns trend into a three-state regime classifier — fanned to ride, compressed to wait, tangled to stand aside.
