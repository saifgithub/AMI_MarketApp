# MACD — trend + momentum

The Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD) is two EMAs subtracted from each other, plus a signal line and a histogram. The default settings are 12, 26, 9: MACD line = EMA(12) − EMA(26), signal line = EMA(9) of the MACD line, histogram = MACD − signal. When the MACD line crosses above the signal line and the histogram flips from negative to positive, momentum is turning up. When it crosses below and goes negative, momentum is turning down. The zero line tells you trend regime — above zero means the fast EMA is above the slow EMA, below zero means the opposite. MACD is RSI's slower, sturdier cousin: fewer signals, more weight per signal.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at AAPL through Q4 2025. On October 14, AAPL closed at 226 with the MACD(12,26,9) line at -1.8, below its signal line, histogram negative. Over the next two weeks, the histogram bars shortened — still negative, but each bar smaller than the last. That's momentum decelerating to the downside. On October 30, AAPL closed at 232, MACD line crossed above signal at -0.4, histogram flipped positive at +0.3. The MACD line itself crossed back above zero on November 7 at 238, confirming the trend regime had flipped.

AAPL then ran to 256 by early December. The trade signal — bullish MACD cross below zero with the histogram already shrinking — was visible on October 30 before the zero-line cross confirmed on November 7. Earlier signal, more risk; later confirmation, less risk.

Falsification: a bullish MACD cross below zero is invalidated if price closes back below the recent swing low while the histogram is still positive but rolling over. If AAPL had cracked 225 after the October 30 cross, the signal would have failed — that's the wrong-way evidence you act on, not the right-way story you wanted.

On Bursa Malaysia: GENTING in 2024 had a textbook bearish MACD cross above zero at RM4.85, histogram turned negative, price fell to RM4.20 over six weeks. Same mechanics, different exchange.

## The trap

Beginners trade every MACD cross. That's noise. In a choppy, sideways market, MACD whips back and forth across its signal line every few days, generating eight to twelve fake signals a month, each one a small loss. The signal-to-noise ratio collapses when the underlying trend is absent.

The correct mental model: MACD is a trend-and-momentum filter, not a standalone setup. Use it to confirm a setup you already see in price — a pullback in an uptrend, a breakout from consolidation, a divergence at a swing high. The cross is the timing, but the context (trend regime + structure) is the edge. If price action doesn't agree with the MACD signal, the signal is junk.

## Takeaway

MACD describes trend regime (zero line) and momentum turns (signal line); take the cross only when price structure already agrees with it.
