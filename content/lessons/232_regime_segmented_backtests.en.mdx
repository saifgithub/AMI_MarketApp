# Regime-segmented backtests — where does the edge actually live?

The failure-modes foundation (058) said every strategy has a regime where it bleeds. This lesson is how you find it BEFORE the regime hits. A regime-segmented backtest splits the historical sample by regime — bull/bear/sideways, high-vol/low-vol, rates-up/rates-down — and reports performance per bucket instead of a single aggregate. The aggregate number hides everything that matters. A strategy that returns 12% CAGR overall might earn 35% in bull years and -8% in bears, or 18% in low-vol and -2% in high-vol. The composition is the strategy's true profile. After this lesson you can segment your own backtest by regime, name the regime where your rule bleeds, and pre-commit a sizing rule for that regime.

## How this plays out in real markets

Take a long-only momentum rule on US large-caps over 2005-2024. Aggregate CAGR: 10.4%, max drawdown 28%. Now segment.

By trend regime (SPY above/below 200-day MA, 200-day slope positive/negative):
- Bull (above 200-day, positive slope, ~63% of months): CAGR 18.2%, drawdown 14%
- Sideways (above 200-day, flat slope, ~15% of months): CAGR 4.1%, drawdown 11%
- Bear (below 200-day, negative slope, ~22% of months): CAGR -7.8%, drawdown 28%

By volatility regime (VIX above/below 22):
- Low vol (VIX < 22, ~70% of months): CAGR 14.6%, drawdown 13%
- High vol (VIX > 22, ~30% of months): CAGR -1.2%, drawdown 28%

The aggregate 10.4% is real but useless for decision-making. The strategy is two strategies — a strongly profitable bull/low-vol strategy and a money-losing bear/high-vol strategy. The 2008, March 2020, and 2022 drawdowns all came from the bear/high-vol bucket. The synthesis: a regime overlay that reduces gross exposure when SPY is below its 200-day MA AND VIX is above 22 would have cut the max drawdown from 28% to roughly 14% with minimal sacrifice in bull-market upside. The regime segmentation IS the regime overlay — segment first, build the filter from what you find.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is reporting only the aggregate and treating the strategy as a single object. That gives you a misleading expected return (you're averaging incompatible regimes) and hides the regime where you'll blow up. The correct mental model: a strategy is a portfolio of regime-conditional returns, and you can only honestly deploy it by either accepting the bear-bucket drawdown explicitly OR overlaying a regime filter that switches the rule off in its known failure regime. The second trap is over-segmenting — splitting by every conceivable factor until each bucket has 3 trades. The bucket has to contain enough trades to be statistically meaningful (rule of thumb: at least 30 trades per bucket). The third trap is using a regime indicator known only with hindsight ("NBER recession periods") — your regime overlay must use only data observable in real time.

## Takeaway

The aggregate CAGR hides the strategy's true profile. Segment by regime, name the bucket where you bleed, and build the regime filter or accept the drawdown — there is no third option.
