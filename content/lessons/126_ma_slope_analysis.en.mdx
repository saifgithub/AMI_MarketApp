# Moving average slope — measuring trend strength

<Lesson id="026_moving_averages"/> said "trade with the slope, not the level." This lesson is what slope actually means in numbers. Slope is the rate of change of the moving average — typically measured as the percentage change in the MA over a fixed window, like five bars or 20 bars. A 20-bar SMA50 slope of +0.6% per bar describes a strong, sustained climb. The same SMA50 at +0.05% per bar is barely sloping — a flat MA dressed as a rising one. Slope quantifies the conviction behind the trend, and a slope that is decelerating warns you the regime is weakening long before the MA itself turns.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at NVDA from August through November 2025. The SMA50 climbed every session from 118 to 145 — a 23% gain in 60 trading days. Average daily slope: roughly +0.35% per session, with the steepest stretch running +0.55% per session in mid-September.

Then in late October, NVDA's SMA50 was still rising — but the per-bar slope had dropped to +0.08%. The line was still going up; the rate of climb had collapsed to a fifth of its peak. Price went sideways between 142 and 150 for three weeks. On November 18, NVDA broke 142, the SMA50 finally flattened on November 24 at 144, and the line rolled over by December 2.

The slope warned you three weeks before the MA did. Falsifiable claim: when a moving average's per-bar slope falls below 25% of its recent peak slope and stays there for 10+ bars, the trend regime is decelerating and the MA itself will flatten or reverse within roughly 5–15 sessions. Invalidation is mechanical — if slope re-accelerates back above 50% of its peak within 10 bars, the deceleration warning cancels.

On Bursa Malaysia: TENAGA's SMA50 in early 2025 ran at +0.4% per session during a strong leg from RM12 to RM14. By April, slope had decayed to +0.05%; the line finally turned down in mid-May after three weeks of warning.

## The trap

The trap is reading slope binarily — "up or down" — and missing the deceleration phase. A beginner sees SMA50 still pointing up and stays long, even as slope falls from +0.55% per bar to +0.08%. The MA's direction hasn't changed; its strength has, and that's the signal you can use weeks before the cross fires.

The correct mental model: slope is a continuous reading, not a binary one. Track it in numbers — eyeball the steepness, or compute the percentage change over the last 20 bars. A slope chart of the MA itself often gives you a 1–3 week early warning that price is exhausting before the MA flattens. The MA tells you where the trend is; slope tells you how durable it is.

## Takeaway

MA slope is a continuous reading of trend strength — when slope decays below 25% of its peak, the trend is exhausting weeks before the MA itself flips.
