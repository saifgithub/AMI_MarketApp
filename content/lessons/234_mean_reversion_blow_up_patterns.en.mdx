# Mean-reversion blow-up patterns — when "oversold" goes to zero

The failure-modes foundation (058) named trending bear markets as the regime where mean-reversion bleeds. This lesson is the autopsy of the blow-up itself, because the way mean-reversion strategies die is specific and recognisable. The pattern: a normally-reliable RSI(2) or Bollinger signal fires; the trader buys the dip; price keeps falling; the next oversold signal fires lower; the trader doubles down; price keeps falling; the loss compounds. Without a regime filter, the strategy converts what should be a 1.5R loss into a 10R loss because every signal looks valid on the same indicator that ignored the trend. After this lesson you can name the four-stage blow-up pattern, identify it at stage 1 or 2, and exit before stages 3 and 4.

## How this plays out in real markets

The Q1 2020 COVID crash on US large-caps is the museum example. Take any quality name — AAPL, MSFT, JPM. RSI(2) mean-reversion on a quality large-cap had returned ~12% CAGR over 2010-2019 with maximum drawdowns under 8%. Then February 24 to March 23, 2020:

- **Stage 1 (Feb 24-27)**: SPY breaks the 200-day MA decisively. RSI(2) on most large-caps prints below 5 — a "rare" oversold reading historically. Mean-reversion traders enter long.
- **Stage 2 (Feb 28-Mar 6)**: Names fall another 5-12% from entry. RSI(2) prints below 5 AGAIN — another signal. Traders average down. The Bollinger Band lower-band tag has happened twice. The strategy's normal "average 2 days to mean revert" has already failed once.
- **Stage 3 (Mar 9-12)**: Circuit breakers trigger. Names down 20-30% from entry. The RSI(2) signal is STILL firing — every day shows extreme oversold. Traders who haven't already capitulated double or triple down. Some hit margin calls.
- **Stage 4 (Mar 16-23)**: The bottom. Some traders blow up entirely; others sell at the literal worst price. SPY bottoms at 218 — those who survived as cash buyers in late March made historic returns. Those who deployed mean-reversion without regime filters lost 30-50% of strategy capital.

The blow-up is the same shape in 2008 (September-October), in 2022 (January-October on a slower clock), and on individual names that go bankrupt (think any name that delisted). The signature: repeated oversold signals with no mean-revert response, against a 200-day MA falling, with VIX rising. Without a regime overlay that pauses the strategy when SPY is below its 200-day AND VIX is rising, mean-reversion can take you to zero.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is averaging down on each new oversold signal — "the cheaper it gets, the better the deal". That's not edge, that's the strategy's failure mode self-amplifying. The correct mental model: a mean-reversion signal is conditional information — it has predictive value ONLY in regimes where mean reversion is the underlying market behaviour. In a trending bear, the same signal is anti-predictive (oversold gets more oversold). The second trap: refusing to define what "regime change" looks like ahead of time, so when stage 2 starts firing you find reasons to dismiss it ("this is just volatility"). Pre-commit: SPY below 200-day MA AND VIX > 25 AND a single position is showing -2x the strategy's normal stop-loss = exit ALL mean-reversion positions, do not enter new ones, wait until both regime conditions reverse. The third trap: trying to short the bottom — mean-reversion in reverse has its own failure mode (the snapback rally on March 24, 2020 was +9% in a day).

## Takeaway

Mean-reversion dies in trending bears with rising vol. Stop new entries at stage 2 of the blow-up; you cannot dollar-cost-average your way out of a failure mode.
