# False confluence — indicator redundancy

<Lesson id="146_combining_indicators_effectively"/> introduced orthogonality as the test for genuine confluence. This lesson is the failure-mode pairing: false confluence, where multiple indicators agreeing produces a feeling of confirmation that's mathematically empty. The danger is psychological as much as analytical — a trader sees five indicators all flashing green and feels certainty that wasn't warranted. The cure is mechanical: classify each indicator on your chart by what dimension it measures, and treat agreement within a dimension as one vote, not several. This lesson sharpens that into a procedure you can run on any chart in under a minute.

## How this plays out in real markets

A trader looking at NVDA on a hypothetical signal: RSI(14) at 32, Stochastic %K at 18, Williams %R at -82, CCI at -135. Four "oversold" readings. The trader concludes "four-indicator confluence — strong buy."

Decompose the four:

- RSI(14): smoothed price-momentum oscillator (price-derived).
- Stochastic %K: close position within range oscillator (price-derived).
- Williams %R: same calculation family as Stochastic, scaled differently (price-derived).
- CCI: deviation from a simple moving average (price-derived).

All four are price-only momentum oscillators. They're correlated by construction; when a stock is oversold by one, it's oversold by the others. Four readings is one signal in four wrappers. Treating this as "four-indicator confluence" inflates conviction without adding information.

Contrast with a real two-indicator orthogonal pair: RSI(14) at 32 (momentum oversold) and OBV (On-Balance Volume) at a higher low than the prior price low (positive volume divergence). Now you have momentum and participation disagreeing with price action — two distinct signals. The conviction here is genuinely higher than the four-momentum-indicator stack, even though it's "only two indicators."

Falsifiable claim: in a sample of 30+ trades, signals labeled "four-indicator confluence" using four correlated momentum oscillators produce hit rates statistically indistinguishable from signals based on any single one of those oscillators. Invalidation: a backtest where the four-indicator setup outperforms by more than noise. The math is clear — when indicators correlate above 0.85 by construction, combining them adds approximately zero information.

A second example of false confluence: SMA20 above SMA50, EMA20 above EMA50, WMA20 above WMA50 — "trend confirmed across three MA systems." All three are MA crosses of the same periods on the same data; they fire within a session or two of each other always. One vote, not three.

On Bursa Malaysia: a TENAGA setup in late 2024 — a trader stacked five momentum oscillators that all read oversold, took an oversized position on "five-indicator confluence," and watched TENAGA fall another 6% before bottoming. The same trader using one momentum oscillator with proper position sizing would have taken a much smaller loss on the same trade.

## The trap

The trap is conviction inflation. Five agreeing indicators feel like five times the evidence — even when those five are all derived from the same data, measuring the same dimension. The brain weights "many agreeing sources" heavily without auditing whether the sources are independent. In journalism this is called "two sources" when both are quoting the same press release; in technical analysis it's "five-indicator confluence" when all five are derived from the same closing prices.

The correct mental model: indicator redundancy is mathematical correlation, not opinion. Run the procedure: list each indicator's dimension. If two indicators land in the same bucket, they vote once. Conviction should scale with the number of distinct dimensions agreeing, not the number of lines on the chart. A two-dimensional agreement (momentum plus participation) beats a five-line momentum-only "confluence" every time — by construction.

## Try it

Open your charting workspace and count the indicators on a current chart. For each, write one phrase: what dimension does it measure (regime, direction, momentum, volume/participation, volatility)? If any two land in the same bucket, drop one. Then revisit a recent trade journal entry and ask: did I take a larger position than warranted because correlated indicators inflated my conviction? If yes, that's the lesson taking hold.

## Takeaway

Multiple indicators measuring the same dimension is one signal in many wrappers, not many signals — conviction should scale with the number of distinct dimensions agreeing, not the number of lines on the chart.
