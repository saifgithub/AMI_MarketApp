# PASS is not BUY — the verdict is conditional

When the PM stamps PASS on a simulated <Term id="ami_verdict" />, it is reporting one thing only: inside the training scenario, the proposed trade fits inside your <Term id="ami_mandate" />. PASS is not "AMI thinks you should take this trade". PASS is not "the trade will be profitable". PASS is not "the drawdown is capped at the stop". PASS is "compliance: clear, within the simulator". The trading team's BUY line is a separate <Term id="training_artifact" /> — not investment advice, not a recommendation to act on outside the app. <Term id="ami" /> is a <Term id="training_simulator" />, not a licensed advisor: BUY is not what AMI is offering you. AMI is offering you training in evaluating a simulated BUY artifact. Reading PASS as buy-approval is the single most common misreading of the Verdict structure. By the end of this lesson you will be able to articulate the difference cleanly.

## How this plays out in real markets

You convene the Room on MSFT. The Verdict reads:

> **BUY MSFT · size 3% · entry $418 · stop $402 · target $445 · horizon 5 weeks · confidence 0.64**
>
> *Portfolio Manager (compliance check):* PASS — within max_position_pct 5, within max_drawdown_pct 20, no exclusions triggered.

Four distinct statements are bundled here, and each is conditional on different things — *all four are training-scenario constructs, not advice*.

1. **BUY.** The trading team's *simulated training output*. Conditional on the analysis being right within the scenario. Hallucinations, regime changes, and surprises can all invalidate it. It is not a recommendation to take anywhere.
2. **Size 3%.** The Trader's proposal, blessed by the Risk Debators' synthesis. Conditional on your Mandate's `max_position_pct`. If your mandate were 2%, the same debate would land at 2%.
3. **Entry/stop/target/horizon.** The trade *plan inside the simulator*. Each level is conditional — entry triggers if price reaches $418; stop fires if price hits $402; target locks profit if reached within 5 weeks. None of these are predictions and none are advice.
4. **PM: PASS.** Compliance result. Conditional on the *proposed* trade fitting your stated Mandate — nothing more. Same trade re-proposed at 6% would BLOCK without anything else changing.

Now consider the failure mode. A user reads "PM: PASS" as "AMI approves" and treats the BUY as a green light. They are conflating two separate training signals: a simulated BUY (from the trading team) and a compliance PASS (from the PM) — and then converting both into something AMI does not offer, which is an instruction to act. The PM never had a view on whether MSFT is a good trade. It had a view on whether *the proposed-size trade fits your Mandate inside this training scenario*. Even a terrible trade can pass compliance if size is small enough; even a brilliant trade can be blocked if size is oversize.

## The trap

The trap is reading "PASS" through the lens of approval. In everyday language, "the boss passed it" means "the boss agreed". The PM is not a boss; it is a simulated compliance officer. Compliance officers don't sign off on whether the trade is *wise*; they sign off on whether it is *within the rules*. A compliance officer who approved every trade that fit policy and rejected the ones that didn't has done their job perfectly — and has expressed no opinion on outcome. Same with the PM. The Verdict's BUY/SELL/HOLD line is a training artifact for evaluation; the PM's PASS/BLOCK line is the compliance result inside the scenario. Mixing them collapses two training signals into one fake approval and you lose the structure that makes the simulator worth running.

## Try it

<Term id="ami_convene_the_room" /> on a <Term id="ami_watchlist" /> ticker. When the Verdict appears, read the PM compliance line and *do not* read the BUY/SELL headline yet. Ask yourself: *if the PM blocked, would I want this trade anyway? If the PM passed, would I take it without reading the debate?* Then read the rest. Notice how the PM result and the trading-team verdict are doing different jobs.

## Takeaway

PASS = compliance fits inside the simulator. BUY = the trading team's training artifact for you to evaluate. Two independent layers, neither one a recommendation to act on outside the app. AMI is teaching you to evaluate analyst output, not handing you advice to follow.
