# Survival mindset

Survival mindset is the disposition that treats "still in the game next month" as a higher-priority objective than "made money this week." Edges in markets are statistical — they pay off across hundreds of trades, not five. Anyone who blows up before the sample size matures never gets to collect on the edge they thought they had. This lesson is not a math lesson; it is the operating principle that makes every other risk lesson in this module worth following. The PM enforces it structurally; you internalise it behaviourally.

## How this plays out in real markets

In Q3 2024, two simulated CEOs ran identical <Term id="ami_mandate" />s except for one field. Both started at $100,000. Both took the same 60 trades over the quarter using the Room's verdicts. Trader A had `risk_score: 1` (a 1.5% single-name cap) and `max_drawdown_pct: 20`. Trader B left both fields loose and "sized to conviction" — 3% to 5% on trades the Room flagged as high-confidence.

Through the first 30 trades Trader B was up 14%, Trader A up 4%. Trader B's social-media posts about "leverage and conviction" got a lot of engagement. Then a Fed surprise + a TSLA earnings miss hit on the same week. Trader B was overweight three correlated tech names at the time, sized 4% each. The week wiped 31% off the account, and the next two weeks of revenge trading took it to a 47% drawdown. End of quarter: Trader A finished +7% (smaller drawdown, recovered quickly), Trader B finished −44% and effectively out of the game — needing 79% just to get back to even on a base that no longer has the room to take normal-sized trades.

Same setups, same Room, same Execution Desk. The only difference was a Mandate field. Falsifiable claim: across a 60-trade sample, accounts with `risk_score ≤ 2` and a tight drawdown ceiling end the quarter within 80% of peak more than 90% of the time; accounts sized looser than that cross 30% drawdown more than 1 in 4 quarters.

## The trap

The trap is treating risk rules as friction — caps that "slow you down on your best ideas." The beginner who has had three wins in a row genuinely believes the next trade should be bigger, because the edge feels obvious. This is exactly when sizing creeps up, stops widen "to give it room," and the mandate gets renegotiated mid-session. Then one trade goes wrong while sized at 4% instead of 1% and the quarter's gains vanish in an afternoon. The correct mental model: the risk rules exist for the trades you don't see coming. They protect future-you from present-you's confidence. You don't earn the right to relax them by winning a few in a row — winning streaks are when survival mindset is most tested, not least needed.

## Try it

Open the Mandate editor right now. If `risk_score` is above 2, tighten it to 1. If `max_drawdown_pct` is above 20, tighten it to 20. Then open a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Chief Investment Officer and ask "Given my current mandate, what's the worst single-quarter outcome I could realistically have?" The answer is the floor survival mindset is buying you.

## Takeaway

The trader who finishes the year is the trader who collects the edge — protect tomorrow's seat at the table before chasing today's gain.
