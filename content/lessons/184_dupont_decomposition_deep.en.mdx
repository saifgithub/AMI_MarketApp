# DuPont decomposition — splitting ROE into three doors

Lesson 041 introduced DuPont and said the same headline ROE describes very different businesses. This lesson uses the decomposition to actually rank business *quality* by the source of ROE. Margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier. The three numbers compose into one, and the composition tells you what kind of company you own. By the end you should be able to compute each component from a 10-K, recognise the canonical patterns (luxury, retail, bank, software), and name the falsifiable condition under which "ROE improvement" is actually a quality downgrade.

## How this plays out in real markets

Take four real businesses, all near 20–25% ROE, and look at the doors they walked through:

- **LVMH (luxury)**: ~20% net margin × ~0.6 asset turnover × ~2.0 equity multiplier ≈ 24% ROE. Margin-dominant — pricing power is the engine.
- **WMT (retail)**: ~3% net margin × ~2.5 asset turnover × ~2.7 equity multiplier ≈ 20% ROE. Turnover-dominant — thin margins moved many times a year.
- **JPM (bank)**: ~28% net margin × ~6% asset turnover × ~10x equity multiplier ≈ 17% ROE. Leverage-dominant — modest margin on a vast balance sheet.
- **MSFT (software)**: ~36% net margin × ~0.5 asset turnover × ~2.0 equity multiplier ≈ 36% ROE. Margin × low leverage — the rare combination of pricing power on a clean balance sheet.

Same general range of ROE, four completely different businesses. The composition tells you what would damage the ROE: LVMH suffers if luxury demand falls (margin erosion); WMT suffers if turnover slows (inventory pile-up, store underperformance); JPM suffers in a credit cycle (the leverage multiplier compresses to losses); MSFT suffers only if pricing or volume in software falls — historically rare.

Real DuPont signal in action: WMT in 2014–2018 saw asset turnover drift down from 2.6 to 2.4 as e-commerce mix shifted; ROE compressed from ~20% to ~17% even as margins held flat. The DuPont decomposition flagged the issue early. Anyone watching only the headline ROE missed which lever was bending.

On Bursa: PUBLIC BANK's ROE around 13–14% decomposes to roughly 30% net margin × 4% asset turnover × ~12x equity multiplier. MAYBANK's ~10% ROE: similar margins, similar turnover, similar leverage — the gap is mostly credit-loss provisions and operating efficiency. Identical structure, different operating execution.

Falsifiable claim: a one-year ROE improvement is high-quality only when *margin or asset turnover* improves while equity multiplier holds steady or falls. If leverage rises (equity multiplier up) while margin and turnover are flat, the ROE gain is a balance-sheet decision, not a business decision — and it's reversible at the cost of higher fragility.

## How this plays out in real markets — second example

Watch a retailer reporting "improved ROE" of 17% vs 13% the prior year. DuPont decomposition: net margin steady at 3.5%, asset turnover steady at 2.4, equity multiplier rose from 1.5x to 2.0x. The ROE improvement is entirely a debt-financed share repurchase shrinking equity. Same operating business, more debt, higher leverage-adjusted volatility. The market often discounts these "improvements" by *lowering* the multiple it pays for the ROE — recognising that the new ROE is less robust than the old one.

## The trap

The trap is congratulating a company for "improving" ROE without checking which door the gain came through. Three doors: margin (pricing power, mix shift, cost discipline — high quality), turnover (operating efficiency, working-capital discipline, store productivity — medium-to-high quality), leverage (balance-sheet choice — low-to-fragile quality). A reader who sees 17% → 22% ROE and writes it down as "improvement" without decomposition has missed the most important information in the report. The correct mental model: every ROE change should be followed by the question "which of the three doors moved?" — and only margin-and-turnover gains earn the right to a higher P/B. Leverage-driven gains often come with multiple compression because the market sees the change in risk profile. What invalidates a "high-quality" reading: when leverage rises into a declining-rates environment (cheap debt makes the trade temporarily attractive even when fundamentals don't justify it), the eventual rate cycle exposes the fragility — see how leveraged consumer-finance ROEs collapsed in 2022–2023 as cheap funding disappeared.

## Takeaway

ROE has three doors — margin, turnover, leverage. Margin-driven ROE earns a higher multiple; turnover-driven ROE is operational; leverage-driven ROE is fragile and usually doesn't earn the multiple bump it appears to justify.
