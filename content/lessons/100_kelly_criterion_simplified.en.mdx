# Kelly criterion, simplified

This lesson assumes you've completed <Lesson id="014_position_sizing_basics"/> (Position sizing basics); here we go deeper on a different sizing model — Kelly — that uses your historical win rate and average R:R to recommend a per-trade fraction. The Kelly formula tells you the size that maximises long-run compounded growth given a real edge. The catch: it assumes your win rate and payoff are stable and accurately measured, and full Kelly produces stomach-churning drawdowns. Most disciplined traders run fractional Kelly — a quarter or half — for that reason. This lesson teaches the simplified formula, the inputs that break it, and why "half-Kelly" is the working compromise.

## Example

Look at a 60-trade sample on US tech setups from your <Term id="ami_decision_journal" />. You won 38 of them — a 63% win rate. Average winner: +1.8R. Average loser: −1R. The simplified Kelly formula for an asymmetric payoff is f = (W × R − L) ÷ R, where W is win probability, L = 1 − W, and R is the win/loss payoff ratio. Plug in: f = (0.63 × 1.8 − 0.37) ÷ 1.8 = (1.134 − 0.37) ÷ 1.8 = 0.424. Full Kelly says size each trade at 42% of your account.

That number should alarm you. A single losing trade at 42% of account is a 42% drawdown — well past the recovery cliff from <Lesson id="018_drawdown_management"/>. Worse, your "63% win rate" is a sample estimate; the true win rate could easily be 55% (Kelly = 18%) or 50% (Kelly = 5.5%). Half-Kelly on the same numbers is 21%, quarter-Kelly is 10.6% — still aggressive. The 1% rule you already use is roughly tenth-Kelly on a credible edge, which is why it survives sampling error. On Bursa Malaysia: same 38/60 sample on PETRONAS swing setups, same formula, same problematic output. Falsifiable test: if your measured win rate × payoff ratio doesn't exceed 1, your edge is negative and Kelly returns a negative fraction — sit out, not size up.

## The trap

The trap is plugging in optimistic numbers and trusting the output. A beginner runs 12 trades, wins 8, computes Kelly at 40%, and sizes the next position at 40% of account. The 12-trade sample is statistical noise — the true win rate could be 50% with the same expected value. Full Kelly on a wrong estimate doesn't just underperform; it can blow up the account on a normal losing streak. The correct mental model: Kelly is an upper bound, not a target. Run fractional Kelly (typically 0.25× to 0.5×), require a minimum sample of 50 trades before trusting the inputs, and cap the output at whatever your mandate allows. The 1% rule is the floor; Kelly is the ceiling; you live in between.

## Try it

Open the Decision Journal, filter to closed trades, and tally: total trades, wins, average winner R, average loser R. Compute full Kelly with the formula above. Then open a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Portfolio Manager, paste the numbers, and ask "Given sampling error on this size, what fractional Kelly would you recommend, and how does it compare to my current 1% rule?"

## Takeaway

Full Kelly is the ceiling; the 1% rule is the floor — live between them with fractional Kelly only after 50+ real trades, not a hot start.
