# Volatility compression: the calm before a regime change

<Lesson id="061_sideways_range_markets"/> and <Lesson id="062_volatility_regimes"/> defined range and volatility regimes separately. This lesson teaches the transition signal — *compression* — that often precedes a regime change in either direction. When realised volatility drops to multi-month lows while price holds a tight band, the market is loading energy for a directional move. After this lesson you will be able to detect a compression setup using ATR-percentile and Bollinger-Band-Width readings, name the strategies that pair with the resolution, and state the condition that confirms a true breakout vs a false one.

## How this plays out in real markets

SPX in late 2017 ran a famous compression: from August to December 2017, SPX 20-day ATR fell to ~0.36% of price — the lowest reading since 1965 by some measures. Bollinger Band Width (the BB width / middle band, 20-day, 2-sigma) compressed to ~2.6% in late November 2017. The compression resolved upward — SPX gained another 7% into the 26 January 2018 high of 2,872. Then the regime broke violently: the 5 February 2018 "volmageddon" session saw SPX drop 4.1%, VIX spike to 50.30 intraday, and the XIV inverse-VIX product de-listed.

A cleaner two-sided compression: SPX August 2023. After the June–July range from <Lesson id="061_sideways_range_markets"/>, SPX 20-day ATR fell to 0.45% of price by 1 August 2023. Bollinger Band Width compressed to ~3.1%. The compression resolved DOWN — SPX fell 8.3% from 4,588 on 27 July 2023 to 4,217 on 27 October 2023. A breakout strategy that traded the *direction* of the resolution would have caught it; a strategy that assumed up-resolution would have lost.

Detection rule for compression: 20-day ATR-as-%-of-price reads in the bottom 15% of its trailing 1-year distribution AND Bollinger Band Width (20, 2) reads under 4% AND the move occurs while the regime is range-classified per <Lesson id="061_sideways_range_markets"/>. All three present is the compression cluster.

Confirmation rule for the resolution: a daily close OUTSIDE the prior 20-session high-low band on volume at least 1.5x the 20-day average. Without the volume confirmation, the breakout is suspect — Q1 2024 SPX printed two such low-volume breakouts that briefly retraced before the eventual move.

## The trap

The compression trap is predicting the direction. The story-driven trader looks at the macro narrative and decides "this will break up" or "this will break down" before the price prints. They build a directional position inside the compression, get caught on the wrong side of the resolution, and turn a setup that historically pays asymmetric returns into a 50/50 coin flip with bad timing.

The correct model: compression is a setup for *either* direction. The trade is built after the resolution prints on volume, not before. Strategies that fit: long-volatility (options-based) inside the compression for traders with that mandate flag; breakout-following on the price-volume confirmation; pair-with-stop strategies that go with the resolution and place the stop back inside the compression range.

## Takeaway

Compression loads energy; direction is the market's vote, not yours. Trade the volume-confirmed resolution, place stops back inside the range, and size for the regime uncertainty until the move survives.
