# On-Balance Volume — accumulation vs distribution

<Lesson id="029_volume_confirmation"/> framed volume as the participation count behind a price move. On-Balance Volume (OBV) is a cumulative running total of that participation, signed by price direction. The rule is mechanical: on every up-close, add the bar's volume to OBV; on every down-close, subtract it. The level of OBV is meaningless on its own — what matters is its slope and its agreement (or disagreement) with price. When OBV climbs alongside a rising price, accumulation is confirming the trend. When OBV diverges — price makes a new high but OBV does not — buyers are participating less than the price chart suggests. OBV divergence is to volume what RSI divergence is to momentum: a warning that the trend's foundation is weakening before price shows it.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at a stock over a four-month stretch. Over the first two months (price 138, OBV at an arbitrary baseline 0) through the point price reaches 156, OBV rose from 0 to +480 million — a clean accumulation pattern. Every meaningful up-day printed above-average volume; down-days were lighter. The stock's trend had volume backing.

Over the next seven weeks, the stock continued to push higher to 162 — but OBV stalled and rolled over to +420 million. Price made a new high; OBV made a lower high. Classic bearish OBV divergence: the up-days had lighter volume than the down-days, so cumulative participation was actually fading even as price extended. Three weeks later, the stock was back at 149.

Falsifiable claim: when OBV makes a lower high while price makes a higher high (or vice versa) and the divergence persists for 10+ sessions, the trend is at heightened risk of reversal. The invalidation is mechanical — if OBV makes a new high before price breaks its most recent swing low, the divergence cancels and accumulation is back on track.

The OBV slope by itself is a faster regime read than counting up-volume vs down-volume manually. A flattening OBV with rising price is the textbook distribution-into-strength pattern smart money uses to exit.

## The trap

The trap is reading OBV's level instead of its slope. OBV starting value is arbitrary (it's cumulative from whenever you started counting), so a chart showing OBV at +800 million tells you nothing absolute. Beginners look at the number and think it carries meaning; it doesn't. The signal is entirely in the shape — is OBV rising with price, flat, or making lower highs against price's higher highs?

The correct mental model: OBV is a participation tape that runs in parallel with the price tape. Compare the two shapes, not the two levels. Aligned shapes = trend has volume support. Disagreeing shapes = volume foundation is weakening and price is on borrowed time. OBV does not replace a 20-day average volume read; it adds a different dimension — cumulative direction-weighted participation over time, rather than spot participation at a single bar.

## Try it

In the Market Analyst <Term id="ami_one_on_one" />, paste a trending ticker and ask: "Compare the slope of OBV against the slope of price over the last 30 sessions — are they aligned, or is there a divergence?" Then verify on the chart. The exercise is to read OBV's shape, not its number — flatten the OBV line in your mind and check whether it's tilting up or down relative to price.

## Takeaway

OBV is a cumulative participation tape — read its shape against price's shape, not its level; a persistent divergence (10+ sessions) is the volume-side warning that a trend's foundation is failing.
