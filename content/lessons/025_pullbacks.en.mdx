# Pullbacks

A pullback is a temporary move against the prevailing trend — a breather inside a larger advance or decline. In an uptrend, pullbacks are where you get to buy at a discount without chasing; in a downtrend, they're where short opportunities reset. The trick is telling a healthy pullback from a trend reversal in progress. By the end of this lesson you should know what a normal pullback looks like in size, depth, and structure, where a trend-following entry sits inside one, and the exact condition that flips a pullback into something you should stop calling a pullback.

## Example

Look at AMZN through February to April 2026. AMZN broke out above $215 in early February and ran to $238.60 by 21 February — an uptrend with clearly defined higher highs and higher lows. Then between 22 February and 4 March, AMZN pulled back: nine sessions of lower daily closes carrying price down to $224.10 on 4 March. That's a 6.1% retracement from the $238.60 high — well within the typical 3%–10% pullback range for a trending mega-cap. The pullback came to rest at the prior breakout level around $225, with a daily close that printed a long lower wick on 4 March (buyers stepping in at the level).

On 5 March, AMZN closed at $228.40 — a higher low confirming the pullback was complete. By 18 March price had pushed to a new swing high of $244.10. The pullback was a 6% dip inside a trend that ultimately delivered another ~9% leg.

A trader who entered the pullback on the 5 March higher-low confirmation, with a stop just below the $224.10 swing low, risked roughly $4.30 to make $15.70 to the eventual new high — a ~1:3.6 risk/reward.

The pullback thesis invalidates the moment AMZN closes a daily candle *below* the most recent higher-low of the prior uptrend. In this case, that meant a daily close below the $215.70 prior higher-low. If AMZN had closed at, say, $213, the uptrend's structure would have been broken — and what you were calling a pullback would now be the first leg of a possible new downtrend.

## The trap

The trap is calling every dip a pullback and buying it without checking whether the trend that was supposed to be there is still actually there. A beginner sees a stock down 8% from its high, remembers it was an uptrend last week, buys "the dip", and discovers a week later that the most recent higher-low has been taken out — the trend is broken and they're now long a stock in a fresh downtrend. The correct mental model: a pullback is only a pullback while the trend's structural higher-low (uptrend) or lower-high (downtrend) is still intact. The moment a daily close breaks that swing level, what you thought was a pullback becomes a reversal, and trend-following logic no longer applies. Always know the exact price that flips the label.

## Takeaway

A pullback is only a pullback while the trend's last higher-low (or lower-high) is still intact — know that price, mark it before you enter, and let a daily close through it be what changes your mind, not your mood.
