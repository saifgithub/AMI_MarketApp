# Stochastic oscillator — %K / %D crosses

<Lesson id="027_rsi_momentum_oscillator"/> introduced RSI as a momentum oscillator that compares average up-closes to average down-closes. The Stochastic oscillator measures something different: where the current close sits within the high-low range of the last N bars. %K is the raw reading — (close − lowest low) / (highest high − lowest low) × 100, computed over a 14-bar lookback by default. %D is a 3-bar SMA of %K. The conventional overbought/oversold levels are 80/20, slightly tighter than RSI's 70/30. The headline signal is the %K / %D crossover: %K crossing above %D from below is a momentum-up signal; %K crossing below %D from above is a momentum-down signal. Cross direction matters; cross location matters more.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at a stock after a multi-week pullback from 215 to 195. Daily Stochastic %K crossed up through %D from a reading of 17 — both lines deep in oversold territory below 20. Three sessions later the stock was at 201; ten sessions later at 209. The bullish %K / %D cross at the oversold extreme caught the turn.

Some weeks later, the same stock at 217 produced another bullish %K cross, but this time the cross fired at a %K reading of 64 — middle of the range, not oversold. Same indicator, same direction. The stock traded sideways for two weeks then gave back the entire move. The cross in the middle of the range carried no edge.

Falsifiable claim: %K / %D crosses occurring below 20 (bullish setup) or above 80 (bearish setup) have materially higher follow-through than crosses occurring between 30 and 70. Invalidation: a six-month sample where mid-range crosses produce equal or greater forward returns means location is irrelevant — but that has not held up in practice, and the example above shows why.

The complementary signal is the failure swing — %K dips below 20, rallies back above 20, pulls back without making a new low, then crosses %D from below again. This double-pump out of oversold is the highest-conviction stochastic setup.

## The trap

The trap is treating every %K / %D cross as a signal regardless of location. Beginners read "stochastic crossover = entry" and act on crosses at 50 or 60 the same as crosses at 15. The indicator fires constantly because its 3-bar smoothing makes minor wiggles look like crossovers. Most of those crossovers happen in the middle of the range, where the stochastic has no edge.

The correct mental model: the cross is the trigger, but the location is the qualifier. Crosses below 20 or above 80 are extreme-zone signals where reversal odds are favourable; crosses elsewhere are noise. The 80/20 thresholds aren't decoration — they're the filter that turns a fast-flipping oscillator into a usable tool. Without the location filter, you have a noise generator with a fancy name.

## Takeaway

The Stochastic %K / %D cross is the trigger — but the cross is only a signal when it fires below 20 or above 80, or as the second leg of a failure swing. Mid-range crosses are noise; the location filter is the edge.
