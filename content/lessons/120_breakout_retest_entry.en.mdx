# The breakout retest entry

This assumes you've completed 024 (breakouts) and 118 (role reversal). Here we go deeper on a specific high-quality breakout entry that lesson 024 mentioned and didn't expand: the retest entry. Instead of buying the breakout candle itself, you wait for price to pull back and tag the prior resistance level — now acting as support — and you enter on confirmation that the level held. The retest entry sacrifices some upside if price never comes back, but it dramatically improves risk/reward and weeds out failed breakouts. By the end of this lesson you should be able to plan a retest entry with entry, stop, and target, and know precisely when to abandon the retest plan entirely.

## Example

Look at JPM on the daily chart in early March 2026. JPM had ranged $258–$272 since mid-January. On 5 March JPM closed at $275.40 — a 1.2% close above $272 on 1.9x average volume. Clean breakout candle. A trader could have bought at $275.40 with a stop at $269 ($3 below the level for wick room), risking $6.40 to whatever target.

Instead, the retest trader waits. On 11 March JPM pulls back from $278 to a low of $272.40, closes the session at $274.20 — a daily close holding above the prior resistance with a wick that tagged the level. That's the retest confirmation candle.

The retest entry is the next-day open: 12 March open at $274.80. Stop placement is below the retest's low at $271.60 (a buffer below the $272.40 wick). Risk: $274.80 minus $271.60 = $3.20. Target the prior swing high projection at $290 — a $15.20 reward. Risk/reward: ~1:4.75.

Compare to the breakout-day entry: $275.40 entry, $269 stop, $290 target. Risk $6.40, reward $14.60, R/R ~1:2.3. Same trade, half the risk on the retest entry.

The retest plan invalidates if (a) price closes below the prior resistance of $272 — flip voided, see lesson 118 — or (b) price runs without ever retesting and you've simply missed the trade. Both are acceptable outcomes; the retest discipline is precisely about declining trades where the math is worse, even if it means watching some runners go without you.

## The trap

The trap is treating "I'll wait for the retest" as licence to buy any pullback as long as it tags the level — including pullbacks that close back through it. A retest entry has two preconditions: (1) the breakout itself was clean (close beyond on expanded volume, lesson 024's standard); (2) the pullback's lowest daily close stays above the prior level. Buying on the intraday touch without waiting for the close is how traders get long right before the breakout fails. The correct mental model: the retest entry is the second confirmation of the same thesis the breakout candle started. The first signal (breakout close) opens the question; the second signal (retest holds on a close) answers it. Two confirmations beat one. Don't trade halfway through the second.

## Takeaway

The retest entry is the second confirmation of the same thesis the breakout candle started — better risk/reward, but only if the prior level holds on a close; the moment that close prints below the level, there is no retest to enter.
