# Pig-butchering and affinity fraud

Two of the most destructive scam patterns in 2023–2026 operate by hijacking trust rather than by inventing a financial pretext from scratch. "Pig-butchering" — a crude direct translation of the Chinese term — is a long-arc romance or friendship scam that ends in a crypto-investment ask. Affinity fraud uses shared community membership (religious, ethnic, professional, alumni) to skip the verification step entirely. Both work because the relationship does the work the math should be doing. This lesson teaches the structural arcs of each and the one falsifiable rule that defeats both: the verification checklist applies *regardless* of who is asking.

## How this plays out in real markets

The FBI's IC3 reports on "investment fraud" and "confidence/romance fraud" — and parallel publications from the FCA, ASIC, and SC Malaysia — document the structures.

**Pig-butchering** typically begins on a dating app, social network, or wrong-number text. The scammer (usually operating from a trafficking compound in Southeast Asia, per US State Department reporting) cultivates a relationship over weeks. There is no investment pitch in the first month. The pitch arrives organically: "my uncle taught me to trade crypto, I make a few hundred a week, want me to show you?" The victim is directed to a polished but fraudulent platform — branded as a regulated exchange, often with a clone of a real platform's interface. Deposits work, small withdrawals work; large withdrawals trigger the withdrawal-block sequence from the previous lesson. Losses average in the tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars per victim per IC3 filings, and the total US losses since 2021 have run into the billions.

**Affinity fraud** uses community access in place of romantic relationship. The operator is — or pretends to be — a member of a specific religious congregation, ethnic diaspora, professional association, or alumni network. The pitch travels through trust networks: someone at the mosque, the church, the temple, the alumni WhatsApp group vouches for the opportunity. SEC and SC Malaysia case files document affinity fraud across nearly every community type. A representative pattern documented in regulator reports: an operator presents as a fellow community member, often invoking shared faith or ethnic identity, and pitches a "halal-compliant" or "community-only" investment with the same Ponzi math underneath. Early payouts to respected community elders are seeded deliberately to manufacture endorsements.

Signature traits:

- Long pre-pitch relationship cultivation (pig-butchering) or shared-identity endorsement (affinity).
- Crypto deposits or community-pool collections as the funding mechanism.
- Platform shown is unregistered or a clone; verification fails the standard contact-detail match.
- "You're family / community / a brother" framing used to discourage formal due diligence.
- Withdrawals graduate from working to blocked in the documented escalation pattern.

## The trap

The trap is the trust override. The victim runs the same risk assessment they would for any opportunity, then assigns the relationship or community membership as additional evidence in favour. The mistake is that trust evidence and verification evidence are different categories — and a manufactured relationship or a community endorsement is not protective. The Conservative Debator's view inside <Term id="ami" /> is direct: the verification checklist applies regardless of who is asking. A genuine friend, a genuine community member, would *welcome* the verification rather than treat it as an insult. Resistance to verification is itself the diagnostic. The fix is not to abandon trust — it is to refuse to let trust substitute for the regulator-register lookup, the contact-detail match, and the bank-account check.
