# Relative-strength momentum — top decile mechanics

The momentum foundation (056) introduced ranking the universe and holding the top decile. This lesson is the operating manual: how relative-strength is actually computed, why the lookback is conventionally 12-month-minus-1-month, how rebalance frequency interacts with turnover and tax, and how to size positions inside the basket. Relative-strength momentum is one of the most-replicated anomalies in finance — Jegadeesh and Titman documented it in 1993; it has survived in samples through 2024 in most equity markets. After this lesson you can specify a relative-strength rule that names the universe, the lookback, the rebalance cadence, and the rules for entries/exits that constitute the actual strategy rather than the academic abstraction.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at a 2024 implementation across S&P 100 names. The rule: rank every name in the universe by total return from 12 months ago to 1 month ago — the "12-1" lookback (excluding the most recent month avoids short-term reversal noise). At each monthly rebalance, hold the top 10 names equal-weighted. In January 2024, the top decile included NVDA, META, AMD, AVGO, LLY, CRM, ORCL, GE, UBER, and PANW. By April, the basket rotated to drop UBER and PANW and add MU and DELL. By July, NVDA, META, AVGO, LLY remained; new entrants included TSM (where applicable), GOOGL, and INTU. A trader running this rule mechanically owned roughly half the year's biggest winners and rotated systematically without trying to time tops. Annual gross return on the basket exceeded 50% versus ~25% for the S&P 500 — though with materially higher volatility and a ~12% pullback during the July-August rotation episode.

Entry criteria: at monthly rebalance, ticker is in the top decile by 12-1 trailing return AND has minimum dollar-volume liquidity to size within <Term id="ami_mandate" /> caps. Hold: until the next monthly rebalance. Exit/rotation: ticker drops out of the top decile at rebalance — sell, replace with the new entrant. Individual-trade invalidation (the <Term id="ami_pm_safety_floor" />): a hard 25% drawdown stop on any single position regardless of strategy state, to cap damage during a momentum crash. Sizing: equal-weight inside the basket; basket gross exposure capped at mandate-defined max (commonly 60-80% of capital, with the rest in cash or a defensive allocation). The strategy FAILS in two regimes: (1) momentum crashes — short violent reversals (Q1 2009, Q1 2016, November 2020 vaccine-rotation week) where high-momentum names crash and low-momentum names rally; (2) post-bubble unwinds where the most-momentum names ARE the bubble (March 2000 Nasdaq).

## The trap

The beginner mistake is using the wrong lookback — most commonly the latest 3 months or 1 month, which actually shows short-term MEAN REVERSION, not momentum. Buying last month's winners loses money on average; buying the last 12-but-not-the-last-month winners makes money on average. The correct mental model: the 12-1 (or 12-month total return) lookback is empirically validated; shorter lookbacks invert the effect. The other trap: cherry-picking which top-decile name to "skip". You look at the rebalance basket, decide one of them "looks toppy", and exclude it. Over time, the names that look toppy at rebalance dates are disproportionately the biggest forward winners — your discretion bleeds the strategy's return.

## Takeaway

Relative-strength momentum lives in the 12-1 lookback; shorter lookbacks invert the effect. Hold the basket the system gives you; cap single-name weight, not single-name inclusion.
