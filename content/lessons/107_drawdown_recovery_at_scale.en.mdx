# Drawdown recovery at different per-trade sizes

This lesson assumes you've completed <Lesson id="018_drawdown_management"/> (Drawdown management); here we go deeper on the specific link between per-trade risk and how many trades it takes to climb out of a drawdown. <Lesson id="018_drawdown_management"/> showed that a 30% drawdown requires a 42.9% gain. This lesson answers the follow-up: at 1% risk per trade and a 55% win rate with average 1.5R winners, how many trades is that? At 2%? At 5%? The numbers are sobering and they're why the 1% rule earns its place — the recovery path at higher per-trade sizes looks shorter on a single-trade basis but exposes the account to a fresh, deeper drawdown well before the first one is healed.

## Example

You're in a 30% drawdown on a $100,000 simulated account that now sits at $70,000. Target is back to $100,000, requiring 42.9% on the current base. Assume a 55% win rate with average 1.5R winners and average 1R losers — modest, realistic numbers for disciplined trading.

At 1% risk per trade: per-trade expectancy = (0.55 × 1.5R) − (0.45 × 1R) = 0.825R − 0.45R = +0.375R per trade. At 1% per trade, that's roughly +0.375% per trade. Trades needed to compound to +42.9% on the current base: about 96 trades. At one trade per trading day, that's roughly five months of disciplined execution.

At 2% risk per trade: expectancy per trade ≈ +0.75% per trade. Roughly 48 trades to recover. Two and a half months. Looks twice as fast. But: the worst expected drawdown at 2% per trade is also roughly twice as deep — a normal 8-loss streak that costs 8% at 1% sizing costs 16% at 2% sizing. You are climbing out faster only if no further drawdown hits during the recovery. Fresh 16% drawdowns happen.

At 5% risk per trade: expectancy ≈ +1.875% per trade. Roughly 19 trades to recover on paper. But a typical 8-loss streak at 5% per trade is a 40% drawdown — which, layered on the 30% you started in, takes you to about 58% from peak. You no longer have an account to recover with. On Bursa Malaysia the same math works in MYR; the percentages don't care about currency. Falsifiable test: simulate 10,000 sequences of 50 trades each at the same 55% win rate and 1.5R payoff — at 5% per-trade sizing, more than 35% of sequences hit a drawdown deeper than 50% from peak before completing.

## The trap

The trap is "I'm down, I need to size up to recover faster." It is the exact behaviour from <Lesson id="018_drawdown_management"/>'s trap section, and here is the quantitative version: doubling per-trade size cuts the recovery trade count roughly in half, but it more than doubles the depth of any new drawdown during the recovery — and recovery-on-top-of-recovery compounds the math in the wrong direction. The correct mental model: during a drawdown, halve per-trade size if anything. A 0.5% per-trade sizing during recovery makes the trade count roughly double, but the worst-case fresh drawdown during the recovery period also halves. You trade speed for survival, which is the right side of the trade-off when you're already in the hole.

## Try it

Open the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> and find your worst peak-to-trough drawdown to date. Compute the recovery percentage required on the current base. Then open a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Portfolio Manager and ask "At my current win rate and average R, how many trades until I recover? And what would the same recovery look like at half my current per-trade size?" The answer is the trade-off you're making consciously instead of by accident.

## Takeaway

Recovery is measured in trades, not weeks — halving per-trade size during a drawdown doubles the trade count but halves the depth of the next hole you can fall into.
