# Risk budgeting across simultaneous positions

This lesson assumes you've completed <Lesson id="014_position_sizing_basics"/> (Position sizing basics) and <Lesson id="017_portfolio_exposure_and_correlation"/> (Portfolio exposure and correlation); here we go deeper on the next layer — how to split a total open-risk budget across multiple positions at once. 1% per trade is the per-position rule; total open risk is the portfolio rule. A reasonable working ceiling is 5% total open risk across all positions, which translates differently depending on whether you hold 3 concentrated bets or 10 small ones, and depending on whether they cluster in one sector. This lesson teaches the layered caps — per-trade, per-sector, per-name — that the PM enforces structurally.

## Example

Your simulated account is $100,000. <Term id="ami_mandate" />: per-trade risk 1%, total open risk 5%, single-sector cap 2.5%, single-name cap 1.5% notional concentration. You hold five positions:

- NVDA — 1.0% risk, 17% of notional (semis)
- AMD — 1.0% risk, 12% of notional (semis)
- JPM — 1.0% risk, 14% of notional (banks)
- KO — 0.8% risk, 9% of notional (staples)
- XOM — 0.7% risk, 10% of notional (energy)

Total open risk: 4.5% — under the 5% ceiling, room for one more 0.5% position. Sector breakdown: semis 2.0% risk (under 2.5% cap), banks 1.0%, staples 0.8%, energy 0.7%. Single-name notional max: NVDA 17%, well under any reasonable 25% concentration cap. The book passes every layer.

Now consider a sixth position: TSM at 1.0% risk. Per-trade: passes (1% ≤ 1%). Total open risk: 5.5% — breaches the 5% ceiling. Sector: semis goes to 3.0% — breaches the 2.5% sector cap. The PM rejects with two reasons stacked. Two corrective options: reduce the TSM size to 0.5% risk (passes total but still breaches sector), or close one of NVDA/AMD before opening TSM (passes both). On Bursa Malaysia: same logic with a MAYBANK + CIMB + PUBLIC stack — three Malaysian banks at 1% each is a 3% banking-sector concentration, well past a 2.5% sector cap. Falsifiable test: any time the PM rejects a trade for a "soft" reason like sector cap, attempting to bypass it via <Term id="ami_coach_your_agent" /> fails by design — sector caps are part of the <Term id="ami_pm_safety_floor" /> for accounts with concentration limits in the mandate.

## The trap

The trap is treating each position as an independent 1% decision. The beginner adds positions one at a time, each passing the per-trade check, and discovers at month-end that they have 9% of total open risk across 8 positions, with 5% of it clustered in semis. Per-trade discipline alone does not produce a sensible portfolio. The correct mental model: every new ticket competes for a finite total-risk budget. Before opening a position, ask three questions — does it fit under per-trade (always), does the new total stay under the ceiling, and does the sector cluster stay under its sector cap? The PM runs these checks automatically; you can ask the Portfolio Manager in <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> to walk through them before you submit.

## Try it

Open the Mandate editor and set `total_open_risk_pct` to 5, `max_sector_exposure_pct` to 2.5, `max_single_name_notional_pct` to 20. Then open a 1-on-1 with the Portfolio Manager and ask "Walk through my current book against each cap." If any cap is breached or close, the PM will name it. That's the next trim, not the next entry.

## Takeaway

Per-trade risk is the floor; total open risk, sector caps, and single-name caps are the ceilings — every new ticket competes against all three before the per-trade check matters.
