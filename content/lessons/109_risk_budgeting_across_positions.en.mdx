# Risk budgeting across simultaneous positions

This lesson assumes you've completed <Lesson id="014_position_sizing_basics"/> (Position sizing basics) and <Lesson id="017_portfolio_exposure_and_correlation"/> (Portfolio exposure and correlation); here we go deeper on the next layer — how to split a total open-risk budget across multiple positions at once. 1% per trade is the per-position rule; total open risk is the portfolio rule. A reasonable working ceiling is 5% total open risk across all positions, which translates differently depending on whether you hold 3 concentrated bets or 10 small ones, and depending on whether they cluster in one sector. This lesson teaches the layered caps — per-trade, per-sector, per-name. Of these, AMI's safety floor mechanically enforces only the per-trade cap (derived from your `risk_score`) and the account drawdown ceiling; sector concentration, total open risk, and single-name notional concentration are ceilings you set and track yourself, with the Chief Investment Officer as your sounding board in <Term id="ami_one_on_one" />.

## Example

Your simulated account is $100,000. Your <Term id="ami_mandate" />'s `risk_score` caps per-trade risk at 1% — that one's enforced. You've also set yourself three tracking ceilings AMI doesn't check for you: total open risk 5%, single-sector cap 2.5%, single-name cap 1.5% notional concentration. You hold five positions:

- NVDA — 1.0% risk, 17% of notional (semis)
- AMD — 1.0% risk, 12% of notional (semis)
- JPM — 1.0% risk, 14% of notional (banks)
- KO — 0.8% risk, 9% of notional (staples)
- XOM — 0.7% risk, 10% of notional (energy)

Total open risk: 4.5% — under the 5% ceiling, room for one more 0.5% position. Sector breakdown: semis 2.0% risk (under 2.5% cap), banks 1.0%, staples 0.8%, energy 0.7%. Single-name notional max: NVDA 17%, well under any reasonable 25% concentration cap. The book passes every layer.

Now consider a sixth position: TSM at 1.0% risk. Per-trade: passes (1% ≤ 1%, and AMI will hold you to that one). Total open risk: 5.5% — breaches your 5% ceiling. Sector: semis goes to 3.0% — breaches your 2.5% sector ceiling. Working through your own numbers, that's two breaches stacked — AMI's safety floor will only have flagged the per-trade line, not the other two. Two corrective options: reduce the TSM size to 0.5% risk (passes total but still breaches sector), or close one of NVDA/AMD before opening TSM (passes both). On Bursa Malaysia: same logic with a MAYBANK + CIMB + PUBLIC stack — three Malaysian banks at 1% each is a 3% banking-sector concentration, well past a 2.5% sector ceiling. Falsifiable test: if you catch yourself excusing a sector or total-open-risk breach with "just this once," that costs you nothing mechanically — AMI won't block it — which is exactly why it costs you everything in discipline. The per-trade cap and drawdown ceiling, by contrast, genuinely are enforced by the <Term id="ami_pm_safety_floor" /> regardless of what you argue in <Term id="ami_coach_your_agent" />.

## The trap

The trap is treating each position as an independent 1% decision. The beginner adds positions one at a time, each passing the per-trade check, and discovers at month-end that they have 9% of total open risk across 8 positions, with 5% of it clustered in semis. Per-trade discipline alone does not produce a sensible portfolio. The correct mental model: every new ticket competes for a finite total-risk budget. Before opening a position, ask three questions — does it fit under per-trade (AMI checks this one), does the new total stay under your ceiling, and does the sector cluster stay under its cap (you check these two)? AMI runs the per-trade check automatically; ask the Chief Investment Officer in <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> to walk through the sector and total-open-risk checks before you submit, or work them out yourself in the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" />.

## Try it

Write down three ceilings for yourself — total open risk 5%, single-sector cap 2.5%, single-name notional concentration 20% (AMI doesn't have fields for these yet). Then open a 1-on-1 with the Chief Investment Officer and ask "Walk through my current book against each ceiling." The PM will reason through it with you in conversation, but it won't block a trade that breaches one — naming the breach is on you. That's the next trim, not the next entry.

## Takeaway

Per-trade risk is the one AMI enforces for you; total open risk, sector caps, and single-name caps are ceilings you enforce yourself — every new ticket should compete against all three before the automatic per-trade check ever gets involved.
