# Bottom-tick capitulation — selling at the absolute low

The bottom tick is the single worst price of a move — and it is reliably printed by traders who were holding through the entire decline and then, in the final hour, decided they could not hold anymore. Building on lesson 046, this lesson teaches you to recognise *your own* bottom-tick signature before it shows up on the chart. The cruel part is that bottom-tickers do not feel reckless when they sell. They feel finally sensible. That is the symptom.

## How this plays out in real markets

In October 2022, a trader held META from $185 down through $150, $130, $110, and finally $89. He had a written stop at $145 he had ignored at the time because "the level was arbitrary anyway." Each lower price added to the urge to "just be done." On the morning of November 3, with META trading $88.50 and the news feed full of "advertising business is structurally broken" think-pieces, he sold the position in a single market order. The fill: $88.91. META printed $88.09 that afternoon — the low of the entire cycle — and closed November 4 at $98. By February it was $174. By May 2023 it was $234.

The falsifiable bottom-tick fingerprint, as a compound test: (1) the exit price is the worst price of the entire holding period or within 2% of it; (2) the decision was made in the same session as a major bearish news headline or earnings reaction; (3) at the moment of exit, the trader cannot articulate an invalidation thesis distinct from "it is going lower"; (4) the position was held through at least one prior written stop level that was ignored. Three of those four boxes, in one exit, is a bottom-tick signature regardless of what the stock does afterward.

## The trap

The trap is that bottom-tick capitulation feels like *finally* getting risk management right. The trader thinks: "I was holding too long, I see that now, so I am being disciplined by exiting." It is the opposite. Real discipline was breached when the original stop at $145 was ignored. Everything below $145 was already a fear position. Selling at $89 is not discipline rediscovered; it is the same indiscipline reaching its terminal phase. The correct mental model: once you have ignored a written stop, you do not have a position anymore — you have a *prayer*. The only valid exits from a prayer are (a) writing a new stop with a documented invalidation level and waiting for it to trigger, or (b) sizing the position down deliberately while the chart is not in active panic. Selling into the panic itself is the bottom-tickers' tax — you pay it on the way out so that the next holders can collect the recovery.

## Try it

Open the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> and filter to your three worst closed losers by percentage. For each, plot the gap between your written stop and your actual exit price, and note whether you exited within the same session as a major bearish headline. If two of the three exits were below the written stop AND headline-coincident, you have a documented bottom-tick pattern. Take one example to a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Bear Researcher and ask: "What would a structural invalidation actually have looked like for this trade?" Compare that answer to the price at which you actually exited.

## Takeaway

Selling into panic feels like discipline returning. It is usually indiscipline finishing. Never make the exit decision at the moment of maximum fear — write the new invalidation when the tape is calm, or do nothing.
