# ATR trailing stops for trend trades

Trend-following foundations (052) used a moving-average exit; the trade-off is that MA exits ignore volatility — a 50-day MA stop on a quiet utility is 3% wide, on a high-beta growth name it can be 18% wide, and the position-size math collapses. An ATR-based trailing stop fixes that: you size your stop in multiples of average true range, so every trade has comparable risk regardless of the instrument's volatility. After this lesson you can build a trend rule whose stop adapts to each ticker's natural breathing room, and whose trail follows the highest reached "chandelier" point on the trade.

## Example

Look at NVDA from April through October 2024. On April 22, NVDA closed at $79.20 (post-split) just after a sharp pullback. The 22-day ATR was about $3.10. A 3-ATR chandelier exit trails from the trade's highest close: stop = highest_close - 3 * ATR. On entry near $79, initial stop is $79 - $9.30 = $69.70. By June 18, NVDA closed at $135.58 with ATR around $4.80 — chandelier stop ratchets up to $135.58 - $14.40 = $121.18. The stop only ever moves UP, never down. In late July NVDA pulled back from a ~$140 high to a low near $98 on August 5 — but the 3-ATR chandelier from the $140 high was around $125, which had already triggered on a daily close around $118.50 on July 30. Exit fired mechanically. The trader then re-entered on a fresh trend signal in mid-August and rode NVDA to $148 by November with the chandelier following each new high.

Entry criteria: any defined trend entry (MA cross, Donchian, breakout). Stop on entry: entry price - (N * ATR), where N is 2.5 to 3.5 — pick one and stick with it. Trailing: stop = highest_close_since_entry - (N * ATR), ratcheting up only, never down. Exit/invalidation: daily close below the trailing stop. The strategy FAILS in low-ATR creeping uptrends interrupted by single volatility-expansion days: a normally-quiet name has a one-day ATR spike that retroactively makes the prior trailing stop look tight, then the close-below fires on a wick that recovers next session. You took the loss; the trend continued without you.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is recomputing ATR every day and letting the trailing stop move DOWN when volatility contracts. ATR drops in quiet markets, so your stop appears to "loosen" — except the rule is one-way only. The correct mental model: chandelier stops are monotonically increasing during a long trade. ATR can shrink, price can chop, but the trailing-stop level only ever ratchets higher. The other trap is overreacting to a single volatile day — if today's ATR spikes 50%, that doesn't move your existing trailing level higher unless the new highest_close also rose. Stop math is a function of the peak, not the latest volatility reading.

## Takeaway

ATR makes risk comparable across instruments. The chandelier only ratchets up. The peak close, not the latest volatility, decides where your stop lives.
