# Breakouts

A breakout is what happens when price spends weeks coiled inside a range, then closes decisively beyond the boundary on volume. It is a hand-off: the side that was containing price has lost control, and the side that wins gets a run with no nearby supply or demand to fight. By the end of this lesson you should know what counts as a real breakout versus a fake one, the exact conditions under which a long-side breakout invalidates, and why volume matters more than the magnitude of the breakout candle itself.

## Example

Look at META on the daily chart from mid-February through early April 2026. Between 18 February and 26 March, META traded in a tight horizontal range — repeated daily highs clustered between $483 and $487, repeated lows clustered between $462 and $466. Roughly five weeks of sideways grind. Average daily volume across that consolidation was around 12 million shares.

On 27 March 2026, META opened at $486.20 and closed at $494.70 — a daily close $7.70 above the prior resistance ceiling of $487. Volume that session was 28.4 million shares, more than 2x the consolidation's average. The very next session opened at $495.10, held above $487 all day, and closed at $499.30. That's a confirmed breakout: a decisive close beyond the level, on expanded volume, followed by a hold session that didn't fall back inside.

By 3 April META was at $511 — roughly a 5% gain in five sessions, with no nearby overhead resistance because the range was the only structure on the chart.

The breakout invalidates the moment META closes a daily candle back inside the prior range — specifically, below $487. If that close had printed, say at $484, the breakout becomes a failed breakout (a "fakeout"), and the long-side thesis is done. That's the falsification rule. You don't argue with it.

## The trap

The trap is buying the breakout candle itself, on the day it prints, on emotion. A beginner sees a green daily candle clearing resistance by 1%, feels the FOMO, buys at the high of the day with a tight stop at the breakout level, and gets stopped out the next session when price retraces to test the level from above (which is normal breakout behaviour). The correct mental model: the breakout candle is the signal, not the entry. Wait for a hold — either a daily close that stays above the level the next session, or a retest where price returns to the broken level and bounces. Volume confirmation matters more than candle size: a small green close on 3x volume is stronger than a huge green close on average volume.

In <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Market Analyst, ask: "What are the upper and lower boundaries of the current range, what would count as a confirmed breakout, and what daily close would invalidate it?" Save the breakout level and the invalidation level to your <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> as a pre-committed plan.

## Takeaway

A breakout is a decisive close beyond a range on expanded volume; the candle is the signal, the hold or retest is the entry, and the day price closes back inside the range, the thesis is dead — exit, don't argue.
