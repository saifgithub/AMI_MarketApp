# What Is a Market?

A market is a place where buyers and sellers agree on a price. That's it. The "stock market" is the same as a fish market, just at a different scale and with electronic systems doing the matching.

When you tap "Buy 10 shares of NVDA" in your sim, here's what happens in a real-money equivalent:

1. Your broker sends the order to an **exchange** (the New York Stock Exchange, NASDAQ, etc.).
2. The exchange looks at the current order book — every other buyer and seller currently offering NVDA.
3. The exchange matches you with a seller at the agreed-upon price.
4. Cash flows from you to the seller. Shares flow from the seller to you.

This all happens in **milliseconds**. The exchange's only job is to make matching fair and fast.

## The two sides

Every trade has two sides:

- A **bid** — what buyers are willing to pay
- An **ask** (or "offer") — what sellers are willing to accept

When bid meets ask, a trade happens. The "stock price" you see quoted everywhere is just the last price at which a trade was completed.

If you want to buy NVDA right now at the market price, you're agreeing to pay whatever the current lowest ask is. That's a **market order** — fast, but no price guarantee.

If you want to set a price and wait, you place a **limit order**. You'd say "I'll buy NVDA at $150 but not a penny more." Your order sits there until someone is willing to sell at that price (or until you cancel).

## Why do prices move?

This is the question that occupies your entire <Term id="twelve_agent_team" />. Prices move because:

- **New information**: earnings, news, macro events
- **New sentiment**: collective mood shifts (greed, fear)
- **Forced flows**: index rebalancing, fund redemptions, tax-loss harvesting
- **Pure random noise**: lots of small unrelated trades adding up

Your team's job is to filter signal from noise. The Fundamentals Analyst reads earnings. The Market Analyst reads charts. The News Analyst reads headlines. The Social Media Analyst reads crowd mood. Each of them is sorting noise from signal in their domain.

## Two types of "market" you'll hear about

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **The market** | Usually shorthand for "the S&P 500" or a similar broad index — the average behavior of US large-cap stocks |
| **A market** | A specific exchange or trading venue (e.g., "the Tokyo market") |

When someone says "the market is up 1% today," they usually mean the S&P 500. When someone says "I trade the US market," they mean US stock exchanges generally.
