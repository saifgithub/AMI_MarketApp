# "AI trading bot" scams

The "AI trading bot" pitch updates the managed-FX Ponzi for the LLM era. The story: a proprietary AI runs trades 24/7, delivers smooth returns of 1–3% per *week*, and is too sophisticated for ordinary traders to understand. The structure underneath is identical to the FX-Ponzi structure from the last lesson — a withdrawal-blocking offshore platform — with one new layer of marketing gloss. This lesson teaches the falsifiable test that strips the costume off: an AI strategy that genuinely produces alpha is auditable in code, in trade history, and in cash flow. If you cannot audit any of the three, you are not buying AI; you are buying a story.

## How this plays out in real markets

Since 2023 the SEC has charged operators of "AI investment platforms" who collected hundreds of millions of dollars from US investors. The pattern, repeated nearly verbatim across cases: a platform that calls its strategy "AI-driven", "machine-learning", or "neural network" without publishing any specifics, audited backtests, or a track record on a recognised platform like a verified trader profile on a regulated execution venue. The SEC's enforcement releases — searchable on sec.gov/litigation — name several such cases per quarter. The FCA's 2024 warning on "AI investment scams" mirrors the language. SC Malaysia's Investor Alert List has added "AI bot" entries since 2024.

A representative scam architecture: a platform claims its AI achieves 1.5% per week (≈100% per year compounded). Members deposit USDT or fiat. A dashboard shows continuous green PnL. Members can withdraw small amounts in the first month — the bait — then withdrawals slow, then require "upgrade fees" or "tax certificates", then stop entirely. The "AI" was a number in a database the operator could set to any value.

Signature traits:

- "AI" or "neural network" used as the explanation for the edge, with no model architecture, no parameter count, no training-data description, no published backtest results.
- Returns presented per *week*, smooth, never negative.
- No on-chain or broker-statement proof of trades — only an internal dashboard.
- Affiliate / referral commissions, often tiered.
- Deposits in crypto (USDT, mostly) to avoid bank-level scrutiny.

## The trap

The trap is technical-sounding language doing the work of evidence. The reader hears "transformer model", "reinforcement learning", "GPU cluster", and the brain treats the jargon as proof. <Term id="ami" /> itself uses LLMs in its 12-agent stack — so the *category* of "AI in trading" is real. The mistake is letting the existence of a real category vouch for a specific operator. The correct mental model: a real algorithmic strategy producing real alpha leaves a real audit trail — trades hit a real exchange, fills appear on a real broker statement, the strategy code or its statistical fingerprint is reviewable by someone other than the operator. A platform that asks you to trust the dashboard without any external corroboration is asking you to trust a number it makes up.

## Takeaway

A real algorithmic strategy leaves a real audit trail — exchange fills, broker statements, on-chain settlement. If the only evidence of returns is an internal dashboard, the dashboard is the product.
