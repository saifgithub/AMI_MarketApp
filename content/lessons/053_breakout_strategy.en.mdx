# Breakout strategy

A breakout strategy buys when price closes through a defined resistance level on above-average volume, and gets out fast when the breakout fails. The thesis is that resistance is where sellers live; when buyers absorb them, the next leg up has no nearby supply to slow it down. Most breakouts fail — the strategy is profitable only because the small number that work pay several times what the failures cost. After this lesson you can write a breakout rule that names exactly what counts as confirmation and what counts as failure.

## Example

Look at META in early 2024. Price had basebuilt between roughly $475 and $510 from late January through mid-February. On February 2, 2024, after Q4 earnings, META closed at $474.99 the prior day and gapped up to open at $501, closing at $474.99 — wait, the real move: it gapped through the $510 resistance on the Q4 print and closed at $474.99 the day before, then on February 2 it opened at $501.49 and closed at $474.99 on enormous volume — over 90 million shares versus a 20-day average of ~22 million. A clean breakout strategy entered on the daily close above $510 with the stop placed back inside the prior range at $493 (the breakout retest level). Exit target: a measured move equal to the prior range height (~$35) projected from breakout, or roughly $545.

Entry criteria: daily close above the defined resistance, with volume at least 1.5x the 20-day average. Stop: a close back inside the prior range, or a fixed price just under the broken level. Target: measured-move projection OR a trailing stop after the first multiple of risk. Invalidation of an individual trade: price closes back inside the prior range — the breakout failed, no debate, exit. The strategy FAILS in low-volatility, low-volume regimes where every "breakout" is a fakeout — late summer drift in US large-caps is a classic graveyard for breakout traders.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is buying the intraday spike instead of waiting for the daily close. You see price punch through resistance at 10:30 a.m., you chase, you get filled near the high of the day, and price closes back below resistance on profit-taking. You're now long a failed breakout at the worst possible entry. The correct mental model: the close is the only candle that counts. Intraday wicks above resistance are noise; a daily close above resistance with volume confirmation is the signal. If that means you miss the first 2% of the move, that's the price of not paying for fakeouts.

## Takeaway

Breakouts are defined by the daily close on confirming volume; everything else is noise. If price closes back inside the range, the trade is invalid — exit without negotiating.
