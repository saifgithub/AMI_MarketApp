# Money Flow Index vs RSI — momentum with volume baked in

<Lesson id="027_rsi_momentum_oscillator"/> used RSI to read momentum from price alone. The Money Flow Index (MFI) is structurally similar — a 0–100 oscillator with conventional overbought/oversold thresholds at 80/20 (slightly wider than RSI's 70/30) — but the calculation includes volume. Where RSI averages up-closes vs down-closes, MFI averages up-day dollar volume vs down-day dollar volume. The result is a momentum reading that weights each bar by participation. When MFI and RSI agree, you have momentum confirmed by volume — a stronger read. When they disagree, the volume tape is telling you the price momentum isn't backed by participation. MFI is the volume-conscious sibling of RSI, and most divergences worth trading show up in MFI first.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at META through Q4 2025. From October through mid-November, META climbed from 580 to 612 with both daily RSI(14) and MFI(14) tracking the up-trend cleanly — RSI peaks at 68 and 72, MFI peaks at 75 and 79, no divergence between them. Strong trend with volume support.

Then on December 4, META printed a new closing high at 618 — but the readings split. RSI(14) reached 65 (lower than the prior 72, so RSI showed bearish divergence). MFI(14) only reached 58 — a much steeper drop from its prior 79, signalling that the new high was being made on materially lighter participation than the prior high. MFI's divergence was deeper than RSI's divergence by about double.

META fell to 569 over the next 14 sessions. The trade signal was the same as RSI divergence — but MFI's deeper drop quantified the volume shortfall that RSI couldn't see directly.

Falsifiable claim: when MFI divergence is meaningfully deeper than RSI divergence at the same swing point (the MFI lower high is at least 10 points lower than the prior MFI peak, while the RSI lower high is only 5–7 points lower), the underlying participation is fading faster than momentum, and the reversal probability is higher than the RSI signal alone would suggest. Invalidation: if MFI prints a new high before price breaks its swing low, the divergence cancels.

The reverse holds at lows — when MFI hits oversold and rebounds before RSI does, on lighter-than-average down-day volume, distribution is exhausting and reversal odds rise.

On Bursa Malaysia: TENAGA in Q3 2024 — RSI showed mild bearish divergence at a swing high (peaks 70 → 64), but MFI showed a much steeper drop (78 → 51). Price fell 8% over the next month — the kind of move the MFI divergence had quantified that RSI alone understated.

## The trap

The trap is using only RSI and missing the volume-weighted picture. A stock can produce a new high with rising RSI but collapsing MFI — meaning the price momentum looks healthy but the volume on up-days has dried up. Beginners look at the RSI chart, see momentum confirmed, and miss that participation is failing. Conversely, a trader using only MFI may discount RSI signals in low-volume stocks where MFI is noisy by definition.

The correct mental model: RSI and MFI measure related but distinct things — RSI is pure price momentum, MFI is volume-weighted momentum. Use them in tandem. Agreement between the two strengthens any divergence call. Disagreement is the signal — the one that's diverging more sharply is exposing the weaker leg of the trend. Don't pick a favourite; read both, and trust the deeper divergence as the higher-conviction read.

Then verify on the chart. Build a habit of reading both in parallel; the gap between them is often where the highest-conviction signal sits.

## Takeaway

MFI is RSI with volume baked in — when the two agree, momentum is confirmed by participation; when MFI diverges more sharply than RSI, volume is leaving the trend before momentum is, and that earlier warning is worth acting on.
