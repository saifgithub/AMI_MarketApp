# Moat erosion signals: how to see it two years early

The previous moat lessons taught you how to identify a moat. This one teaches you how to spot when one is breaking. Moats rarely collapse overnight; they erode in patterns you can read 12–24 months before the market re-rates the stock. The combination of margin compression, market-share loss, and rising customer-acquisition cost is the canonical pattern — any one of those signals alone is noise, but the three together are a structural warning. By the end of this lesson you should know the specific multi-metric early-warning pattern, and the false-alarm pattern that looks similar but isn't moat erosion.

## How this plays out in real markets

INTC (Intel) is the case study for moat erosion missed by bulls. From roughly 2017 onward, several signals fired together: gross margin slipped from the 62%+ band to the high 50s, then mid-50s, then low 40s by 2024. Market share in server CPUs eroded as AMD took share from ~5% in 2017 to ~25% by 2024. Capex requirements rose as INTC tried to catch up on manufacturing process, but the returns on that capex stayed weak. Customer-acquisition wasn't the relevant metric (this is B2B), but design-win pipeline visibly thinned across hyperscalers. Each signal alone was dismissible; together, by 2020, the moat-erosion pattern was readable. The stock didn't fully re-rate until 2022–2024 — the patient bear had 18–24 months of warning.

Compare to GE in the late 2010s — a different moat-erosion pattern, but the same three-signal logic. Industrial gas-turbine market share fell as competitors won design slots; gross margin compressed as service-revenue mix weakened; capex stayed high but ROIC fell. The market re-rated GE Industrial through 2018–2020.

Now look at the false alarm pattern. AAPL through 2016 showed a single bad year of revenue decline ("peak iPhone" was the narrative). But gross margin held in the 38–40% range, share in premium smartphones stayed dominant, and services revenue began compounding — the moat hadn't eroded, the product cycle had paused. A patient observer who waited for two of the three signals to confirm avoided the false sell.

On Bursa Malaysia: TOPGLOV (Top Glove) during the post-COVID normalisation showed all three signals — gross margin compression from pandemic-era 50%+ to single digits, market-share concession to competitors, and capex commitments that no longer produced ROIC. That was genuine moat erosion (or more precisely, the disappearance of a temporary moat).

The falsifiable claim: a moat is materially eroding when at least two of the three signals fire together across at least two consecutive years — gross-margin contraction of more than 3 percentage points sustained, market-share loss of more than 2 points sustained, and rising customer-acquisition cost or design-loss to competitors. One signal alone is noise. Two together for 1 year is a yellow flag. Two or three together for 2+ years is the structural warning.

## The trap

The trap is reacting to a single signal in isolation. A beginner sees one bad quarter of gross-margin compression and exits a long-term holding, missing the post-cycle recovery. The correct mental model: moats are durable, and durable things don't break on one data point. Wait for the multi-signal pattern across multiple quarters before treating the moat as broken. The opposite version of the trap is more dangerous: assuming a moat is permanent because it has been durable in the past. Every moat erodes eventually — the question is whether yours is eroding now. The disciplined posture is to set up the three-metric watch list when you initially buy a moat business, and check it quarterly. If you don't have the watch list, you don't have a thesis review process — you have a hope.

## Try it

For every position in your portfolio that you justified on moat grounds, set up the three-metric watch list in the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" />: (1) gross margin vs the original entry-year level, (2) market-share trend over the last 4 quarters, (3) CAC or sales-efficiency trend. Then ask the Bear Researcher in <Term id="ami_one_on_one" />: "For [ticker], are any of the three moat-erosion signals firing right now?" If two or more are firing across the last two years, your thesis needs a written re-review before you add to the position.

## Takeaway

Moats erode in patterns, not in single quarters — two of three signals fired together for two consecutive years is the structural warning, and the early-warning window is exactly when calm holders reduce while panicked ones still hold.
