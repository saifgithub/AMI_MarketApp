# Telegram / WhatsApp pump groups

A pump-and-dump group orchestrates buying pressure in a thinly traded stock so insiders can sell into the demand they manufactured. The group sells the dream of "the next big call" and posts only winning screenshots. The math is simple and brutal: the early organisers buy a low-volume small-cap, the group buys on cue, the price spikes, the organisers exit, the price collapses. Group members hold the bag. This lesson teaches you to recognise the structure before you take the first trade.

<Animation name="pump_dump_curve" />

## How this plays out in real markets

The SEC has charged dozens of pump-and-dump rings since 2020. The template is consistent: a Telegram channel with 15,000–50,000 members, a charismatic "founder" posting trade ideas, only profitable trades shown, paid promoters seeded in chat agreeing with every call. ASIC in Australia, the FCA in the UK, and SC Malaysia have all issued near-identical warnings.

The pattern on a price chart is recognisable. A small-cap stock — daily volume of under 500,000 shares — gets named in the group at, say, $0.40. Members rush in. Over 6–10 hours volume jumps 30–100x, the price runs to $0.90–$1.20. The next session the price gaps down to $0.30 on heavy selling. The group's pinned messages disappear. The "founder" pivots to the next ticker.

On Bursa Malaysia the structure has appeared around penny ACE-market stocks and low-volume warrants.

Signature traits of a pump group:

- Only winning trades posted; losses never shown.
- Target tickers are low-float, low-volume, low-price — easy to move.
- "VIP" or "inner circle" tier you pay to join for "earlier" calls.
- Synchronised buy time stated in chat ("entry at 09:35 sharp").
- Pinned testimonials from accounts created in the last 30 days.

## The trap

You read the chat for a week. Every call wins. The chart screenshots are real. You decide the next call is the one you'll take. The mistake is treating *visible* track record as *full* track record — you are only seeing the wins because the losses are deleted, censored, or run in a parallel "test" channel members never see. The same group runs four signals; the three losers are never mentioned, the one winner is screenshotted to oblivion. Survivorship bias is doing the work. The PM in <Term id="ami" /> would refuse this trade on three independent grounds — no thesis, no mandate fit, no edge — and would not budge.

In a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the <Term id="concierge_agent" />, paste the name of any "signals group" or "VIP trading channel" you've seen advertised. Ask: "What red flags should I check for? How would I verify this group is not a pump operation?" The Concierge will walk you through SEC and SC Malaysia enforcement-page lookups.

## Takeaway

If a group posts only wins and the target stocks are illiquid, you are not the customer — you are the exit liquidity.
