# The Mandate is your contract with AMI

The <Term id="ami_mandate" /> is not a settings screen. Inside the <Term id="ami" /> <Term id="training_simulator" />, it is the contract you write with your own simulated analyst team — the constraints the PM will enforce against every <Term id="ami_verdict" /> the Room produces. The fields — `max_position_pct`, `max_drawdown_pct`, `halal`, `no_fossil`, `long_only`, `region_allowlist` — are the limits *you* set. AMI does not negotiate the Mandate. AMI does not suggest "you should lower this to 3%" — that would be advice, and AMI is not a licensed advisor. You set it; the simulator lives inside it; you practise judging analyst output under your own stated rules. By the end of this lesson you will understand why authoring the Mandate is the single most consequential act in the app.

## How this plays out in real markets

Suppose you open the Mandate editor on day one and write:

```
risk_tolerance: moderate
max_position_pct: 5
max_drawdown_pct: 20
halal: true
no_fossil: false
long_only: true
region_allowlist: [us, bursa_malaysia]
```

Every Verdict for the next year is checked against those fields. <Term id="ami_convene_the_room" /> on PETRONAS on Bursa: the Trader proposes 4%. PM compliance check — `halal: true` requires the screen, PETRONAS passes the screen, size within limit, region allowed. PASS. Now convene on TOPGLOV: trader proposes a short. `long_only: true` triggers BLOCK before size is even checked.

The Mandate is also auditable. Every block the PM issues references the specific clause: *"Trade blocked — mandate field `long_only` does not permit short proposals."* You can scroll back through your decision history and see which clauses fired most often — that tells you whether your Mandate is shaping decisions the way you intended, or whether you wrote something on day one that no longer fits.

The contract direction matters. You write the Mandate; the PM enforces it. The trading team works *inside* it — the Trader's proposals already try to stay within the bounds, which is why the PM block is less common than you might expect. But when the trading team's enthusiasm overshoots, the PM is the line that doesn't move. Even **<Term id="ami_coach_your_agent" />** changes do not edit the Mandate. The Mandate is a data object you control directly in Settings; it is not in any prompt.

## The trap

The trap is treating the Mandate as a fire-and-forget setting from onboarding. Your life changes — income, obligations, time horizon, risk appetite — and the Mandate should change with it. Users who never re-open the editor find themselves either (a) constantly fighting the PM because their Mandate is tighter than how they actually want to trade now, or (b) drifting into trades the Mandate would have blocked if they had updated it after their last drawdown. The contract is between *you-now* and *AMI*. *You-six-months-ago* may have written something *you-now* would not. Editing the Mandate is not breaking the rules; it is renewing the contract.

## Try it

Open **Settings → Mandate**. Look at the values currently set. Ask yourself: *did I write these or did I accept the onboarding defaults six weeks ago?* Change one field that no longer fits — even by one number — and save. You just exercised the only authority the PM cannot override: your own.

## Takeaway

You write the Mandate; the PM enforces the Mandate inside the simulator. Renew the contract when your life changes — it is the only document in the app AMI cannot edit. AMI is teaching you to evaluate analyst output under your own rules, not handing you advice to follow.
