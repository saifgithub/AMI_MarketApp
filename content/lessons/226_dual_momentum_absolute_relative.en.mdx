# Dual momentum — absolute plus relative

The momentum foundation (056) and the top-decile lesson (225) used pure relative-strength ranking. Dual momentum (Antonacci, 2014) adds an absolute-momentum overlay: hold the top-decile relative-strength names ONLY when the market itself has positive trailing 12-month return; otherwise rotate to cash or a defensive instrument like short-duration bonds. The combination historically reduces drawdown dramatically — pure relative momentum kept loading bear-market losers during 2008 and 2020 because they were still "top decile" of a falling universe. The absolute filter steps you out of the regime entirely. After this lesson you can specify a dual-momentum rule that names both filters precisely and the regime in which even dual momentum still bleeds.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at the S&P 500 (SPY) as the absolute-momentum gate. Through 2024, SPY's trailing 12-month total return remained positive every month — the absolute gate stayed OPEN, and a dual-momentum portfolio held the relative-strength top decile of US large-caps all year. Contrast with 2008: SPY's trailing 12-month return went negative in January 2008 and stayed negative through most of 2009. A dual-momentum rule rotated entirely to T-bills in early 2008, sat out the -38% S&P 500 calendar year, and rotated back to equities in mid-2009 when the absolute gate reopened. The trade-off: in 2009 the rule was late re-entering — the absolute filter signal lagged the market low by several months, and the strategy missed the first leg of the recovery. That's the cost of regime-aware momentum: protection in bears, lag at turns.

Entry criteria: (1) absolute-momentum gate — SPY (or the chosen benchmark) trailing 12-month total return > 0%; (2) relative-momentum rank — ticker is in the top decile of the universe by trailing 12-1 return; (3) liquidity threshold met. Hold: until next monthly rebalance. Exit/rotation: if absolute gate is OPEN, rotate within top decile; if absolute gate is CLOSED, sell entire equity sleeve and hold cash or a defensive instrument until the gate reopens. Individual-trade invalidation: same 25% per-position drawdown floor as pure relative momentum. The strategy FAILS in three regimes: (1) V-shaped recoveries — the lagged absolute gate keeps you in cash while the market rips off the lows; (2) sideways markets — the gate oscillates between open and closed, generating false rotations and transaction costs; (3) crypto-style regime breaks where benchmarks don't represent the actual momentum universe.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is treating the absolute-momentum gate as a tactical timing signal — adjusting allocations based on weekly SPY moves, "anticipating" the gate flipping. The whole point of dual momentum is that the gate is a slow, mechanical filter that catches secular trend breaks, not noise. Trading around it kills its function. The correct mental model: the absolute gate fires on monthly closes only. It says nothing about the next week. If you're going to override the gate, you're back to discretion, and discretion has worse out-of-sample performance than the rule. The other trap: chasing the rule's mid-rebalance "what would the basket be if rebalanced today?" question. The rebalance date is the rebalance date; intramonth peeking just trains you to override.

## Takeaway

Dual momentum stacks an absolute trailing-return gate on top of relative-strength ranking. The gate catches regime breaks; overriding it converts the rule into discretion, and discretion loses.
