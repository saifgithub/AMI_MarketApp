# Drawdown management

A drawdown is the peak-to-trough decline in your account equity — and it is asymmetric: a 20% loss needs a 25% gain to recover; a 50% loss needs 100%; a 75% needs 300%. The deeper the hole, the more disproportionate the climb out. "I'll trade my way back" is the most expensive sentence in this industry.

<Animation name="drawdown_recovery" />

## Example

Take a $50,000 simulated account. You hit a rough patch: a concentrated bet on NVDA into earnings, a correlated chip-sector add-on that crashed with it, and a chase trade on TSLA when the first two went south. Account drops to $35,000 — a 30% drawdown.

To get back to $50,000 you need $15,000 of gains on a $35,000 base. That is 42.9%, not 30%. If you were grinding out 1% wins per month before the drawdown, you needed roughly 30 months to make 30%; now you need 36 months of identical performance to recover from one bad week.

Compare a mandated 15% drawdown ceiling. Same starting $50,000, hard stop on new trades at $42,500. Drawdown of 15% requires only 17.6% to recover — about 16 months at the same 1%-per-month rate. Every percentage point of drawdown you avoid is worth more than a percentage point of gain you might have made.

Falsifiable claim: if your drawdown stays under 20%, recovery is still roughly a two-year grind at typical disciplined returns; cross 35% and the recovery becomes a multi-year problem regardless of skill. On Bursa Malaysia, a RM100,000 account that drops to RM65,000 (35% drawdown) needs RM35,000 on a RM65,000 base — 53.8% to climb out.

## The trap

The trap is increasing size to recover faster. After a 30% drawdown, the account feels broken and slow — 1% wins barely move the equity bar. So the beginner doubles position size: "I need bigger wins to climb out."

This is the exact behaviour that converts a 30% drawdown into a 60% drawdown, because the same volatility that produced the first hole is now hitting a doubled exposure. Drawdowns are recovered by reducing size and tightening discipline, not by chasing scale. You shrink when you are bleeding, not when you are winning.

The PM enforces this for you — a mandated `max_drawdown_pct` halts new entries once equity crosses the threshold, regardless of how confident you feel.

## Takeaway

A 50% loss needs a 100% gain — the hole always digs faster than the climb fills. Cap your drawdown before the math becomes someone else's problem.
