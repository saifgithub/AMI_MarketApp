# Donchian channel entry and exit

The trend-following foundation lesson (052) used a 50/200 moving-average rule. Donchian channels are the older, simpler cousin: you enter when price closes above the highest high of the last N days, and you exit when it closes below the lowest low of the last M days. The original Turtle Traders used 20-in / 10-out and 55-in / 20-out variants. The rule has almost no parameters to fit, which is exactly why it survives out-of-sample tests where prettier strategies fall apart. After this lesson you can specify a Donchian rule with a precise entry, a precise channel exit, and a regime in which the channel chops you to pieces.

## Example

Look at COIN through 2024. A 55-day high / 20-day low Donchian rule entered long on March 1, 2024 when COIN closed at $206.40, taking out its 55-day high of ~$202. Stop was the rolling 20-day low — initially around $156. As the trend extended, the 20-day low rose: by April it was at $215, then $228 by May, then $235 mid-year. The stop trailed the channel mechanically. COIN ran to $343 in late March before chopping, and the 20-day-low exit fired on May 1 around $215 — banking roughly +4% on that segment, with a much larger paper gain that the rule gave back. The rule re-entered on a fresh 55-day high in November at $279 and rode COIN to over $330 by year-end before the next exit signal.

Entry criteria: today's daily close prints a new 55-day high. Exit criteria: today's daily close prints a new 20-day low. Initial stop on entry: the current 20-day low at the time of entry. Individual-trade invalidation: the 20-day-low exit fires — no negotiation, no "let me see one more day". The strategy FAILS in tight, low-volatility ranges where 55-day highs are only marginally above prior highs — you keep entering at the top of a coiled spring and getting stopped at the bottom. The KLCI through much of 2023 was that kind of graveyard.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is optimising the channel length until backtests look gorgeous. You try 55 / 20, then 40 / 10, then 30 / 15, find the best historical curve, and trade it live — straight into a drawdown the optimised parameters never saw. The correct mental model: Donchian works because the parameters are arbitrary-but-fixed. The rule's edge is mechanical execution across regimes, not finding the magic number. Pick a published variant (20/10 or 55/20), trade it for a year, and resist the urge to "tune" it after every losing trade. The other trap is treating Donchian exits like trailing stops you can override; the channel is the stop.

## Takeaway

Donchian's edge is in its boredom. The channel decides; you execute. Optimising the channel length is how traders unlearn the lesson — pick a published variant and let the regime do the rest.
