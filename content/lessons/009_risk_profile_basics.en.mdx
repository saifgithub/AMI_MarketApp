# Risk profile basics

Your risk profile is not a personality test — it is two numbers and a behaviour. The numbers are the worst drawdown you can sit through without selling, and the worst single-position loss you can take without breaking your plan. The behaviour is what you actually do when those numbers hit. Most beginners overstate their tolerance in calm markets and discover the true number in a sharp drawdown, when it is too late to use. Getting honest about this **before** you commit capital is what makes every other lesson in this course usable.

## Example

Consider a real episode: the S&P 500 (SPY) drawdown of late 2018. From the September 2018 high near $293 to the December 24th low near $234, SPY lost about 20% in roughly three months. A reader with a $50,000 portfolio fully invested in SPY watched the account fall to around $40,000 — a $10,000 paper loss — in 13 weeks.

Two profiles, same trade.

Profile X — "moderate" tolerance, written as max_drawdown = 25%. X watched the drawdown, did not sell, kept the position. By April 2019 SPY was back to $290. X was approximately flat over six months and on track for a strong 2019 (SPY ended 2019 up roughly 29%).

Profile Y — said "moderate" during onboarding but had never lived through a 20% drop. Y sold at $245 in mid-December, locking a roughly 16% loss on the $50,000 ($8,000 realised). When SPY recovered to $290 by April, Y was still sitting in cash, "waiting for confirmation," and missed the entire recovery. Y's stated tolerance was 25%; Y's revealed tolerance was closer to 15%.

The trade was identical. The risk profile decided the outcome.

## The trap

The trap is treating risk profile as aspiration ("I'm bold, I can handle anything") rather than evidence ("I sold last time at -12%, so my real number is around 12%"). Aspirational risk profiles fail in exactly the moment they are needed. The honest move is to start with a conservative max_drawdown setting — say 15% — and only widen it after you have lived through a real (or thoroughly simulated) drawdown of that size without breaking your plan. Your risk profile is set by your history, not your hopes.

## Try it

Open the <Term id="ami_mandate" /> editor and set **max_drawdown_pct** to a number you think you can genuinely sit through without selling. Then open the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> and write one line: "If my portfolio drops to [starting_value × (1 - max_drawdown_pct)], my plan is to ___." Fill in the blank with a specific action — "do nothing", "rebalance", "reduce position size by 25%". The pre-commitment is half the value of the mandate.

## Takeaway

Your risk tolerance is set by what you did last time the market dropped, not by what you wrote on a form — start low, widen with evidence, and pre-commit to your action before the drawdown hits.
