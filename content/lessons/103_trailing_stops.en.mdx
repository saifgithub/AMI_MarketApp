# Trailing stops

This lesson assumes you've completed lesson 015 (Stop-loss basics); here we go deeper on one specific stop variant — the trailing stop — which only ever moves in your favour. A fixed stop converts "wrong" into a structural exit; a trailing stop converts "right but the move is over" into a structural exit. It is the mechanism that lets a winner run while ensuring you don't give back the entire gain. The discipline is one-way: trailing stops ratchet up on long positions, never down. This lesson covers chandelier-style ATR trailing, swing-low trailing, and the most common mistake — trailing too tightly and getting shaken out of a trend that hasn't actually broken.

## Example

You're long AAPL from $185, stop at $179 (chart-based, below the prior swing). AAPL rallies to $205 over four weeks. Now the original stop is irrelevant — you have $20 of profit per share and a $179 stop means giving all of it back if the trade reverses. Apply a chandelier trail: stop sits at the highest close since entry, minus 3× ATR. 14-day ATR is $4, so the trail is anchored at the running high − $12. After the close at $205, the stop ratchets to $193. AAPL pulls back over the next week to $197 — trail untouched, you stay in. Then AAPL prints two consecutive closes at $192 — trail triggers, you exit at the next open. Realised gain: roughly $7 per share above the chandelier-set price (entry was $185, exit was around $192). The remaining $13 of unrealised peak gain is the price of letting the trade run rather than guessing the top.

On Bursa Malaysia: long MAYBANK from RM10.20. Runs to RM11.20 over three weeks. 14-day ATR RM0.10, 3× ATR trail = RM0.30 below highest close. After RM11.20 print, trail sits at RM10.90 — well above breakeven and locks in roughly RM0.70 per share if hit. Falsifiable test: a chandelier trail with 3× ATR rarely triggers on healthy trends and almost always triggers when a real top has formed — if it triggers more than once per trade in a single trend, your multiple is too tight.

## The trap

The trap is trailing tight to "lock in profit." A beginner moves the stop to breakeven the second the trade is green, then tightens it further every day. Two weeks later they get stopped out at a 2% gain on what turned into a 30% trend, because every normal pullback in a healthy uptrend is wider than their trail. The correct mental model: a trailing stop is an exit on trend failure, not a profit lock. The trend's normal noise has to fit inside the trail, or you are using the trail to exit noise instead of structural reversals. 3× ATR or a stop below the most-recent confirmed swing low are the two clean approaches; "I'll just trail half a percent" is a guarantee you'll exit early on every trade.

## Takeaway

A trailing stop is an exit on trend failure, not a profit lock — wider than the trend's normal noise, or it's a guarantee you exit early.
