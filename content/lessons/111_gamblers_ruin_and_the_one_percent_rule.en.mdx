# Gambler's ruin and why 1% is the answer

This lesson assumes you've completed <Lesson id="019_survival_mindset"/> (Survival mindset); here we go deeper on the formal math that justifies the 1% per-trade rule — the gambler's ruin problem. Given a positive-expectancy edge, the probability of going broke before the edge plays out depends mechanically on per-trade size and starting bankroll. At 5% per-trade sizing on a real edge, ruin probability is substantial. At 1%, it drops to near zero. This is not opinion; it is provable. This lesson teaches the simplified ruin formula, why a "small edge with small sizing" beats "small edge with big sizing" every time, and the falsifiable test for whether your account is in the ruin zone.

## How this plays out in real markets

Imagine an edge you have measured at a 55% win rate, 1R winners, 1R losers (so expected value per trade = +0.10R). Per-trade size = 1% of account, starting balance = $50,000. The simplified ruin probability formula for symmetric payoffs is r = ((1−p)/p)^N, where p is win probability and N is the number of losing trades it takes to wipe the account. At 1% per trade, N = 100 (you can survive 100 consecutive losses before zero). With p = 0.55: r = (0.45/0.55)^100 = (0.818)^100 ≈ 3 × 10⁻¹⁰. Essentially zero. A real edge with disciplined sizing produces a ruin probability indistinguishable from zero.

Now bump per-trade size to 5%. N drops to 20 (20 consecutive losses wipes the account). r = (0.818)^20 ≈ 0.018, or 1.8%. Eighteen accounts in a thousand go broke just from a normal losing streak even with a real edge. Push to 10% per-trade: N = 10, r = (0.818)^10 ≈ 0.13, or 13%. More than one in eight blow-ups. Push to 25%: N = 4, r = (0.818)^4 ≈ 0.45 — nearly half the time you wipe out before the edge resolves. The edge has not changed across these scenarios; only sizing has. On Bursa Malaysia, the math is currency-agnostic — a RM50,000 account on a 55%-edge strategy at 5% per-trade carries the same ~1.8% ruin probability. Falsifiable test: simulate 10,000 sequences of 1,000 trades each at the same edge — accounts at 1% sizing hit ruin in fewer than 0.001% of runs, accounts at 5% sizing hit it in 1–2%, and accounts at 10% sizing hit it in 10–15%.

## The trap

The trap is "I have an edge, so sizing up captures more of it." The math says no. The edge is the expected value per trade; the ruin probability is the chance you stop playing before the law of large numbers catches up. Sizing up multiplies the per-trade payoff but exponentially raises the ruin probability — because each step toward zero is a step closer to the absorbing barrier where no future edge can rescue you. The correct mental model: the 1% rule is not a starting-point compromise that you graduate from; it is the empirically validated sizing that lets a real edge collect across the sample size needed to realise it. Aggressive traders who graduate to 5% are not "advanced"; they have re-introduced ruin probability to a system that had eliminated it. The PM holds 1% as the default because the math holds it as the answer.

## Try it

Open the <Term id="ami_mandate" /> editor and check your `max_risk_per_trade_pct`. If it's above 2, change it to 1. Then open a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Portfolio Manager and ask "At my current win rate and payoff, what's my approximate ruin probability over the next 1,000 trades at the current sizing — and what would it be at 0.5%?" The two numbers are the survival argument the math has been making all along.

## Takeaway

A real edge plus 1% sizing makes ruin essentially impossible; the same edge at 5% sizing puts ruin back on the table — sizing controls survival, edge controls returns, and you need both alive to collect.
