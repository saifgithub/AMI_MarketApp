# Why risk matters more than profit

Protecting capital comes before making it — not as a slogan, as arithmetic. A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain to recover. A 20% drawdown requires a 25% gain. A 10% drawdown requires only 11.1%. The math is not symmetric: losses compound against you faster than gains compound for you. Every other lesson in Module 3 — sizing, stops, R/R, exposure, drawdown, survival — is a tool to bend this asymmetry in your favour. This lesson establishes the principle the rest of the module enforces.

## How this plays out in real markets

Two simulated traders, both starting 2025 with $100,000, both trading TSLA across the same year.

**Trader A — return-focused.** Sizes aggressively when conviction is high. Q1: rides TSLA from $240 to $310, full-account exposure, +29% portfolio, account at $129,000. Q2: TSLA pulls back to $180 on margin compression; same trader is still 90% allocated, takes a −42% portfolio hit, account at $74,820. Q3: TSLA recovers to $245, trader is now sizing smaller (scared), +18%, account at $88,287. Q4: a clean +22% run, account at $107,710. Year-end: **+7.7%**.

**Trader B — risk-focused.** <Term id="ami_mandate" /> caps single-name exposure at 15% of account, 1% max risk per trade. Same TSLA setups, same direction. Q1: position is smaller, captures +29% on 15% of the book — portfolio +4.4%, account at $104,400. Q2: TSLA's pullback hits a $1,000 (~1%) max-risk stop, account at $103,400. Q3: re-entry, another 15% position, +18% leg = +2.7%, account at $106,200. Q4: +22% leg on 15% = +3.3%, account at $109,700. Year-end: **+9.7%**.

Trader A had the better quarter. Trader B had the better year — and the better account to trade with on day one of 2026. The Q2 drawdown is the entire difference: Trader A needed +34% just to climb back to flat. Trader B needed +1%.

## The trap

The trap is the inner voice that says *"I'll just size bigger on my best ideas."* It feels rational — high conviction, asymmetric reward, why waste a setup on small size? But conviction is not a hedge. The trade you were certain about is statistically indistinguishable from the trade that takes a 50% hit on a guidance miss. Beginners chase the upside of being right and forget that the downside of being wrong is multiplicative, not additive. The correct frame: position size is not a function of how good you think the trade is. It is a function of how much account you are willing to lose if you are wrong. Conviction earns you the right to take the trade; the mandate decides how much.

## Try it

Open the Mandate editor under Settings. Set `max_drawdown_pct` to 50 and submit a trade ticket that would push the portfolio there. Watch the PM's <Term id="ami_pm_safety_floor" /> message reject the action with the math it used. Now set `max_drawdown_pct` to 20 — closer to where the recovery asymmetry is still survivable — and resubmit. Notice the floor changing shape around your number; this is the rule the rest of Module 3 sizes against.

## Takeaway

Returns compound; losses compound faster against you. Set the risk floor first — the upside takes care of itself within it.
