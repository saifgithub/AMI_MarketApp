# News That Moves Markets

Most headlines don't move stocks. A small set genuinely does. Knowing which is which separates a useful news analyst from a noise machine.

## The four headline categories that matter

### 1. Earnings + guidance

Quarterly results vs analyst expectations. The market doesn't react to *good* or *bad* numbers — it reacts to *surprises versus expectations*. A company beating estimates by 1% in a high-expectations environment can fall 8% on results day. A company missing estimates by 5% can rally if the guidance for next quarter is strong.

The News Analyst's job on earnings day is filtering the press release for:
- EPS vs consensus
- Revenue vs consensus
- Forward guidance
- Margin commentary
- Buyback / dividend changes
- Any one-time items hiding the real number

### 2. Central-bank decisions

Fed rate decisions, ECB statements, BOJ policy shifts. These move every equity index, not just one stock. A 25-basis-point surprise hike can take 1–2% off the S&P in an hour.

What matters isn't just the decision — it's the **dot plot** and the **press conference language**. "Restrictive for longer" is a different message from "data-dependent."

### 3. Major macro data

CPI (inflation), payrolls, retail sales, ISM manufacturing. Each release window has a known reaction radius. CPI matters most right now because inflation is the Fed's focus.

A 0.1% CPI surprise can move yields 5–10 bps, which then moves growth stocks. The Market Analyst will see it in the chart; the News Analyst will see it in the headline.

### 4. Company-specific catalysts

| Catalyst | Why it moves the stock |
|---|---|
| FDA approval / rejection (pharma) | Binary outcome on a multi-billion-dollar product |
| Court rulings (any sector) | Antitrust, patent, settlement — concrete cash effects |
| Major contract wins / losses | Often re-rates revenue forecasts |
| C-suite changes | New CEO = new strategy = re-evaluation |
| Activist investor stake disclosure | Forces a corporate response |
| M&A announcements | Premium on target, often hit to acquirer |

## What doesn't move markets (most of the time)

- Vague analyst rating changes from third-tier shops
- "Stock is up X% in the past Y days" headlines (those are *describing* the move, not *causing* it)
- General "AI is hot" / "EVs are dead" think-pieces
- Political news that doesn't change policy

The News Analyst filters all of this out by default. They flag the signal; they ignore the chatter.

## The signal vs the spin

A real News Analyst doesn't just summarize a press release. They tell you what the company is **not** saying:

> *"Earnings beat by 4%, but they pulled the FY2026 revenue range. They didn't replace it with a new one. That's the headline."*

Pulling guidance is rarely accidental. Reading the omissions is the craft.
