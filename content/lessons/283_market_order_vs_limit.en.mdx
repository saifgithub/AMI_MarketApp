# Market Orders vs Limit Orders

Two basic ways to ask an exchange to fill your trade. Each has a tradeoff.

## Market order

> *"Just get me filled. I don't care about the exact price."*

A market order says: take the best available price right now. You get filled almost instantly. You don't get to argue about the price.

**Pros:**
- Instant execution
- Useful when you absolutely need to be in or out of a position

**Cons:**
- No price control
- In fast-moving markets, you can get a *much worse* fill than you expected
- "Slippage" — the difference between your intended price and your actual fill — can be painful

**Use it when:** the trade thesis matters more than a few cents per share. Or when liquidity is high and slippage is negligible.

## Limit order

> *"I'll buy if you can get me $150 or better. Otherwise wait."*

A limit order sets a specific price ceiling (for buys) or floor (for sells). The exchange only fills you if it can do so at that price or better.

**Pros:**
- Price control
- Can pick off temporary dips by setting a buy below market price
- Disciplined — forces you to commit to a number

**Cons:**
- May not fill at all
- May only fill partially
- In fast-moving markets, the price moves past your limit and you miss the trade

**Use it when:** the price matters. Or when liquidity is thin and slippage from a market order would be costly. Or when you want to be patient.

## A worked example

NVDA is currently trading at $152. You think it's a buy at $150.

| Action | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Market order to buy 10 NVDA** | Fills almost instantly at $152 (or $152.05, depending on the ask). |
| **Limit order to buy 10 NVDA at $150** | Sits in the order book. If NVDA dips to $150 today, you get filled. If it goes to $155 instead, your order sits unfilled. |
| **Limit order to buy 10 NVDA at $155** | Fills immediately at $152 — the limit is a *worst-case ceiling*, and $152 is better than $155. |

That last case is a common surprise: a limit order at a price *worse* than current market is equivalent to a market order, because the exchange just gives you the better available price.

## Time in force

A limit order needs a duration. Common choices:

- **Day** — expires at market close today
- **GTC (Good Till Cancelled)** — stays open until you cancel it (most brokers expire after 60–90 days as a safety)
- **IOC (Immediate or Cancel)** — fills what it can right now, cancels the rest
- **FOK (Fill or Kill)** — fill the whole order right now or cancel it entirely

For most beginner trades, Day or GTC is enough.

## In your sim

When you submit a trade in AMI Trade's sim, you'll pick **market** or **limit**:

- **Market** fills at the current (15-min delayed) price immediately.
- **Limit** sits in the journal as "pending" until the price hits your level.

Either way, your PM still runs a compliance check before the trade is recorded. Even simulations enforce your mandate.
