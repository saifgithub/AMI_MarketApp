# Momentum crashes — anatomy and survival

The momentum foundation (056) named momentum crashes as the strategy's worst failure mode. This lesson dissects them. A momentum crash is a short, violent reversal in which the top-decile relative-strength names (longs) underperform the bottom-decile names (shorts or low-momentum) for a brief, intense window — typically 1 to 6 weeks. The largest crashes (March-April 2009, January 2016, November 9-10 2020) produced single-week long-short momentum losses of 15-30%, the largest equity-factor drawdowns on record. Even a long-only top-decile portfolio can lose 10-20% in days when sector rotation flips. After this lesson you can name the volatility and dispersion signatures that precede crashes, and the position-sizing rules that let a momentum trader survive them.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at November 9, 2020 — the day Pfizer announced positive Phase 3 vaccine efficacy results. Before that day, the top-decile US momentum names were dominantly stay-at-home and large-cap-tech stocks: ZM, PTON, DOCU, NFLX, AMZN, AAPL, MSFT, and several work-from-home names. The bottom decile was airlines, energy, banks, and cruises — XOM, JPM, BAC, AAL, UAL, CCL, RCL. On Monday November 9, the long-short momentum factor lost roughly 14% in a single day as the top-decile names sold off 4-7% while the bottom-decile names rallied 10-30% (CCL +39.3%, AAL +15.2%, XOM +12.8%). A pure top-decile long-only portfolio lost 5-7% in a day. The follow-through over the next two weeks pushed the cumulative momentum drawdown to over 20%. Survivors had three things: (1) a hard 25% per-name drawdown stop, (2) basket diversification across non-tech momentum names, (3) a regime-aware sizing rule that had reduced exposure as cross-sectional dispersion widened in the prior month.

Anatomy: a crash typically requires (1) extreme cross-sectional dispersion between momentum winners and losers in the months prior — the spread of cumulative returns between top and bottom decile reaches multi-year highs; (2) a fundamental catalyst that flips the regime narrative (vaccine, central bank pivot, geopolitical resolution); (3) high option-market gamma positioning in the momentum-leader names that forces violent unwinds when the catalyst hits. Pre-crash signals: rising dispersion, rising momentum-factor volatility, and rising correlation among the top-decile names (when the longs all move together, they all unwind together). Survival rules: (a) reduce basket gross exposure when 6-month rolling dispersion is in the top quintile of its 5-year range; (b) cap any single name at 8-10% of portfolio; (c) the 25% per-position drawdown stop is not optional. The strategies that DIDN'T survive momentum crashes were the ones where managers said "this time is different" and refused to size down.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is treating momentum crashes as black swans you can't see coming. The truth is more uncomfortable: dispersion and factor-volatility signatures often build for weeks before the catalyst hits, but momentum is making money in those weeks so traders ignore the warnings. The correct mental model: dispersion-wide regimes are MORE dangerous, not less, even when the strategy is currently profitable. The other trap: trying to "trade around" an anticipated crash by lightening longs and adding shorts of the bottom decile. That's almost always a worse strategy than just sizing down — you've doubled the bet that you can time the unwind, and in most pre-crash windows nothing happens and your hedge bleeds you. The third trap: blowing the system up DURING the crash by panic-selling everything mid-rotation. The 25% stop is precisely so you don't have to make that decision in real time.

## Takeaway

Momentum crashes have a signature — wide dispersion, rising factor volatility, crowded positioning. Survival is sizing down before the catalyst, not selling during it. The <Term id="ami_mandate" /> cap is the floor; everything else is system mechanics.
