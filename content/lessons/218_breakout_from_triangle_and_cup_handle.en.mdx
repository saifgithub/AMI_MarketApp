# Breakout from triangles and cup-and-handle

The breakout foundation (053) used horizontal range resistance; flags (217) used short tight consolidations. Triangles and cup-and-handle are the longer-duration patterns: weeks to months of price coiling into a defined geometric shape that produces an above-average win rate when broken with volume. The patterns matter not because the shapes are magic but because they describe a specific market microstructure — buyers absorbing supply at progressively lower or symmetrical levels — and that microstructure has a measurable resolution probability. After this lesson you can name the three triangle variants and the cup-and-handle, set their entries, stops, and measured-move targets, and recognise the failure modes that fake the pattern.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at META in late 2023. From August through early November 2023, META basebuilt in a rounded U-shape between $300 and $325 — the "cup". From November 7 to mid-November, price formed a tighter pullback near $310 to $325, retracing roughly 35% of the cup depth — the "handle". On November 20, 2023 META closed at $341.42 on volume of 21 million shares, well above its 20-day average of ~15 million, taking out the handle resistance near $330 and the prior cup-high cluster near $325. Cup depth from low ($300) to high ($325) = $25; measured move from breakout = $325 + $25 = $350. META hit $354 on December 13, 2023 — measured move satisfied within three weeks. Stop on the trade was set inside the handle at $318; risk per share $23, reward per share $25 to first target — a 1.1R trade that paid because of high win probability rather than gigantic R:R.

Entry criteria for cup-and-handle: rounded cup over 7+ weeks, handle that retracts no more than 50% of the cup depth, breakout above the cup high on volume 1.5x the 20-day average. For ascending triangle: flat horizontal resistance plus a rising lower-trendline of higher lows; entry on close above horizontal resistance, target = triangle height projected from breakout. For descending and symmetrical triangles: same shape mechanics, lower win rates — symmetrical triangles in particular break in EITHER direction roughly 50/50, so you cannot prefer one side in advance. Stop: a close back inside the pattern. Individual-trade invalidation: a daily close back below the breakout level. The strategies FAIL when patterns form near major resistance overhead — even a valid pattern that resolves into a wall of supply just retests the supply and rolls over.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is anticipating the breakout — entering on the third or fourth touch of the upper trendline before a close above it. You think you're "getting in early" at a better price; you're actually buying into a pattern that may resolve down and have set yourself a stop that no longer maps to the pattern's invalidation. The correct mental model: pattern breakouts are confirmed by the close ABOVE the boundary on volume. Pre-breakout entries trade a different setup with different statistics — usually worse ones. The other trap: ignoring the volume signature. A breakout from a 3-month triangle on average volume has roughly the same false-breakout rate as no pattern at all; the pattern's edge lives in the volume confirmation.

## Takeaway

Triangles and cup-and-handle have an edge only when the close clears the boundary on above-average volume. Anticipating the break trades a different setup with worse statistics. The pattern decides the entry, the stop, the target, and the invalidation — all four, before you click.
