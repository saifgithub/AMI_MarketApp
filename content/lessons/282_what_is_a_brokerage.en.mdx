# What Is a Brokerage?

A brokerage is the company that connects you to the exchange. You can't walk up to the New York Stock Exchange and place a trade — only registered members can. Brokerages are registered members. They take your order and route it to the exchange on your behalf.

Examples: Interactive Brokers, Charles Schwab, Fidelity, E*TRADE, Robinhood, Public.com, Al Rajhi Capital (Saudi), Maybank (Malaysia).

## What brokerages do for you

1. **Account custody.** They hold your shares for you so you don't have to deal with paper certificates.
2. **Order routing.** They send your orders to the exchange and bring back the confirmations.
3. **Settlement.** They handle the actual exchange of cash and shares (which legally takes T+1 or T+2 business days).
4. **Tax reporting.** They send you a 1099 (US) or local equivalent every year for tax season.
5. **Margin lending.** Some let you borrow against your portfolio to buy more — this is **leverage**, and it's dangerous if you don't know what you're doing.

## How do they make money?

Brokerages have several revenue streams:

- **Spread on bid/ask** (sometimes) — they shave a tiny bit
- **Payment for order flow (PFOF)** — they sell your order flow to market makers who pay them for the privilege of being on the other side
- **Interest on uninvested cash** in your account
- **Margin lending fees** — interest on money you've borrowed against your portfolio
- **Subscription fees** — Robinhood Gold, Interactive Brokers Pro
- **Foreign exchange fees** on currency conversion
- **Trading fees** (rarer now in the US, common elsewhere)

The "free trading" you see at Robinhood is funded by PFOF. The trade isn't actually free — there's a small efficiency loss vs an exchange-direct execution, paid for in fractions of a cent per share. For small retail trades, it doesn't matter. For institutions, it would.

## How AMI Trade is different

**We are not a brokerage.** You cannot send a trade through AMI Trade and have it executed in the real market. Every trade in AMI Trade is **simulation** — paper money, real prices, no actual orders.

This is by design. The educational value is in the *reasoning*, not the execution. If you want to execute real trades, you'll do that through a regulated brokerage in your country. AMI Trade is where you train; the brokerage is where you eventually act.

## Choosing a brokerage (for the future)

When the time comes, three things matter:

1. **Is it regulated** in your country? (SEC for US, FCA for UK, SAMA for Saudi, SC for Malaysia, etc.)
2. **What are its fees?** (Commission per trade, FX spread, inactivity fee, account minimum)
3. **What does it offer?** (Local market access, fractional shares, retirement accounts, etc.)

Don't pick a brokerage based on advertising. Pick based on regulator, fees, and feature fit.
