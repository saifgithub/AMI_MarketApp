# Combining strategies for diversification — when 1+1 = 1.4

The failure-modes foundation (058) showed every strategy has a regime where it bleeds. The defence isn't picking a better single strategy — it's running multiple strategies whose failure regimes don't overlap. When trend-following bleeds (chop), mean-reversion thrives. When mean-reversion blows up (trending bear), momentum's regime overlay has cut its exposure. A multi-strategy portfolio with low cross-strategy correlation can have meaningfully lower drawdown than any individual strategy while preserving most of the aggregate return. After this lesson you can pair strategies by their failure-regime overlap, compute the rough diversification benefit, and avoid the most common synthesis trap (running three strategies that all bleed in the same regime).

## How this plays out in real markets

Three strategies over 2010-2024 on US large-caps:

- **Trend-following (50/200 MA crossover)**: CAGR 9.2%, max drawdown 18%, worst year -7% (2015, whipsaw)
- **Mean-reversion (RSI(2) with 200-day filter)**: CAGR 11.4%, max drawdown 14%, worst year -9% (Q1 2020)
- **Cross-sectional momentum (top-decile 12-month, monthly rebalance)**: CAGR 10.8%, max drawdown 22%, worst year -12% (2020 momentum crash)

Equal-weighted portfolio of all three, monthly rebalanced: CAGR 10.4%, max drawdown 11%, worst year -4%. The aggregate CAGR is barely below the average of the three individuals, but max drawdown drops from 18-22% (any single strategy) to 11% (the combo), and the worst-year experience is meaningfully better. That's diversification working — the strategies have correlations of roughly +0.2 to +0.4 with each other, not +1.0.

Crucially, the failure-regime calendar shows why: trend-following lost in 2015 chop while mean-reversion made money (vol was contained). Mean-reversion lost in Q1 2020 while trend-following had already cut exposure (200-day filter). Momentum lost in November 2020 while mean-reversion was thriving in the rotation. NO regime in the 14-year window hit all three simultaneously.

Counter-example: combining 50/200 trend, breakout (Donchian-20), and momentum gives three strategies that ALL bleed in the same regime (chop / low-vol drift). The combined portfolio's drawdown profile barely improves over any one of them — the correlation across "trend-family" strategies is ~0.7, not 0.2. The portfolio LOOKS diversified but isn't.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is thinking "more strategies = more diversification". Diversification comes from low cross-strategy correlation, which comes from non-overlapping failure regimes — not from sheer count. Three trend-family strategies are basically one strategy with three names; three regime-orthogonal strategies (trend + mean-reversion + momentum) are actually diversified. The correct mental model: pair strategies by failure regime, not by mechanic. The second trap is over-allocating to the highest-historical-CAGR strategy — you're tilting the portfolio toward one strategy's failure mode and undoing the diversification you paid for. Start with equal-weight or volatility-equal-weight (covered in 241). The third trap: cutting the strategy that's currently losing money because "it's not working". Each strategy is supposed to lose money in its failure regime; cutting it converts the regime-loss into permanent capital reduction and removes the diversification benefit when the regime resolves.

## Takeaway

Diversification is about non-overlapping failure regimes, not strategy count. Three trend-family strategies is one strategy. Trend + mean-reversion + momentum is actually three.
