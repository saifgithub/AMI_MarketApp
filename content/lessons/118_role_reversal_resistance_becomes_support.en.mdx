# Role reversal: when resistance becomes support

This assumes you've completed 023 (support and resistance) and 024 (breakouts). Here we go deeper on what happens to a level *after* it breaks. A resistance level that breaks doesn't disappear — it usually flips role and becomes support on a subsequent pullback. The same mechanism works in reverse: broken support becomes resistance. By the end of this lesson you should be able to identify a role-reversal retest, understand the order-flow logic behind it, and write the exact close that confirms the flip versus the close that voids it.

## Example

Look at AMZN on the daily chart in February and March 2026. The $215 level had been resistance for AMZN since November 2025 — three separate failed daily closes attempted that ceiling. On 6 February 2026, AMZN closed at $218.40 — a decisive close above $215 on 1.8x average volume. Breakout confirmed.

Then on 24 February, AMZN pulled back to $215.80 intraday — a precise retest of the prior resistance — and closed at $217.20. Buyers stepped in at the level that used to reject them. That's the role reversal: $215 went from resistance (rejecting buyers) to support (catching buyers).

Three reasons this happens. (1) Traders who shorted near $215 the prior times are now sitting on losses; if price returns to their entry they're inclined to break even, generating buy pressure at the level. (2) Traders who missed the initial breakout view a return to the breakout level as a second-chance entry, generating buy pressure. (3) Traders long from below the breakout view a clean retest as proof their position is right and add or hold, removing sell pressure.

AMZN then ran from $217 to $238 over the next three weeks — a 9.7% follow-through after the successful retest. The role reversal invalidates the moment a subsequent daily candle closes back below the prior resistance level — in this case a close below $215 voids the support flip and re-establishes $215 as resistance again. That close didn't come.

## The trap

The trap is assuming role reversal always happens, and entering at the retest level too aggressively without confirmation. Sometimes the breakout was thin or fake (see lesson 119), and price slices back through the level with no hesitation — the level didn't flip, it just got crossed twice. The correct mental model: a role reversal is *confirmed by a hold, not by the touch*. Wait for either (a) a daily close back above the level after the intraday test, or (b) a clean bounce candle with elevated volume and a wick that didn't close below the level. Touching the level isn't the signal; closing back above it on a tested retest is. The level breaking the other direction immediately means the breakout was fake — not that the role reversal failed but that there was never a role to reverse.

## Takeaway

Broken resistance becomes support only when the close confirms — touching the level isn't the signal, a daily close back above it is; the moment a close prints below the prior level, the flip is voided and the breakout itself was a fake.
