# Greed

Greed is what happens when a winning trade stops being a plan and starts being a feeling. After this lesson you will be able to spot the moment your target moves on you, name why it moved, and put a deterministic rule between you and the next "let it run" impulse. The cost of greed is rarely paid on the way up — it is paid in the give-back, when a +40% open position closes at +6% and you remember you had a sell rule at +25%.

## Example

In late 2024, a trader on NVDA bought 200 shares around $115 with a written plan: 25% target, 8% stop, scale-out half at +15%. The position ran. At +18% the Social Media Analyst surfaced a wall of screenshots showing strangers calling for $200. The trader cancelled the scale-out. At +34% he raised the target to "open-ended". At +41% he added 50 more shares on a green day. The position then chopped sideways for three weeks, broke below the prior breakout level, and he closed the whole stack at +7% — paying tax on the round-trip and watching the original plan profit evaporate.

The falsifiable pattern: if your sell target was raised at least twice while the position was open, and each raise happened on a day the stock closed green, you were trading on greed, not on thesis. The thesis did not change between Tuesday and Friday — only the P&L did.

## The trap

The trap is the feeling that selling a winner is "leaving money on the table". It is not. Selling at your written target is *executing the plan you would have begged for two weeks earlier*. The correct mental model: your exit rule is a contract you signed with your past self when you were calm. Breaking it because the position is up is the same character flaw as breaking it because the position is down — both are the present version of you overriding the only version of you who was thinking clearly. If the thesis has genuinely changed, write a new plan with a new target *before* you act. If you cannot articulate the new thesis in one sentence, the new target is not a thesis. It is greed.

## Try it

Open the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" />, filter to your three best closed trades, and for each one note the gap between the *peak unrealised P&L* and the *exit P&L*. Then open a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Social Media Analyst and ask: "On the day each of these positions peaked, what was the crowd saying?" The pattern usually answers itself.

## Takeaway

Selling at your target is not leaving money on the table — it is keeping the agreement you made with the only version of you that was thinking clearly. Raise a target only when the thesis changes, never when the P&L does.
