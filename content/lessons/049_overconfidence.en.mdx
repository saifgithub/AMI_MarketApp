# Overconfidence

Overconfidence is what a winning streak does to position sizing when nobody is watching. After this lesson you will be able to spot size creep in your own history, distinguish a strategy that is genuinely working from a strategy that is benefiting from a forgiving regime, and put a rule in your mandate that ties size to a quantity other than your mood. Most traders do not blow up on their worst idea. They blow up on the trade right after their best one — the one they sized 3x because the last three "couldn't lose either."

## How this plays out in real markets

A trader was profitable on AAPL for eight straight months in 2023, running 1% per-trade risk with a 60% win rate and a 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio. By the ninth month, the trades were "obvious." He moved to 2% per trade, then 3%, then "I'm only sizing up on the highest-conviction names." A high-conviction long on NVDA in early 2024 was opened at 6% of account risk — six times the written mandate. The trade gapped against him on an earnings miss the next morning, locking in a single-position loss of -11% of the account. He had not had a worse month in his entire trading life — and the *strategy* had not changed. Only the sizing had. The eight months of "edge" became, on review, two months of edge and six months of a favourable regime.

The falsifiable pattern: you are overconfident if your average per-trade risk over the last 20 trades is at least 1.5x your average per-trade risk over the prior 20, and the size escalation correlates with a positive equity curve rather than a documented change in setup quality. Two numbers, both auditable. The chart is not the evidence; the position sizes are.

## The trap

The trap is the belief that confidence is information. It is not. Confidence is a memory of recent outcomes wearing the costume of a forecast. The correct mental model: position size should be a function of *setup quality and account risk*, not of how the last few trades closed. A "high-conviction" trade is not one that *feels* high conviction — it is one whose setup checks more boxes on a pre-written list than the average trade. If you cannot point to the extra checked box, you do not have a high-conviction trade. You have a high-confidence trader, which is a different animal — and a more expensive one. The Conservative Risk Officer's role here is uncomfortable on purpose: it asks you to argue against your own streak.

## Try it

Open the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> and pull your last 20 closed trades. Note the per-trade risk percentage for each. Compute the average for the most recent 10 versus the prior 10. If the ratio is above 1.5x, take that screen to a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Conservative Risk Officer and ask: "What in my written setup criteria has changed to justify this?" If you can't answer in one sentence, set your <Term id="ami_mandate" /> `risk_score` back to the original number before the next session opens.

## Takeaway

Position size should be a function of setup quality, not of recent outcomes. A streak is not new information — it is old information in a flattering light.
