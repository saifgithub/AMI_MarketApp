# Bear-market rallies: anatomy and how to fade them

<Lesson id="060_bear_markets"/> named the bear-market trap: buying dips with bull-regime muscle memory. This lesson teaches the structural anatomy of bear rallies so you can identify them, name what they share, and either sit out cleanly or fade them with a tight stop. After this lesson you will be able to recognise a bear-rally setup from three structural markers, name the levels where bear rallies typically fail, and state the exact condition that would invalidate the bear regime and end fading.

## How this plays out in real markets

The 2022 SPX bear printed five rallies of 6%+. Anatomy of the largest:

The summer 2022 bear rally: SPX closed at 3,666 on 16 June 2022 and rallied to 4,305 by 16 August 2022 — a 17.4% closing gain over 43 trading days. Structurally:

- It began from a deeply oversold reading (RSI on SPX daily under 30, only ~16% of SPX components above their 50-day SMA).
- It carried higher on falling volume — NYSE composite volume declined steadily from the 13 June peak through August.
- It topped at 4,305 on 16 August 2022 — a tick above the falling 200-day SMA (which sat at ~4,290 that day).
- VIX fell from 34.0 on 16 June 2022 to 19.5 on 16 August 2022 — a collapse, but not below the 15-line that would signal regime calm.
- It gave the entire 17.4% back by 30 September 2022 when SPX closed at 3,585.

The Nasdaq Composite ran an even cleaner version: 17 June 2022 low at 10,646 → 16 August 2022 high at 13,128 (+23.3%) → back to 10,213 by 30 September (-22.2% in six weeks).

Detection rule for a credible bear-rally fade entry: SPX has rallied 6%+ in 3–6 weeks, approaches the falling 200-day SMA (within 1%), volume is lower than the rally's first week, and VIX has fallen by 30%+ but is still above 18. Stop: tight, ~1.5% above the 200-day SMA on a daily close.

## The trap

The bear-rally trap is the FOMO inversion: a trader who held cash through the first leg watches the 17% rally, panics about missing the bottom, buys aggressively near the falling 200-day SMA, and rides it back to new lows. Every 2022 SPX rally produced this pattern at scale — retail equity inflows spiked in late July 2022, exactly at the rally's most-extended point.

The correct model: in a confirmed bear regime, the prior is "rallies fail at the falling 200-day SMA". The right responses are (a) sit out, or (b) take a small counter-trend short *only when the four anatomical markers cluster*, with a tight invalidation. The bear regime is invalidated — and the fade stops — when SPX closes above the 200-day SMA for 2 consecutive weekly closes AND the 200-day flattens or turns up.

## Takeaway

Bear rallies share an anatomy: 6%+ in 3–6 weeks, declining volume, VIX collapse, top near the falling 200-day SMA. Sit out or fade with a tight stop; invalidate only on a structural reclaim, not a magnitude reading.
