# Missing-the-bottom FOMO

Missing-the-bottom FOMO is the urge to chase a stock or index after a sharp recovery off a low — driven by the feeling that "I should have bought the panic, and now it's too late so I have to buy this rally before I miss the rest." After this lesson you will be able to recognise this FOMO subtype, distinguish a tradable post-capitulation setup from a chasing-the-bounce trap, and apply a delay rule that converts the urge into either a documented re-entry plan or a skip. The cruel logic: the trader who actually bought the bottom did not feel like a hero in the moment — they followed a written rule. The trader chasing the bounce is buying the *feeling* the bottom-buyer's reward triggered in them.

## How this plays out in real markets

S&P 500, March–April 2020. The bottom on March 23 was at roughly 2,237. By April 6 — nine sessions later — the index was at 2,663, a +19% recovery. A trader who had been in cash through the entire decline watched the recovery for those nine sessions and entered SPY-equivalent exposure on April 7 around 2,659 with the rationale "the bottom is in, I missed the panic, this is the second-best entry." Two weeks later the index pulled back to 2,747 (held above the trader's entry, by luck), but the *pattern* of his behaviour repeated in November when he entered after a +14% rally off a swing low and got stopped out three sessions later for -5% on a normal pullback.

A different trader, same March 2020 setup, had a written rule: "After a >20% drawdown in the index, do not enter on the first leg of the recovery; enter on a documented retest of the low that holds, or on a higher-low structure forming with confirmation." She did not enter on March 23 or April 7. She entered in late April on a higher-low retest at roughly 2,790, missing the first 25% of the recovery and capturing the next 40% over the subsequent six months. Her rule cost her the headline-grabbing first leg and preserved her stop discipline. The first trader had no rule and was, on every recovery thereafter, paying the same psychological tax.

## The trap

The trap is the belief that you must "catch up" to the recovery. You do not. The market does not pay you for participating in the leg you missed — it pays you for participating in legs you can size correctly and stop out cleanly. The correct mental model: a recovery off a low has three identifiable phases — the initial bounce (typically the first 10–25% of the move, almost always too fast and too headline-driven to enter without chasing), the *consolidation or retest* (where structure forms), and the *continuation* (where stops are placeable on real levels). Your edge lives in phase two and three, not phase one. Chasing phase one because you missed it is not bottom-fishing; it is the same FOMO that buys meme momentum, with a different chart shape. The rule: a recovery-FOMO trade must wait for either a documented retest that holds or a higher-low structure with a stop placeable on a chart level — never the panic-bottom's wick.

Was a higher-low structure visible? Was your stop on a chart-drawn level? Tag each entry as "structured" or "chasing." Compute the win rate of each tag. The pattern shows up reliably in the count.

## Takeaway

You do not get paid for participating in the leg you missed. Wait for entry geometry — a retest that holds or a higher-low with a placeable stop — or do not enter at all.
