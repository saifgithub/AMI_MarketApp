# Capstone: playing it straight

You now hold the full M22 toolkit: the trust discount, the two-prong MNPI test, the three manipulation shapes, and the duty-plus-provenance test for front-running. This capstone runs them all against a single, realistic week — the kind where the red flags arrive stacked, wearing the costume of opportunity. The skill being tested: seeing four separate integrity problems inside one "hot" trade, and knowing which makes a trade illegal versus merely foolish.

## One scenario, every M22 concept

On Bursa Malaysia: TOPGLOV sits on your watchlist. Over one week, four things happen. **One:** a Telegram group you were added to declares it "the next 300% glove rally" and coordinates buy times; volume triples with no filing or announcement anywhere. **Two:** your cousin, a manager at the company, mentions an unannounced supply contract that "will be huge when it drops." **Three:** watching the order book, you notice large bids stacking two ticks below the market, then vanishing whenever price dips toward them — repeatedly. **Four:** a group admin posts gain screenshots urging members to "load up before the announcement we all know is coming."

Run the toolkit. The volume spike with no catalyst plus coordinated hype is a **pump-and-dump** in progress — you would be the exit liquidity, and the tape being cited as proof is the group's own manufactured signal. The vanishing bids are **spoofing** — phantom demand propping the ramp. Your cousin's contract is **material non-public information** — trading on it fails both prongs, and being family doesn't dilute tippee liability. And the admin's "announcement we all know is coming" claims MNPI is circulating — meaning if it's true, the pump is built on insider information; if false, the narrative itself is the manufactured product. The clean actor's move: no trade on any of it, and note what the week told you about who else is in this stock.

## The steelman

The strongest case against playing it straight: "These rules bind professionals. Retail traders never get prosecuted — a small trader ignoring them risks nothing and sometimes catches a pump early." Two falsifiers. Legally: enforcement reaches retail scale — Martha Stewart's case was one avoided loss of $45,673. Economically: the pump's structure makes the follower the product — organisers accumulate *before* recruiting you, so your expected entry is above their exit. You don't need the law to lose; the arithmetic of manufactured demand is already against you.

## Try it

Convene the Room on TOPGLOV (or any small-cap from your watchlist). Read each agent's argument in the simulated verdict and mark every claim that depends on information you could not verify from a public source. Log the count in your Decision Journal — that number is the integrity risk of the thesis. Remember: AMI is a training simulator; the verdict is a practice artifact, not investment advice.

## Takeaway

When integrity red flags stack — manufactured volume, phantom depth, whispered announcements — the trade isn't early, you're the product. Playing it straight means the arithmetic and the law both stay on your side.
