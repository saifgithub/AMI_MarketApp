# Bollinger Band mean reversion in range markets

The mean-reversion foundation (055) used RSI(2). Bollinger Bands give you a different mean-reversion lens — bands plotted at the 20-day moving average ± 2 standard deviations of daily returns. In range-bound markets price reliably oscillates between the bands and the centre line, and a strategy that fades touches of the outer bands with a target at the mean has real edge. But the strategy collapses entirely when the regime shifts to trending — exactly when "the bands are stretched" becomes "the bands are riding the trend". After this lesson you can specify a Bollinger mean-reversion rule that includes the regime gate that decides whether to trade it at all.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at KLCC Property on Bursa Malaysia through 2024 — a relatively range-bound REIT that traded between RM7.20 and RM7.70 for most of the year. Bollinger Bands (20, 2) were tight around RM7.45 mid-band. A representative trade: July 3, 2024 — KLCC closed at RM7.21, tagging the lower band. ADX(14) was at 14 — well below the trending threshold of 25 — confirming range regime. Entry on the close at RM7.21 with a stop at RM7.10 (below the prior range low). Target: the 20-day MA mid-band at RM7.45. Within four sessions KLCC closed at RM7.46 — exit at the mid-band. Profit per share: RM0.25 on RM0.11 risk = 2.3R. Across 2024 the rule fired roughly 8 times on KLCC, with a hit rate around 70% and a positive expectancy of ~1.2R per trade. Now compare with what would have happened in 2023 on KLCC's parent index when MAYBANK was trending — Bollinger touches of the upper band were entries TO BE SHORT in a strict mean-reversion rule, and most of them got run over by the continuing trend.

Entry criteria for long: (1) price closes at or below the lower Bollinger Band, (2) ADX(14) < 20 (range regime filter), (3) price above the 200-day MA (broader-trend filter). Entry: close of the band-tag bar. Stop: prior range low or the most recent swing low, whichever is tighter. Target: the 20-day mid-band. Exit: close at or above the mid-band, OR 7 sessions elapsed (time stop). Individual-trade invalidation: a daily close below the stop. The strategy FAILS in trending regimes — ADX above 25 means the bands "ride" the trend and outer-band touches are continuation, not reversion. It also FAILS in expanding-volatility regimes where bands widen quickly and the mid-band itself is moving away from price.

## The trap

The beginner mistake is fading every band-tag without checking ADX or trend context. You see price at the lower band, you buy, and you're trying to mean-revert a stock in a strong downtrend that's been making new lows for weeks — the band is just expanding to accommodate the trend. The correct mental model: Bollinger mean reversion is a REGIME-CONDITIONAL strategy. The bands themselves don't tell you whether to fade them — ADX (or another regime indicator like the slope of the 50-day MA) does. The other trap: trading the strategy across instruments without checking whether each instrument is in a range. Bands work on KLCC in a range year; they fail on NVDA in a trend year. Same indicator, opposite outcome — the regime decides.

## Takeaway

Bollinger mean reversion only works in range regimes — ADX is the gate, not the bands. In trending markets band-touches are continuation, not reversion; the same indicator points the opposite direction.
