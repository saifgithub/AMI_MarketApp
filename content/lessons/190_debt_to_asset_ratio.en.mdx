# Debt-to-asset — the asset-side leverage view

<Lesson id="042_debt_to_equity"/> introduced debt-to-equity, where the denominator is shareholders' equity. Debt-to-asset uses a different denominator: total assets. Formula: D/A = total debt / total assets. It answers the question "what fraction of the company's assets are funded by debt?" — a more stable view than D/E because the asset base doesn't swing wildly with share buybacks, dividends, or one-time write-downs the way equity does. By the end of this lesson you should be able to compute it, explain why D/A is cleaner than D/E for comparing across companies that buy back stock heavily, and recognise the specific scenario where D/A and D/E tell opposite stories.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at AAPL's balance sheet in fiscal 2024: total assets ~$365B, total debt ~$100B, equity ~$65B. D/A = 100 / 365 ≈ 27%. D/E = 100 / 65 ≈ 1.5x. Now consider what's happening underneath. AAPL has spent over $700B cumulatively on buybacks since 2012, which shrinks the equity base without shrinking the asset base proportionally. The D/E has climbed mechanically over a decade because of buybacks, not because debt has grown out of control. D/A has stayed in a much narrower band — debt has grown modestly, assets have grown along with it.

Compare a company that hasn't bought back stock aggressively. JNJ in 2024: total assets ~$185B, total debt ~$33B, equity ~$70B. D/A = 33 / 185 ≈ 18%. D/E = 33 / 70 ≈ 0.47x. Both ratios tell the same low-leverage story because the equity base hasn't been engineered down.

The scenario where the two ratios diverge: McDonald's (MCD) for years carried negative shareholders' equity — buybacks plus accumulated dividend distributions exceeded the retained-earnings base. D/E in that period was technically negative (or undefined), making it useless. D/A remained computable and gave the honest leverage picture: roughly $35B of debt against $55B of assets, or about 64%. D/A worked when D/E failed.

On Bursa Malaysia: TENAGA has total assets near MYR 175B with total debt around MYR 65B, giving D/A ~37%. The utility's asset base is largely physical plant — D/A is anchored to the regulated asset base, which is also the base on which TENAGA earns its allowed return. Comparing TENAGA's D/A to a software company's would be apples-to-oranges; the physical-asset-heavy sector is structurally different.

Falsifiable claim: for non-financial companies, D/A above 60% is high leverage regardless of D/E, and D/A above 75% is the territory where any modest asset write-down (say, a 10% goodwill impairment) materially compresses the equity base and triggers covenants. The signal invalidates for regulated utilities and infrastructure where high D/A is the regulated normal.

## The trap

The trap is treating D/A and D/E as redundant — picking one and ignoring the other. They measure different things. D/E is the owner-vs-creditor split (governance and equity-multiplier effect on ROE). D/A is the asset-funding split (solvency under asset stress). A company can post a "low" D/E by buying back stock aggressively while its D/A creeps up. A company can post a "high" D/E because of a one-time dividend that doesn't change D/A at all. The correct mental model: D/A is the cleaner cross-company comparator, especially for mature companies that engineer their equity base. What invalidates the headline: companies with material intangible assets (goodwill, software capitalised) have D/A inflated by the asset side — strip out goodwill if a recent acquisition spiked the asset base artificially and recompute.

## Try it

In <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Fundamentals Analyst, pick a name that has bought back stock aggressively (AAPL, ORCL, or HD) and ask: "Give me D/E and D/A for each of the last ten years. Show the buyback dollar amount per year alongside." Save the table. Watching D/E rise while D/A stays flat is the visual signature of buyback-engineered leverage.

## Takeaway

D/A is the asset-side view of leverage and the buyback-neutral comparator. When D/E and D/A move in opposite directions, the equity base is being engineered — read D/A.
