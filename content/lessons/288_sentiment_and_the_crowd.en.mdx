# Sentiment and the Crowd

Price reflects what people *think*. Sentiment is the measurement of that thinking — what the crowd believes, with what intensity, with what conviction.

## Why sentiment matters

Markets oscillate between two emotional poles:

- **Greed.** Everyone is bullish. Cash on the sidelines is small. The next dollar in pushes prices up less. Risk of disappointment is high.
- **Fear.** Everyone is bearish. Cash on the sidelines is huge. Any good news has lots of dry powder to reward it.

These aren't permanent states. The crowd alternates between them. The historical pattern is that **extreme sentiment is contrarian**: peak greed often marks a top; peak fear often marks a bottom.

This doesn't mean sentiment *causes* turning points. It means by the time everyone agrees on the direction, most of the move is already done.

The Social Media Analyst doesn't just count mentions — they measure **intensity** and **direction**.

## Useful sentiment patterns

### 1. Mention-spike without news

A ticker suddenly trending on Reddit with no underlying news catalyst is usually a momentum chase. It can keep going for days, but the unwind is fast and brutal.

### 2. Sentiment vs price divergence

- **Bullish divergence:** price keeps falling, but sentiment is no longer getting worse. The bottom may be in.
- **Bearish divergence:** price keeps rising, but bullish sentiment is plateauing. Energy is leaving the move.

### 3. The "this time it's different" headline

Whenever a major financial outlet runs that exact phrase on a sector or asset, it's a high-probability top signal. Empirically, every cycle has its own version. The crowd's conviction is most fragile right when it expresses certainty.

### 4. The crash sentiment floor

When even bullish CNBC guests turn bearish — when permabull X-account influencers start posting "this is bad" — that's usually within weeks of a tradable bottom.

## What sentiment is *not*

- It's not a predictive model. Extreme sentiment can stay extreme for a while.
- It's not a substitute for fundamentals. A meme stock can sentiment-spike *and* be a bad business.
- It's not the same as price action. Sentiment leads, lags, or diverges from price — that relationship is the actual signal.

Used right, sentiment is a *secondary* input. Your Bear and Bull researchers may already weigh fundamentals heavily; the Social Media Analyst tells them whether the market is currently emotionally aligned with the case they're building.
