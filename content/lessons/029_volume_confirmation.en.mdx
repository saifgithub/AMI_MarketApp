# Volume confirmation

Volume is the number of shares (or contracts) traded in a bar. Price tells you what people did; volume tells you how many people did it. A breakout on light volume is a guess by a few participants; a breakout on heavy volume is a verdict from many. The standard reference is the 20-day average volume — when a bar's volume is 1.5× to 2× that baseline, the move has unusual participation behind it. When a breakout or breakdown happens on volume below the 20-day average, treat it as suspect until it proves itself with follow-through.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at AMZN on November 4, 2025. The stock had been consolidating between 198 and 207 for five weeks, with the 20-day average volume sitting around 38 million shares. On November 4, AMZN closed at 211.20 — a clean breakout above the 207 ceiling — on volume of 71 million shares, roughly 1.9× the average. Heavy participation, confirmed breakout. Over the next twelve sessions AMZN ran to 224.

Compare that to October 21, when AMZN had briefly poked above 207 to print an intraday high of 208.40 on volume of 29 million — well below the 20-day average. Price closed back at 205.10 the same day and slid to 199 within a week. Same level, two attempts; the difference was who showed up.

The falsifiable claim: a price breakout above a resistance level on volume above 1.5× the 20-day average has materially higher follow-through probability than a breakout on below-average volume. The invalidation is mechanical — if AMZN had closed back below 207 within two sessions of the November 4 breakout, the volume-confirmed signal would have failed and the setup invalidated, regardless of how heavy the breakout bar's volume was.

On Bursa Malaysia: TENAGA broke RM14.00 in mid-2025 on roughly 18 million shares against a 20-day average of 9 million — 2× participation. The breakout held; the move extended to RM14.85 over three weeks.

## The trap

Beginners look at the green candle and ignore the volume bar underneath. A 3% up-day on 40% of average volume looks identical on the price chart to a 3% up-day on 200% of average volume — but they mean very different things. The first is drift; the second is conviction. Treating them the same is how false breakouts cost you.

The correct mental model: volume is the participation count behind a price move. Use it as a confirmation filter, not a standalone signal. No setup that depends on a breakout, breakdown, or trend continuation should be taken without checking whether volume corroborates. If price did one thing and volume did another, trust the volume.

## Takeaway

Volume is the participation count behind a price move — use it to filter out drift-grade breakouts, and don't let a heavy volume bar override a failed close back through the level.
