# ADX directional crosses — DI+ and DI−

<Lesson id="143_adx_trend_strength"/> covered ADX as the strength gauge. ADX itself is computed from two underlying directional indicators: DI+ (positive directional indicator) and DI− (negative directional indicator). DI+ measures the share of recent price action that was directionally up; DI− measures the share that was directionally down. When DI+ is above DI−, more recent movement is up; when DI− is above DI+, more recent movement is down. The DI+ / DI− cross — when one crosses above the other — is the directional signal that ADX itself cannot give. Together: ADX answers "how strong?", DI+ vs DI− answers "which way?"

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at AMD through Q4 2025 / Q1 2026. On November 18, 2025, with AMD at 121, DI+ crossed above DI− for the first time in three months — DI+ rose from 18 to 24 while DI− dropped from 26 to 19. At the same time, ADX was 22 — just inside the developing-trend zone. Over the next eight weeks AMD ran to 145, with ADX climbing to 31 and DI+ extending to 33 while DI− compressed to 12. A clean uptrend signal: DI+ cross plus ADX rising into trend territory.

Then on January 27, 2026, the cross flipped: DI− crossed above DI+ at AMD 138 with ADX still at 29. AMD fell to 118 over the next five weeks. The DI− cross marked the regime flip from uptrend to downtrend; ADX confirmed the strength persisted on the way down.

Contrast a false cross: on October 6, 2025, AMD's DI+ briefly crossed above DI− at price 116 — but ADX at the time was 17 (below the 20 trend threshold). Within four sessions, DI− was back above DI+, and AMD chopped between 115 and 119 for two weeks. The cross without the ADX gating fired in a no-trend regime and produced no follow-through.

Falsifiable claim: DI+ / DI− crosses occurring with ADX above 20 produce materially better follow-through than crosses occurring with ADX below 20. Invalidation: a sample of 40+ crosses where below-20 crosses match above-20 crosses on forward returns.

The combined signal: DI+ above DI− with ADX above 20 and rising = strong long bias; DI− above DI+ with ADX above 20 and rising = strong short bias; either DI configuration with ADX below 20 = no signal, regardless of which DI is on top.

On Bursa Malaysia: KLCC in Q2 2025 — DI+ crossed above DI− on April 14 with ADX at 21; price ran from RM7.20 to RM7.80 over the next six weeks. The earlier January cross at the same exchange, with ADX at 16, produced no move.

## The trap

The trap is acting on every DI+ / DI− cross regardless of the ADX context. Beginners learn "DI+ above DI− = long signal" and treat it as a standalone rule. In low-ADX regimes, the DI lines cross repeatedly with no follow-through; trading every cross is a whipsaw machine.

The correct mental model: the DI cross gives direction; ADX gives the gate. Without ADX confirmation, a DI cross is the same as any other noisy crossover indicator in a flat market — it fires constantly and means nothing. The discipline is the same as the <Lesson id="139_stochastic_k_d_crosses"/> Stochastic-location filter: the cross is the trigger; the regime is the qualifier. Trade crosses only when ADX puts you in a trend regime.

## Try it

In the Technical Strategist <Term id="ami_one_on_one" />, paste a ticker that just produced a notable price move and ask: "What were the DI+, DI−, and ADX readings on the day of the move, and at the most recent prior DI cross — was that prior cross above or below the 20 ADX threshold?" Then verify on the chart. The point is to internalise the ADX-gating habit before any DI-cross-based action.

## Takeaway

DI+ vs DI− tells you direction; ADX above 20 tells you whether the direction matters. Cross plus regime gate is the signal; cross alone in a flat market is whipsaw.
