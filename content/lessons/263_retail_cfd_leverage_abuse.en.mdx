# Retail forex / CFD: the leverage-abuse pattern

Lesson 068 covered FX Ponzis where you never trade. This lesson covers a different but adjacent abuse — offshore CFD and forex platforms where you *do* trade, but the leverage offered is so extreme that the platform's house edge guarantees most clients lose almost all their capital quickly. Tier-1 regulators cap retail leverage for exactly this reason. The structural fact is published by the brokers themselves: offshore CFD platforms, when forced to publish loss disclosures, report that 70–90% of retail accounts lose money over a quarter. This lesson teaches the leverage-math underpinning that statistic and the regulatory caps that exist to neutralise it.

## How this plays out in real markets

Tier-1 retail leverage caps (as documented on each regulator's public site):

- **ESMA / FCA (UK and EU):** retail leverage on major FX pairs capped at 1:30, equity CFDs at 1:5, crypto CFDs prohibited for retail in many jurisdictions.
- **ASIC (Australia):** since 2021, retail leverage capped at 1:30 on majors, 1:20 on minors, 1:5 on equities, 1:2 on crypto.
- **MAS (Singapore):** similar tier-1 retail caps.
- **CFTC / NFA (US):** retail FX leverage capped at 1:50 on majors, 1:20 on minors.
- **SC Malaysia and BNM:** retail FX trading via unlicensed entities is not permitted; licensed derivatives products carry their own conservative caps.

An offshore platform — typically licensed in Saint Vincent, Vanuatu, or similar — commonly advertises 1:200, 1:500, or even 1:1000 leverage. The math: at 1:500 on a major FX pair, a 0.2% adverse move wipes out the entire account margin. Major FX pairs routinely move 0.5–1% in a session. A retail trader at 1:500 is statistically near-certain to be liquidated within days or weeks.

The platform earns spread, swaps, and (in many cases) acts as the counterparty to the client's trade — so the client's loss is the platform's revenue. ESMA's 2018 review of retail CFD outcomes, the FCA's 2019 review, and ASIC's 2020 product-intervention order all converged on the same statistic: 70–90% of retail CFD accounts lose money under the leverage previously offered. The caps were imposed because of that data, not despite it.

A representative pattern: a Telegram channel claiming 95% accuracy directs followers to open an account at an offshore CFD broker offering 1:500 on EUR/USD. The "system" trades aggressively, the account is liquidated within two weeks, and the broker collects spread and swap on every trade leading to the wipeout.

## The trap

The trap is treating extreme leverage as an opportunity rather than a structural disadvantage. The reader sees "1:500 — you can turn USD 1,000 into USD 500,000 of exposure" and reads it as upside potential. The mistake is that the same leverage that magnifies winners liquidates losers in fractions of a percent. The math of position decay under high leverage (Module 3) is unforgiving — the expected outcome under any realistic edge assumption is near-certain ruin, which is why every tier-1 regulator has capped retail leverage at a small fraction of what offshore platforms offer. The correct framing: a 1:500 offer is not generosity, it is a structural near-guarantee that the operator will eventually own your account balance.

## Takeaway

Tier-1 retail leverage caps exist because the data shows extreme leverage produces near-certain retail ruin. A broker offering 1:200 or higher on FX retail accounts is selling a structural disadvantage, not an opportunity.
