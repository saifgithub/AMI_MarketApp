# Volatility regimes

Volatility is a regime distinct from direction. A market can be a bull and calm, a bull and volatile, a bear and calm, or a bear and explosive. The volatility regime is what should set your position sizes and stop distances — not your conviction. After this lesson you will be able to detect a volatility regime using VIX and ATR thresholds, name the strategies that pair with each regime, name the strategies that quietly destroy capital when volatility shifts, and state the invalidation condition for a vol-regime call.

## How this plays out in real markets

Three real volatility regimes inside the same SPX bull from 2023–2024:

Low-vol bull (mid-2023): VIX closed under 15 for 47 of 65 trading days from 19 June to 22 September 2023. SPX 20-day ATR averaged 38 points (≈0.84% of price). A 2%-of-account risk trader could carry an 8–10% position with a 2% chart stop and still be inside mandate.

Vol-shock spike (5 August 2024): VIX closed at 38.57 on 5 August 2024 after the yen-carry unwind — a single-day move from a VIX of 23 the prior session. SPX 20-day ATR jumped from 50 to 88 points (1.0% to 1.6% of price) within five trading days. Same 2% risk-per-trade now forced position size down to under 5%.

Sustained-high-vol (October 2022): VIX held above 25 for 30 consecutive trading days during the bear-market low. SPX 20-day ATR averaged 75 points (~2% of price). Position sizes that worked in mid-2023 would have produced 2x the intended drawdown.

Detection rule: VIX < 15 for 10 of last 15 sessions → low-vol; VIX 15–25 → normal; VIX > 25 for 10 of last 15 sessions → high-vol; intraday spike where VIX rises >40% in a single session → vol-shock. ATR confirms: a low-vol read on VIX with ATR > 1.2% of price flags a divergence that often precedes a shock.

## The trap

The volatility-regime trap is keeping the same dollar position size across regimes because conviction hasn't changed. The number that has to change is share count, not opinion. When VIX moves from 13 to 28, the same 2% chart stop now represents a 4% loss because price moves twice as far between bars. The trader who didn't resize watches a "small" position deliver a "large" loss and concludes they were wrong about the stock — when they were actually wrong about the regime.

The correct model: risk-per-trade is fixed by mandate; position size is the variable that absorbs the volatility change. As ATR doubles, share count halves. Conviction stays the same; the math protects you.

## Takeaway

Risk-per-trade is fixed; position size flexes with the volatility regime. When ATR doubles, share count halves — conviction does not change, the math does.
