# The 'system' delusion — lucky vs skilled

After a winning streak, every trader believes they have found "a system." Most of the time, they have found a *regime that flattered their behaviour* and given it a name. Building on <Lesson id="049_overconfidence"/>, this lesson teaches you the sample-size math that separates a real edge from a lucky run, the falsifiable test for "is my system actually working", and the rule that prevents you from sizing up against a regime you have not yet seen end. The market gives every trader the same illusion eventually — usually after 15–25 winning trades, which is exactly the streak length at which your conviction is unfalsifiable from random.

## How this plays out in real markets

A trader on US tech-momentum names ran a strategy from January through August 2024: long breakouts on names above their 200-day MA with positive earnings revisions. Across 32 trades, win rate was 66%, average winner +9.1%, average loser -3.4%, expectancy +4.8% per trade. He concluded the system was robust. He wrote a research note titled "tech-momentum-breakout-v1, live edge confirmed" and raised per-trade risk from 1% to 2%. From September 2024 to January 2025 — the same strategy, on the same names, run unchanged — the win rate was 38%, average winner +5.2%, average loser -4.6%, expectancy -1.4% per trade. The strategy had not failed. The *regime* had changed; the 2023–early-2024 tape was a uniformly trending one that flattered any breakout strategy, and the late-2024 tape was choppy. His 32 trades were not enough sample to distinguish "this strategy works" from "this strategy works in this regime." The doubling of per-trade risk converted a regime-correction into an 11% account drawdown.

For statistical context: at a true 50% win rate with no edge, the probability of observing a streak of 20 wins in 32 trades (66% observed) by chance is non-trivial — roughly 6%. A 6% probability is not "I have an edge"; it is "one in 17 random traders would observe this without an edge." Most strategies need 100+ live trades across at least two distinct regimes before a single-trader sample distinguishes skill from a flattering tape.

## The trap

The trap is the certainty that comes from a winning streak. Certainty is not evidence — it is the absence of recently-falsifying data. The correct mental model: a "system" is a hypothesis until it has been falsified by adverse conditions and recovered. A strategy that has never traded through a regime change does not have a known edge; it has an *unmeasured one*. The Conservative Debator's role here is the most uncomfortable in the agent team — it asks you to lower your conviction on a strategy that is currently working. That feels wrong, which is exactly why it is necessary. The disciplined trader treats a winning streak as a regime-conditional sample, sizes for the worst regime the strategy has not yet seen, and raises position size only when sample size and regime diversity together justify it — never when only the equity curve does.

## Try it

Open the <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> and tag every trade in your current strategy's history with the market regime at the time (trending up, trending down, choppy/range-bound, volatility spike). Compute the strategy's expectancy *within each regime*. If a single regime accounts for more than 60% of the sample, your strategy's measured edge is regime-conditional, not absolute. Take the screen to a <Term id="ami_one_on_one" /> with the Conservative Debator and ask: "Under what regime would this strategy have negative expectancy?" The answer is the regime your sizing must be calibrated for.

## Takeaway

A strategy that has never traded through a regime change does not have a known edge — it has an unmeasured one. Size for the worst regime your sample has not yet seen, not for the streak it has.
