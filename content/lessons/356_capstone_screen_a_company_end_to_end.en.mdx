# Capstone: screen a company end to end

You now hold the full M25 toolkit: the four prohibitions and the risk-sharing principle beneath them, the two-stage screen (business activity, then three financial ratios), why standards disagree at the margin, purification of any remaining impermissible income, and how AMI's halal Mandate flag wires all of this into the PM's uncoachable floor. This capstone runs one real ticker through the entire pipeline in sequence — business screen, three ratios computed exactly, naming the standard applied, and a final decision — the same discipline the PM runs behind the halal flag every time you Convene the Room.

## One scenario, every M25 concept

Take a hypothetical balance sheet shaped like a large, permissible-sector semiconductor company — think of the scale **NVDA** operates at. **Stage 1, business activity (lesson 2):** semiconductor design and manufacturing is not in any excluded category (no alcohol, tobacco, gambling, conventional banking/insurance, weapons, adult content, or non-halal food) — it clears stage one and proceeds to the ratio screen. **Stage 2, the three ratios (lesson 3), computed via AMI's `sharia_screen`** with interest-bearing debt of **$8,500M**, cash plus interest-bearing securities of **$34,000M**, market cap of **$3,200,000M**, non-compliant income of **$500M**, and total revenue of **$130,000M**: **debt ratio 0.3%** (cap 33% — passes), **liquidity ratio 1.1%** (cap 33% — passes), **income ratio 0.4%** (cap 5% — passes). All three clear comfortably — a sharp contrast with lesson 3's own worked example (a heavily-levered real-economy business that failed at 107.8% debt ratio despite an equally permissible business line). **Naming the standard (lesson 4):** this pass-result holds under the AAOIFI-style 33%/33%/5% thresholds used here; a stricter or more lenient index methodology (Dow Jones Islamic, S&P Shariah, MSCI Islamic, FTSE Shariah, or Bursa SAC) could apply a different denominator or snapshot convention, so the honest answer is "compliant under this AAOIFI-style screen" — not "compliant, full stop." **Decision:** the halal Mandate flag (lesson 9) would PASS a trade on this ticker, and any dividend received would still be checked for a purification amount (lesson 5) against the disclosed 0.4% income ratio, even though the stock cleared the screen.

## The steelman

The strongest case against running this full six-lesson pipeline every time: "For a company this comfortably clean — 0.3%, 1.1%, 0.4% against 33%/33%/5% caps — the ratio screen was never going to be close, so tracing the entire pipeline in detail is more process than the moment actually required; a quick sector check plus a glance at the debt ratio would have reached the same PASS." That's a fair efficiency critique for an obviously clean case. The falsifier: this capstone's own worked ratios are far from the caps precisely because NVDA-scale market cap dwarfs its debt — but lesson 3's contrasting example (107.8% debt ratio on an otherwise permissible real-economy business) shows the same pipeline reaching the opposite verdict on a company that looked, at the business-activity stage, identical in kind. You cannot know in advance which case you're in without running the ratios — the discipline pays for itself precisely on the companies that are NOT obviously clean or obviously excluded, and a habit of skipping the ratio stage on "obviously fine" businesses is exactly the shortcut lesson 3 warned against.

## Takeaway

Screening a company end to end means both stages, the named standard, and the purification step that survives even a clean pass — held throughout as methodology, never a ruling.
