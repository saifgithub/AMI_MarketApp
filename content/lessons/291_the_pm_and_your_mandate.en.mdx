# The PM and Your Mandate

The Portfolio Manager is the final voice in every <Term id="ami_convene_the_room" />. They are not just the loudest opinion — they are the gatekeeper that enforces *your own rules* on yourself.

## What your mandate is (recap)

Your mandate is the configuration you set up during onboarding:

| Field | Examples |
|---|---|
| Primary goal | Retirement / long-term wealth / income / specific goal |
| Horizon | Short / medium / long / very long |
| Risk score | 1–5 |
| Max drawdown | 10% / 20% / 30% / 50% / 100% |
| Compliance flags | Halal, ESG-lite, no tobacco/alcohol/gambling, no fossil fuels, long-only, liquid-only |
| Blocklist / allowlist | Specific tickers you won't / will trade |
| Path | Active / long-horizon / both |
| Learning style | Quick / story / visual / hands-on |

Every agent gets a slice of this. The PM gets all of it, plus a **<Term id="ami_pm_safety_floor" />** that is uncoachable.

## What the PM does at the end of a Room

After the Trader proposes a trade and the Risk Debators argue sizing, the PM runs through three layers:

### Layer 1: AMI's judgement

The PM's prompt reads the full transcript — every analyst, the Bull, the Bear, the Research Manager, the Trader, the three Risk Debators — and forms an opinion. They write a verdict in plain language:

> *"The setup is clean. Bull's growth thesis is well-defended; Bear's compression case is real but priced in. Trader's 3% size is consistent with your risk_score=3 mandate."*

This is the PM as senior partner. Style varies — terse, verbose, hedged, decisive — and you can brief it.

### Layer 2: Mandate compliance check (deterministic)

Before the verdict is published, a **deterministic function** runs every check against the proposed trade:

- Ticker on your blocklist? → Reject.
- Ticker fails halal screen, and you're halal-flagged? → Reject.
- Position size > 50% of portfolio? → Reject.
- Drawdown projection > your `max_drawdown_pct`? → Reject.
- Short proposed while you're long-only? → Reject.

This isn't <Term id="ami" /> talking. It's a pure function. AMI can be briefed, jailbroken, or hallucinate — but the function can't.

### Layer 3: Final verdict assembly

If everything passes: the verdict ships with the Trader's parameters (size, entry, stop, target, horizon).

If anything failed: the verdict is `REJECT` with the specific violations listed. The user sees *why*. Critically, this is enforced even if every analyst, the Trader, and AMI's PM all agreed on the trade.

## Why the safety floor exists

Without the safety floor, <Term id="ami_coach_your_agent" /> would be dangerous — a user could coach their PM into "always approve" mode and bypass their own protection. The floor is there to make sure your coaching shapes *style* and *priority*, not *compliance*.

You can brief the PM to be more or less verbose, more or less cautious on borderline cases, more or less narrative in their reasoning. You cannot brief the PM to skip the compliance check. The path to looser limits is **editing your mandate** — which is honest, visible, and intentional.

## The PM's three-line verdict format

After enough Room runs you'll recognise the PM's house style:

```
[VERDICT] APPROVE — BUY NVDA 3% AT $152 STOP $148 TARGET $172
[RATIONALE] Growth thesis defended; compression risk priced in.
[MANDATE CHECK] PASS — halal ✓, drawdown 24% < 30%, single-name 3% < 50%
```

Or, when the floor catches a violation:

```
[VERDICT] REJECT — single-name cap violation
[RATIONALE] Trader proposed 60%; your mandate caps 50%.
[MANDATE CHECK] FAIL — concentration
[OVERRIDDEN_FROM_AMI] yes
```

That last line — the **overridden_from_ami** tag — is the safety floor at work. AMI said approve. The function said no. You're protected by both.
