# "Guaranteed return" scams

A real investment cannot guarantee a return. The market is a probability distribution, not a payroll. Anyone who promises a fixed monthly or weekly payout — 5% a month, 2% a week, "your capital is 100% protected and you earn 20% a year" — is either lying or running a Ponzi structure that pays old investors with new investors' money. This lesson trains one reflex: when you hear the word "guaranteed" attached to a return number, stop reading and walk away.

## How this plays out in real markets

The pattern repeats across decades. The US case the SEC and FBI break down in their investor-alert pages: a fund that promises 10–12% per year, "smoothed", never a losing month, with audited-looking statements. For years it works — because returns are not real, they are paid out of the next investor's deposit. When redemptions exceed deposits, the structure collapses in weeks.

The Malaysian variant is documented on SC Malaysia's Investor Alert List (sc.com.my). The pitch is usually: "RM10,000 in, RM500/month back, guaranteed by the company". The "company" is often a Labuan or offshore shell, sometimes wearing the logo of a real bank or licensed broker. SC Malaysia and BNM regularly issue joint warnings; the Investor Alert List grows monthly.

Verifiable signature traits across both:

- A specific, suspiciously smooth return number ("8% per month").
- "Capital guaranteed" language alongside an equity-style return.
- Payouts in the first few months that match the promise — recruiter testimonials follow.
- A WhatsApp or Telegram channel as the primary interface, not a regulated platform.
- An "agent" who earns commission for bringing you in.

Real licensed funds publish a prospectus, a fund factsheet with monthly NAVs that go up *and down*, and a registration number you can look up on the SC's public register.

## The trap

The trap is that the early payments are real. You receive RM500. Your cousin receives RM500. The scheme looks like proof. Greed plus social proof plus a small initial win is the same neurochemistry FOMO runs on (Module 8). The mistake is treating "I got paid once" as evidence the scheme is solvent. A Ponzi pays everyone until it can't pay anyone — the only test that matters is *where the return comes from*. If the operator cannot show you a real economic activity producing the yield (rental income on a real property with a title deed, dividends from a listed company with a ticker, interest from a regulated bank), the cash is coming from the next investor. Your "return" is someone else's deposit.

## Takeaway

If a return is guaranteed, it is not an investment — it is either a bank deposit (low, regulated, capital insured up to the local limit) or a scam. There is nothing in between.
