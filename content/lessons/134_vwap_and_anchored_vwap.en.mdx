# VWAP and anchored VWAP — volume-weighted reference lines

<Lesson id="029_volume_confirmation"/> used average volume as a confirmation filter. Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) takes that idea further — it computes the average price weighted by volume traded at each price, giving you the level where the average participant actually transacted. Standard VWAP resets at the start of every session, so it's a same-day reference line institutions use as their fill benchmark. Anchored VWAP (AVWAP) is the same calculation but starts from a date you pick — a major earnings release, a regime change, a 52-week high. That anchor turns VWAP into a multi-day or multi-month reference of "average participant cost since this event." Price relative to an anchored VWAP is one of the cleanest reads of whether participants who entered after the anchor are in profit or loss.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at TSLA after its November 6, 2025 earnings release. TSLA gapped from 268 to 285 on the print, then traded actively for the next eight weeks. Anchoring VWAP to the November 6 open at 285 produced a slowly-rising AVWAP that reached 291 by December 12 — the volume-weighted average cost of every buyer since the earnings event.

On December 18, TSLA closed at 290 — right at the AVWAP. Bounce: TSLA ran to 308 over the next three weeks. The post-earnings buyers were defending their average cost, exactly as the level predicted.

Then on January 22, 2026, TSLA closed at 286 — below the AVWAP for the first time. The post-earnings cohort was now underwater on average. Over the next 12 sessions, TSLA fell to 269 as that cohort capitulated. AVWAP from earnings became resistance instead of support.

Falsifiable claim: anchored VWAP from a major event (earnings, gap, swing high or low) acts as support when price is above it during a confirmed trend and as resistance once price closes below it for 3+ sessions. Invalidation is mechanical — if price re-closes above the AVWAP for 3 consecutive sessions, the role reversal cancels and the AVWAP is acting as support again.

Standard intraday VWAP works the same way at the session-day scale: institutions buying at-or-below the day's VWAP are getting a better-than-average fill, which is why price often respects it during the trading day.

On Bursa Malaysia: MAYBANK after its Q3 2025 earnings release at RM10.60 — AVWAP rose to RM10.85 over six weeks. Price touched and bounced at the AVWAP three separate times, then broke below in mid-December as the cohort flipped underwater. The line went from support to resistance within a week.

## The trap

The trap is anchoring VWAP at random dates and treating the resulting line as predictive. AVWAP only carries meaning when the anchor is itself a significant event — an earnings release where a defined cohort entered, a major gap where one cohort got trapped, the 52-week high where late-cycle longs piled in. An AVWAP anchored on a meaningless Wednesday is just a slow-moving average with extra steps.

The correct mental model: AVWAP is a behavioural reference line — it tells you whether a specific cohort of participants is currently in profit or loss. That information predicts how they're likely to behave. Above their average cost, they defend the line on dips (support). Below their average cost, they sell rallies to break-even (resistance). Pick the anchor that defines the cohort you care about — then the line tells you what that cohort will do.

## Takeaway

VWAP and anchored VWAP are behavioural reference lines — they tell you whether a specific cohort of participants is currently winning or losing, and that read predicts whether the line acts as support or resistance.
