# Engulfing patterns: bullish and bearish

This assumes you've completed 020 (anatomy of a bar). Here we go deeper on a two-bar reversal pattern: the engulfing. A bullish engulfing is a small red candle followed by a green candle whose body fully covers the prior red body — the green opens at or below the prior close and closes at or above the prior open. The bearish version is the mirror. By the end of this lesson you should be able to spot a strict engulfing, distinguish it from a near-miss, and know exactly what would invalidate the reversal thesis.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at JPM on the daily chart on 13 and 14 February 2026. JPM had been sliding since 28 January from $278 down to a 14 February low of $254.80 — about 8% in two weeks. On Friday 13 February, JPM printed a red candle: open $258.10, close $255.40, a $2.70 body in a down session. On Monday 14 February (a typical post-weekend session), JPM opened at $254.90 (gapping down below the prior close) and closed at $260.20 — a green body spanning $254.90 to $260.20, a $5.30 body that fully engulfed the prior $2.70 red body, both opening below the prior close and closing above the prior open. Strict bullish engulfing.

Context made it count: the engulfing printed at a level (the $255 zone had been support in November), the prior trend was clearly down for two weeks, and volume on the engulfing day was 1.7x average. The next session held the gains and closed at $262.40. Over the following three weeks JPM ran to $279.

A trader entering near the close of the engulfing day at $260 with a stop below the engulfing low at $253.80 risked $6.20 to a target near $278, a roughly 1:2.9 risk/reward. The pattern invalidates the moment a subsequent daily candle closes below the engulfing low — in this case a close below $253.80 voids the read.

## The trap

The trap is calling near-engulfings "close enough". A green body that covers 80% of the prior red body is not a bullish engulfing — it's a strong reversal candle, but the strict definition matters because the loosened version stops being statistically distinguishable from noise. The correct mental model: the engulfing rule is a binary filter. Either the green opens at-or-below the prior close AND closes at-or-above the prior open, or it doesn't. If it doesn't, you can still read the strength of the day, but don't sell yourself a "bullish engulfing" story when the pattern isn't actually there. Second trap: trading an engulfing in the middle of a sideways chop. Like every candlestick pattern, engulfings need a prior trend to reverse. No trend, no signal.

## Try it

Find a daily chart for any ticker that recently bounced off support. Scroll to the bounce day and check whether the prior session's candle and the bounce candle form a strict bullish engulfing. If yes, ask the Market Analyst in <Term id="ami_one_on_one" />: "Confirm the open/close anchors and tell me the invalidation price." Save that invalidation level to your <Term id="ami_decision_journal" /> next to a note: "Stop on a close, not a wick."

## Takeaway

Strict engulfings are binary — open and close anchors must both hold — and even a clean one is only a signal when trend and level provide context; the invalidation is always a close beyond the engulfing's extreme.
