# ADX — measuring trend strength

The Average Directional Index (ADX) is the strength gauge for a trend. Designed by J. Welles Wilder, ADX runs from 0 to 100 and answers one question: how directional is the price action, regardless of which way it's heading? An ADX below 20 means weak or no trend — price is chopping. ADX between 20 and 25 is a developing trend; 25 to 50 is a strong trend; above 50 is rare and signals a near-vertical move. Crucially, ADX is direction-blind — a strong uptrend and a strong downtrend both read high ADX. Pair ADX with any directional indicator (MA slope, MACD, price structure) to get strength plus direction.

## How this plays out in real markets

Look at MSFT through October 2025 to January 2026. Through most of October, MSFT chopped between 425 and 445 with daily ADX(14) reading 14–18 — clearly below the 20 trend threshold. A trader using a trend-following MA-cross system during that window would have eaten three whipsaws.

On November 5, MSFT broke above 445 on volume. By November 12, ADX had risen to 24 — crossing into developing-trend territory. By November 24, ADX was at 33, with price up to 462. Through December and January, MSFT trended cleanly to 488 while ADX held in the 28–38 range. The trend lasted exactly as long as ADX stayed above 25.

Then on January 28, 2026, MSFT closed at 481. ADX peaked at 38 around January 18 and began declining — by January 28 it was at 28 and still falling. The price was still rising, but ADX's decline was warning that the trend was losing strength. MSFT proceeded to chop between 475 and 488 for the next four weeks; the next MA-cross system would have whipsawed.

Falsifiable claim: trend-following entries taken when ADX is below 20 have materially worse outcomes than the same entries taken when ADX is above 25. The invalidation: a sample of 30+ trades where below-20 entries match above-25 entries on win-rate. Empirically, the spread is significant — choppy markets eat trend systems.

The threshold rule: if ADX is below 20, you're in a range; trend-following strategies will whipsaw and mean-reversion strategies fit. Above 25, you're in a trend; trend-following fits and mean-reversion strategies will get steamrolled. The 20–25 zone is the no-man's-land where strategy choice is least certain.

On Bursa Malaysia: SIME in mid-2025 — ADX below 20 from April through June (range-bound), then crossed 25 in July as price broke out, holding above 25 through September during the trend run. The strategy choice flipped exactly with the ADX threshold.

## The trap

The trap is using ADX direction as a directional signal. "ADX is rising — buy." But ADX rises in both uptrends and downtrends. A crashing stock has rising ADX just as a rallying stock does. Confusing ADX direction with price direction is the most common ADX error.

The correct mental model: ADX is the volume knob on trend strength. It tells you how loudly the trend is "speaking," not what it's saying. Use a separate directional indicator (price above/below MA, MACD above/below zero) to read direction, and use ADX to gate which strategy family applies. ADX above 25 plus price above SMA50 = trend-following long territory; ADX above 25 plus price below SMA50 = trend-following short territory; ADX below 20 = no trend, switch frameworks.

## Takeaway

ADX measures trend strength, not trend direction — above 25 means trend-following fits, below 20 means range-bound regime where mean-reversion fits. Read direction from a separate indicator; use ADX to choose strategy family.
