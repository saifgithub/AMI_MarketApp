# Backer FAQ — objections and honest answers

**Status: DRAFT. Internal preparation document.** Hand over selectively, or use as preparation for
the conversation. Do not pre-empt objections nobody raised — volunteering all fourteen of these
unprompted reads as anxiety.

**Two absolute rules.** Never improvise a legal answer — route to counsel. Never answer Q5 with
anything but the literal truth.

---

## The eight that decide it

### Q1. You're one person. What if you get hit by a bus?

**Real, and not solved. Say so.**

> "You'd lose the thing that's making this move. There's no co-founder and no team, so that's a
> genuine risk you're taking on and I'm not going to dress it up.
>
> What exists: the whole project is documented in the repository — every change recorded, the
> specification written down, the architecture explained. Someone competent could pick it up. That
> is not the same as someone *would*.
>
> If that risk is unacceptable to you, don't do this. I'd rather hear that now."

Do not offer key-man insurance, a successor plan or a data-escrow arrangement unless one actually
exists. Inventing a mitigation is worse than admitting the gap.

### Q2. Zero revenue. Why should I believe anyone will pay?

> "You shouldn't yet. Nobody has ever been asked to.
>
> Here's the honest split: the build risk is gone — 1,406 commits, 342 lessons in three languages,
> thirteen working agents, 938 completed debates, shipping on both stores' test tracks. The demand
> risk is entirely intact. Zero revenue, zero real users, zero waitlist signups.
>
> That's not a gap in the pitch, it's the pitch. Seventy percent of this money is advertising. What
> you're funding is the experiment that tells us whether people pay."

### Q3. There's no company. Am I contracting with you personally?

> "Yes, today. There's no incorporated entity, so until there is, the obligation is mine
> personally, backed by my personal assets. That's a real difference and you should understand it
> before signing anything.
>
> The agreement has a novation clause — if I incorporate during the twelve months, I can transfer
> the obligations to the company by written notice, your rights carry over unchanged, and you can
> object within thirty days if you think the company is a worse counterparty than me.
>
> I'm also weighing incorporating first. If that's what makes this workable for you, tell me — it
> may change the sequence."

### Q4. Is this a security? Should we be doing this?

**The one where improvising is genuinely dangerous.**

> "That's a lawyer's question and mine is looking at it. I'm not going to guess in front of you.
>
> What I can tell you is what the document does: it's structured as a private contract for a share
> of profit — no shares, no ownership, no voting rights, and deliberately non-transferable so it
> can't trade like a security. There's no offer to the public: no website page, no post, no
> platform. I'm having this conversation with people I already know, one at a time, for exactly
> this reason.
>
> Whether that's sufficient under Malaysian law is what counsel is confirming, and nothing gets
> signed until they've answered. If the answer is that it needs restructuring, we restructure."

Never say "it's not a security." Never say "we found an exemption." Both are legal conclusions and
neither is yours to reach.

### Q5. Who else is in?

**Answer with the literal truth, whatever it is.**

If nobody: *"Nobody yet. You'd be first."*
If some have: the number and roughly the amount, no names without their permission.

> "And I'll say the obvious thing: being first is worse than being fifth, because you're taking the
> risk without knowing whether anyone else found it credible. I'm not going to pretend otherwise to
> make it easier."

Implied momentum is the one lie that retroactively destroys every true thing in the conversation.
It is also the easiest to check.

### Q6. What stops Finelo just copying the thirteen-agent debate?

> "Nothing stops them trying. Four things make it harder than it looks.
>
> The orchestration is one thing; making a thirteen-way argument *legible* to someone who isn't
> technical is the harder half, and it's most of the design work here.
>
> The personalisation is at the agent-prompt level, not the curriculum level — every agent reads
> the user's mandate. Retrofitting that into a lesson-based app means rebuilding the product.
>
> The journal compounds. A two-year user's record of their own reasoning isn't replicable by a
> competitor at any price.
>
> And the Sharia and ethical screening is a market mass-market apps have consistently declined to
> serve. That's a judgement, not a certainty — but it's held so far.
>
> Could a funded team out-execute me? Yes. That's a real risk, and my only defence is being faster
> and caring more about a niche they find too small."

### Q7. On-prem inference doesn't scale. What happens when it breaks?

> "Correct, and it's disclosed in the agreement rather than buried.
>
> Today the model server is hardware I own, so inference costs electricity. That's a real advantage
> at this stage and a temporary one. If usage grows past what it handles, the plan is Google Cloud
> and a paid model provider, which puts infrastructure somewhere between USD 750 and 2,550 a month
> — at the top end, more than the whole raise.
>
> If that happens during the twelve months, the agreement says I reallocate from advertising rather
> than exceed contributions or come back to you for more. Which means growth spend gets cut to pay
> for the infrastructure that growth caused. That's the honest trade and it's written into
> Schedule B.
>
> And note the good version of that problem: it only happens if usage grows."

### Q8. What happens in month 13?

> "The contribution period ends and you stop paying. Your profit share doesn't stop — it continues
> until you've received [SET]× your contribution, or until AMI Trade ceases operating, whichever
> comes first. There's no expiry date on the right itself.
>
> What you don't keep: nothing accrues if the app never turns a monthly profit. There's no residual
> claim, no equity conversion, no asset claim. If it fails, it fails and you've lost the money."

## Six practical ones

### Q9. How do I actually pay?

> "Twelve equal monthly instalments by bank transfer, to an account I'll nominate. Your
> participation starts on your first payment, not on signature — so you're not exposed to twelve
> months on day one."

### Q10. What if I miss a payment?

> "Missing two consecutive instalments without agreeing it first suspends your profit-share rights
> until you catch up. That's at my discretion, and the intent is to handle a rough month, not to
> catch you out. Talk to me before it happens and it won't be a problem."

### Q11. Can I get out early?

> "By mutual written agreement, yes. There's no unilateral exit and no refund mechanism — ending it
> early doesn't return contributions already made.
>
> Which is the same point as the risk warning: only put in money you can walk away from."

### Q12. Can I sell or transfer my share?

> "No, not without my written consent — and that restriction is deliberate. Freely transferable
> profit rights start to look like a tradeable security, which is the thing we're specifically
> avoiding. Keeping it non-transferable is part of what keeps this a private contract between named
> people."

### Q13. What if you incorporate and the company is an empty shell?

> "Then you object. The novation clause gives you thirty days in writing if you reasonably believe
> the company isn't a fit substitute — undercapitalised shell being the example named in the
> document. Absent an objection it transfers automatically, so the thirty days is your protection
> and you should actually use it."

### Q14. What reporting do I get?

> "A written summary each quarter: monthly revenue, direct costs, monthly gross profit, and what
> was paid to the backer pool, for each month in the quarter. You can ask for supporting detail
> once a year.
>
> What you won't get is audited financials — none exist for a solo-founder company at this stage
> and I'm not going to imply otherwise. The summaries are confidential to you and your own
> advisors."

Hand over the blank `quarterly_report_template.md` here. It answers the question better than the
paragraph does.

## The one they may not ask, and you should raise anyway

**Founder compensation is excluded from Direct Costs.**

Buried in §1 of the agreement: the Founder's own salary or draw is deliberately excluded from the
cost line, so gross profit — and therefore the backer pool — is not reduced by what Saiful pays
himself. The document itself flags this as *"a real conflict of interest to manage in practice, not
just on paper."*

**Raise it before they find it.**

> "One thing I want to point at myself, because you'd find it and it would look worse discovered
> than disclosed. The profit calculation excludes my own pay. So I can't shrink your share by
> paying myself more — the number you're sharing in is calculated before I take anything.
>
> The flip side is I have no salary discipline imposed by this contract. My answer is that I'm not
> paying myself out of a USD 24,000 raise at all, and the quarterly report will show you the cost
> lines. If you want that written in differently, say so now — it's a placeholder, not a position."

Raising this unprompted is the single highest-trust move available in the conversation. It also
requires P5 in `investor_marketing_plan.md` §2 to be resolved first — do not raise it without an
answer.
