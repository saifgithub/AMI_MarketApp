# "So what?" — the user's-eye case for AMI Trade

Working document for positioning and marketing planning. Written from the user's
seat, not the builder's. No agent architecture talk here — users buy outcomes.

---

## Q1: "So what — how can my life be better with this app?"

**The problem it solves:** most people who want to invest face two bad options —
pay for opinions they can't evaluate (gurus, newsletters, Reddit), or learn by
losing real money. AMI Trade is the third option: you practice the actual skill —
*making a call under uncertainty* — with zero financial downside.

**What your day looks like:** you open the app and your analyst team has already
done the work — fundamentals, news, sentiment, a bull case, a bear case, three
risk debaters arguing it out. You don't read 40 tabs of research; you read their
debate, interrogate them 1-on-1 ("why do you disagree with the bear?"), and then
you — and only you — make the call. Buy, sell, hold, size, risk limits. The app
executes it in simulation and tracks how you did.

**How life gets better, concretely:**

- **You build judgment, not just knowledge.** Lessons teach concepts; the room
  teaches decision-making. You learn what a bad thesis sounds like because the
  Bear Researcher dismantles it in front of you.
- **You find out if you're actually good at this — for free.** Most people
  discover they're not cut out for active trading only after losing thousands.
  Here the tuition is zero. Either you learn discipline, or you learn to buy
  index funds — both are wins.
- **It fits in minutes.** Daily brief, a challenge, a journal entry. It's a
  habit loop, not homework.
- **You keep a journal of your calls and reasoning**, so six months in you can
  see your own biases — chasing, panic-selling, overconfidence — with receipts.

One-line version: **it's the difference between reading about swimming and
getting in the pool — except the pool has no sharks.**

Never lead with "AI agents." Users don't buy agents. They buy *"I'll finally
understand my own investments"* and *"I can practice without losing money."*
The agents are just how it's delivered.

## Q2: "How is this better than just asking a chat AI?"

**Where chat AI already wins:**

- One-off questions ("explain a covered call") — instant, free, no app needed.
- Freeform conversation; you can go off-script anywhere.
- If you want an *answer*, chat AI is faster. Full stop.

**Where AMI Trade is a different thing entirely:**

1. **A chat AI forgets you.** It doesn't know you panic-sold three positions
   last month, that your mandate caps position size at 10%, or that you've been
   long NVDA since Tuesday. AMI Trade's state — portfolio, journal, mandate,
   streaks — is the product. The debate is about *your* book, not a hypothetical.
2. **A chat AI agrees with you.** Ask "should I buy TSLA?" and you get a
   balanced essay leaning whichever way your question tilts. In AMI Trade the
   Bear Researcher's *job* is to attack your thesis, the debaters argue risk,
   and the Portfolio Manager can refuse a trade that breaks your own mandate.
   Structured disagreement is the feature.
3. **It makes you act, then scores you.** Chat AI ends at advice. AMI Trade
   forces a decision, executes it in simulation, and holds the receipts. Six
   months later you have a track record of *your* calls with your reasoning
   attached. No feedback loop, no skill — chat AI gives you none.
4. **The data is wired in.** Live prices, news, your positions — the room runs
   on real market data, not a training cutoff or data you paste in yourself.
5. **It's a game with a floor.** Streaks, challenges, leagues, the journal —
   the habit loop. Nobody opens a chat AI daily to practice trading; there's
   nothing to come back to.

One-line version: **chat AI is a library; AMI Trade is a gym.**

Strategic consequence: if a user's goal is *answers*, chat AI wins and it's
free. AMI Trade only justifies $14.99/mo for users whose goal is *skill +
track record*. That positioning decision drives everything — onboarding, the
daily loop, the journal — and it's why simulation and memory are
non-negotiable parts of the moat.

## Target user profiles

Personas for marketing planning. Each lists the hook (the message that lands),
the product surface that closes them, and the plan they fit.

### 1. The Curious Beginner — "Aisha, 26"

- **Who:** Has a salary, a savings account, maybe a small ETF position her bank
  sold her. Follows finfluencers but doesn't trust them. Intimidated by
  brokerages; terrified of "doing it wrong."
- **Hook:** *"Learn to invest by doing it — with none of your money at risk."*
- **Product surface:** Onboarding with the Concierge, lessons, the room as a
  guided show, daily challenges. Floor Pass (free, ads) entry point.
- **Message test:** Does she say "I'd actually open that every day"? The
  beginner converts on safety + habit, never on feature lists.
- **Channels:** TikTok/IG short-form, app-store featuring, finfluencer
  partnerships (positioned as the *antidote* to finfluencer tips).

### 2. The Burnt Retail Trader — "Marcus, 34"

- **Who:** Traded meme stocks or crypto in 2021. Lost real money. Still watches
  the market but is gun-shy. Knows the vocabulary, lacks the discipline.
- **Hook:** *"You already paid tuition once. Rebuild your edge where losses
  don't cost you."*
- **Product surface:** The debate room (he'll respect the Bear), the journal
  with receipts, mandate enforcement, portfolio stats. Trader plan ($14.99/mo).
- **Message test:** He converts on *respect* — the app must feel like it takes
  trading seriously, not a toy. Show him the risk debaters shutting down a bad
  trade.
- **Channels:** Reddit (r/stocks, r/investing — careful, they hate marketing),
  YouTube trading-education channels, X/FinTwit.

### 3. The Competitive Optimizer — "Priya, 29"

- **Who:** Treats everything as a game to win. Duolingo streak type, Strava
  segment hunter, spreadsheet enjoyer. Investing is one more leaderboard.
- **Hook:** *"Prove you'd beat the market. We keep score."*
- **Product surface:** Leagues, daily challenges, streaks, stats dashboards,
  credit packs. Trader or Floor Manager plan.
- **Message test:** Leaderboards and streak mechanics in the first 30 seconds
  of any ad. She converts on competition and quantified self-improvement.
- **Channels:** App-store search ads, habit/productivity app cross-promos,
  Discord communities.

### 4. The Serious Learner — "David, 45"

- **Who:** Professional, has a real portfolio (advisor or index funds), wants
  to understand what he owns and maybe manage a slice himself. Reads
  Morningstar, listens to investing podcasts.
- **Hook:** *"Understand every investment you own — well enough to defend it."*
- **Product surface:** 1-on-1 agent interrogation, fundamentals depth, the
  journal as a thinking tool, briefings. Floor Manager plan ($34.99/mo).
- **Message test:** Depth and calm. No gamification in his ads — show the
  research quality and the "interrogate your own thesis" loop.
- **Channels:** Podcast sponsorships (investing/economics shows), newsletters,
  LinkedIn.

### 5. The Young Speculator — "Leo, 21"

- **Who:** First paycheck, high risk appetite, would otherwise open a leveraged
  trading app or ape into whatever's trending. This is a *protective*
  positioning: redirect the adrenaline into simulation before real leverage
  finds him.
- **Hook:** *"Run the riskiest strategy you can dream up. Only the wins are
  real."*
- **Product surface:** Fast room runs, aggressive mandate templates, leagues,
  shareable P&L cards. Floor Pass → Trader upgrade.
- **Message test:** Speed and bragging rights. Screenshots of simulated P&L
  beat feature copy.
- **Channels:** TikTok, YouTube Shorts, campus finance clubs.

### 6. The Language-First Learner — v1.0 expansion (ar / ms)

- **Who:** Arabic- and Malay-speaking markets where quality investing education
  in-language is thin. Same beginner/optimizer archetypes, but the differentiator
  is *native-language depth*, not novelty.
- **Hook:** *"World-class investing education, in your language."* (Localised,
  not translated.)
- **Product surface:** Full RTL experience, localised lessons/glossary/agents,
  local payment rails where relevant.
- **Channels:** Local finfluencers, regional app-store featuring, partnerships
  with banks/fintechs running financial-literacy campaigns.

## What this means for the marketing plan

- **Lead with the pool-without-sharks framing**, not the technology.
- **Segment creative by persona** — the beginner gets safety, the burnt trader
  gets respect, the optimizer gets leaderboards. One generic ad serves none.
- **Free tier is the funnel**: Floor Pass must deliver the "aha" (first room
  debate + first simulated call) within one session.
- **The journal is the retention story** — every re-engagement message should
  point back at the user's own track record ("your last 5 calls: ...").
- **Position against both extremes**: smarter than a tip, safer than a
  brokerage. The chat-AI objection (Q2) is answered by *memory + disagreement
  + receipts*, and that trio should appear verbatim in objection-handling copy.
