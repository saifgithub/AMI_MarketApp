Not on the client either: `short_rules.dart`'s `stopIsWrongSide`/`targetIsWrongSide` both take an `isShort` flag and handle the long case **correctly**, and `_localRefusal` calls them behind `if (intent != SellIntent.opensShort) return null` — after already returning early for every buy. **That is P21 — a check present in the source and inert at runtime — on both sides of the wire at once**, which is also why reading either implementation made the rule look covered. **The cost is not cosmetic.** `bracket_hit` fires a long stop on `mark <= stop`, so a buy whose stop sits at or above entry is already through it at the moment of purchase: `evaluate_outcomes` liquidates the position on the **next** market-hours sweep, at market, and books it as a stop-out — which then trips the post-stop-out cooldown that hard-blocks the user's next BUY for the configured window. One transposed field costs an instant liquidation *and* a trading ban, with nothing anywhere saying why. A target below entry is the mirror: it fires `mark >= target` immediately and books a win the position never made. **Raised by Saiful while reviewing the order journey** — *"we also need to check the numbers in stop loss and take profit makes sense, like the stop loss cannot be higher than the buy/sell."* **Fix:** `short_bracket_is_wrong_side` becomes `bracket_is_wrong_side(is_short=...)`, carrying both directions, and lives beside `bracket_hit` in `order_pricing.py` — deliberately adjacent, because *where a level belongs* and *when it fires* are one fact and this defect is what writing them apart produces. The call moves out of `_open_short_fill` and up to **`_execute_fill`**, the one chokepoint every fill path crosses, which knows the long case too. **It is judged against the FILL price, not the mark at submit**, and that placement is load-bearing for resting orders: Rule 2 books a buy stop at the worse of named and observed, so a target above the named trigger can be below the actual fill — judged at placement it would pass, and exist inverted. An exit (a sell that closes a long) is deliberately not checked: its levels are meaningless, the same reason `bracket_hit` branches on what the POSITION is rather than on the row's side. **Guarded:** `test_def312_bracket_side.py`, 18 tests.
