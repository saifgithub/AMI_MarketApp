| DEF255 | 2026-08-11 | room | **The PM states a multi-year holding period and a stop that ordinary weeks-long volatility will hit — the horizon is the user's mandate label restated, not a thesis window, and the two numbers ship to the user side by side.** Found by the CR164 backtest harness (batch `pit-pilot-2`, 13 approvals with complete PM levels, first measurement of the Room's trade *instruction* rather than its name selection). **What the data shows.** `time_horizon_days` across the 13 approvals: 19, 26, 47, 90, 90, 145, 201, 730, 900, and **1095 four times**. 1095 is exactly three years, and exactly the floor of the string the mandate renders into every agent prompt: `overlay_generator._horizon_label` maps `Horizon.LONG` → **"3–10 years"** (`app/agents/overlay_generator.py:218-226`), which the benchmark's `horizon: "long"` mandate puts in front of the PM. The PM is not forecasting three years — it is restating the user's *investment* horizon as this *trade's* horizon. Nothing in the fact sheet could support a three-year view: the technicals block is a 3-month window (`technicals._HISTORY_PERIOD = "3m"`), the fundamentals are TTM off four quarters, the range is 52 weeks, and the only forward datum is an FOMC countdown in days. **Why it is a defect and not a preference.** The stop is sized for weeks while the horizon is stated in years, and the harness proved the collision empirically: of the five approvals carrying a 1095-day thesis (STZ, CRM, MCHP, XPEV, M), **four were stopped out inside the data window** — CRM on a 14.3% stop that produced the book's largest loss, on a position whose stated horizon was three years. Across all 12 filled trades, 8 stopped, 3 hit target, 1 reached its horizon; win rate 33%. A three-year hold whose stop sits 6% below entry is internally incoherent — three-year volatility on any US equity is many multiples of that — so either the horizon or the stop is wrong, and the Room asserts both. **This reaches the user.** `time_horizon_days` is not internal: `mobile/lib/models/room.dart:53` carries it and `room_board_mappers.dart:56` renders it on the verdict board, so a user reads "hold 3 years" beside a stop that triggers in a fortnight. **Root cause is an unspecified field.** The PM's JSON contract (`room_prompts.py:195`) asks only for `"horizon_days": <integer, required if APPROVE>` — no definition of what the horizon measures, no admissible range, and no requirement that it be consistent with the stop distance or with the evidence window the agent was actually given. The code's own fallback disagrees with the model's habit by 26×: when the PM omits the field, `room_runner.py:1097` defaults to `ctx.trader_horizon_weeks * 7` = **42 days**, while the PM's modal answer is 1095. Both are valid integers, so nothing has ever flagged the gap. **Remedy (design, then prompt+check — P2 says wording alone will not hold).** Decide first what the field means: this codebase's Trader already reasons in `trader_horizon_weeks` (default 6), so the honest reading is *thesis horizon*, distinct from the mandate's investment horizon, and the two must be named differently everywhere they are rendered. Then (a) state the definition and an admissible range in the PM contract, anchored to the evidence actually supplied; (b) add a deterministic coherence check beside the existing geometry annotations in `room_runner` — a stop whose distance from entry is implausible for the stated horizon is a contradiction the code should catch, exactly as `_verify_and_annotate_geometry` already catches R:R contradictions; (c) decide whether the board should render a thesis horizon at all when the stop will resolve the position far sooner. **Acceptance:** re-run the CR164 pinned regression batch and require that no approval pairs a horizon > 365 days with a stop closer than the ticker's own realized volatility over that horizon, with the residual hand-read; and that the rendered board copy no longer states an investment horizon as if it were the trade's expected duration.
