No `RetryInterceptor`, no backoff, no attempt counter anywhere in `mobile/lib`. A single stalled request on a mobile network is final. **(2) The retryability signal exists, was correct here, and the portfolio path throws it away.** `isRetryable()` (`friendly_error.dart:107-132`) returns `true` for `receiveTimeout` — exactly the error that fired. It was built by DEF151/DEF164 for precisely this purpose, and its own docstring states the contract: *"the caller needs to know whether to offer one, not just what to say."* `sim_providers.dart:68-71` takes the "what to say" half and drops the "whether to offer" half — the catch block calls `friendlyError(e, action: 'load your portfolio')` and stores the string, never calling `isRetryable(e)`, so the state carries copy and no flag and no callback. **Only two widgets in the entire app consume the signal** (`ticker_chart.dart:102`, `portfolio_equity_chart.dart:59`); both are charts. **(3) So "Try again." was the tail of a sentence, not a control.** The user's only recourse was to leave the tab and return, re-triggering `load()`. **This is DEF151 inverted, and the inversion is the finding.** DEF151 shipped a retry *button* over a 422 that could never succeed — a promise the app could not keep. This ships retry *text* over a timeout that would very likely have succeeded — a promise the app never offered to make. Both come from copy and affordance being decided in different places; `_statusRetryable` was extracted by DEF164 so *"the copy and the retry affordance cannot disagree"*, and here they disagree by one simply not existing. **Fix, and the constraint that makes it non-trivial.** A blanket transport-level retry is WRONG here and would be a data defect, not a UX win: `SimNotifier.load()` (`sim_providers.dart:63-67`) fires **three** calls, and the first is `simEvaluate()` — a **POST** to `/v1/sim/trades/{id}/evaluate` (`api_client.dart:1061-1062`) that evaluates stops/targets and can close positions. The other two are GETs (`simPortfolio`, `simListTrades`). Re-sending the POST on a timeout re-runs a mutation whose first attempt may have succeeded server-side after the client gave up — the classic timeout-is-not-a-failure trap, and `receiveTimeout` (30s) specifically means the request *was* sent. So: retry GETs only (idempotent by verb), or give `evaluate` an idempotency key before it is ever retried; never a verb-blind interceptor. Separately, surface the affordance: thread `isRetryable(e)` into `SimState` alongside the message and render a real retry control, so the sentence and the button are decided by the same call — the DEF164 rule, applied to the branch it never reached. **Guard:** a test asserting every surface that renders a `friendlyError` string also reads `isRetryable` for the same error — the mechanical version of a rule two defects have now been filed against.
