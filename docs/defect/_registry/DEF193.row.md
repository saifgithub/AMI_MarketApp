For `sector_cap_pct` and `single_name_cap_pct`, `None` does **not** mean "off": CR101-BE1 made them explicit settable fields but kept a server-side preset fallback (`resolved_sector_cap_pct` / `risk_tier_cap`, keyed off `concentration_tolerance` / `risk_score`), and that preset is a **real, binding cap**. `GET /v1/mandate/{id}` echoes the raw stored value, so an un-overridden cap comes back `null` — indistinguishable on the wire from the five CR101-BE2 fields where `null` genuinely means unenforced. **The builder handled it correctly and I want that on record:** my own assign told it "`None` means the limit is OFF" for all seven fields; it measured that against BE1's bridge, found the claim false for two of them, and refused to render "OFF" over a cap that is actively enforced — that would be the CR046 shown-vs-enforced lie in its purest form. Right call, incomplete outcome — CR101's own invariant requires each limit be **disclosed in its own units**, and a user who cannot see that they are capped at 40% has not had it disclosed. **What makes this a defect rather than a feature request:** the number is already computed and already served. `GET /v1/portfolio/allocation` returns `max_allowed`, read from the mandate and explicitly "never hard-coded" (`api/portfolio.py:60`), and the allocation donut has been showing it to users for months.
