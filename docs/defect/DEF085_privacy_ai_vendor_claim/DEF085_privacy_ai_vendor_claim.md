# DEF085 — Privacy Policy claims prompts are "never sent to any third-party AI vendor"; an Anthropic fallback and third-party sub-processors already exist

**Area:** legal/docs · **Source:** prompt (AT:legal legal-review pass) · **Round:** AT:legal · **Status:** fixed
**Severity:** medium — a factually inaccurate claim in a published, customer-facing legal document, on the exact topic (AI infrastructure/data handling) the review was scoped to.

---

## Summary

`website/privacy/index.html` (and its markdown source) stated, in two places:

> §7: "The AMI analyst language model that powers your agents runs on infrastructure that we
> operate ourselves. **Your prompts and the responses generated by the AMI agents are not sent
> to any third-party AI vendor** (see clause 8)."
>
> §8 "Your AMI agent conversations stay within AMI": "Unlike services that route your prompts
> through OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, or other third-party AI providers, the AMI analyst
> agents are powered by a large language model that we host on our own infrastructure...
> processed entirely inside AMI's environment and are **not sent to any third-party AI
> vendor**."

This is an absolute, unqualified claim. It conflicts with internal documentation:

| | |
|---|---|
| `CLAUDE.md`'s LLM-provider row | "Gateway prefers `vllm > anthropic > mock`." Anthropic is a documented, live fallback path, not a hypothetical. |
| `docs/initial_specs/08_tech/stack.md` | Lists OpenRouter + direct Anthropic/OpenAI/Google keys for "LLM routing/fallback," alongside the on-prem vLLM primary. |
| `docs/initial_specs/09_compliance/disclaimers_and_privacy.md` | Its own GDPR sub-processor table states: *"We have DPAs with: Supabase, GCP, OpenRouter, Anthropic, OpenAI, Google AI, RevenueCat, OneSignal, Twilio, Resend, Sentry, PostHog, Azure, ElevenLabs."* |

So the Privacy Policy's own "differentiator" clause was contradicted by a sibling document in
the same folder that lists the very vendors clause 8 says are never used.

## Why this matters here specifically

The legal-review pass this defect was found under was scoped exactly to protecting AMI against
claims that "the LLM gave bad advice." A privacy document that makes a confident, specific,
falsifiable claim about data handling — right next to the AI-output disclaimers — is exposure
in the same direction: if a user (or regulator) can point to one inaccurate factual claim in
the legal documents, it undermines the credibility of the surrounding disclaimers too.

## Fix

Corrected under [CR068](../../forward_planning/CR068_legal_docs_hardening/CR068_legal_docs_hardening.md), applied to both `legal/policies/privacy_policy.md` and
`website/privacy/index.html` (v1.0 → v2.0):

- §7 now reads: primary AI infrastructure is self-hosted; **in some circumstances** a request
  may route to a third-party AI infrastructure provider operating under contract with
  confidentiality/data-protection obligations equivalent to AMI's own; prompts/responses are
  never used to train models for other customers, never sold, never shared with advertisers —
  regardless of which infrastructure processes a given request.
- §8 retitled "Where your AMI agent conversations are processed" and rewritten to match — no
  longer contrasts against named competitors (OpenAI/Anthropic/Google Gemini), no longer claims
  an absolute "never."
- **Deliberately generic on vendor identity** (Saiful's steer, AskUserQuestion round for CR068):
  "a third-party AI infrastructure provider," not a named vendor — accurate today and doesn't
  require a legal-doc update every time the specific fallback vendor changes.
- Version-history entry on the published policy explicitly names this as a correction, not just
  a wording tweak, so the audit trail is honest about what v1.0 got wrong.

## Guard

None added — this is a content-accuracy fix to a hand-authored legal document, not a code path.
The general safeguard is procedural: `legal/README.md` and `VERSIONING.md` now make `legal/`
(not `docs/initial_specs/09_compliance/`) the single canonical location, reducing the chance of
a stale duplicate drifting from the actual architecture again. Future factual claims in these
documents about vendor/infrastructure specifics should be cross-checked against
`docs/initial_specs/08_tech/stack.md` before publishing.
