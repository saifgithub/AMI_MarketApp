# BEDROCK Mandate
## A Defensible Investment Process, v1.0

Prepared by ATM Market Intel (AMI) Research
Date: 10 September 2026
Scope: the US mid-cap equity allocation (the BEDROCK mandate). This document does not set the size of that allocation within the principal's overall holdings; it governs what happens inside it.
Status: analysis and process design, not personalised financial or tax advice. Tax and estate points are to be verified with a qualified adviser.

---

## 0. What "defensible" means, and what it does not

Defensible: every rule below cites the finding that justifies it (marked [E-n], listed in Section 9). The process is built so that its realised return equals the return of its holdings minus a small, known toll, and so that the documented ways individuals and fund clients lose money cannot enter it without breaking a written rule.

Not probable: the process does not claim to beat its benchmark net. The evidence supports one claim only: an investor who holds cheap, diversified factor exposure and does not trade it finishes ahead of roughly 90% of professional active managers over 15 years and ahead of the average hedge fund client, because those participants pay tolls this process does not pay [E-1, E-2, E-3]. Any return above that comes from factor premia that have paid historically and may not [E-4], and from a small satellite that is permitted to exist only under conditions the evidence says winners meet [E-5, E-6, E-7].

Expected outcome, stated as judgment: core excess return over the S&P MidCap 400 of 1% to 2% a year before withholding drag, tracking error 4% to 6%, relative drawdowns of 10% to 15% that can last two to three years. Probability of beating the plain index over ten years perhaps 60% to 65%. Probability of beating the average active fund client or hedge fund client over ten years: high, by arithmetic of costs rather than by skill.

## 1. Structure

Three sleeves, sized at the annual review and nowhere else.

| Sleeve | Weight | Content | Claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core | 85% to 100% | Equal thirds S&P MidCap Quality (XMHQ), Momentum (XMMO), Value-with-Momentum (XMVM) | Default win: factor beta at about 0.33% a year, zero manager, zero timing |
| Plain-index option | 0% to 50% of core | Irish-domiciled S&P 400 fund (SPY4) in place of part of the core | Structural only: estate-tax and administration, not return |
| Satellite | 0% to 15%, default 0% | At most five concentrated, patient positions meeting every test in Section 4 | Possible skill; unproven; sized so that being wrong changes nothing |

Cash is not a sleeve. The mandate is fully invested; liquidity is held elsewhere.

## 2. Core rules

2.1 Initial purchase. Lump sum at the next annual review date or immediately if the mandate is being established. Staging purchases over months is a behavioural aid, not a return improver; if staging is used for comfort, it is three equal tranches at monthly intervals and nothing more. [E-8]

2.2 Composition. Equal thirds of the three funds. The equal weighting is a prior, not an optimisation, chosen because the three active-return streams are lowly or negatively correlated and the blend halves the tracking error of a two-fund mix without reducing excess return in the available history [E-9]. No other weighting is permitted without a new version of this document.

2.3 Rebalancing. Once a year, on a fixed calendar date, back to equal thirds, only if any fund has drifted more than five percentage points from target. No rebalancing at any other time for any reason, including market events, news, or a change of view. [E-10]

2.4 Dividends. Reinvested at the annual rebalance. Not spent, not timed.

2.5 Benchmarks. Two, reported annually: IJH (S&P MidCap 400) and SPY4. The core is compared against both. The comparison is recorded, not acted on (Section 6).

2.6 Replacement of a fund. A fund is replaced, at the next annual review only, if any of the following occurs: it closes; its index methodology changes; its fee rises above 0.50%; a fund tracking the same index becomes available at least 10 basis points cheaper with comparable assets. Replacement is with the closest available equivalent. Performance is never a replacement trigger.

2.7 Prohibited in the core. Leverage of any kind, options, single-stock or leveraged ETFs, tactical tilts, sector overlays, hedges, and any position added because of a news event. [E-11, E-12]

## 3. Plain-index option (structural, not return-seeking)

3.1 The arithmetic. For a Saudi-resident holder, US-domiciled funds and directly held US stocks face 30% dividend withholding with no treaty relief [E-13]. An Irish-domiciled fund pays 15% at fund level but charges 0.30% against IJH's 0.05%, so on income the two are roughly a wash. The Irish fund's advantage is that it is not a US-situs asset for estate-tax purposes and requires no US withholding administration. [E-13]

3.2 Rule. If US-situs holdings across all accounts exceed the threshold at which US estate-tax exposure becomes material (to be confirmed with an adviser; the non-resident exemption is small), the plain-index portion of the core is held in SPY4 rather than IJH, up to 50% of the core. The factor tilts have no Irish-domiciled equivalent and remain US-domiciled.

3.3 What this option is not. It is not a return decision and is never adjusted on performance grounds.

## 4. Satellite rules

The satellite exists because the only individual investors with documented persistent outperformance are concentrated, patient, and informed, and their excess returns concentrate in stocks outside the S&P 500 with thin coverage [E-5, E-6], and because among professional managers only high-conviction portfolios held for more than two years outperform [E-7]. The same evidence shows that concentration without information is the profile of the losing majority [E-14]. The rules below exist to make the second case impossible to enter by accident.

4.1 Default state: empty. A satellite position may be opened only when every test in 4.2 is passed in writing before any order is placed.

4.2 Pre-trade tests (all must be yes; the written answers are filed in the decision journal, Section 5):
1. Information: I can state, in one paragraph, the specific fact or interpretation I hold that the marginal price-setter does not, and its source. The source is public information combined with domain expertise. It is not material non-public information from any relationship, employment, or consulting engagement; if there is any doubt, the answer is no.
2. Coverage: the company is not in the S&P 500 and has fewer than eight sell-side analysts.
3. Horizon: the intended holding period is at least two years, and the thesis names the events that would confirm or refute it over that period.
4. Attention: I did not learn of this name through a price move, a headline, or social media in the past 30 days, and the stock has not risen more than 20% in the prior 20 trading days. [E-11]
5. Size: the position is at most 3% of the mandate at cost; the satellite in total is at most 15%; there are at most five positions. Being wrong on all five costs at most 15% of the mandate and nothing else.

4.3 Prohibited in the satellite. Options, leverage, short positions, IPOs younger than two years, companies with an Item 4.02 restatement in the prior 24 months, names failing the Beneish or Altman screens from the BEDROCK specification, and any name in which the principal has a professional relationship that could constitute non-public information.

4.4 Exits. Each position has pre-registered exit conditions written at entry: the thesis-refuting events from test 3. A position is sold when a refuting event occurs, at the next annual review, regardless of price. A position is not sold because its price fell, and is not added to because its price fell, unless the thesis is re-verified in writing against the original entry paragraph. Winners are not sold because they rose; they are sold only when the thesis completes or the two-year minimum has passed and the annual review judges the information advantage gone. [E-15]

4.5 Turnover cap. Satellite turnover may not exceed one-third of satellite value in any twelve months. If the cap is reached, no further satellite trades until the next annual review.

4.6 Satellite kill rule. The satellite is measured against the core, not against zero. If, after five years or fifteen completed positions, whichever comes later, the satellite's cumulative return net of costs is below the core's, the satellite is closed permanently and this section is deleted from the next version of this document. Fifteen decisions is the minimum at which a positive record is distinguishable from luck at even a weak standard; fewer than that proves nothing in either direction. [E-16]

## 5. Decision journal

5.1 Every satellite entry, exit, and rebalance is recorded before execution with date, the pre-trade test answers, expected holding period, and the refuting events. Every annual review is recorded with the benchmark comparison and any rule invoked.

5.2 The journal is append-only. Entries are never edited. This is the individual-investor version of the trial registry in the BEDROCK specification: it makes hindsight editing impossible and makes the five-year kill rule enforceable.

5.3 Once a year the journal is read in full before the review. The purpose is to compare what was written at entry with what actually happened, because the evidence on individual learning is that it is slow and, for active traders, absent [E-17].

## 6. Behavioural safeguards

These rules are the reason the process is defensible rather than merely reasonable. Each one blocks a documented loss mechanism.

6.1 Attention control. Portfolio values are checked no more than monthly; the mandate account has no price alerts and no news feed attached. Attention-induced trading is the single most reliable predictor of individual losses [E-11].

6.2 Cooling-off. Any action not scheduled in this document requires a written proposal, a 30-day wait, and a second written confirmation. Aggressive, immediate orders are where individual losses come from [E-14].

6.3 Pre-mortem letter. Attached to this document at adoption is a letter from the principal to the principal, written now, describing the two-to-three-year relative drawdown the development data says will occur [E-18], the arguments the principal will find persuasive at that time, and the pre-agreed response, which is to do nothing until the next annual review. The letter is re-read at every annual review and at any point the core trails IJH by more than 10% over any trailing window.

6.4 Performance is not a trigger. No rule in this document is triggered by the performance of the core. Underperformance of any size and any duration changes nothing. The only triggers are structural (Section 2.6), tax or legal (Section 3), and the satellite kill rule (Section 4.6). This is deliberate: the documented behaviour of fund investors is to leave after losses and arrive after gains, and that behaviour is the toll [E-3, E-10].

6.5 Override. The principal can of course override any rule; it is his money. The process requires only that an override be written in the journal with the reason, and that the annual review record what the override cost or earned. Overrides are then counted against the satellite kill rule as satellite decisions, because that is what they are.

## 7. Costs and taxes, stated as a toll

| Item | Annual cost | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Blended fund fees, core | about 0.33% | verify at adoption |
| Spreads and rebalance costs | about 0.05% | annual rebalance only |
| Dividend withholding, 30% on about 1% yield | about 0.30% | applies to US-domiciled funds and direct holdings alike [E-13] |
| Capital gains tax | none | non-resident alien below 183 days of US presence [E-13] |
| Total | about 0.7% a year | the entire toll; compare 64% of gross for the average hedge fund client [E-2] |

Estate exposure on US-situs assets is a separate matter and is the reason Section 3 exists.

## 8. Annual review agenda (the only scheduled decision point)

1. Read the pre-mortem letter and the full journal.
2. Record core return versus IJH and SPY4 for the year and since inception. Record; do not act.
3. Apply Section 2.6 (structural replacement) and Section 3.2 (estate threshold).
4. Rebalance the core to equal thirds if any fund drifted more than five points.
5. Review each satellite position against its own entry paragraph and refuting events. Apply 4.4.
6. Apply the satellite kill rule if five years or fifteen positions have elapsed.
7. Record any override from the year and its outcome.
8. Re-confirm, in one sentence, that the process makes no claim of skill in the core and no proven claim in the satellite.

Nothing else. The review takes one afternoon.

## 9. Evidence register

- E-1: Over 15-year periods roughly 90% of active funds underperform their benchmarks; top-quartile persistence is near random (SPIVA, S&P Dow Jones Indices, year-end 2024 and 2025).
- E-2: Hedge fund fees consume 64% of gross returns; effective incentive rate about 50% against a nominal 20% (Ben-David, Birru, Rossi, 2020 to 2023). Dollar-weighted hedge fund investor returns 6% versus S&P 500 10.9%, 1980 to 2008 (Dichev and Yu, 2011). Post-2008 hedge fund alpha about -1% a year net (Sullivan, 2021; Bollen, Joenväärä, Kauppila, 2021).
- E-3: Fund investors' dollar-weighted return trails fund total return by about 1.2 points a year; investors in US stock funds captured 12.8% of 13.3% (Morningstar Mind the Gap, 2025 and 2026). Attribution to timing is contested (Financial Analysts Journal, 2026).
- E-4: Post-publication factor decay of 26% out of sample and 58% post-publication (McLean and Pontiff, 2016); US is where decay is most reliable (Jacobs and Müller). Own analysis: value spreads in the mid-cap band near zero 2017 to 2025; profitability halved but positive; momentum positive (Addendum C to the BEDROCK specification).
- E-5: Top-decile individual investors persist at about 6% a year risk-adjusted; cross-period correlation of individual performance about 10% (Coval, Hirshleifer, Shumway, 2021).
- E-6: Concentrated households outperform diversified ones, strongest in non-S&P 500 and local stocks, consistent with information advantages (Ivković, Sialm, Weisbenner, 2008). A replication finds local holdings do not outperform remote ones, so locality is a proxy, not a rule.
- E-7: High active share funds with holding durations over two years outperform by over 2% a year; frequent traders underperform even with high active share (Cremers and Pareek, 2016).
- E-8: Lump-sum investment outperforms staged investment in most historical windows; staging is behavioural insurance (Vanguard and others; widely replicated).
- E-9: Own analysis of the three-fund blend, July 2019 to July 2026: excess 3.0% a year over IJH, tracking error 4.1%, information ratio 0.74, maximum relative drawdown 3.0% (etf_blends_2019_2026.csv). Short window; treat as flattered.
- E-10: Return-chasing withdrawal after losses is the mechanism behind residual hedge fund fees and the investor return gap (Ben-David et al.; Morningstar).
- E-11: Attention-induced buying predicts negative returns; the most intensely bought stocks by Robinhood users lose about 4.7% afterward (Barber, Huang, Odean, Schwarz, 2022). Retail options portfolios lost $2.1 billion between November 2019 and June 2021 with gross monthly losses of 1.81% (Bryzgalova, Pavlova, Sikorskaya, 2023).
- E-12: Leverage is negatively related to individual performance through costs and forced liquidation (futures brokerage evidence cited in the concentration literature).
- E-13: No US-Saudi income tax treaty; 30% withholding on US-source dividends to non-resident individuals; capital gains not taxed below 183 days of presence (IRS; Bogleheads NRA taxation; practitioner sources). SPY4: Irish-domiciled, accumulating, TER 0.30%, about EUR 5.5bn.
- E-14: Aggregate individual investors in Taiwan lose 3.8 points a year, equal to 2.2% of GDP, traceable to aggressive orders; institutions gain 1.5 points (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, 2009). Average US discount-broker household 16.4% versus market 17.9%; most active 11.4% (Barber and Odean, 2000).
- E-15: The disposition effect, selling winners and holding losers, is documented in every retail dataset studied (Odean, 1998; Barber et al., 2004b).
- E-16: Statistical minimum for distinguishing a record from noise; consistent with the trial-registry logic and Deflated Sharpe gate in the BEDROCK specification.
- E-17: No evidence of learning among Brazilian day traders; learning among Taiwanese day traders is slow, suboptimal, and costly (Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti, 2020; Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, Zhang, 2020).
- E-18: Own analysis: the revised factor blend in the development window had underwater stretches of 32 months (2002 to 2005) and 31 months (2009 to 2011); 72% of the 2000 to 2016 spread came from 2000 to 2002 (Addendum D to the BEDROCK specification).

## 10. What would upgrade this from defensible to probable

Three things, and only these:
1. A satellite journal showing fifteen or more completed decisions over at least five years with cumulative net return above the core. That is the point at which the principal has evidence of membership of the top decile in E-5 rather than a belief in it.
2. A source of cheap, non-callable leverage, which would allow the Buffett structure (factor exposure at 1.6 to 1) rather than its unlevered half. No such source is available to an individual through a brokerage.
3. An engine: a capacity-limited strategy run as a business with the principal on the fee-collecting side. That is not an investment process and is outside this document.

Until one of those exists, the process claims the default win and nothing more, and that claim is the most defensible one in the evidence.
