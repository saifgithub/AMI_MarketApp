# C01 — RESULTS — "A neural network predicts tomorrow's stock price"

**Verdict: `NOT SUPPORTED`** — by the letter of our own rule. Three of our four predictions were
met and none of the three claim-supporting conditions was, but one prediction (H2) failed, and the
pre-registration reserves `DISPROVED` for the case where all four hold.
Pre-registration: commit `3ac320eb` (2026-09-17), before any C01 code existed. Run: 2026-09-17 ·
10 tickers × 3 seeds × 4 arms = 120 trained networks · 7,209 s on Apple-silicon GPU · `code/`
4 tests green. Every figure is in [`out/results.json`](out/results.json); the direction and money
figures in §3–§4 were recomputed independently from the per-cell records and match.

---

## 1. Deviations and disclosed assumptions

No recipe parameter, ticker, window, metric or threshold was changed. The compute contingency
(drop to 50 epochs if one run exceeds 4 minutes) did **not** trigger: the timing run took 52 s and
the 30 A1 runs 68–97 s each.
Implementation-level readings are listed in [`out/DEVIATIONS.md`](out/DEVIATIONS.md). The ones
that matter:

| # | Choice | Effect |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | **Variant A2's architecture is our assumption** (its source does not show it): LSTM(50) → LSTM(50) → Dense(25) → Dense(1), 20 epochs, batch 32. Pre-registered. | A2 results are for that network. A1 is fully specified by its source. |
| 2 | PyTorch, not Keras. Same layer sizes, loss and optimiser. Pre-registered. | — |
| 3 | **How the M2 interval was pooled was not pre-registered; it was fixed in code before the run.** All 30 ticker-seed series of (predicted change, actual change), in dollars, are concatenated and block-bootstrapped. The three seeds of a ticker share the same actual prices, and high-priced tickers weigh more. | The interval is probably **too narrow**. That works against us here: H2 failed because this interval excludes zero (§2). We have not re-pooled it after seeing the result. |
| 4 | **M3 pooled interval is a Wilson interval on pooled counts**, not the block bootstrap the pre-registration names. | Too narrow as well. No conclusion rests on its width: every pooled hit rate is below its base rate at the point estimate. |
| 5 | The run script compared each ticker's hit rate with the **highest** base rate of any ticker. The pre-registration's natural reading is each ticker's **own** base rate. We recomputed it that way (§3). | The stricter reading could have denied the claim a condition it had met. It did not: 0 of 10 tickers in either fair arm under both readings. |
| 6 | M4 is pooled over all three seeds, and per seed. The claim-supporting condition is read on the pooled figure and on seed 0. | Same answer on all of them (§4). |
| 7 | For the return-target arm, M1's naive forecast is "tomorrow's return = 0", which is "tomorrow's price = today's" restated. | — |
| 8 | After the run the per-day return series were taken out of `results.json` (27 MB → 0.3 MB) and replaced by per-cell summaries (`code/slim_results.py`). Pooled figures are untouched and were checked identical. | The full file is regenerated by `run_c01.py`. |
| — | **Seed matters more than it should.** Same recipe, same stock, different random seed: SPY's error ratio is 1.24, 3.92 or 1.43; KO's trading rule is in the market 0%, 0% or 58% of days. | One trained network is one draw. A tutorial shows one. |

## 2. M1 and M2 — the advertised metric, and what the forecast follows

**M1 = the network's test-set RMSE ÷ the RMSE of the forecast "tomorrow's close = today's close".**
Below 1 means the network beat doing nothing.

| Arm | Median M1 | Range | Runs below 1 |
|:--|--:|:--|--:|
| **A1** — as taught (100-day window, 3 LSTM layers, 100 epochs) | **1.98** | 1.09 – 13.04 | **0 of 30** |
| **A2** — as taught (60-day window, 2 LSTM layers, 20 epochs) | **3.76** | 1.39 – 11.68 | **0 of 30** |
| B-price — same network, scaler fit on training data only | 4.56 | 1.27 – 24.57 | 0 of 30 |
| B-return — same network, target = next-day return | 1.0025 | 0.998 – 1.030 | 9 of 30 |

- On the metric the tutorials quote, **no price-forecasting network beat "no change" in 90 runs**.
  Example: AAPL, A1, seed 0 — network RMSE $17.12, "no change" RMSE $3.44.
- Much of the error is level error: 92% of AAPL's test days closed above anything in its training
  segment, and the network under-predicts there. But on INTC, where only 8% of test days did, the
  ratio was still 1.09 – 1.47 in all three runs.
- Asked for returns instead of prices, the network's median error is 0.25% **above** a forecast of
  zero — it learned to say "about no change". The best single run beat zero by 0.2%.

**M2 — does the forecast move with tomorrow, or with yesterday?**

| Arm | corr(predicted change, **tomorrow's** actual change) | corr(forecast's own change, **yesterday's** actual change) | Lag at which forecast best matches price |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| A1 | **0.019** (0.009 … 0.029) | **0.81** (0.77 … 0.85) | 1 day behind in 19 of 30 runs, 2–4 days in the rest |
| A2 | **0.013** (0.001 … 0.025) | 0.34 (0.31 … 0.36) | 2–5 days behind in 30 of 30 |
| B-price | 0.005 (−0.002 … 0.011) | 0.84 (0.80 … 0.87) | 1–3 days behind in 27 of 30; NVDA's three runs (error ratio 23–25) peak at the edge of the ±10-day search window |
| B-return | 0.004 (−0.007 … 0.014) | −0.11 (−0.16 … −0.06) | n/a |

- The forecast trails the price in **60 of 60** as-taught runs. A line that trails a price series
  by a day or a few, drawn over five years, sits on top of it. That is the near-perfect overlay
  (`out/overlay_AAPL.png`, left panel); the right panel zooms to 60 days and adds "no change".
- **H2 predicted no information about tomorrow — an interval including zero. In both as-taught
  arms the interval excludes zero.** The correlation is 0.019 and 0.013: about 0.03% and 0.02% of
  the variation in daily changes. It is positive in 24 of 30 and 28 of 30 runs. It is small, it
  did not show up as direction or as money (§3, §4), and deviation 3 says its interval is too
  narrow — but it is not the zero we wrote down, and we score it as a miss.
- A2's echo of yesterday is 0.34, not obviously "high" (we set no threshold); its forecast is a
  smoother, longer lag rather than a one-day echo.

## 3. M3 — direction

Hit rate of "up or down tomorrow" against always saying "up" over the same days.

| Arm | Pooled hit rate (Wilson 95%) | Always-up, pooled | Tickers beating their own base rate and 50% |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|
| A1 | **48.5%** (48.0 … 49.0) | 52.5% | 0 of 10 |
| A2 | **47.8%** (47.1 … 48.5) | 53.5% | 1 of 10 (TSLA: 52.8% vs 50.7%) |
| B-price | 47.7% (47.2 … 48.2) | 52.6% | 0 of 10 |
| B-return | 50.9% (50.4 … 51.4) | 52.6% | 0 of 10 |

The price-forecasting arms are **below a coin flip**. A forecast that sits under a rising price
says "down" most days, in a market that rose on 52–54% of them.

## 4. M4 — money

Long when the network forecasts a rise, otherwise flat; 5 bps a side; test segment only
(A1, B: 2021-07-12 → 2026-08-28; A2: 2023-09-21 → 2026-08-28).

| Arm | Mean daily return minus buy-and-hold, next-open fills (95% interval) | Same-close fills | Days in the market | Median total return vs buy-and-hold | Runs that beat buy-and-hold |
|:--|:--|:--|--:|:--|--:|
| A1 | **−6.3 bp** (−8.1 … −4.5) | −7.4 bp (−9.2 … −5.6) | 34% | +24% vs +92% | 3 of 30 |
| A2 | **−8.1 bp** (−10.8 … −5.6) | −9.1 bp (−11.6 … −6.7) | 27% | +19% vs +84% | 3 of 30 |
| B-price | −7.8 bp (−9.7 … −5.9) | −8.2 bp (−10.2 … −6.3) | 24% | +8% vs +92% | 2 of 30 |
| B-return | −2.8 bp (−4.0 … −1.6) | −3.1 bp (−4.3 … −1.9) | 72% | +74% vs +92% | 2 of 30 |

"Days in the market" is the share of test days on which the rule's return was not zero.

Seed 0 alone, next-open: A1 −7.4 bp (−10.9 … −4.0); B-price −8.5 bp (−12.2 … −4.9); B-return
−5.9 bp (−8.8 … −3.0). One B-return seed has an interval that includes zero (seed 2: −0.2 bp,
−0.8 … +0.3) — those ten networks are long on 98% of days, so they *are* buy-and-hold.

As in [C02](../C02_chatbot_wrote_my_strategy/RESULTS.md), most of the shortfall is time out of a
rising market, not bad timing while in it. The "generous" same-close reading came out slightly
worse than next-open.

## 5. M5 and M6 — reported, not part of the verdict

- **The leak.** A1 fits its price scaler on the whole series, test segment included. Fit on the
  training segment only, the same network's test RMSE is worse in 26 of 30 runs (median +$2.49).
  The advertised RMSE is flattered by the leak — and still loses to "no change".
- **The "30-day forecast".** Fed its own output for 30 steps, the A1 network's path has a median
  12% of the real series' day-to-day variability (range 3% – 41%); the return-target network's,
  0.1%. The 30-day forecast is a smooth line because the network has nothing to say about day 2.

## 6. Pre-registered hypotheses — scored (Arm A)

| | Prediction | Result | |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| H1 | Median M1 ≥ 1.0 | 1.98 (A1), 3.76 (A2); 0 of 60 runs below 1 | **met** |
| H2 | Forecast echoes yesterday (second correlation high) **and** first-correlation interval includes 0 | echo 0.81 (A1), 0.34 (A2); first interval 0.009 … 0.029 (A1), 0.001 … 0.025 (A2) — **excludes zero** | **not met** |
| H3 | M3 does not exceed the base rate | 48.5% vs 52.5%; 47.8% vs 53.5% | **met** |
| H4 | M4 interval includes or lies below zero | below zero, both variants, both fill rules | **met** |

What would have supported the claim, in the fair arm (any one):

| Condition | Result | |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Median M1 < 0.95 and M1 < 1 in ≥ 80% of runs | 4.56 / 0% (B-price); 1.0025 / 30% (B-return) | **not met** |
| Pooled M3 above max(base rate, 50%), interval excluding it, and in ≥ 7 of 10 tickers | 47.7% and 50.9% against 52.6%; 0 of 10 tickers | **not met** |
| Pooled M4 interval strictly above zero, next-open | −7.8 bp and −2.8 bp, intervals below zero | **not met** |

→ `DISPROVED` requires H1–H4 to hold in Arm A. H2 did not. No claim-supporting condition was met,
so it is not `PARTLY HOLDS`. **→ `NOT SUPPORTED`.**

## 7. What can and cannot be said

Can be said:

- On the error metric it is advertised with, the recipe lost to "tomorrow = today" in 60 of 60
  as-taught runs and 90 of 90 price-forecasting runs, by a median factor of 2 to 4.6.
- The forecast trails the price. Over years, a trailing copy of a price chart overlays it almost
  perfectly; zoomed to 60 days, the gap and the lag are visible.
- Its direction calls were right 48–49% of the time, below a coin flip and below always saying
  "up" (52–54%). Trading them returned a median +24% where holding returned +92%.
- The low RMSE is helped by a scaler that has seen the test data, and the 30-day forecast is
  smooth because the network carries almost nothing forward.
- There is a trace of information about tomorrow in the as-taught forecasts — correlation 0.01 to
  0.02, interval excluding zero as we computed it. We predicted none.

Cannot be said:

- "Disproved." One of our four predictions failed; the verdict is `NOT SUPPORTED`.
- That the 0.02 is an edge, or that it is not. It did not survive as direction or as money under
  the rule the claim implies, and we did not test where it comes from.
- That neural networks, or machine learning, cannot forecast anything in markets. Volatility is
  forecastable (P20). This is one recipe — closing prices in, next close out — and its evidence
  standard. [P01](../P_prior_work/P01_ml_predicts_direction/VIDEO_BRIEF.md) covers gradient-boosted
  direction models.
- Anything beyond nine large US stocks and SPY, daily closes, 2012–2026, tested on 2021–2026
  (A1, B) and 2023–2026 (A2) — both rising markets. We did not tune
  the networks; the claim is about the recipe as taught.
