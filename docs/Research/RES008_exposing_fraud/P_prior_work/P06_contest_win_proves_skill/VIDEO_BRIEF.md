## The one idea

Someone always wins a contest — so a winning trophy, on its own, can't be told apart from what a
big enough field of pure luck produces by construction.

## What we must not say

- This is a conditional statistical statement, full stop. Never suggest it shows any specific
  contest, contestant, or result was luck — the source explicitly did not verify entrant counts or
  contestant volatility for any real contest, and says the antecedents are unverified.
- Never say "proves it was luck" — the source is explicit: "'Consistent with luck' is not 'was
  luck.'" It cannot show skill was absent, and it explicitly allows that the winner may be the most
  skilled person in the field.
- No creator, channel, contest name, or person is named or alluded to, on screen or spoken —
  this claim in particular must not be personalized in any way.
- Do not cite the source's mention of an audited-broker-statement contest structure as if it
  strengthens or weakens the point being made here — the source raises it only as a separate,
  uncomputed consideration ("divisions are scored monthly and quarterly as well as annually, so the
  selection surface is wider"); it is not part of the tested statistic and should not appear in
  this brief's numeric beats.
- No annualising or extrapolating the volatility multiples — they are as computed in the source
  table, for those four specific entrant counts only.

## Description (first 160 characters are the search snippet)

We tested the logic behind "the contest winner proves the method works." Using only statistics: how
big does a contest need to be before a +100% month is simply the expected top result — no skill
required anywhere in the field?

At 50 entrants trading near 6× SPY's own volatility, or 500 entrants near 4×, a +100% winner is the
expected maximum, not a surprise. This says nothing about any specific contest or person — it's a
statement about what big competitions produce by construction.
