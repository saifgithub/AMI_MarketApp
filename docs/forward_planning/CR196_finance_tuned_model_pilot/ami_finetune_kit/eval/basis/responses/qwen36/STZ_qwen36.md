### 1. Financial Health
The balance sheet is heavily leveraged but manageable. Total debt stands at $11.20B (OpenBB annual) or $10.53B (yfinance TTM summary), against $8.08B in equity. This results in a Debt/Equity ratio of ~1.39x (annual) or 123% (TTM summary), which is elevated for a consumer staples name but not insolvent. Liquidity is tight: the current ratio is 0.91 and quick ratio 0.25, indicating reliance on cash flow generation rather than liquid assets to meet short-term obligations. However, cash generation is robust: Operating Cash Flow is $2.69B and Free Cash Flow (FCF) is $2.21B (TTM). The company generates more than enough cash to service debt ($10.53B debt vs $2.69B OCF) and fund dividends (~$700M implied by yield) and buybacks ($924M in 2026). The net debt position ($10.47B - $11.10B range) is high, but the interest coverage (EBITDA $3.46B / estimated interest expense) appears sufficient given the low beta (0.40) and stable cash flows.

### 2. Profitability & Quality
This is a high-quality business with exceptional margins. Gross margin is 52.72%, operating margin is 35.93%, and net margin is 20.14%. These figures are top-tier for the beverage industry, reflecting the pricing power of the Corona/Modelo portfolio. Return on Equity (ROE) is 23.69%, and Return on Assets (ROA) is 8.57%, indicating efficient capital deployment despite the leverage. The EBITDA margin of 38.16% further confirms strong operational efficiency. The business model is durable, driven by brand loyalty and distribution moats in the premium beer segment.

### 3. Growth
Growth is currently negative or flat, which is the primary headwind. Revenue declined 3.30% YoY (from $10.21B in 2025 to $9.14B in 2026 per OpenBB data). However, earnings growth is surprisingly strong at 30.70% YoY. This divergence suggests significant margin expansion or non-operating gains, but the revenue decline is concerning. The multi-period trend shows revenue peaked at $10.21B in 2025 and is now contracting. The 2025 net income figure of -$81.40M is an outlier (likely due to one-time charges or restructuring) compared to the $1.69B in 2026 and $1.73B in 2024, making the "earnings growth" metric noisy. The underlying trend is a revenue contraction, not growth.

### 4. Valuation
The stock trades at a Trailing P/E of 12.52x and Forward P/E of 10.62x. These multiples are historically low for a company with 20%+ net margins and a dominant market position. The EV/EBITDA of 9.58x is also cheap relative to peers. The PEG ratio of 1.66 suggests the market is pricing in low or negative growth, which aligns with the -3.3% revenue decline. The valuation implies that investors expect the revenue decline to persist or margins to compress. Given the high quality of the business and the low multiples, the market is likely over-penalizing the short-term revenue softness.

### 5. Data Quality
There is a material discrepancy between yfinance and OpenBB regarding balance sheet items.
*   **Total Debt:** yfinance reports $10.53B, OpenBB reports $11.20B.
*   **Total Cash:** yfinance reports $96.6M, OpenBB reports $102.4M.
The difference in debt is ~$670M. I trust the OpenBB annual balance sheet figures ($11.20B debt) as they are derived from the filed statement for the period ending 2026-02-28, which is more granular and audited than the TTM summary from yfinance. The discrepancy does not change the fundamental leverage assessment (both are high), but it affects net debt calculations. The revenue and income statement figures are consistent across the 4-period trend, though the 2025 net loss is an anomaly that requires scrutiny.

### 6. Red Flags / Watch Items
*   **Revenue Decline:** The -3.3% YoY revenue drop is the biggest red flag. Is this a cyclical dip or a structural shift in consumer demand for premium beer?
*   **2025 Net Loss:** The -$81.40M net income in 2025 is a significant outlier. It distorts the "earnings growth" metric. The core business profitability is better reflected in the $1.69B (2026) and $1.73B (2024) figures.
*   **Liquidity Risk:** A quick ratio of 0.25 is very low. While FCF covers it, any disruption in cash flow could create liquidity stress.
*   **High Leverage:** Net debt of ~$11B is substantial. Rising interest rates could pressure margins if debt is floating rate.

### 7. Bottom Line
Constellation Brands is a high-quality, high-margin business trading at a cheap valuation, but it is facing a revenue contraction. The balance sheet is leveraged but supported by strong cash flow generation. The low P/E and EV/EBITDA multiples suggest the market has priced in a worst-case scenario for growth. The key question is whether the revenue decline is temporary or permanent. Given the brand strength of Corona/Modelo, a temporary dip seems more likely, making the current valuation attractive for long-term investors.
