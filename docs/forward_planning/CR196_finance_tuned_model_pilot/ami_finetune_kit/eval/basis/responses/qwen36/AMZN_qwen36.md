### 1. Financial Health
The balance sheet is robust but shows a structural shift in capital intensity. Leverage is manageable: the annual Debt/Equity ratio is 0.37x (37.2%), and Net Debt is a modest $66.18B against $411B in equity. However, there is a significant data discrepancy regarding liquidity. Yfinance reports Total Debt at $251.6B and Cash at $123.0B, while OpenBB (and the balance sheet) reports Debt at $153.0B and Cash at $86.8B. The $100B+ variance in both debt and cash suggests Yfinance may be including long-term lease liabilities or other off-balance-sheet obligations in "Total Debt," whereas OpenBB likely reflects reported interest-bearing debt. I trust the OpenBB/Balance Sheet figures for core solvency analysis as they align with the reported equity and asset base. Liquidity is tight: the Current Ratio is 1.03 and Quick Ratio is 0.84, indicating Amazon operates with minimal working capital buffers, relying on its massive operating cash flow ($161.4B TTM) to service short-term obligations.

### 2. Profitability & Quality
Amazon has transitioned into a high-quality, high-margin business. The Operating Margin has expanded dramatically from 2.38% in 2022 to 13.69% TTM, driven by cost discipline and the high-margin growth of AWS and Advertising. The Net Margin of 17.44% and Return on Equity (ROE) of 30.56% are exceptional for a company of this scale, indicating strong pricing power and operational efficiency. The Gross Margin of 50.77% is stable, suggesting that while revenue growth is strong, the underlying unit economics of the retail business are not deteriorating. The EBITDA margin of 21.78% further confirms that cash generation potential is high, despite heavy capital expenditures.

### 3. Growth
The top-line growth is healthy and accelerating: Revenue grew 19.60% YoY, moving from $513.98B (2022) to $716.92B (TTM/2025 projection). More importantly, earnings growth is explosive: Net Income surged from a loss of $2.72B in 2022 to $77.67B in the current period, a 242.30% YoY increase. This is not just top-line growth; it is operating leverage in action. The compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of revenue from 2022 to 2025 is approximately 12.5%, while earnings CAGR is significantly higher, reflecting the scaling of high-margin segments (AWS, Ads) and margin expansion.

### 4. Valuation
At a Trailing P/E of 21.29x and Forward P/E of 25.43x, Amazon is trading at a premium to its historical average but reasonable relative to its growth profile. The PEG ratio of 1.40 suggests the stock is fairly valued to slightly expensive given the >20% earnings growth rate. The EV/EBITDA of 17.33x is attractive for a tech conglomerate with double-digit growth and high ROE. The Price/Sales of 3.68x is elevated but justified by the shift from low-margin retail to high-margin services. The market is pricing in continued margin expansion and AWS dominance, not just revenue growth.

### 5. Data Quality
There is a material disagreement between Yfinance and OpenBB regarding Total Debt and Cash.
*   **Disagreement:** Yfinance Total Debt: $251.6B vs. OpenBB: $153.0B. Yfinance Cash: $123.0B vs. OpenBB: $86.8B.
*   **Resolution:** I trust the OpenBB figures derived from the filed Balance Sheet. The Yfinance "Total Debt" likely includes capital lease obligations or other non-interest-bearing liabilities that are not typically classified as "Debt" in fundamental solvency ratios. The Balance Sheet explicitly lists Total Debt as $152.99B for 2025-12-31, which matches OpenBB. The Cash figure also aligns with the Balance Sheet ($86.81B). The discrepancy does not affect the core profitability or growth analysis, but it distorts leverage metrics if Yfinance is used.

### 6. Red Flags / Watch Items
*   **Capital Expenditure Surge:** CapEx jumped from $52.73B (2023) to $83.00B (2024) and is projected at $131.82B for 2025. This is a massive increase, likely driven by AI infrastructure build-out for AWS. This will pressure Free Cash Flow (FCF), which dropped from $32.88B to $7.70B in the annual data. While Operating Cash Flow is strong, the heavy investment cycle is a key watch item.
*   **Liquidity Tightness:** A Current Ratio of 1.03 is dangerously low for a company of this size. Any disruption in operating cash flow could create liquidity stress, though the massive OCF buffer mitigates this risk.
*   **Valuation Sensitivity:** The stock is priced for perfection. Any slowdown in AWS growth or margin compression in retail could lead to multiple contraction.

### 7. Bottom Line
Amazon is a high-quality, cash-generative machine with expanding margins and dominant market positions in e-commerce, cloud computing, and digital advertising. The financials show a clear inflection point where operating leverage is driving earnings growth far faster than revenue growth. However, the balance sheet is being leveraged to fund a massive capital expenditure cycle, which is currently suppressing free cash flow. The valuation is fair given the growth trajectory, but the tight liquidity and high CapEx introduce execution risk.
