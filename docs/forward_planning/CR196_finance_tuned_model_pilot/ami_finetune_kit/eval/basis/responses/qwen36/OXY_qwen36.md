### 1. Financial Health
The balance sheet is structurally sound but carries significant leverage that is actively being reduced. Total debt stands at $23.35B (OpenBB annual) against $27.75B in equity, resulting in a Debt/Equity ratio of 0.84x. While this is elevated, the trend is positive: debt has increased from $20.77B in 2022 to $23.35B in 2025, but free cash flow generation ($4.11B in 2025) is robust enough to service this. Liquidity is adequate with a current ratio of 1.41 and quick ratio of 1.04. The critical data point here is the discrepancy in debt/cash figures between sources (see Section 5); however, even using the conservative OpenBB balance sheet figures, the company generates $10.98B in operating cash flow, providing ample coverage for interest and capex.

### 2. Profitability & Quality
Margins are exceptionally high, indicating a high-quality, asset-heavy business with significant pricing power or cost advantages in its core operations.
*   **Gross Margin:** 73.32%
*   **Operating Margin:** 45.44%
*   **Net Margin:** 30.32%
*   **EBITDA Margin:** 57.34%

These margins are well above historical averages for E&P peers, suggesting the current cycle is highly profitable or that OXY’s specific asset mix (e.g., Permian Basin efficiency) is outperforming. Return on Equity (10.63%) and Return on Assets (4.68%) are healthy, though ROE is suppressed by the high debt load. The durability of these margins depends on oil prices remaining above the breakeven point for their full-cycle cost structure, which appears to be low given the FCF generation.

### 3. Growth
The growth metrics are distorted by base effects and accounting anomalies.
*   **Revenue:** TTM revenue is $23.93B, but the annualized trend shows a decline from $36.63B (2022) to $21.59B (2025). This suggests the "53.40% YoY growth" figure is likely a trailing-quarter anomaly or a comparison against a depressed prior-year quarter, not a structural revenue expansion.
*   **Earnings:** Reported earnings growth is 964.90% YoY. This is mathematically driven by a massive drop in net income from $13.30B (2022) to $2.33B (2025). The 2022 peak was likely inflated by one-time gains or exceptional commodity prices. The 2025 net income of $2.33B is actually *lower* than 2023 ($4.70B) and 2024 ($3.06B). Therefore, the "growth" is a recovery from a non-recurring peak, not organic expansion. The business is not growing top-line; it is stabilizing earnings after a volatile period.

### 4. Valuation
At a Trailing P/E of 17.81x and Forward P/E of 15.20x, OXY is trading at a moderate premium to deep-value energy peers but at a discount to its own historical averages given the margin quality.
*   **EV/EBITDA:** 5.77x. This is the most relevant multiple for E&P. It implies the market expects modest growth or stable cash flows, not a re-rating to growth-stock multiples.
*   **PEG Ratio:** 0.83x. This suggests the stock is undervalued relative to its earnings growth, but as noted, the "growth" is mean-reverting, not structural.
*   **Price/Book:** 1.80x. This is reasonable for an E&P company with strong FCF, implying the market is willing to pay for the quality of the balance sheet and asset base.

### 5. Data Quality
**Critical Discrepancy:** There is a material disagreement between yfinance and OpenBB regarding the balance sheet.
*   **Total Debt:** yfinance reports $14.63B; OpenBB reports $23.35B.
*   **Total Cash:** yfinance reports $4.15B; OpenBB reports $1.97B.

**Trust Decision:** I trust **OpenBB**. The OpenBB figures align with the "Derived from Filed Balance Sheet" section (Debt/Equity 0.84x, Net Debt $21.38B). The yfinance figures appear to be netting debt or using a different definition (perhaps excluding lease liabilities or specific debt instruments) that is inconsistent with the standard "Total Debt" line item used in the summary ratios. The OpenBB balance sheet is internally consistent with the derived ratios. The discrepancy creates a false impression of liquidity if one uses yfinance data.

### 6. Red Flags / Watch Items
1.  **Leverage Level:** With $23.35B in debt and only $1.97B in cash, the company is highly leveraged. Any sustained drop in oil prices below $60-70/bbl could strain debt service capabilities, despite current FCF.
2.  **Earnings Volatility:** The swing from $13.30B net income (2022) to $2.33B (2025) highlights the cyclical nature of the business. Investors must not mistake 2022's peak for a new baseline.
3.  **Carbon Capture Investment:** While not explicitly quantified in the P&L, OXY's significant investment in carbon capture (via Carbon Engineering) is a long-term bet that may not contribute to near-term FCF, potentially dragging on returns if commercialization lags.
4.  **Dividend Sustainability:** The dividend yield is 1.87%. With $3.79B in FCF and a market cap of $60B, the payout ratio is manageable, but any cut in FCF would threaten the dividend.

### 7. Bottom Line
Occidental Petroleum is a high-quality, cash-generative E&P company trading at a reasonable valuation (EV/EBITDA 5.77x) with strong margins (45% operating margin). However, the balance sheet is heavily leveraged ($23.35B debt), and the reported "964% earnings growth" is a statistical artifact of a 2022 peak, not organic expansion. The business is stable and profitable, but the high leverage and cyclical exposure limit the upside unless oil prices remain elevated. The data discrepancy in debt/cash is significant but resolvable by trusting the audited balance sheet figures.
