### 1. Financial Health
The balance sheet is structurally typical for a capital-intensive regulated utility but shows signs of aggressive expansion. Leverage is elevated, with a Debt/Equity ratio of 1.48x (annual) and 157.85% (MRQ), indicating that for every dollar of equity, there is roughly $1.50 in debt. This is high for a utility but manageable given the regulated nature of the cash flows. Liquidity is tight; the current ratio of 0.53 and quick ratio of 0.26 suggest the company relies heavily on short-term borrowing or operating cash flow to meet immediate obligations, a common trait in utilities where working capital is minimized. Cash generation is strong at the operating level ($3.25B OCF), but Free Cash Flow (FCF) is deeply negative (-$1.79B TTM in summary, -$821M in 2025 annualized view) due to massive capital expenditures ($4.17B). The business is "sound" in terms of solvency risk (low beta, regulated revenue), but the balance sheet is stretched to fund growth.

### 2. Profitability & Quality
The business exhibits high-quality, durable profitability characteristics. Gross margins of 52.41% and operating margins of 25.24% are robust for a utility, reflecting the pricing power inherent in regulated electric and gas distribution. Net margin of 18.58% and ROE of 11.94% are healthy. The EBITDA margin of 46.49% (OpenBB) further confirms strong cash conversion before capex. The durability is anchored in the regulatory compact: costs are recovered through rate cases, providing predictable returns. The high ROE relative to the sector average suggests efficient capital allocation or favorable regulatory treatment of the rate base.

### 3. Growth
Revenue growth is volatile and currently contracting. TTM revenue is $8.41B, but the annualized data shows a decline from $8.80B (2025) to $7.62B (2024), a -6.20% YoY drop. However, earnings are growing: EPS increased from $4 to $5 (25% growth) and TTM earnings growth is cited at 11.90%. This divergence indicates that the company is growing earnings through margin expansion and cost control rather than top-line volume growth. The capex trend ($3.38B in 2022 to $4.17B in 2025) suggests a significant investment cycle is underway, likely for grid modernization or renewable integration, which will suppress FCF in the near term but potentially drive future rate base growth.

### 4. Valuation
The stock trades at a premium. A P/E of 19.25x and P/B of 2.21x are above the historical average for regulated utilities (typically 15-18x P/E and 1.2-1.5x P/B). The PEG ratio of 2.91 suggests the market is pricing in significant future growth that is not currently visible in the flat/negative revenue trend. The EV/EBITDA of 13.37x is also elevated. The market is paying for the *future* rate base growth funded by the current negative FCF. If the regulatory environment fails to approve the necessary rate increases to support this capex, the valuation is unjustified.

### 5. Data Quality
There is a material discrepancy in the debt and cash figures between sources.
*   **Disagreement:** yfinance reports Total Debt as $21.81B and Cash as $12.00M. OpenBB reports Total Debt as $19.83B and Cash as $13.00M.
*   **Analysis:** The "Derived from Filed Balance Sheet" section explicitly states the annual debt is $19.83B. The summary section lists "Total debt $21.81B" under Leverage & Liquidity. The difference is ~$2B. Given that the "Derived" section is calculated directly from the provided balance sheet figures ($19.83B debt vs $13.40B equity = 1.48x), I trust the **OpenBB/Annual Balance Sheet figures ($19.83B debt)** as the more accurate reflection of the reported financials. The $21.81B figure in the summary likely includes additional liabilities or is a data error. I will proceed with the $19.83B debt figure for analysis.

### 6. Red Flags / Watch Items
*   **Negative Free Cash Flow:** The company is burning $1.79B in FCF annually. This is sustainable only if the regulatory agencies approve rate hikes to cover the cost of capital. Any delay in rate recovery is a major risk.
*   **Revenue Decline:** The -6.20% YoY revenue drop is concerning. It suggests volume loss, lower energy prices, or one-time items. The earnings growth is decoupling from revenue, which is a margin-of-safety concern if margins compress.
*   **High Leverage:** 1.48x Debt/Equity is high. In a rising interest rate environment, refinancing this debt becomes expensive, squeezing net income.

### 7. Bottom Line
Ameren Corporation is a high-quality regulated utility with strong margins and a clear path to earnings growth through margin expansion, despite a recent revenue contraction. The balance sheet is stretched to fund a significant capital investment cycle, resulting in negative free cash flow. The current valuation is rich, pricing in successful regulatory recovery of these costs. The investment thesis hinges entirely on the regulatory environment allowing the company to earn its cost of capital on the growing rate base.
