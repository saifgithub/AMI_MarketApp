### 1. Financial Health
The balance sheet is structurally leveraged but operationally robust. Total debt stands at $6.42B (annual) or $5.69B (TTM summary), while cash is $5.41B (annual) or $7.13B (TTM summary). There is a material discrepancy in the reported cash/debt levels between sources (see Data Quality), but even using the more conservative annual figures, the company is effectively net debt neutral ($1.00B net debt). The Current Ratio of 0.80 and Quick Ratio of 0.64 indicate tight short-term liquidity, which is typical for travel platforms that collect cash from customers before paying suppliers (float). However, the Debt/Equity ratio is extreme: 5.00x (annual) or 230% (TTM summary), driven by aggressive share buybacks that have reduced equity to $1.28B. The balance sheet is sound in terms of solvency (cash covers debt), but fragile in terms of equity cushion.

### 2. Profitability & Quality
This is a high-quality, asset-light business model. Gross margins are exceptionally high at 90.43%, indicating the company acts as a marketplace/agent rather than holding inventory. Operating margins of 18.98% and Net margins of 12.97% demonstrate strong operating leverage. Return on Equity (ROE) is 89.49%, which is artificially inflated by the low equity base due to buybacks, not necessarily superior operational efficiency. Return on Assets (ROA) of 6.11% is more representative of the underlying asset productivity. The business is durable; travel demand is sticky, and the network effects of Expedia, Hotels.com, and Vrbo create significant switching costs for suppliers and consumers.

### 3. Growth
Growth is accelerating significantly. Revenue grew 14.00% YoY, but Earnings growth exploded by 188.70% YoY and 166.10% QoQ. This divergence suggests operating leverage is kicking in: costs are growing slower than revenue. The Income Statement confirms this: Net Income jumped from $797M (2023) to $1.23B (2024) to $1.29B (2025 TTM projection/actual depending on period alignment). The trend is clearly upward, with revenue CAGR from 2022-2024 being ~10.5%, but the recent acceleration in profitability is the key driver.

### 4. Valuation
The stock trades at a Trailing P/E of 20.87x and a Forward P/E of 13.76x. The massive gap between trailing and forward multiples reflects the recent surge in earnings. A PEG ratio of 1.03 suggests the stock is fairly valued relative to its growth rate. EV/EBITDA of 13.29x is reasonable for a tech-enabled consumer platform. The Price/Sales of 2.54x is premium but justified given the 90% gross margins. The market is pricing in continued high growth, but the forward multiple implies expectations are not excessively frothy.

### 5. Data Quality
There is a **critical disagreement** between yfinance and OpenBB regarding the balance sheet.
*   **Total Debt:** yfinance reports $5.69B; OpenBB (and the annual balance sheet) reports $6.42B.
*   **Total Cash:** yfinance reports $7.13B; OpenBB (and the annual balance sheet) reports $5.41B.
*   **Analysis:** The OpenBB annual balance sheet figures ($6.42B debt, $5.41B cash) are internally consistent with the "Derived from Filed Balance Sheet" section (Debt/Equity 5.00x, Net Debt $1.00B). The yfinance figures appear to be TTM or MRQ estimates that may include short-term borrowings or exclude certain long-term instruments, or vice versa.
*   **Trust:** I trust the **OpenBB annual balance sheet** and the derived metrics because they are self-consistent (Net Debt = Debt - Cash matches the derived $1.00B). The yfinance data likely reflects a different definition of "debt" (e.g., excluding capital leases) or "cash" (e.g., excluding restricted cash). The discrepancy is large enough ($1.7B) to alter the net debt position significantly, but given the company's strong cash flow, the solvency risk remains low regardless.

### 6. Red Flags / Watch Items
*   **Leverage via Buybacks:** Equity has shrunk from $2.28B (2022) to $1.28B (2025). The company is using its balance sheet to buy back shares aggressively. If cash flows stall, the low equity cushion becomes a risk.
*   **Liquidity Ratios:** Current/Quick ratios below 1.0 are a red flag for general corporations but standard for travel intermediaries. However, it requires constant monitoring of working capital cycles.
*   **Concentration Risk:** The business is heavily reliant on discretionary travel spending. A macroeconomic recession could hit revenue hard.
*   **Data Discrepancy:** As noted, the cash/debt figures are unreliable across sources. Investors must verify the exact capital structure from the latest 10-K/10-Q.

### 7. Bottom Line
Expedia Group is a high-margin, asset-light travel platform experiencing a significant inflection point in profitability, with earnings growing nearly 190% YoY. The balance sheet is leveraged due to aggressive share buybacks, resulting in a thin equity cushion, but strong free cash flow generation ($3.43B TTM) supports the debt load. Valuation is fair relative to growth (PEG ~1.0), and the forward multiple suggests the market has not yet fully priced in the sustained earnings power. The primary risk is a macroeconomic downturn impacting travel demand and the reliance on continued cash flow generation to service debt and fund buybacks.
