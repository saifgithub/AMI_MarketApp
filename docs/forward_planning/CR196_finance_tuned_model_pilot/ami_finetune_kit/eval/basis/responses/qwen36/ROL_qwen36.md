### 1. Financial Health
The balance sheet is leveraged but manageable, with a clear trajectory of increasing debt. Total debt rose from $336M in 2022 to $1.04B in 2025 (OpenBB annual), a 209% increase, while equity remained relatively flat ($1.27B to $1.37B). This has pushed the Debt/Equity ratio to 0.76x (annual) and 78% (TTQ), indicating significant financial engineering or acquisition funding. Liquidity is tight: the current ratio is 0.63 and quick ratio 0.42, meaning the company cannot cover short-term liabilities with liquid assets without relying on operating cash flow. However, cash generation is robust. Free Cash Flow (FCF) grew from $435M in 2022 to $650M in 2025 (OpenBB cash flow statement), a 49% increase. The company generates more than enough cash to service debt ($1.12B TTM debt vs $647M OCF), but the reliance on continuous cash flow to service a growing debt load reduces the margin of safety.

### 2. Profitability & Quality
Rollins demonstrates high-quality unit economics. Gross margins are stable at ~52%, and operating margins expanded from 18.3% in 2022 to 19.3% in 2025 (derived from OpenBB income statement: $726M Op Inc / $3.76B Rev). Return on Equity (ROE) is exceptional at 37.01%, driven by high leverage and efficient capital allocation. Net margin is 13.55%. The business model is durable: pest control is a recurring revenue model with high switching costs and low elasticity, protected by brand (Orkin) and franchisee network. The high ROE and stable margins suggest a wide economic moat, though the leverage amplifies the risk to this quality.

### 3. Growth
Growth is decelerating. Revenue growth slowed from 13.3% (2022-2023) to 10.5% (2023-2024) to 10.9% (2024-2025) based on OpenBB annuals, but the TTM summary shows 7.9% YoY growth. More critically, earnings growth has stalled: Net Income grew from $368M to $434M (2022-2023, +18%), then to $466M (2023-2024, +7%), and finally to $526M (2024-2025, +13%). However, the TTM earnings growth is listed as only 3.10%, and EPS is flat at $1.00 across all four periods in the OpenBB data. This discrepancy suggests either a massive share count increase (dilution) or a data error. Given the repurchases in 2023 ($315M) and 2025 ($216M), share count should be down, making flat EPS suspicious. If EPS is truly flat while Net Income rises, the share count must have increased significantly, which contradicts the buyback data. This points to a data inconsistency that obscures true per-share growth.

### 4. Valuation
The stock is expensive. Trailing P/E is 33.2x, Forward P/E is 28.0x, and EV/EBITDA is 21.4x. For a company with ~8% revenue growth and flat earnings, these multiples imply expectations of sustained double-digit earnings growth and margin expansion. The PEG ratio of 3.11 confirms the stock is priced for perfection. At a 37% ROE, the company deserves a premium, but 33x earnings is historically high for a service business unless growth is accelerating. The recent -36% price decline has brought it closer to value, but it is still trading at a significant premium to its historical averages (implied by the high multiples).

### 5. Data Quality
There are material discrepancies between yfinance and OpenBB.
1.  **Total Debt:** yfinance reports $1.12B, OpenBB Balance Sheet reports $1.04B (2025 annual) and $812M (2024). The "Derived from Filed Balance Sheet" section shows $1.04B. I trust the OpenBB annual balance sheet figure ($1.04B) as it is derived from the formal statement, whereas yfinance may include off-balance sheet items or use a different cut-off.
2.  **Total Cash:** yfinance reports $109M, OpenBB reports $100M. The difference is immaterial ($9M) relative to the $1B debt.
3.  **EPS Consistency:** The OpenBB income statement shows Net Income rising from $368M to $526M, but Diluted EPS remains exactly $1.00 for all four years. This is mathematically impossible unless the share count increased by ~43% over 3 years, which directly contradicts the cash flow statement showing net buybacks (negative repurchase of capital stock) in 2023 and 2025. This is a critical internal inconsistency. I will assume the Net Income figures are correct and the EPS figure is a data error or placeholder, as buybacks are explicitly reported.

### 6. Red Flags / Watch Items
*   **Leverage Spike:** Debt nearly tripled from 2022 to 2025. While cash flow covers it, the rising leverage increases sensitivity to interest rate hikes or economic downturns.
*   **EPS Data Error:** The flat EPS despite rising net income is a major red flag for data integrity. It prevents accurate assessment of per-share value creation.
*   **Liquidity Crunch:** Current ratio of 0.63 is low for a stable business, indicating tight working capital management or aggressive use of cash.
*   **Growth Deceleration:** Revenue growth is slowing, and earnings growth is volatile.

### 7. Bottom Line
Rollins is a high-quality, high-ROE business with a durable moat, but it is currently overvalued and increasingly leveraged. The data contains significant inconsistencies regarding EPS and debt levels, making precise valuation difficult. The recent price decline has improved the entry point, but at 33x earnings, the stock still prices in strong growth that is not materializing. The leverage is manageable but rising, and the liquidity position is tight.
