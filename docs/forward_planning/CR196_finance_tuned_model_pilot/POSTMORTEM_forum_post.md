# LoRA fine-tune for financial-statement reasoning moved nothing. What did we do wrong?

We spent 42 GPU-hours on two LoRA runs trying to make a model better at fundamental analysis.
Held-out evaluation came back at parity with the untrained base on every capability we targeted.
Looking for a sanity check on the approach — details below, questions at the bottom.

## What we were training for

We run a 10-agent analyst "Room" for a trading-education product (simulation only). The agent that
matters most reads a fundamentals brief and writes an analyst report. We wanted it to be better at
the things a careful analyst does and a general model doesn't:

- **Basis discipline** — don't compare an annual figure to a quarterly one; don't compare gross debt
  to net debt; when a brief is ambiguous about basis, say so
- **Don't invent conflicts** — two sources differing *by definition* (cash vs cash+equivalents) is not
  a data conflict, and calling it one is a specific failure we can detect
- **Ratio and trend reasoning** grounded in the actual statements rather than plausible-sounding prose
- **Earnings-quality flags** — accruals vs cash flow, receivables outrunning revenue
- **As-of-date discipline** — don't use knowledge from after the brief's date
- **Refuse** when the brief genuinely doesn't contain the answer

These are all programmatically checkable, which is why we picked them.

## What we did

**Base:** `fastino/Fastino-Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-Finance` (31.6B hybrid Mamba-MoE). Note this is
*already* a finance fine-tune — it beat a general 35B model 43/44 vs 21/43 on our basis rubric, which
is why we chose it as the base.

**Data — 20,021 rows:**

| Source | Share | What |
|---|---|---|
| Our generators | ~51% | Briefs built from real filings/market data where **the label is computed, not judged** — basis class, correct ratio, trend direction, whether a refusal is warranted |
| Public finance SFT | ~41% | FinQA, TAT-QA, financial-RLVR, Finance-Instruct (sampled) |
| Chat replay | ~8% | UltraChat slice, anti-forgetting |

Output formatting was one recipe of sixteen — 8% of the mix, not the point.

**Training:** LoRA r=32, α=64, lr 1e-4, cosine, 2 epochs, bs=1 × grad_accum 16, bf16, seq len 4096.
Targets: attention q/k/v/o + Mamba `in_proj` + shared-expert up/down (93 modules, 22.7M trainable
params). Single DGX Spark GB10 (121GB unified memory). ~17h for the successful run.

**Evaluation:** 468 held-out prompts across 8 production surfaces, scored by the *same programmatic
checks that generated the training labels*. Held-out tickers are disjoint from training tickers, and
we assert no eval prompt appears verbatim in the training file.

## What we wanted to get

Movement on the capabilities above — measurably fewer basis errors, fewer invented conflicts, more
correct refusals. We'd have accepted a few points on any of them.

## What we actually got

| Capability targeted | Base | Fine-tuned |
|---|---|---|
| Analyst report correctness | 43/44 | 43/44 |
| False conflicts invented *(lower is better)* | **0/44** | **2/44** |
| Correct refusals | 45% | 50% (CIs overlap) |

Parity on report quality, slightly *worse* on invented conflicts, refusals inside the noise.

The only thing that moved was output formatting — the 8%:

| Formatting check | Base | Fine-tuned |
|---|---|---|
| Size-ladder compliance | 57% | 100% |
| Trader block parses | 26% | 100% |

And when we finally ran the control, constrained decoding (a JSON schema enforced at decode time)
got the **untrained** model to the same 100% in about 50 minutes, n=69 and n=68. So the one gain
didn't need training either.

Two things we got wrong in our own process, in case they invalidate the result for you:

- **37% of our first eval set was verbatim training data** (three surfaces were generated by calling
  the training-data generators). We caught it and rebuilt those surfaces; all numbers above are from
  the clean set.
- We found **six defects in our scoring harness**, in both directions. All fixed before these numbers.

## What we're asking

1. **Is this expected?** Should ~10k programmatically-labelled analytical examples move this class of
   capability at all with SFT, or is that naive? Everything we taught is verifiable, but we taught it
   as next-token prediction over a correct answer, which may just be the wrong lever.
2. **Is it the base?** Stacking LoRA on a model that's already fine-tuned for finance may leave little
   headroom, or may fight the existing adaptation. Should we be training from the vanilla instruct
   model instead and accepting we lose its finance skills?
3. **Is it capacity?** r=32 / 22.7M trainable on a 31.6B MoE, with the routed experts frozen (we only
   touch attention + Mamba in_proj + shared experts). Too small a surface to move reasoning?
4. **Is RL the honest answer?** Every label we have is verifiable, which is the RLVR/GRPO setup.
   Is SFT simply the wrong tool for "don't make this class of error", and should we not have expected
   it to work?
5. **Should we do anything at all?** Genuine question. The formatting win is free via grammars. The
   analytical gap is real but the base is already decent. Is there a version of this worth another
   run, or is the correct answer to stop and spend the effort on retrieval/tooling instead?

Happy to share the eval harness, scorers, or per-surface breakdowns if useful.
