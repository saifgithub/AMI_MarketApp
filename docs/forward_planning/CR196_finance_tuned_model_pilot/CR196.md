## §2 Training data plan

Decontamination rule: never train on the 45-ticker `basis_ground_truth` set, the captured AAPL case,
FinanceBench, or any benchmark's dev/test split. Train tickers ∩ eval tickers = ∅, frozen in a committed file.

**Tier A — generated from our own feeds; labels computed, not judged.** The Yahoo feed (yfinance) is the
backbone: statements, `.info`, price history are enough to *generate* grounded briefs with programmatic
labels. (License posture: the raw feed is never redistributed — the dataset stays private and weights carry
derived analytical text; same posture as production's existing yfinance use.) Generator recipes:

1. **Basis-mismatch fixtures** — extend the `basis_fixture.py` pattern (copied into our workspace) to
   ~300–500 train tickers; annual-vs-quarter, definitional (cash vs cash+STI, gross vs net debt), genuine
   conflicts — with target answers that *do* reach L2/L3.
2. **Ratio computation + interpretation** — exact numeric ground truth from statements.
3. **Trend reasoning** — computed deltas/CAGRs as the verifiable spine.
4. **Earnings-quality flags** — accruals vs cash flow, receivables outrunning revenue, inventory builds.
5. **Basis-trap generalization** — TTM vs FY, diluted vs basic EPS, rendered as deliberately ambiguous briefs.
6. **As-of-date discipline** — briefs "as of X" from data available then; refusing post-date knowledge is
   the correct behaviour (targets lookahead contamination, `01_prediction_edge_and_caution.md`).
7. **Mandate-overlay compliance** — briefs × mandate overlays via the salvaged CR032 recipes
   (`original_silent_scout_research/02_data/recipes/`), regenerated against current schemas, overlay
   user-side; labels from the mandate rules.
8. **Structured-output formatting** — our exact Room JSON verdict shapes; trains in what CR143 documented
   as reformatter/tolerant-parser scaffolding.
9. **Refusal/abstention** — missing/invalid-data briefs where "cannot be determined" is correct.
10. **Real prompt distribution** — melehost `llm_audit` prompts as inputs; targets best-of-n from open
    teachers (DeepSeek-R1 / self-distilled Fastino), filtered by the programmatic verifiers. **NOT Claude**
    (Anthropic's terms bar training competing models on Claude outputs — the Silent_Scout "Opus
    distillation ladder" is retired for this purpose).
11. CR164 historical sheet / outcome arm → **eval only**.

Primary-source additions: **SEC EDGAR/XBRL** (public domain; the SEC-Num skill Fastino's mix prioritized)
and earnings-call transcripts (`kurry/sp500_earnings_transcripts`). Recipes 1–7 double as an **RLVR
environment** (verifiable rewards) if a GRPO stage is ever warranted.

**Tier B — public SFT:** FinQA/TAT-QA/ConvFinQA train splits (verify licenses; double as replay protecting
the Fastino skills), Finance-Instruct-500k (Apache-2.0) slice, Fin-R1-Data (gated — verify access/license/
CN-EN split first), financial-rlvr-10k-enterprise (MIT), UltraChat-200k replay slice. Target ~30–60k total,
cutoff 8192, chat-JSONL per `02_data/samples/sample_shape.jsonl`. License gate per the Silent_Scout
checklist (CC-BY-NC ⇒ not for distributed weights; untagged ⇒ skip).

**Mixture/feature optimization (Saiful's pointer): SA-FDR** — simulated annealing over feature subsets
with the Fisher discriminant ratio as a cheap quality proxy (arXiv 2507.23568; finance sibling 2303.02223).
(a) recipe-level: select the *discriminative* brief features against programmatic labels (closed-form, no
training in the loop); (b) mix-level: SA over mixture weights with an embedding-FDR or short-probe
objective, capped at ≤10 probes — replaces the 45/45/10 starting guess with a measured mix.

**How Fastino was trained (card + blog, read 2026-08-19):** LoRA rank 32, lr 1e-4, 2 epochs, packing
disabled, 13,698 dedup examples prioritizing SEC-Num + BizFinBench with FinQA/TAT-QA/FinEntity retained;

## §2c Run-2 regime — production-brief SNR (Saiful, 2026-08-19)

Saiful: *"if we look at the data we are sending to the LLM, would it make sense to add these data
in some kind of training regime, hopefully increasing the signal-to-noise ratio."* Yes — three
mechanisms, deliberately sequenced into run 2 (run 1 ships with the skill-recipes as built):

1. **Exact-format rendering** — regenerate recipes through the real production line-builders
   (`app/services/fundamentals.py` via `room_prompts.py` composition) instead of approximated
   briefs, so the tuned model has seen the serving format thousands of times.
2. **Recipe 10 executed** — real `llm_audit` prompts as inputs (true ticker/mandate/phase
   distribution). Logged responses are NEVER targets — that would imitate the measured failure
   mode (17/43 false conflicts); targets are regenerated best-of-n and pass the programmatic
   verifiers. Concierge free-text excluded entirely; Room briefs only (derived market data +
   mandate parameters). Real-user-text-in-weights is acknowledged and gated on Saiful.
3. **Prompt compression, measured** — after SFT, A/B contract-holding with a slimmed system
   prompt (the explainers the weights now carry). Shorten production prompts only on evidence
   (CR143 discipline); payoff is tokens/call and prefix-cache economics (CR077: Room gets 0%
   benefit today).

SA-FDR gains a consumer here: with real prompts + verifier labels, measure which sheet fields
discriminate correct-vs-incorrect verdicts — evidence for what the brief itself should carry.

### §2c.4 Role-behaviour recipes — the gap run 1 leaves open (Saiful, 2026-08-21)

Run 1 trains the **shared analytical substrate** (recipes 1–6): read the statements correctly,
right basis, right period, computed ratios. That is the right target — one model serves all 13
roles in `content/agents/`, so lifting the floor lifts every lane at once.

What it does **not** train is the roles themselves. Audited 2026-08-21: recipe 8 (22 examples,
PM verdict JSON) is the only role-shaped data in the entire 25,695-example build. Nothing trains
`bear_researcher` to attack a thesis, `bull_researcher` to argue one without inventing facts,
`research_manager` to adjudicate between them, or the three debators to hold distinct risk
postures. The Room's buy/sell verdict is produced by that argument structure — so role quality is
what the verdict actually rests on.

**Why this is NOT "train it to predict direction."** One model serves all 13 roles. An
unconditional directional prior would be inherited by bull and bear alike — they would stop truly
disagreeing and the debate would become consensus in costume, scoring well on eval right up until
it is wrong together. Role behaviour is **conditional on the system prompt** (`content/agents/*.md`
selects the role at inference), so it trains as "given you are the bear, attack" without ever
installing an opinion the model carries into every other lane. Conditional = safe; unconditional =
diversity collapse. This distinction is the design constraint for all recipes below.

**How the labels stay verifiable** (no LLM judge — CR038's "prompt instructions are not controls"
applies to graders too): we *generate* the briefs, so we know their ground truth by construction —
the same property that makes recipes 1–6 exact-match checkable, applied to a different output shape.

| # | Role | Generated input | Programmatic label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | `bear_researcher` | brief with a **planted** known weakness (accrual divergence, receivables outrunning revenue, FCF-conversion decay — reuse recipe 4's flag engine) | must name planted flaw X; must not fabricate flaws absent from the brief |
| 13 | `bull_researcher` | brief with **planted** genuine strengths | must name strength Y; **hallucination check** — every claimed fact must appear in the brief (we know all of them) |
| 14 | `research_manager` | a bull case and a bear case, one constructed on data present, the other on data we deliberately withheld | must pick the case we made stronger, and say why |
| ~~15~~ | ~~three `debator`s~~ → **`risk_officer`** | ladder + evidence, one JSON contract (`build_risk_officer_instruction`) | one entry per handed size, no invented size, `recommended` ∈ handed sizes, `key_number` quoted not computed, `confidence` genuinely separating — **superseded by [CR201](../CR201_structured_risk_officer/CR201_structured_risk_officer.md) §9.2** |
| 16 | `trader`, `portfolio_manager` | recipe 7's mandate overlays, extended past PM to trader | mandate-rule compliance (already programmatic via the real `overlay_generator`) |

Recipes 12–16 read the **live** role prompts from `content/agents/*.md`, the same
read-the-real-source discipline recipe 7 uses for `overlay_generator` and recipe 8 for
`_PM_VERDICT_FORMAT` — so drift in a role prompt cannot silently invalidate its training data.

Sequencing: run 2, alongside §2c.1–3. Recipe 8's 22 examples are the pilot for this shape; scale to
~1–1.5k per role lane to match Tier A's per-recipe density. Does **not** touch outcome labels;
CR164's outcome arm stays eval-only.

## §5a Pre-registered acceptance bar (Saiful, 2026-08-21)

Written **while run 1 is still training** (step ~1,170/3,212) and deliberately before any result
exists. A bar chosen after seeing the numbers is not a bar. CR197's own history is the argument:
its pre-registered statistic turned out to be the wrong instrument and returned "not demonstrated"
— that was catchable *because* it had been registered in advance. Registering late would have
turned the same slip into a post-hoc story.

**Saiful's direction, same date: run 1 completes regardless of this section.** Nothing here is a
stop condition for the training job; it governs what happens to the artifact afterwards.

### The competitor is vanilla Fastino, not Qwen3.6

§1's table settles this: on the 45-ticker basis rubric, vanilla Fastino already reaches L1 on 43/44
with **0** false conflicts, against Qwen3.6's 21/43 with **17**. Beating Qwen3.6 would prove only
that Fastino's fine-tune works — which is already known, and is not ours. The question this CR must
answer is whether **our** LoRA added anything on top of Fastino. Qwen3.6 stays in the table as the
production reference, never as the bar.

### The bar

| Dimension | Vanilla Fastino (§1, re-read at Phase 4) | Win requires |
|---|---|---|
| **L2/L3 rate** — reconciles matched periods, names the STI scope, notes the shared upstream vendor | **0/44** | **materially above 0** — the one thing run 1 exists to move |
| L1 rate — raises basis/period at all | 43/44 | **no regression** |
| False "sources disagree" | **0** | **stays 0** — a fabricated conflict is worse than a missed one |
| Vendor-trust language | 35/44 | directional only, not a gate |
| Mandate compliance + refusal behaviour | — | **no regression** — non-negotiable, gates on its own |
| BF16 vs our NVFP4 | — | quantisation must not undo the gain |

A model that gains L2/L3 while inventing conflicts, or while getting looser on mandate rules, is a
**loss**, not a trade-off. Those two rows are vetoes, not weights.

### Large-or-nothing, because n=45 cannot resolve small effects

At n=45 a binary rate carries roughly ±15 points of 95% uncertainty near the middle of the range.
So "L2/L3 moved from 0 to 40%" is a readable result; "31% vs 24%" is not — that sits inside the
noise, and calling it a win would be reading tea leaves. CR197 met this wall from the other side:
effects under ~8–10pp were unresolvable at n=136, and n=45 is far coarser.

**Rule: if the improvement is not large enough to see plainly at this sample size, the verdict is
"not demonstrated" — never "slightly better."** L2/L3 starting from a measured 0/44 is the
favourable case for this: any real movement off zero is visible.

### Known gaps in this bar, recorded rather than papered over

1. **Sample size.** 45 tickers is what the existing rig has. Extending it is cheap (the generators
   already exist and the GPU is busy) and would let us resolve smaller effects. **Not authorised —
   open decision for Saiful.**
2. **No role-behaviour eval.** §2c.4's recipes are not in run 1, so nothing here measures them.
   Run 2 cannot be judged by this bar; it needs the programmatic role eval described in
   [ROLE_TRAINING_PLAN.md](ROLE_TRAINING_PLAN.md) §F, where the generator code doubles as the scorer.
3. **§1's numbers were read mid-run** (2026-08-19 21:42 +03, scoring still active). They are
   re-read at Phase 4 before any comparison is made; the bar above binds to the re-read values, not
   to the snapshot.

### What clearing the bar does and does not authorise

Clearing it authorises the §4 adoption-gate measurements — nothing more. A `:8000` production swap
remains Saiful's explicit call and is not implied by winning the eval. Per
[CR201 §9.3](../CR201_structured_risk_officer/CR201_structured_risk_officer.md), such a swap must
not share a window with `ROOM_RISK_OFFICER_ENABLED` or neither effect is attributable.

### §5a.1 Two additions from CR201's live enablement (2026-08-21)

CR201 enabled `ROOM_RISK_OFFICER_ENABLED` on Alpha the same day §5a was written. Its measurement
history supplies one new gate item and one eval-design requirement, both of which §5a as first
written would have missed.

**1. Decoding must be pinned for the Phase-4 comparison.** CR197 measured **three byte-identical
replays disagreeing on 26 of 132 convenes (19.7%)** — same model, same prompt, different verdict,
because the served path samples. Our own kit is already safe here (`eval/quick_eval.py` and
`train/merge_and_generate.py` both pass `do_sample=False`), but the basis-rubric comparison runs
against a *served* endpoint, where CR197's row records production sending no temperature or top_p
at all — i.e. the provider default, not greedy.

Left unpinned, a single-sample n=45 comparison carries sampling noise **on top of** §5a's ±15pp
binomial bound, and the two are not separable after the fact. **Phase 4 therefore pins
`temperature=0` (or takes n samples per ticker and reports the spread) for every arm, and records
which was used.** An arm run under different decoding than its comparator is not a comparison.

**2. A parse-failure gate the basis rubric cannot see.** Per
[CR201 §9.4(b)](../CR201_structured_risk_officer/CR201_structured_risk_officer.md), DEF352 had a
parser silently eating 42.6%/44.1% of Risk Officer payloads, dropping the Room to the ladder-only
floor — 12.0% approval against an 18.5% baseline — with nothing raised and, in CR201's own words,
*"in a place nobody was looking."*

That failure is model-specific, and **run 1 contains no training on the officer's JSON contract**
(recipe 17 did not exist when the mix was built). So a model that clears every row of §5a's table
could still degrade the live Room the moment it takes the slot.

**Added to the §4 adoption gate: measure `fallback_reason="the reply could not be parsed"` on
`_run_risk_officer` for our model and require it under 1%**, matching the live watch §10 sets.
Neither the basis rubric nor the 3-lens batch exercises this contract, so it cannot be inferred
from them — it needs its own measurement against a served instance of our model.

**A note on §5a's large-or-nothing rule, now with an empirical precedent.** CR201's round 1 showed
a 6.5pp gap that read as a real effect and was a parser bug; round 2, after the fix, landed 9/9
net 0, p=1.000. Its own conclusion — *"Two runs disagreeing in direction is what 'not resolvable'
looks like"* — is the same discipline §5a pre-registers, arrived at independently and the hard way.

| | |
|---|---|

`use_rslora=false`, `use_dora=false`, `modules_to_save=null`.

### The merge

`ami_finetune_kit/train/merge_lora_to_disk.py` — folds the adapter into the base by walking the
14 safetensors shards, peak resident one ~5GB shard. It exists because `merge_and_unload()`
materialises the whole 31.6B model and OOM'd on 121GB during smoke9; the kit's
`merge_and_generate.py` sidesteps that by generating *through* the adapter, which is fine for a
smoke gate but produces no checkpoint to quantize.

**The risk the script is built around is a name mismatch, not memory.** The adapter names its
targets by the loaded module path (`base_model.model.model.layers.N.mixer.X`) while the checkpoint
stores them as `backbone.layers.N.mixer.X`. A partial match would fold some of the training in and
drop the rest — silently, since nothing downstream would raise. So every pair is resolved to a
real tensor and shape-checked against `B@A` before any byte is written, and the run aborts unless
the folded count equals the adapter's pair count.

Adapter accounting came out exact: 93 pairs over 52 layers — 23 Mamba (`in_proj`), 23 MoE
(`shared_experts.up/down_proj`), 6 attention (`q/k/v/o_proj`). Scaling is a flat α/r = 2.0.
Output: `models/ami-finance-r1-bf16`, 62G, 13 of 14 shards modified, ~2.5 minutes.

### Verifying the merge carried the training

Two measurements, because the first one alone cannot distinguish "the merge worked" from "the
merge did almost nothing".

**Next-token logits** (`ami_finetune_kit/eval/verify_merge.py`, 3 fixed prompts, greedy): top-1
token agrees on all three, max logit delta 1.375, generated text diverges after 134–238 characters.
Folding LoRA into bf16 weights rounds differently than applying it as a runtime side path, so the
two are close but never bit-identical — demanding identical text would fail on one coin-flip token.

**Held-out loss** (`ami_finetune_kit/eval/val_loss_arm.py`, 64 examples of `val.jsonl`, identical
masking across arms) is the measurement that settles it:

| Arm | Held-out loss |
|---|---|
| Base, untrained Fastino | 2.2744 |
| Base + adapter applied at runtime | 0.2507 |
| **Merged checkpoint** | **0.2506** |

Training moved loss by 2.02; merged and base+adapter differ by 0.0001. The logit deltas above are
bf16 rounding at that scale, not lost training. **Merge verified.**

(The 0.2506 here and the run's own 0.2384 are not the same measurement — this scores 64 examples
with `labels=input_ids` over the whole sequence, the trainer scored the full split under its own
masking. Only the three arms above are comparable to each other, which is all this gate needs.)

### One operational note for Phase 3/4

`device_map="auto"` misjudged its headroom on this box and offloaded part of the model to CPU,
leaving meta tensors that NemotronH's mixer cannot run through — *"Tensor on device meta is not on
the expected device cuda:0"*. 62GB of weights fit in the 116GiB free, so both eval scripts pin
`device_map={"": 0}` and let an OOM be an OOM rather than a silent partial offload. Anything that
loads this model on a GB10 should do the same.
