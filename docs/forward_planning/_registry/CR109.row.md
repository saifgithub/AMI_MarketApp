The title multiplier is the one element that looks like a downward flow and isn't — it multiplies **within** layer 3, so scores compound with seniority while two players in a field always face identical conditions. **A run is an entry, not an account** — 10,000 AMI Cash fresh at entry, same at every cadence, own open/close/board; everyone starts dead equal on day one, the mid-period-join problem vanishes, every run has a natural ceremony boundary. Entry closes at the bell; **entry is free and always will be — an entry fee is *consideration*, one of the three legs of the gambling test**. **One live run per cadence** (answering *"can you enter multiple long term games?"* — no: the `+100`/`−40` curve makes parallel entries strictly +EV, so 12 rolling Annuals played maximally aggressive is a farm). **Full run/entry state machines** (`ANNOUNCED→ENTRY_OPEN→LOCKED→LIVE→SETTLING→CLOSED→ARCHIVED`, plus `ABANDONED` when a demand-gated field never fills, and `ENTERED→ACTIVE→FINISHED|FORFEIT|VOID`), scoring idempotent at close. **Trade rules were never specified and contained a live exploit:** `sim_engine.submit()` fills at the current quote whenever called, so outside US market hours a player trades against a stale last price *already knowing what happened after it* — harmless in training, a time machine in a scored contest. **Fills only during market hours; out-of-hours orders queue to the next open.** Explicitly NOT covered by the no-rules decision, because it is the difference between simulating a market and simulating one with hindsight. Also settled: long-only (`sim_engine` models no short), market orders, fractional allowed, cash earns nothing (interest would reward sitting out), no leverage ever, open positions marked not liquidated at close. **Thin fields — the most serious hole, from Saiful (*"on a new platform, we may find a month with a single player!"*):** `p=(n−rank)/(n−1)` is **undefined at n=1** and a coin flip at n=2; **a field of one pays the game's largest prize for beating nobody**; and rolling starts **break the very property that justifies placement** (§6.2 chose it because everyone in a field faced the same market), so merging cohorts across start dates — the obvious first idea — cannot work.
