| CR185 | 2026-08-15 | process | **The Dilemma protocol — when a problem has beaten us twice, stop trying harder and ask several agents blind.** Saiful, 2026-08-15, after DEF220/DEF308: *"here is my suggestion on how we handle this dilemma and future ones too."* **What it is.** A third work type beside CR and Defect, for the narrow case where the *framing* is suspect rather than the effort: a shared problem statement, several agents solving it independently in their own model-named subfolders, nobody reading anyone else's, then a judged verdict. Lives in [`docs/dilemmas/`](../../dilemmas/DILEMMA_PROTOCOL.md); IDs are `ISS###`, minted by the Architect like `CR###`/`DEF###`. **Deliberately NOT the default path.** The protocol names its own exit conditions — a merely large problem is a CR with slices, a merely risky one is a CR with `GATE: independent`, a broken-vs-spec one is a Defect, and a product call is Saiful's. A Dilemma needs at least two of: we already shipped a fix and it recurred; the obvious answer is the one that already failed; the choice is structural and expensive to reverse; the solution space spans different *kinds* of answer. **The load-bearing rule is that the brief carries no solution — not even the convening agent's.** Anchoring is the failure being designed against: one agent's framing, read by the next, becomes the shape of every later answer, and if the framing was the problem then more effort inside it produces more confident wrongness. Saiful's ruling on this was explicit — *"symptoms and analysis only. your research and proposal should be in your folder"* — so analysis of what is broken is shared and every idea about what to do is quarantined. Enforced by instruction rather than permissions, so the invite states it plainly and asks a contributor who peeks to **say so** rather than hide it. **Saiful's three other protocol rulings, recorded because they are reusable defaults:** contributors get **read the whole repo, write only their own subfolder** (no risk to the shared checkout, but grounded in real code rather than producing textbook answers); **new dependencies are allowed if justified** (deliberately looser than the standing lean-stack rule — the point of asking several agents is to surface what we do not already know); and the open defect stays **frozen** rather than fixed by hand, so the real sites remain the proving ground for whatever wins. **First instance: `ISS001_DB_INSERT_RACE`.** Check-then-INSERT under a UNIQUE constraint — a pattern this codebase has written **20 times**, has a documented failure class for (P15), has an automated guard for, and has two defects against (DEF220 fixed 7; DEF308 pins 11) — and which was still written **three more times in the six days after the guard went live**. It qualifies on all four criteria at once, which is what prompted the protocol rather than another point fix. **Also produced: the answer to "do we have a guards document" is YES and no second one was created.** [`docs/initial_specs/08_tech/failure_patterns.md`](../../initial_specs/08_tech/failure_patterns.md) already holds 20 classes each with its enforcing check, and CLAUDE.md already points at it; a parallel governance copy would have been two derivations of one fact, which is the DEF098 shape this project keeps filing defects about.
