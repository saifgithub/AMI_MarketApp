| CR144 | 2026-08-08 | process | **Before a pattern reads agent prose, name the field it could have read instead.** DEF231 took six audit rounds and one live defect (DEF234, `$1,073.46` parsed as `$1.00` and rendered "107900.0% ABOVE $1.00") to close a check that compares two numbers — and its own filing row had specified the right design in the sentence that justified filing it: the level is *"already a structured number the verdict carries… so a comparison is available without parsing free text"*. A general prose parser was built instead, and **all four of its defects were figures that were not levels of the run at all**. Same shape one function over: **DEF235**, where `\bsize\b` followed by any number in a 15-char gap matched *"a MEDIUM size entry at $188.62"* and AMI published a drawdown contribution **63× too large** under the words "These are the figures of record". And **CR106 B1** is the same lesson learned once already and not generalised (level provenance moved out of prose into a typed field). Nothing required anyone to ask the question, so this CR adds the rule: any regex or parser reading a value or claim out of LLM-authored text carries a comment at its definition site naming **the structured field considered instead and why it lost** — and if none exists, says whether making one is cheaper than the parser. Deliberately a comment, not a lint rule: the failure is never that someone wrote a pattern, it is that nobody asked, and a mechanical check would be satisfied mechanically while a comment is reviewable ("is that really why it lost?"). **The law behind it, now P16 Amendment 1:** a prose pattern's false-positive surface is proportional to how much **wider than the structured answer** it is, so build the structured half first and let the prose half cover only the residual — usually grammar, which no field can supply. Measured, so the trade is not theoretical: narrowing DEF231 to the run's own levels and spending the safety it bought on recall (an offline LLM sweep that found three whole verb families) took the corpus from **121 figures extracted** to **365 extracted, 246–277 matched to a structured level and actually checked**. Narrowing did not cost coverage — it is what made tripling it affordable. **P16 Amendment 2: never select the guard's corpus with the thing it guards.** The P16 fixture selected rows carrying a `$` AND a directional verb stem, making its own population a function of the pattern under test; a broadening moved the count for two reasons at once, and a **narrowing** would have shrunk the population in lockstep and hidden its own coverage loss — the one failure that fixture exists to prevent. Now `$` alone, 811 rows. **P16 Amendment 3:** two more measurement-harness bugs (`auto_adjust=False` against an app using yfinance's default; the 52-week range omitted, understating retention by 26 points), both caught by the *shape* of the unmatched list rather than by any number looking wrong. Both live prose patterns brought into compliance: `_DIRECTIONAL_CLAIMS` states its division of labour, and `_LEVEL_PATTERNS` carries the question **as a question** — DEF235 is open and owned by the CR143 track, so this CR records what must be asked and explicitly does not pre-empt the fix.
