**Amendment D — this revision. The review fold-in.** Saiful commissioned an independent review

1. **The day-trade rule is rejected; the escalator is held in reserve.** Asked to choose between a
   PDT-style cap and a price-shaped equivalent, he took the escalator. The reason it was even a
   question is in §7: the review's own frictions doctrine says *"nothing caps what a player may
   do"*, and a day-trade cap does exactly that. Prices, not prohibitions.
2. **The agent post-mortem ships, for paying customers only** (§10.2). This amends *"Games is only
   about trade"* at the close boundary and gives the game a direct revenue line that never touches

**Four corrections to the review, made as its recipient.** Each is a place the series was right

1. **F1 understates itself (§6.5).** §6.5 clamped the *headline*, not the ledger — so the median

   variable-ratio reinforcement on a status number, which the review's own dark-pattern register
   marks *never*. **Clamp the ledger.**
2. **S1 and S2 are one mechanism, and it taxes the wrong cohort (§6.6).** The fee is

3. **F5's arithmetic contradicts F1's (§6.4).** A 100-point first rung at a 5-point stipend is 20

4. **C1 reopens F1's wound (§11.4).** Points-capped private fields give zero progression to players
   in the genre's most retentive mode. Split it — placement capped, **stipend still pays.**

## 1. Why

Saiful: *"You are my new game designer for the app. This is a game of portfolio success. think
about the game, how to be fair to new players, and to older players, high score, etc etc."*

Then, setting the frame when asked where the D-060 line sits:

> *"This is a new game. Specifically designed for P&L of your AMI CASH. Measured only by the
> win/loss during the game period. game period could be weekly, monthly, quaterly, 6th months,
> annual cadence."*

**What exists today measures effort, not skill.** `reputation_service.py` (519 lines) +
`league_service.py` (326 lines) score process only: `challenge_correct: 5`, `lesson_passed: 5`,
`room_verdict: 3`, `trade_disciplined: 2`. Its own source states the constraint: *"no P&L-linked
event exists or ever will."* A user can top the weekly league without their portfolio ever having
made money, and nothing in the product tells them whether they are any good at the thing the
product is about.

### 1.1 The lab — and the inversion it produces

Saiful, framing what the game is for:

> *"I see the games as our version of 'paper trading'. this is where you try things and go wild, and
> see that the wider you go, the less likely you are to make money."*

The game is the exam to the Room's classroom (§2) and it is also **the lab**: the sanctioned place
to run experiments with play money and read the result off a scoreboard. Three consequences, each
resolving a tension elsewhere in this document:

1. **The game's job is not to prevent wildness but to make its consequence legible.** The support a
   beginner gets in the arena is not advice — that is the Room's job — it is **measurement**.
   Mirrors instead of guardrails. This re-words §16.10: the game is not the app's *unsupported*
   mode, it is its *differently supported* one.
2. **It inverts the D-060 objection.** The worry was that a P&L game teaches gambling psychology.
   Under the lab framing it does the opposite — it **falsifies gambling psychology with the
   player's own data.** "The wider you go, the less likely you make money" stops being a line in a
   curriculum and becomes a correlation the player watches emerge from their own run history. That
   belongs in the §15 narrative: the game is an instrument for *demonstrating* why wild trading
   loses, in play money, with no prize.
3. **The trading cost (§5.3) is the lab's price signal, not a punishment.** Go wild — the meter
   runs. The experiment is only honest if the frictions are.

The mechanics that operationalise this — intent tags, the wildness index, counterfactual closes and
the heat gauge — are L1–L4 in [`playability_addendum.md`](playability_addendum.md), folded into §10
and §13. **Their governing rule: never reward wildness itself.** Wild runs are sanctioned; only the
*learning* is celebrated. A farmable wild identity would invert the lesson.

## 2. Two surfaces, cleanly split

Saiful, on whether the game convenes the analyst Room:

> *"Do we convene rooms for games, I think we do not. **Games is only about trade.**"*

That settles the separate-account question by implication. Two rulesets this different cannot
share one portfolio row.

| | Training | The Game |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio | existing `sim_portfolios`, **unchanged** | new, one per run |
| Room / 12 agents | yes | **no** |
| Mandate + safety floor | yes, enforced at submit | **none** (§7) |
| Scored | never | always |
| Reset | free, unlimited | restart = forfeit + career debit |
| Compliance surface | zero | all of it |

**The frame: the Room is the classroom, the game is the exam.** You prove you internalised what
the agents taught, without the agents. This gives the product's own fantasy — you are the CEO of
a 12-agent analyst desk — something to pay off, which round 1 never used.

### 2.1 Why the split is load-bearing, not cosmetic

Three verified facts; the middle one is the weakness Amendment B was reacting to.

1. **`submit()` hard-rejects mandate breaches today.** `sim_engine.py:607-640` calls
   `check_mandate_compliance` and returns `accepted=False` on position-size, sector, drawdown,
   cooldown or over-trading violations. This **corrects round 1's risk register**: the "pure P&L
   rewards 100% concentration" risk was overstated for the training path — that trade cannot be
   submitted at all.
2. **The mandate is per-*user*, and the user can edit it.** `MandateRow` is keyed on `user_id`;
   `mandate_store.py:6` records that *"Settings → My Mandate can edit a single user's mandate."*
   On a shared account, **loosening your own mandate becomes a win-the-game move** — the
   curriculum's core instrument turned into a min-max dial.
3. **`resolve_mandate` falls back to a client-supplied `mandate_override`.**
   `mandate_store.py:193` prefers the stored mandate, but onboarding is anonymous-first and the
   row is only created at claim (`api/auth.py:82`), so an anonymous player has no stored mandate
   and **supplies their own rules** via the request body (`api/sim.py:67`). Moot under §7's
   no-rules decision, but it is why the game must not reuse the training submit path unexamined.

Compliance also improves: the *training simulator* — which is what the App Store declaration
describes — stays P&L-unscored, and the game becomes a separable surface that can be
feature-flagged off without gutting the product.

## 3. How it all fits — the three-layer model

**This section is the spine of the design.** Every element belongs to exactly one layer, and one
rule governs how they interact.

| Layer | What lives here | Lifetime | Resets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · The Stake** | AMI Cash, positions, cash, the NAV series | one run | **every entry** |
| **2 · The Contest** | TWR, the field, board rank, placement `p` | one run | **collapses at close** |
| **3 · The Record** | career points, titles, flair, cosmetics, run history | forever | **never** |

### 3.1 The one-way rule

> **Value flows UP the layers, never DOWN.**
> Stake → Contest → Record. Nothing flows back.

Your trading produces a return (Stake → Contest). Your placement produces career points
(Contest → Record). **The Record never grants capital and never grants advantage.**

This is not a slogan; it *derives* most of the design's otherwise-arbitrary-looking choices:

| Choice | Falls out of the rule |
|---|---|
| Capital is a flat 10,000 for everyone | the Record cannot buy Stake |
| Rewards are cosmetic only (§8) | the Record cannot buy Contest advantage |
| Tokens/credits are refused (§8.1) | money is a fourth layer *outside* the game; paying into it makes the Record convertible, and paying out of it makes Stake purchasable |
| Scored on % TWR, never absolute AMI Cash | otherwise a larger Stake buys Contest rank, and plan tier becomes pay-to-win |
| Career points come from placement, not raw return | raw return leaks *market conditions* into the Record — recording when you played, not how well |
| A day-one player is competitive immediately | they start with a full Stake and an empty Record, and the Record does not enter the Contest |
| The title multiplier is safe | it multiplies **within** layer 3 — scores compound, contests do not |

**The title multiplier deserves the explicit note.** It is the only element that looks like a
downward flow and isn't: a Floor Veteran earns more career points for the same placement, so
*scores* compound with seniority — but nothing about their Stake or their Contest changes. Two
players in the same field face identical conditions regardless of title. Keep every future reward
on that side of the line and the game stays fair by construction rather than by tuning.

**The rule survived the review series intact.** Roughly forty additions across five documents and
not one crosses it — the trading cost is layer 1 and burned (§5.3), the finish stipend is a layer-3
award for a layer-1 event, the skill stat and PR board are layer-3 display, challenge cards pay
markers and never points, and the review's own dark-pattern register refuses rewarded video on
exactly this reasoning. §3 caught the rewarded-ad case unaided before the reviewers reached it.

### 3.1.1 The fourth layer: money, and where it now touches

Credits and plan tier sit **outside** the three layers (§3.2). Amendment D puts one thing on that
boundary for the first time, and it needs stating rather than assuming.

**The agent post-mortem is a paid feature (§10.2).** Paying does not buy Stake, does not buy Contest
rank, and does not alter a scored outcome — the run is over before the post-mortem exists. But it
does buy **teaching**, and teaching improves future performance. That is a genuine, if soft,
downward pressure on the one-way rule, and the honest position is:

- **It is not new to the product.** Plan tier already routes better models per (plan, agent) in
  `tier_policy.py::pick_tier`; the Room a Floor Manager convenes is already better than the one a
  Floor Pass user convenes. The game inherits an axis the product already has.
- **It is new to a scored board**, which is what makes it worth flagging. A free player and a paying
  player enter the same field on the same terms with the same capital, and only one of them gets
  coached afterwards.
- **Two fences make it survivable** (§10.2): the free player's Close is complete on its own — rank,
  delta, curve, counterfactuals — so nothing is *removed* to create the upsell; and the paid layer
  is strictly retrospective, so it can never be a signal.
- **The lawyer must see it** (§15). "No purchase necessary" is undisturbed — entry is still free and
  the prize is still cosmetic — but a reviewer asking *"does paying improve your chance of winning?"*
  deserves this paragraph rather than a discovery.

> **Fence, to be tested: no paid feature may ever alter a run's inputs, its scoring, or its
> ranking.** Post-close explanation is the entire permitted surface. The moment a paid tier touches
> Stake or Contest, §3.1 is broken and the pay-to-win argument becomes true.

### 3.2 The element map

Every game element, and where it sits:

| Element | Layer | Visible to | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| AMI Cash | 1 Stake | you only | play money; never rendered for another player (§14.9) |
| Positions / trades | 1 Stake | you only during a run | revealable after close — roadmap mode #3 |
| NAV series | 1 Stake | you only | `portfolio_nav_daily`, the one new table |
| TWR | 2 Contest | everyone in the field | the only number on a board |
| The field | 2 Contest | everyone | one open field per cadence per period |
| Board rank | 2 Contest | everyone | raw TWR sorted, no formula |
| Placement `p` | 2 Contest | you | the bridge from layer 2 to layer 3 |
| Career points | 3 Record | you | signed; **the ledger is clamped at zero, not the display** (§6.5) |
| Trading cost | 1 Stake | you | burned at fill — never pooled, never redistributed (§5.3) |
| Intent tag · wildness index · counterfactuals · heat | 1 Stake / personal | **you only** | mirrors, never rendered on a board (§10) |
| Personal records (PRs) | 3 Record | you | thin-field-proof: available at `n = 1` (§8.4) |
| Title | 3 Record | everyone, on every board | the strongest cheap reward — it is *worn* |

| Run history | 3 Record | you | entered / finished / forfeited |
| Credits | **outside** | — | never crosses into any layer (§8.1) |

### 3.3 The loop

```text
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        ↓                                                             │
   ENTER ──► TRADE ──► DAILY BEAT ──► FINAL STRETCH ──► SETTLE ──► CLOSE
   layer 1    layer 1     layer 2         layer 2       layer 2   → layer 3
```

Daily decision, weekly resolution at the shortest cadence, seasonal prestige at the longest. §10
is this loop's ritual layer; §8 is what falls out the far end.

## 4. A run is an entry, not an account

Entering a cadence opens a **run**: **10,000 AMI Cash**, the same stake for every entrant at every
cadence, with its own open and close, its own board, scored on TWR. AMI Cash is the game's money —
Saiful: *"AMI Cash is the 'money' used in the game. Like Monopoly money."*

**Why this replaced the five-windows design.** Fresh capital per entry means **everyone starts
dead equal on day one**, so fairness is structural rather than tuned — and unlike five windows
over one curve, each entry is a genuinely separate decision with its own strategy and tension. The
mid-period-join problem disappears, every run has a natural ceremony boundary, restart is just
re-entry, and alpha can ship **one** cadence without designing the others away.

**Entry closes at the bell.** You enter before the run opens or wait for the next one. A race has
a start line, and the closing window is itself an anticipation beat.

**Rolling starts at every cadence.** Amended after the playability review
([`playability_review.md`](playability_review.md) §1) found that fixed calendar starts plus
entry-closes-at-the-bell produce a **dead start**: a player arriving on Tuesday could not enter
until the following Monday, so their first experience of the headline feature was *"come back in
six days."* That is defect-grade, not a preference.

- **Weekly** fields open **every trading day**, each running five trading days. Wednesday's field
  runs Wed→Tue. Maximum wait to play: **one day.**
- **Monthly** fields open weekly.
- **Quarter / Half / Annual** open on the demand gate in §6.6.

Everyone inside a field still starts on the same day against the same market, so the same-clock
fairness argument is untouched — this changes *how often a field opens*, not what a field is. It is
the same machinery §6.6 already specifies, applied at every cadence.

### 4.1 One live run per cadence, maximum

Saiful asked: *"can you enter multiple long term games?"* **No** — and the reason is an exploit in
§6's curve, not a UI limit.

Career points pay `+100` for a win and only `−40` for a loss, so **parallel entries are strictly
+EV.** With rolling monthly Annual starts and no entry limit, the optimal play is to hold 12
concurrent Annual runs, play each maximally aggressive, and let the best one place high while the
losers cost 40% of what the winner pays. Same shape as the restart-on-red exploit the career debit
closes.

A player may hold at most one Weekly + one Monthly + one Quarterly + one Half + one Annual — five
books, **never two of a kind.** Rolling starts still do their job: you never wait more than a month
to begin a long run, you just can't stack them.

### 4.2 Entry has no cost

Entry is free and unlimited in time. **This is a legal load-bearing choice, not a generosity
one:** an entry fee is *consideration*, which is one of the three legs of the gambling test
(§15). There is no scenario in which entering a run costs credits, money, or a consumable.

### 4.3 Run lifecycle — the state machine

The build team needs states, not just beats. A **field** (the contest) and an **entry** (one
player's participation in it) have separate lifecycles.

**Field states:**

```text
ANNOUNCED ──► ENTRY_OPEN ──► LOCKED ──► LIVE ──► SETTLING ──► CLOSED ──► ARCHIVED
                   │
                   └──► ABANDONED        (demand gate never met and max wait elapsed
                                          with < 2 entrants — see §6.6)
```

| Transition | Trigger |
|---|---|
| `ANNOUNCED → ENTRY_OPEN` | schedule (W/M) or the previous field of that cadence locking (Q/H/Y) |
| `ENTRY_OPEN → LOCKED` | the bell: calendar time (W/M), or min-entrants reached / max-wait elapsed (Q/H/Y) |
| `ENTRY_OPEN → ABANDONED` | max wait elapsed with fewer than 2 entrants; entrants are rolled into the next field, not penalised |
| `LOCKED → LIVE` | market open on day 1 |
| `LIVE → SETTLING` | period end; **positions lock, results withheld** (§10) |
| `SETTLING → CLOSED` | settlement window elapses; scoring runs, career points post |
| `CLOSED → ARCHIVED` | the result is written to the Record and the field is read-only forever |

**Entry states:**

```text
ENTERED ──► ACTIVE ──┬──► FINISHED     (ran to close, ranked, scored)
                     ├──► FORFEIT      (restart — §6.7)
                     └──► VOID         (mock-priced days — §14.1; no points, no placing)
```

Scoring runs **once**, at `SETTLING → CLOSED`, and is **idempotent** — the same guarantee
`league_service.weekly_roll()` already provides, and for the same reason: the tick that drives it
can fire twice across a container restart.

## 5. The trade rules of a run

Round 1 never specified these, because it inherited the training portfolio's behaviour. Once the
game is a separate surface with no mandate, they have to be stated — and one of them is a live
exploit.

| Rule | Decision |
|---|---|
| Universe | US equities, same provider path as training (`yfinance`, mock fallback) |
| Direction | **Long only.** Not a new restriction — `sim_engine` only models BUY and SELL-to-close; there is no short. State it so it is not mistaken for an oversight. |
| Order type | Market, filled at the current quote — what `sim_engine.submit()` already does |
| Fractional | Allowed. `SimHoldingRow.quantity` is `Numeric(12,4)`, so it already works, and it keeps a 10,000 stake usable against a $900 share. |
| Cash | Uninvested AMI Cash earns nothing. No interest — interest would reward sitting out, which is the opposite of the game. |
| Leverage / margin | None, ever. Not modelled, and it would push the product's risk profile somewhere it shouldn't go. |
| Position count | Unlimited (no mandate — §7) |
| **Trading cost** | **10 bps of notional, minimum 1.00 AMI Cash, per fill. Game path only.** §5.3 |
| **Day-trade limit** | **None.** Rejected — see §7; the escalator (§5.3.2) is the reserve answer. |
| Splits | Quantity and cost basis adjusted in place, snapshot row flagged (§12). Not a capital event — economic value is unchanged. |
| Dividends | **Not modelled at MVP**, and the benchmark is therefore price-only (§6.6). Credited before Q/H/Y cadences ship (§12). |

### 5.1 Trading outside market hours — an exploit that has to close

**In training this is harmless; in a scored contest it is a free option.** `sim_engine.submit()`
fills at the current quote whenever it is called. Outside US market hours the "current quote" is a
stale last price — so a player can trade all weekend against Friday's close while already knowing
what happened after it. That is not a strategy, it is a time machine.

**Rule: fills only occur during US regular market hours.** Orders placed outside them are
**queued and filled at the next open**, with the UI stating so plainly at order time. Note this
interacts with the daily NAV snapshot (§13) — a queued order must not appear in a snapshot before
it fills.

This is the one place where the no-arena-rules decision (§7) does *not* apply, because it is not a
constraint on strategy — it is the difference between simulating a market and simulating a market
with hindsight.

#### Queue-first is the primary flow, not the edge case

The single most consequential UX finding in the review series, and it inverts how §13.3 should be
designed. **US regular hours are evening in the Gulf and past midnight in Malaysia.** For the target
markets, placing orders outside market hours is not an exception to handle — it is what most players
will do most of the time.

So the queue is the main path: **plan the book tonight, fills execute at the open, the morning beat
card reports what filled and at what price.** Free cancellation any time before the fill. The lobby,
the ticket and the daily beat should all be designed around that rhythm rather than around a
live-market assumption imported from US-centric comparators.

It also improves the product: a player who places a considered order the evening before is doing the
thing the curriculum teaches, and is not watching a ticker. §10's one-beat-per-close rule and this
one point the same direction.

### 5.2 Open at close

Positions still open when a run closes are **marked to the closing price, not liquidated**. TWR is
computed from NAV, so there is nothing to settle: the run simply ends with the book where it
stands. Liquidating would inject a fake trade into the record and punish anyone holding through
the boundary.

**Note for §6.6:** because the run ends marked rather than liquidated, a player pays the trading
cost on the way *in* and not on the way out. One fill, not a round trip — which is what sets the
achievable-benchmark adjustment.

### 5.3 The trading cost

Saiful: *"we have to have some fee attached to the trade then. lets build it so that 'spray and
pray', while may still work, is not the preferred strategy!"*

**A fee is not a rule, it is a market property.** Real markets charge for every trade — spread,
impact, commission — and a game that simulates trading without its costs simulates a market that
does not exist. Same family as §5.1: not a constraint on strategy, a correction to the simulation.
Nothing here caps what a player may do. Spray-and-pray stays legal; it stops being free.

| Property | Decision |
|---|---|
| **What** | **10 bps of notional, minimum 1.00 AMI Cash**, per fill (first cut — §18) |
| **Denominated in** | AMI Cash, inside the run. Never credits, never money — §4.2 and the ad invariant untouched. Layer 1 only. |
| **When** | At fill. A queued order shows an estimate at placement and charges the actual at fill. |
| **Both sides** | BUY adds it to cost (rejected if cash < notional + fee); SELL deducts it from proceeds. |
| **Where it goes** | **Burned.** Never redistributed, never a prize pool — a pool is a wagered stake and §15 dies. |
| **Displayed** | On the ticket before confirm: *"Est. trading cost: 1.24 (modeled)"*. Labelled *modeled* honestly — fills are at last price, so this approximates spread and impact rather than simulating a book. |
| **Scope** | **Game path only.** Training keeps zero friction — its over-trading control is the mandate, which is curriculum, not market. Regression-tested on both sides, like the §7 split. |
| **Desks** | House desks pay identical costs (§11.2's integrity line, §14). |
| **Scoring** | No special handling: the fee reduces cash → NAV → TWR. Skill is measured after costs, as everywhere real. **Not a capital event** — it must not break the TWR chain. |

#### 5.3.1 Why bps-with-a-minimum rather than flat

Two abuse shapes need two dampers, and one number pair carries both:

- **Churning big** — the bps component makes drag proportional to turnover. A disciplined run
  (build ~5 positions, adjust a few times, close) turns the book over ~2× and pays ≈ 20 bps for the
  week. A sprayer turning it 20× pays ≈ 2% of stake, roughly a whole week's typical market move.
- **Spraying dust** — the 1.00 minimum is the flat floor. Fifty micro-probes cost 50 AMI Cash
  regardless of size, so probe-everything stops being free information.

#### 5.3.2 The escalator — held in reserve

If the flat cost proves too blunt at Gate 1 (§17.1), the reserve answer is the Fantasy Premier
League move: **the first N fills per run at base cost, fills beyond N at 3×**, framed in-world as
*"your desk's cheap tickets."* Transfers still work; they are just never free, and managing the
allowance becomes a skill.

**This is what Saiful chose over a day-trade cap** (§7). Not shipped at MVP — one number is easier
to learn than two, and the minimum fee may already do the job. Recorded so the option survives.

**A silent daily fill ceiling** (~20 orders/day) runs as an API rate limit rather than a published
rule: abuse backstop and yfinance-quota protection, not game design.

### 5.4 The trade ticket — three taps

The fee adds thought-cost per trade, so the UI must remove hand-cost. Target: **a considered trade
in under 10 seconds.**

```text
TAP 1 — ticker      chips: watchlist first (live — state/watchlist_providers.dart),
                    then recent-in-this-run, then search
TAP 2 — size        chips: 10% · 25% · 50% · All-in  (of current cash, precomputed)
TAP 3 — confirm     one card: shares · est. cost (modeled) · "this is 42% of your book"
                    optional pre-set chips: stop −5%/−10% · target +10%/+20%
```

- **The 25% chip renders as the visually-default choice.** Not a rule and not a block — choice
  architecture. The no-rules arena keeps every option; the *default* quietly echoes the position
  sizing the curriculum teaches. It costs a border colour.
- **Stop and target chips are nearly free.** `sim_engine.submit()` already accepts `stop` and
  `target` per trade and `evaluate_outcomes()` already watches them. Exposing them as one-tap
  presets teaches plan-the-exit-at-entry — and a set stop reduces the compulsion to watch the
  screen, which is the direction §10's one-beat rule already leans.
- **The confirm card is information, not friction.** Fast never means thoughtless; cost and
  book-percentage on one card is the fee's disclosure surface and the sizing lesson together.
- **Caching is mostly built.** `market_data.py`'s `CachingProvider` already wraps Yahoo with a
  60-second per-ticker TTL, so heavier ticket use adds no yfinance pressure — which is also an
  *integrity* win, since fewer rate-limit fallbacks means fewer mock-priced days and fewer §14.1
  voids. Client-side, cash and holdings cache so the size chips compute with zero round trips.
- **Rebuild last book.** At entry, one tap: *"Rebuild your last run's opening allocation — 5
  positions, same %, est. cost 4.20."* Queued as normal orders at normal cost. Every run starts from
  fresh capital, so re-establishing a book is the re-entry moment's biggest hand-cost, and this
  serves §10.3's keystone directly. **A choice, never a default** — repetition is offered, not
  encouraged, and it grants no advantage a new player lacks (it saves taps, not time in the market).

## 6. Scoring — P&L to points

Two numbers doing two different jobs. Keeping them separate is most of the design, and §3 explains
why: one lives in layer 2 and dies at close, the other lives in layer 3 forever.

### 6.1 Board rank — no formula

Raw run TWR, sorted. P&L becomes a percentage; the percentage is the rank. Nothing else.

```text
per sub-period between capital events:  rᵢ  = (NAV_end / NAV_start) − 1
run return:                             TWR = Π(1 + rᵢ) − 1
```

Chain-linked sub-period returns split at capital events — the GIPS standard. It earns its place
three times: restart becomes a clean capital event; stake size stops mattering
([`tiers_and_pricing.md`](../../initial_specs/06_monetization/tiers_and_pricing.md) promises
$10K/$100K/$1M sim capital by plan, unbuilt — `_STARTING_CAPITAL = 10_000.0` at
`sim_engine.py:195`); and any future top-up is neutralised automatically.

> **Invariant, to be written down and tested: boards rank on % TWR only, never absolute AMI Cash.**
> The moment a board ranks money, capital tier becomes pay-to-win and the "no purchase necessary"
> position in [`competition_rules.md`](../../../legal/policies/competition_rules.md) collapses.

### 6.2 Career points — placement, never raw return

**The load-bearing choice.** Points off the percentage directly would let a bull quarter hand
everyone points and a bear quarter debit everyone — the Record would then store *market
conditions* rather than skill, which §3.1 forbids as an upward leak of the wrong thing. Placement
cancels the market out for free: everyone in a field faced the same one.

```text
career_points = base(p) × cadence_weight × title_multiplier
p = (n − rank) / (n − 1)          over the WHOLE open field · 1.0 = first, 0.0 = last

p ≥ 0.5   base = +100 × ((p − 0.5) × 2) ^ 1.5      convex — the very top is worth a lot
p < 0.5   base = − 40 × ((0.5 − p) × 2)            linear and shallow
```

Winning is worth 2.5× what losing costs; the damping is in the curve, not bolted on after.

| Rank of 30 | 1 | 3 | 6 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | +100 | +80 | +53 | +23 | ~0 | −12 | −26 | −40 |

Placement over a large open field stays well-behaved — 400th of 5000 gives `p ≈ 0.92`.

### 6.3 Cadence weight is asymmetric

Gains scale with committed time; losses with its **square root**. A long *bad* run is mostly market
conditions, a long *good* run is hard to fake, and a player should not be crushed for having been
invested through a bear half-year.

| | Week | Month | Quarter | Half | Year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gain × | 1 | 4 | 13 | 26 | 52 |
| Loss × | 1 | 2 | 3.6 | 5.1 | 7.2 |

The loss row is √cadence — √52 ≈ 7.2. One number, retunable from real data without touching
anything else.

**Balance check:** win the year at Analyst = `100 × 52 × 1.4` = **+7,280**. Win 52 weeks =
`100 × 1 × 1.4 × 52` = **+7,280**. Neither cadence is a farm.

### 6.4 Titles are thresholds, not a promotion event

Saiful chose to inherit the five shipped names but spotted the consequence of rolling starts:
*"since we have rolling starts, we need to consider how this is done."* With staggered closes there
is no synchronized boundary to promote on — which is exactly what `league_service.weekly_roll()`
relied upon.

Titles are driven off the **running career-point total**, recomputed whenever any run closes. No
roll, no cohort assembly, no relegation event — relegation is just the total falling back through a
threshold.

| Title | `title_multiplier` | Threshold (first cut) |
|---|---|---|
| apprentice | 1.0 | 0 |
| *(new rung — unnamed, §18.1)* | 1.2 | **milestone: 3 runs finished, none forfeited** |
| analyst | 1.4 | 500 |
| trader | 1.6 | 2,500 |
| senior | 1.8 | 10,000 |
| floor_veteran | 2.0 | 30,000 |

The multiplier is **fixed at run open** so it cannot drift mid-run.

**Why the first rung is a milestone and not a number** (Amendment D correction 3). The review found
the goal gradient never engages: a *good* player — consistently 75th percentile, ≈ +35 per weekly
run — reaches Analyst after ~14 weeks, and the median player never reaches it at all. It proposed a
100-point rung. But its own stipend sizing (§6.5, ~5 per weekly finish) makes 100 points **twenty
finishes**, which is five months — worse than the problem it was fixing. Raising the stipend instead
breaks the anti-farm guard: at +20/run, Analyst falls to pure attendance in half a year.

So lower the gate rather than raise the stipend. **Three finished runs, none forfeited** is
reachable in three weeks, cannot be farmed because it fires once, and rewards exactly the behaviour
the re-homed streak already celebrates — *finish what you start*. It also removes a tuning constant
instead of adding one. Rungs 2 and up stay on points.

### 6.5 Decided values — and the median player's problem

- **×0.5 partial credit while absolute TWR is negative.** Top of the field in a bear run still
  scores, at half. Progression never stalls through a downturn; losing money never pays like
  winning.
- **Career points clamp at zero — the ledger, not the display.** See the correction below.

#### The median player's Record does not move — the review's best finding

Run the §6.2 curve for the middle of the field:

```text
p = 0.60  →  base = +100 × (0.2)^1.5  ≈  +8.9
p = 0.55  →  base ≈ +3.2
p = 0.50  →  base =  0
p = 0.45  →  base = −4
p = 0.40  →  base = −8
```

A player oscillating between the 40th and 60th percentile — **which is most players, most of the
time, by definition** — nets approximately zero per run. Their total sits at the floor, the first
title needs 500, and the ladder never starts. **Placement is zero-sum, and bolting a progression
system to a zero-sum score excludes half the population from progression in every field.** The
playability review saw the symptom and misjudged its size: this is not *slow* progression, it is
*no* progression for the median player, indefinitely, by construction.

**Correction — the original floor made it worse, not better** (Amendment D correction 1). The
decided value was *"the ledger is signed but the headline cannot go below 0"* — a clamp on the
**display**. That produces two failures the review did not reach:

1. **An invisible debt.** A player whose signed total is −30 earns +8 and **nothing happens on
   screen.** They are climbing out of a hole the UI never told them about. The one number meant to
   represent their history silently lies to them for weeks.
2. **A counter that moves on noise.** When it does move, it moves on a ≈ zero-drift random walk —
   which is **variable-ratio reinforcement on a status number**, the exact pattern the review's own
   dark-pattern register marks *never*. Building it accidentally is no better than building it on
   purpose.

> **The ledger clamps, not the display.** A run that would take the total below zero writes a debit
> of exactly the distance to zero, and no more. The displayed number is always the real number.

**The fix for the underlying wound is the finish stipend.** A small fixed career-point award for
*finishing* a run: entered, at least one executed trade, never forfeited, held to the close. Order
**~5 per weekly finish, scaled by cadence weight** — sized so the counter visibly moves at every
close but titles cannot be farmed by attendance (500 points at +5/run is two years of showing up;
all real progression still runs through placement). The three guard conditions are what keep it from
being a check-in in disguise, and it celebrates the same ethos the re-homed streak does.

**The stipend and the fee are a pair, and must be sized together** (§18). The fee is scoring-neutral
under placement but scoring-negative under the benchmark path (§6.6) — so shipping the fee without
the stipend tilts the median early-player experience negative. Neither ships alone.

### 6.6 Thin fields — the cost of rolling starts

Saiful: *"we need to think the cohorts for the longer game, since we allow rolling start. on a
popular platform, this is great. but on a new platform, we may find a month with a single player!"*

The most serious hole in the design, and it is three problems:

1. **`p = (n − rank)/(n − 1)` is undefined at `n = 1`** and degenerate at `n = 2`, where it is
   exactly 1.0 or 0.0 — maximum payout or maximum debit on a coin flip.
2. **A field of one pays the largest prize in the game for beating nobody.** First of one at the
   annual cadence is `+100 × 52 × title_multiplier`, so the rational play on a thin platform is
   *find the rolling start month nobody entered.*
3. **Rolling starts break the property that justifies placement scoring.** §6.2 chose placement
   because everyone in a field faced the same market — so thin fields **cannot** be fixed by
   merging cohorts across start dates, which is the obvious first idea.

The problem is confined to the long cadences; weekly and monthly are calendar-driven, so thinness
means a small field, never a stale one.

**Fix, three parts:**

- **Demand-gated starts for Q/H/Y.** A rolling field opens at a minimum entrant count **or** a
  maximum wait, whichever comes first. Self-scaling: monthly or faster on a busy platform,
  quarterly on a new one, with no tuning. Everyone in a field still starts the same day, so the
  same-market property survives. The lobby shows the queue honestly.
- **Benchmark-relative career points below the placement threshold.** Score
  `alpha = TWR − benchmark TWR` over the same window instead of placement. Not a fallback hack: it
  preserves the *exact* property placement was chosen for — market cancellation — while working at
  any field size including `n = 1`, and it kills the beat-nobody farm. **No new infrastructure:**
  `sim_engine.current_history(ticker, period)` serves any ticker including SPY and already returns
  a source flag for §14.1; `trading_math/portfolio_stats.py` already ships `beta()`.
- **Titles require a real field.** A thin field can score, but a *title* needs a minimum field.
  Prestige needs witnesses.

**Degrade loudly** (CR040): a thin-field run states its basis on the board and in the Close —
*"Field of 3. Scored against the S&P 500, not against the field."*

First-cut thresholds: placement needs `n ≥ 8`; titles need `n ≥ 20`; maximum lobby wait 30 days.

#### 6.6.1 The alpha → points formula

**This was specified nowhere**, in any document, until Amendment D — and §16.4 concedes it is the
default code path for months. The placement curve, which will not run until fields clear `n ≥ 8`,
had a formula, a table and a balance check; the path every real alpha player meets first had an
excess-return number and no mapping. The first close is the moment that decides retention, and its
number was undefined.

```text
alpha       = run_TWR − achievable_benchmark_TWR        (see 6.6.2)
a           = clamp(alpha / ALPHA_FULL, −1.0, +1.0)     ALPHA_FULL = the alpha that pays a full win

a ≥ 0       base = +100 × a ^ 1.5                       same convex shape as placement
a < 0       base = − 40 × |a|                           same linear, shallow damping
```

Four requirements, so the two bases feel like one game rather than two:

- **The same 2.5 : 1 asymmetry** as §6.2. Beating the market is the convex top; losing to it is
  survivable.
- **Capped at the placement extremes.** `a` clamps at ±1, so no alpha outcome ever pays more than
  winning a field outright. Without the cap a lucky moonshot in a field of one out-earns a genuine
  win in a field of thirty.
- **Positive alpha must pay visibly.** *"You beat the S&P 500"* is the most legible boast in
  investing and the product's natural first-win framing.
- **`ALPHA_FULL` is one tunable constant** (§18), chosen so a typical good week — roughly +1–2%
  excess — pays in the same range as a good placement finish. The ×0.5 partial-credit rule (§6.5)
  and the cadence weights (§6.3) apply unchanged.

#### 6.6.2 The achievable benchmark — correcting the fee's hidden tax

**The review found half of this and celebrated the other half without noticing they are the same
mechanism** (Amendment D correction 2).

The fee behaves differently under the two scoring bases:

| Basis | Effect of the fee |
|---|---|
| **Placement** | **Neutral.** Every entrant in the field pays it, so ranks are unchanged. |
| **Benchmark** | **Negative.** The index pays no fees; the player does. |

Thin fields route to the benchmark, and fields thicken over time. So the fee **systematically
penalises the alpha-window cohort — the first players, the ones the product can least afford to
lose — and stops penalising anyone once fields clear `n ≥ 8`.** Same skill, worse score, decided by
join date. That is a fairness defect, and it is invisible in the fee's own design because the fee
looks obviously fair: everyone pays it.

**The fix is a principle, not a fudge.** A costless index is not an alternative the player could
have chosen — buying and holding SPY *inside this game* costs one fill (§5.2: runs end marked, not
liquidated). So the fair yardstick for **scoring** is the benchmark bearing the same entry cost the
player bears:

> `achievable_benchmark_TWR = benchmark_TWR − ENTRY_FEE_BPS`
> One constant, applied on the scoring path only.

**The gross comparison survives, and it is still the best line the game can print.** The Close
displays *"the index pays no fees. You beat it anyway."* — the industry's own lesson, embedded: this
is exactly why most active managers underperform their benchmark, discovered by the player in their
own numbers rather than taught. The losing version is the index-fund lesson delivered at peak
attention. **Display gross, score achievable.** Both statements are true and neither is a spin.

#### 6.6.3 The benchmark is price-only

Dividends are not modelled (§12): nothing credits a dividend to any NAV. Players therefore cannot
earn them, so the yardstick must not either — **the benchmark is price-only SPY** until dividend
crediting lands ahead of the Q/H/Y cadences. This resolves §18's open benchmark question at the
*basis* level; which index remains open.

### 6.7 Restart — rescues capital, never the record

Always available; nobody is ever trapped. On restart the run is **FORFEIT** — removed from the
board, so the player neither holds a rank nor takes one from anyone else — and a fresh run can be
entered.

**The loss still debits career points**, scored at the standing held when they walked. Saiful's
rule, and it closes the biggest asymmetry: without it, restart is free when losing, so the rational
strategy is *restart on red, ride on green*, and every recorded result is a survivor. With it,
**restarting and riding it out cost the same**, so the hit cannot be dodged and restarting can
never improve an outcome. The real choice becomes *crystallise now and get fresh capital* versus
*hold and keep the option of recovering*.

**Re-entry is immediate, not exile.** The debit *is* the price; benching on top would punish the
same act twice.

**The entry model opens a churn hole round 1 did not have.** The debit is placement-based, so
forfeiting from mid-field (`p ≈ 0.5`) costs ≈ **0**, and with rolling starts there is always
another field to join. The zero floor makes it worse for the players most likely to do it — at 0
there is nothing to debit. Two additive fixes, neither an arena rule:

- **A minimum forfeit debit** — forfeiting costs at least a fixed floor per cadence regardless of
  standing. Sized so one forfeit stays cheaper than a genuinely bad finish; the mercy rule must
  remain merciful.
- **Forfeits counted visibly on the Record** — entered / finished / forfeited. A forfeit-heavy
  record reads as what it is with no mechanical penalty at all.

The existing 24h reset cooldown stays and blunts weekly churn; it does nothing for annual churn,
which is what the minimum debit is for. Below a blowup threshold (~NAV ≤ 50% of stake) the app
*offers* the restart rather than making the user hunt for it.

## 7. No arena rules — and how that is implemented honestly

Saiful, asked whether the game should run a locked competition mandate:

> *"do games have mandate? I think games do not have mandate. It's up to you if you want to follow
> your mandates. There should not be any mandate. Games is about showing your capabilities. your
> mandates is not in play. convince me otherwise"*

He was shown the counter-argument and reaffirmed. **The game submit path does not call
`check_mandate_compliance`.** No position cap, no sector cap, no drawdown cap, no cooldown, no
over-trading limit, **and no halal / locale / long_only screen.**

The counter-argument, recorded because it was made and rejected rather than never raised:

1. **The halal and locale screens are not risk rules, and they live inside that same function.**
   `safety_floor.py:289` is `if c.halal:`, driven by a user-set mandate flag; the locale screen
   sits at `:339`. Bypassing the compliance check bypasses both.
2. **The store declaration.** CR064 bound the zero-P&L rule as a binding invariant because
   "simulated gambling: **No**" depends on it. A P&L contest with no position limits and a restart
   button after a blowup is the most gambling-shaped configuration available.
3. **Placement stops measuring skill at short cadences.** With no position cap, optimal weekly play
   is 100% of stake into the highest-volatility name; if everyone plays optimally the weekly board
   is decided by a coin flip, and the Record then stores luck.

**Accepted risk, explicit (Saiful, 2026-07-30).** A user who declared halal-only in their mandate
trades **unscreened** on a scored path, and locale restrictions do not apply there. Recorded the
way D-060 was recorded.

**Two obligations follow, neither of which is a gate:**

- **Disclose it.** The entry surface states plainly that the arena has no constraints and that your
  mandate and screens do not apply here. A screen that silently stops existing is exactly the CR040
  failure mode; saying so out loud is not a rule.
- **Never let the training path drift.** `check_mandate_compliance` stays mandatory on the training
  submit path — the uncoachable safety floor is untouched by this CR.

The position cap is not lost, it is **re-homed as an opt-in mode** — survival runs, #10 in
[`games_roadmap.md`](games_roadmap.md).

**Note the boundary with §5.1.** Market-hours filling is *not* an arena rule and is not covered by
this decision: it is the difference between simulating a market and simulating one with hindsight.

### 7.1 The market-frictions doctrine — and the line it does not cross

Amendment D adds a second item to that boundary, and rejects a third. The distinction is worth
naming because it will be tested again by every future proposal.

> **The *market* half of the simulation is in. The *mandate* half stays out.**
> A market property applies to everyone in the world who trades. A rule applies to a player.

| Item | In or out | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Market-hours filling** (§5.1) | **in** | a property of the market. Without it, out-of-hours trading is a time machine. |
| **Trading costs** (§5.3) | **in** | a property of the market. Every real trade costs something. Nothing is forbidden — it is priced. |
| **Day-trade limit, PDT-style** | **out — rejected** | see below |

**The day-trade rule was proposed and Saiful rejected it.** The review recommended a PDT-style cap
(3 same-day round trips per 5 trading days), framed as *"realism, not restriction."* Two things
argue against it, and he took the second:

1. **It is not a market property.** PDT is a **broker constraint on one account type** — a FINRA
   rule for margin accounts under $25k. A cash account is not subject to it. So it does not clear
   the bar market hours and trading costs clear; it is a rule wearing realism's clothes.
2. **It contradicts the doctrine it arrived with.** The addendum proposing it opens *"nothing below
   caps what a player may do"* and then, two sections later, caps what a player may do. A cap is
   the thing this design decided not to have.

**The escalator (§5.3.2) is the doctrine-clean version of the same goal** — it prices repeated
fills instead of refusing them, so spray-and-pray stays legal and stops being cheap. Held in
reserve behind Gate 1 rather than shipped, because one number is easier to learn than two.

So the disclosure has one clean sentence: *"No mandate, no position limits, no screens. Real market
frictions apply: trading costs, and fills only during market hours."*

## 8. Rewards and progression — what a Champion gets

Saiful: *"What do they get for being Champion? Scoreboard for fun? Tokens? Special
title/badge/avatar/skin for agents."*

§3.1 answers most of this before it is asked: rewards live in layer 3, and **nothing in layer 3
may flow down into layer 1 or 2.** Two rules follow.

> **Rule 1 — competition rewards are never purchasable, and purchasable things are never
> competition rewards.** Two disjoint catalogues, permanently. The moment a champion prize can also
> be bought, it has a market price, and "no prize of monetary value" is what keeps this off the
> gambling test entirely. It also destroys the prestige: a trophy you can buy is not a trophy.

> **Rule 2 — no reward may improve future performance.** No extra capital, no better data, no extra
> agents, no fee break. Otherwise the game compounds toward whoever won early, destroying the
> fresh-capital fairness §4 exists to create.

### 8.1 Tokens: no

Credits are a purchasable currency (packs at $4.99–$49.99). Awarding them on a P&L outcome is
exactly the step §15 flags for the lawyer, and it would make the Record convertible to money —
breaking rule 1 structurally rather than incidentally. Saiful's own earlier answer: *"for now just
points."*

Credits for lessons and streaks are **unaffected**. The gambling test is about
**prize-for-outcome**, not whether a currency exists in the app.

#### Ads — the same rule from the other direction

Saiful: *"The players will be a source of income if they view adverts etc."* Ads are already the
Floor Pass model, and the game monetises by driving sessions. But the standard mobile pattern —
**rewarded video** — is fatal here, because it attacks the *consideration* leg rather than the prize
leg:

> **INVARIANT — no ad may ever be required, rewarded, or gate anything in the game.**
> Not entry, not re-entry, not restart, not capital, not information, not timing, and not cosmetics
> that double as competition rewards.

*"Watch an ad to restart"*, *"watch an ad for a second entry"*, *"watch an ad for extra AMI Cash"* —
each makes some part of the game purchasable with attention, and non-monetary consideration counts.
The first two break §15's consideration leg; the third is money buying Stake, which §3.1's one-way
rule already forbids. **The one-way rule catches all of them unaided**, which is the clearest
evidence so far that §3 is load-bearing rather than descriptive.

Ads therefore run as **ambient inventory on non-contest surfaces** — the lobby, the Record, app
open. **Never on the board, never on the trade ticket, and never in the Close**: an ad in the trophy
moment cheapens the one emotional payoff the period arc exists to deliver.

**No ad infrastructure exists** — no SDK, no units, no consent flow (`Floor Pass` is a tier name,
not an implementation). Ads are **their own CR**, and should not be entangled with this schedule.
See [`final_review.md`](final_review.md) §1.

### 8.2 Reward density scales inversely with cadence frequency

The answer to "what does a Champion get" is **which champion** — 52 weekly winners a year cannot
all receive something permanent, or it is worthless by March.

| Champion of | Per year | Gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly** | 52 | Career points and a mention on the closing board. **Nothing permanent** — there is no scarcity to spend. |
| **Monthly** | 12 | A badge on the Record. |
| **Quarterly** | 4 | A **displayed title riding your handle on every board you enter next quarter.** Temporary, but *worn* — visible to opponents, which makes it the strongest cheap reward here. |
| **Half** | 2 | Permanent flair, plus the agent frame treatment. |
| **Annual** | 1 | A title that **never expires**, the full agent skin, a **plaque in the Room**, and entry to a Champions invitational (roadmap #12). |

### 8.3 Agent cosmetics — and the fact that there is no art

The strongest reward idea, for a non-obvious reason: the *player* is a pseudonymous handle
(anonymous-first, so there is barely an identity to decorate), but the **12 agents are characters
the user already has a relationship with**, and they render in the Room, where the user spends real
time. Prestige that appears during the activity beats prestige on a screen nobody visits.

**But `mobile/assets/` contains no agent artwork** — fonts, glossary, `hex_mesh.svg`,
`logo_hex.svg`, and nothing else. Agents render as Material icons plus a resolved accent colour
(`accentForOutcome`, `room_board.dart:46`). So a skin cannot mean illustration: 12 agents × N
variants is a commission, and there is no designer.

**In AMI's own language a skin is `accent + hex frame + material treatment`** — which the design
system already generates programmatically. A champion's Room renders its agent cards in amber hex
frames instead of slate. Zero art cost, on-brand for Hex-Reinforced Precision, and it reads
instantly as different.

**The Room plaque** is the other cheap, native idea: the Room is where twelve agents convene, and a
real trading floor displays its hardware on the wall. It costs a widget and it renders during the
thing the user does most.

### 8.4 What everyone else gets — and why it matters more

**If one player in 26 receives something, twenty-five received nothing, and that is the churn.**
This is a bigger retention lever than the champion prize.

Every close yields a **progress marker** where one is true: a personal best, first time in the top
ten, five finishes without a forfeit, first positive run, a longest-run-held streak. Evidence of
movement, not participation trophies. The multi-category Annual Awards (§10) do this at the top end;
this extends it to every run at every cadence.

**The finish stipend (§6.5) is the same idea expressed in the ledger** — one ethos, said twice: a
marker is the qualitative version, the stipend is the number that moves.

#### The PR board — self-competition, and it works at `n = 1`

Markers as specced fire *transiently*, at the close, and then vanish. Standing personal records
solve the alpha window better than anything else in this design, because **they need no other
players at all** — exactly the condition §6.6 says will hold for months.

> **The Record carries a permanent PR board:** best weekly TWR, best alpha, best drawdown control,
> longest hold, longest streak of finishes — each stamped with the run that set it.

Every close gains a second way to matter: *not a win, but your best April.* Cheap — a max-query over
`portfolio_nav_daily` plus a Record section — and it extends §8.4 from a moment to a monument.

**Challenge completions are markers, never points** (§10.4). The career ledger must never pay for a
self-imposed constraint, or constraints become the farm this section exists to avoid.

### 8.5 Eligibility — who can rank but cannot win

Saiful: *"we can say that employees and the family of AMI Trading cannot win awards etc."*

Standard competition hygiene, and needed independently of the house desks — **the operator plays.**
Saiful will enter boards, and *"2026 Annual Champion: Saiful"* is a problem whether or not anyone
doubts he earned it. The automated UAT rig is the same issue from the other direction: Hermes drives
a device on melehost, and those runs can enter fields.

Saiful's framing collapses these into one rule rather than a list — the house desks *are* the
company's entrants, in the same way its staff are:

> **Anything operated by, employed by, or affiliated with AMI Trading may rank, but may not hold a
> title, a champion reward, or permanent flair.**

Four instances of that single rule:

| Who | Why it is the same rule |
|---|---|
| **House desks** (§11.2) | entrants the company operates — the top of the ladder stays human |
| **The operator** — Saiful, and anyone with staff or admin access | a competition whose operator can win its own prizes looks rigged even when it isn't |
| **Household and immediate family of the above** | the standard clause, and it closes the obvious proxy |
| **Test, UAT and seed accounts** | the Hermes rig, automated runs, and the seed rows already excluded from real-user metrics |

Stating it as one principle matters for drafting: it survives cases nobody has thought of yet — a
future contractor, a partner integration, a demo account — without needing the list re-opened each
time.

**Ineligible does not mean invisible — and that distinction is the point.** They still enter, still
trade, still rank on the board, and the board still shows what actually happened. Only the *title*
passes down, to the highest eligible entrant. This is how real competitions work, and it is what
avoids the tell Saiful identified in §11.2: there is no hidden exclusion to notice, because the rule
is published and the ranking is honest.

**When an ineligible entrant places first, say so plainly** — *"Title: SLATE_07 (2nd overall)"* —
rather than silently renumbering. A board that quietly promotes second place looks like a bug; one
that explains itself looks like a rule.

**Scope is narrow on purpose:** ineligibility covers titles, awards and permanent flair only.
Career points still accrue, so Saiful can dogfood progression and the desks can populate a
meaningful spread.

**This belongs in [`competition_rules.md`](../../../legal/policies/competition_rules.md) v2.0**, not
only here — that is the document with standing, and §15 already re-versions it for the P&L change.

**Forward dependency:** with no prize of monetary value, operator participation is unremarkable.
**If credits are ever paid on a P&L outcome (§8.1), operator and staff participation becomes a much
larger question** and this clause stops being hygiene and starts being load-bearing. Flag it for the
same lawyer review.

### 8.6 Progression — what career points unlock

| Threshold | Unlocks | Affects play? |
|---|---|---|
| Title thresholds (§6.4) | the title, worn on every board; a higher `title_multiplier` | **no** — multiplies score only |
| Half/Annual championships | flair, agent cosmetics, the Room plaque | **no** — cosmetic |

## 9. Fairness

**New players**

1. **Fresh capital per entry** — everyone starts dead equal on day one of every run. Strictly
   stronger than round 1's "every window opens at 0%", because there is no carried book either.
2. TWR — capital size is irrelevant to rank.
3. No mid-period join problem: entry closes at the bell, so every entrant runs the same clock.
4. Weekly ships first, so a day-one player has a real, scored, ceremonied result in **5 trading
   days**.
5. No global all-time ranked ladder. Career points are personal — *"for personal satisfactions"* —
   never a lifetime leaderboard, which is structurally unwinnable for anyone who joined later.
6. **The Record does not enter the Contest** (§3.1). This is the whole argument in one line.

**Veterans**

1. Career points persist forever as a signed net record (floored at zero).
2. Long cadences are the prestige events; the annual title is the one that means something.
3. `title_multiplier` rewards winning while carrying a higher title, which also removes the
   incentive to smurf.
4. Permanent titles — "2026 Annual Champion" never expires.
5. Multi-category Annual Awards (§10) mean depth of play is recognised in more than one way.

**MVP field structure: one open field per cadence per period.** Saiful: *"what we deliver in MVP is
only open games, meaning all players all at the same time, no duals."* This drops round 1's cohorts
of ≤30 and the `_assemble_week` reuse. Bracketed fields split by title are laned as roadmap mode #5.

**The fairness argument has a floor, and it is §6.6.** An open field is only fair while it is a
field.

## 10. The period arc — the end-game ceremony

Round 1 rolled periods silently: no close-out moment, no reveal, no trophy, no shareable result —
one of Amendment B's three named gaps.

| Beat | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Entries close** | countdown; the last-call moment |
| **The bell** | field revealed — size, stake, who's in |
| **Live** | one rank-movement beat per US market close. **Not per tick** — per-tick movement is a slot machine, and this is a training product. The card carries **attribution**, not just standing: *"NVDA drove +1.9% of your +2.3%."* |
| **Final stretch** | "3 days left. You're 4th. 2nd is 1.1% ahead." The anticipation engine. |
| **Settlement freeze** | positions lock at period end, results withheld ~1h. Anticipation manufactured by delay — the cheapest ceremony in games. |
| **The Close** | full-screen, **three beats** (§10.2): the result, one insight, the way back in |
| **The Record** | permanent career entry + share card + any §8 reward |
| **The Wind-Up** | the *loss* ceremony. A blowup gets a dignified post-mortem with real numbers and immediate re-entry — never a fail screen. Losing must be a chapter, not an ending. |
| **Annual Awards** | multi-category: Best Return, Best Risk-Adjusted, Best Drawdown Control, Most Improved. **More categories = more winners**, serving §9's fairness at the ceremony layer. |

**Reuse.** `Celebrate.micro/.meso/.major` ships at `mobile/lib/services/celebration.dart`, though
`major` is hardcoded to `AgentUnlockedScreen` and needs generalising. `ShareCardData` is a sealed
class with four variants at `mobile/lib/widgets/share/share_card.dart:21` — the run result is a
fifth. Annual Awards are nearly free because `trading_math/returns.py` already ships
`sharpe_ratio` and `max_drawdown_pct`.

### 10.1 Notification matrix

**Push notifications do not exist.** The only trace in the repo is
`note="TODO B1: fire APNs push notification here"` at `api/room.py:134` — no tokens, no APNs, no
FCM. Saiful chose **in-app ceremony now, push next**.

| Beat | In-app | Push | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entries closing | ✓ | needed | the highest-value push in the design |
| The bell | ✓ | optional | |
| Daily rank move | ✓ | needed | once per US close, never per tick |
| Final stretch | ✓ | **needed** | this beat is the anticipation engine and is inert without push |
| Settlement / Close | ✓ | needed | |
| Wind-Up offer | ✓ | no | in-context only; a push telling someone they are losing is cruel |

Until push lands, every beat above reaches only players who already opened the app. Stated rather
than glossed.

#### The reminder rule

Saiful: *"We need to make this addictive. We need to remind them to play."*

> **Remind on events that need a decision. Never on events that only invite watching.**

| Send it | Don't |
|---|---|
| Entries close in 2h, you are not entered | AAPL is up 2% |
| Your run closes tomorrow | your position moved |
| Momentum Desk has challenged you | someone else traded |
| Your run has settled — results are ready | the market opened |
| You dropped out of the top 10 — **once per run** | rank changed — every time |
| Your last run closed 6 days ago | — |

**This is not restraint for its own sake; it is what protects the channel.** Ship *"your position
moved"* daily and users disable push — and then **the Close**, the message carrying the entire
emotional payoff of the period arc, never arrives. The cheap notification costs the valuable one.

It also lands on the same side as §10's *one beat per market close, never per tick*: per-tick
movement is a slot machine, and this is a training product whose curriculum warns against the exact
behaviour a position-moved push would train.

**Caps:** ≤1 push/day, ≤4/week, quiet hours in the user's timezone. The Close and a received
challenge are exempt from suppression but never duplicated.

**The retention backbone** is the playability review's accepted set: the streak re-homed onto **runs
finished / periods entered** (never check-ins), progress markers at every close, provisional rank
from the first trade, near-miss lines, and Rivals pulled forward.

**Two fences on that set, both from the review:**

> **Near-miss framing points *up* only, never at a loss.** *"You were 0.3% off 2nd"* motivates the
> next attempt. *"You almost avoided the debit"* is the slot-machine near-miss — the pattern that
> measurably prolongs gambling persistence — and it teaches nothing. One sentence here so a future
> copywriter, or a model writing copy, cannot cross it.

> **The streak gets a bye week.** One earned skip per quarter, framed in-world: *a desk goes on
> leave; the book is flat.* Duolingo learned that a streak's power cuts both ways — the longer it
> grows, the more its loss becomes a churn cliff, and after 200 days the rational response to
> missing one is to quit entirely. The streak survives one missed period; two ends it. Cost is a
> counter on the streak query.

### 10.2 The Close — a ceremony budget of three beats

**The review series nearly destroyed the Close by improving it.** Count what five documents attached
to one screen: rank and career delta, the field reveal, near-miss lines, a progress marker, best and
worst decision, two counterfactual lines, an agent post-mortem, intent-versus-outcome, the
thin-field basis disclosure, and the re-entry CTA. **Eleven blocks on a screen whose entire job is
one emotional payoff.** The peak-end rule that justified the Close forbids what the Close was
becoming: a report with confetti.

> **Budget: three beats.**
> **1 — The result.** Rank, career-point delta, the curve replayed.
> **2 — One insight**, chosen by priority: the duel verdict on a first run · the post-mortem on a
> blowup · the near-miss when it applies · otherwise the counterfactual.
> **3 — The way back in** (§10.3).
>
> Everything else lives one tap deeper, in a **full debrief** panel, and permanently on the Record.
> **A ceremony with an appendix — not a report with confetti.**

#### The agent post-mortem — paid, and fenced

Saiful ruled: **yes, for paying customers.** This amends *"Games is only about trade"* (§2) at the
close boundary only — the agents stay out of the run itself, which is what that decision was
protecting. Post-close is a different act: the contest is over, nothing can be influenced, and what
remains is explanation.

**Why it is worth amending for.** The product owns an asset no competitor in the genre has: twelve
named analysts the user already has a relationship with. Chess.com's Game Review is the single
feature most credited with making *losing* retentive rather than churning, and this is a stronger
version of it. A loss with a coach beats a loss with a stat block — which is exactly what the
Wind-Up needs to land.

**It also answers the monetisation question without touching the ad invariant** (§8.1). The game now
has a direct revenue line, and it sits at the highest-attention moment in the product.

Four fences, all structural:

1. **Free players keep the entire Close.** Rank, delta, curve, counterfactual lines, markers,
   re-entry. Nothing is removed to manufacture the upsell — the free Close must stand on its own as
   a complete ceremony. Otherwise the peak-end payoff becomes a paywall and §10.3's keystone dies
   for the half of the base that funds ad revenue.
2. **The upsell never appears inside the three beats.** The locked card lives in the debrief panel —
   one tap deeper, which is where an interested player already goes. An upgrade prompt inside the
   ceremony sours the one moment the whole period arc exists to deliver.
3. **Retrospective only, enforced structurally.** Never a forward view, never a recommendation.
   CR038 is explicit that agents ignore even emphatic prompt instructions ~70% of the time, so this
   is a **fixed template filled from computed run data**, not an instruction in a prompt. The
   §11.3 licensing fence applies unchanged.
4. **No paid feature may alter a run's inputs, scoring, or ranking** (§3.1.1). Explanation is the
   entire permitted surface.

Which agent speaks is matched to the run's story — Risk on a blowup, Momentum on a hot streak — or
rotates. One LLM call per close per *paying* user, against data the scoring pass already assembled;
gating to paying customers also bounds the cost, which was the objection to shipping it broadly.

### 10.3 The loop-back — the arrow that is the whole game

§3.3 draws `CLOSE → ENTER` as an arrow, and nothing specified the surface behind it. **The 60
seconds after the Close is the highest-leverage moment in the design:** emotion is peaked (win or
loss — the Wind-Up ends warm by design), the fresh-start effect is primed, and the next field is at
most a day away. A player who leaves that screen without re-entering goes cold until a push
notification that does not exist yet tries to resurrect them.

- **The final beat of the Close and of the Wind-Up is an entry CTA** — the next field of that
  cadence, its countdown, its current entrant count, one tap to enter. Never a menu, never "back to
  lobby". *Rebuild last book* (§5.4) sits here, because re-establishing a book from fresh capital is
  this moment's biggest hand-cost.
- **`close → re-entry` is the game's primary health metric**, instrumented from slice 3 day one,
  with **first-run activation** (entered → finished) beside it. These two numbers are the evidence
  §17.1's funding gates run on. Their targets are set **before** the build, so the gate cannot be
  argued backwards from the data.

### 10.4 The mirrors — measurement instead of guardrails

§1.1's lab framing, made concrete. Every item is computed from data the scoring pass already walks,
and **every one of them is private** (§3.2): intent, wildness, counterfactuals and heat never render
on a board or in another player's view. Billboarding them would turn self-knowledge into
performance.

| Mirror | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Intent tag** | One tap at entry: *going wild · testing a thesis · playing it disciplined.* The Close scores the run against the player's own declaration: *"You said wild. −7.2%. Your disciplined runs average +0.4%."* Naming "wild" as sanctioned removes the shame that suppresses experimentation. One enum on `game_entries`. |
| **Wildness index** | A per-run width score from stored data — concentration, effective position count, turnover, book volatility. The Record then shows **returns by style**: *"Wild runs: 1 of 7 positive. Disciplined: 5 of 8."* Saiful's own sentence turned into the player's dataset. Tag-versus-index is a coaching moment no comparator has: *"you said disciplined, you traded wild."* |
| **Counterfactual lines** | Two, at every close: *"If you'd held your first picks untouched: +2.1%. You made −0.8% trading around them."* and *"If you'd just held the S&P: +0.6%."* The buy-and-hold counterfactual is the most instructive stat an overtrader can see; the second is the index-fund lesson at peak attention. Pairs with §10.2 — the agent narrates the gap. |
| **Book heat gauge** | Live on the my-run screen: *"82% in 2 names · high heat."* Not a rule, not a warning, no gate — a mirror held up **at the moment width is being decided**, rather than only after. |
| **Challenge cards** | Opt-in self-imposed constraints at entry: *"max 3 positions" · "every position gets a stop" · "no trades after day 1."* Completion is a predicate over the trade log and earns a **progress marker, never career points** (§8.4). The deepest fit with the lab framing: the curriculum's discipline rules become **testable hypotheses**. Nothing is imposed — a constraint exists only because the player picked it up. |

**The counterfactual never scolds.** *"You made −0.8% trading around them"* is a number, not a
judgment. The Wind-Up's dignity rule governs every mirror here.

**Attribution on the daily beat** belongs to the same family. A no-rules weekly arena is
substantially luck (§16.2, accepted), and players who sense that outcomes are not contingent on
their decisions stop playing — learned helplessness is the churn nobody exit-surveys. *"NVDA drove
+1.9% of your +2.3%"* restores perceived contingency, and it teaches, which is the brand. Same
reason: **best decision / worst decision** on the Close, and roadmap #9 (risk-adjusted board, XS)
shipped early as the skill-legible counterweight.

## 11. Customer-to-customer interaction

Amendment B's second named gap. Originally *"only open games… no duals."*

### 11.1 Duels are in — and they are the fix for the thin-field problem

Saiful, after the playability review: *"I really like the Dual….. how can we bring it back?"*

Bringing them back is not a concession, it is a correction. §6.6 established that **the open board
needs `n ≥ 8` before placement means anything**, and §16.4 concedes that at alpha *every* field is
below that. So the flagship competitive surface is inert exactly when the product most needs
players to feel something.

**A duel needs `n = 2`.** It is the only competitive format that works at alpha field sizes — and
it produces far better ceremony than a thin board can: *"You vs VECTOR_11. 3 days left. They're
1.1% ahead"* beats *"1st of 3, scored against the S&P"* by a wide margin.

| | Open field | Duel |
|---|---|---|
| Players needed to mean anything | ≥ 8 | **2** |
| Works at alpha | no | **yes** |
| Ceremony quality when thin | flat | **strong** |
| New machinery | — | matchmaking + head-to-head scoring |

**Scoring is head-to-head, and must be carved out of §6.2 explicitly.** Higher TWR wins. A duel
must **never** route through the placement formula — at `n = 2`, `p` is exactly 1.0 or 0.0, so
placement would pay a duel winner the maximum in the game and debit the loser the maximum. It must
also not route to §6.6's benchmark path, because in a duel `n = 2` is the *intended* field size,
not a shortfall. Duels therefore get their own scoring: a **duel record (W–L)** on the Record
surface, plus a modest fixed career-point delta scaled by cadence.

**MVP duels are auto-matched only.** This is the load-bearing constraint, and the reason is
collusion: onboarding is anonymous-first, so creating a second account is nearly free. Direct
challenge-by-handle plus cheap accounts equals a trivial farm — make an alt, throw the duel, bank
the win, repeat. Auto-matching removes the ability to choose your victim, which closes the farm
structurally rather than by detection. **Challenge-a-friend comes later, and when it does it should
either not award career points or be capped per period.**

Everything else stays out of MVP. **Rivals** (roadmap #2) is the natural next addition — the
playability review ranks it as the cheapest daily reason to open the app.

**The queue keeps a pairing key.** Auto-matching pairs whoever is waiting, which is right at alpha.
At scale, repeated blowout mismatches demotivate both sides — flow needs challenge ≈ skill — so
pairing should move to career-point or title proximity when the pool allows, with a W–L-informed
Elo-lite as the natural step after. Nothing to build now; recorded so the queue's schema does not
preclude it. The rolling skill stat (§11.6) is the natural key.

#### The first run is always a duel — against the Index Desk

Three separate problems have one fix, which costs almost nothing on top of machinery already
planned.

The problems: at alpha the open field is thin, so a new player's first Close reads *"1st of 3,
scored against the S&P"* — a footnote, not a result. Their first act is unbounded stock-picking, the
highest-choice-load task in the product, and choice overload is a documented first-session killer.
And §6.6's benchmark path, however correct, is an explanation rather than an opponent.

> **Your first run: you versus the market. Five trading days. Beat the Index Desk.**

- **A guaranteed opponent** — works at `n = 1` real players, with no thin-field caveat on the first
  close.
- **A guaranteed narrative.** The first Close says *you beat the market* or *the market beat you*.
  Both are stories; neither is a footnote. It is §6.6.1's formula made concrete.
- **The most legible fantasy in investing** — "beat the market" needs no tutorial.
- **Bounded difficulty.** The Index Desk holds the benchmark, so it never embarrasses a beginner the
  way a concentrated moonshot on the open board would.
- **Already in the build** — desks and duels are both planned. This is a routing rule, not a feature.

House duels are recorded **separately from human duels** on the Record. Beating a desk is a real
result, but a W–L that mixes the two would let a deterministic opponent inflate a social stat.

This composes with, and does not decide, the open question of gating first entry behind a training
milestone (§16.10).

### 11.2 House players — the strategy desks

Saiful: *"we can run 'bot' players to make up the league, and we can have the bot players challenge
the real players for a duel."*

This answers what scoring alone cannot: §6.6 keeps the *numbers* honest when a field is small, but
it cannot make a board feel alive or supply a duel opponent when no other human is online. House
players do both.

#### Disclosure — decided, reversed, and the reason is Saiful's

Undisclosed house players were chosen first and then reversed by Saiful on his own argument:

> *"if we do not disclose that it's a bot, then if the copy happens (and it can happen off app) the
> user is copying what they thought is a person!"*

That is the decisive framing, and it is sharper than the licensing objection that preceded it. The
harm is not primarily regulatory — it is that **the user's belief about the source is false.** They
are not copying *a strategy that did well*; they believe they are copying **a person who knows
something**. And once a result leaves the app — a screenshot, a group chat, *"look what VECTOR_11
did"* — there is no disclosure surface left and no way to correct it.

**Decision: house players are disclosed.** They compete under strategy names — **Momentum Desk,
Index Desk, Dividend Desk** — with their rule published in the rules surface. The word "bot" is
never used; a *desk* reads as a rival worth beating, which is what the feature is for.

Disclosure buys four things at once:

1. **Copying becomes education.** Saiful's own reaction — *"can you imagine if a bot wins.. I would
   copy the bots trading!"* — is the feature working, once the rule is published. Studying a stated
   method is what the product exists to teach; following an anonymous winner is signal-chasing, and
   it is what `rejected_features_register.md` rejects on brand grounds.
2. **It removes the advice exposure.** A hidden house account whose trades users follow is
   advice-adjacent from an entity explicitly not licensed to give investment advice. A published
   strategy is a worked example.
3. **It dissolves the title tell.** Saiful, on excluding undisclosed bots from titles: *"it would be
   a bit of a give-away if they can be seen winning, but did not win title."* Correct — an exclusion
   you can observe advertises what it conceals. With disclosure there is nothing to conceal:
   *"house desks are ranked but do not hold titles"* is simply a rule.
4. **It lets them enter every cadence.** Undisclosed desks would have had to stay out of Quarter,
   Half and Annual to avoid that tell — which is precisely where fields are thinnest and stay thin
   longest. Disclosed, they can populate all five.

#### Scope

| Capability | Decision |
|---|---|
| Fill the board (presence) | **yes**, at every cadence |
| Issue duel challenges | **yes** — declinable, no penalty, rate-limited so it never nags |
| Win duels sometimes | **yes, necessarily** — an opponent that always loses teaches nothing |
| Count toward career points | **yes** (Saiful) |
| Hold titles or champion rewards | **no** — a published rule, not a hidden exclusion |

#### The integrity line — identity is disclosed, performance must be real

**House desks run real, rule-based strategies against real market data.** Their returns are computed
exactly as a human's are: real prices, the same NAV path, the same TWR.

> **A house desk's P&L is never generated, sampled, or tuned to look plausible.**

This is separate from the disclosure question and harder, because §11.2 lets house results **feed
real users' career points**. A fabricated return would propagate into a real person's permanent
Record — the silent-fallback failure CR040 exists to prevent, and the same shape as DEF059. It also
has a practical payoff: because the strategies are real, nobody is tuning difficulty, so the spread
of house returns is whatever the market produced.

Suggested roster:

| Desk | Holds |
|---|---|
| Index | the benchmark |
| Momentum | top-N trailing 12-month winners |
| Dividend | high-yield names |
| Equal weight | N names, evenly sized |
| Contrarian | the biggest recent losers |
| Concentrated | 2–3 names, high conviction — supplies the tail |

The last one matters: a roster of only diversified strategies produces a suspiciously narrow spread.
Real fields have blowups and moonshots.

#### Scaling and control

- **Fill to a target field size**, never a fixed count. As real entrants grow, desks taper
  automatically and eventually stop appearing.
- **A kill switch** — one setting that removes them from all future fields, forwarded in
  `docker-compose.yml`'s `api-alpha` block or `test_config_compose_parity.py` fails the build (CR040).
- **Excluded from every real-user metric.** The project already excludes the CR035 room-benchmark
  synthetics and the seed rows; desks join that filter. A desk must never inflate a number Saiful
  uses to make decisions — that would be the design deceiving *him*.

#### Engineering fences

- A desk is **visibly a desk on every surface that renders an entrant** — board, duel, Close, share
  card. One shared renderer, so a new surface cannot forget.
- Desks are excluded from bug-report and support surfaces.
- If prices go mock, desks void with everyone else (§14.1) — the field voids as a whole, so their
  treatment is identical to a human's.

### 11.3 Discussion and tips — phased, and each phase gated

Saiful: *"we need to enable the social aspect of the game. they can discuss trades, and give tips."*

**The app has zero user-generated content today.** The only user-authored value anywhere is the
pseudonymous handle — system-generated, regenerable once. There is **no report mechanism, no block,
no profanity filter, no moderation tooling** in the repo at all. So this is not incremental.

**Apple Guideline 1.2 requires four things of any app with UGC**, and shipping without them is a
rejection rather than a warning: a filter for objectionable material, a report mechanism, the
ability to block abusive users, and published contact with **action within 24 hours**. That last
item is a standing daily obligation for a one-person team, and it is the real cost of this feature.

An unmoderated stock-tip channel is also the highest-risk UGC category there is — pump schemes,
"DM me for signals" funnels, affiliate scams, and unlicensed advice on a platform run by an entity
**explicitly not licensed to give investment advice**. Arabic and Malay content compounds it:
Saiful cannot moderate what he cannot read.

| Phase | What | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Structured reactions** — a fixed vocabulary on a closed run (*clean run · brave · lucky · disciplined · ouch*). No typing, so **no moderation surface at all.** | none |
| **B** | **Post-close rationale notes** — a short, length-capped note on **your own trades in a run that has already closed.** Roadmap #3 plus a sentence of *why*. | full Guideline 1.2 checklist |
| **C** | **Open discussion** | a moderation budget that does not exist today |

Phase B is the safest possible reading of "discuss trades", because of what it structurally cannot
become: **retrospective**, so it can never be a signal; **scoped to your own trades**, so it cannot
become a generic tip channel; **length-capped**, limiting both abuse surface and moderation load;
and **attached to a real scored decision**, which makes it analysis rather than opinion — the
brand's own register.

**The "tips" itch is already largely served without any of this.** The strategy desks (§11.2)
publish their rules, so *"what is Momentum Desk doing and why is it beating me"* is a tip from a
source that cannot scam anyone — Saiful's *"I would copy the bots trading!"* is exactly that
working. And the **12 agents** are a licensed-safe way to comment on a closed field: on-brand,
moderation-free, and closer to what a user actually wants than a stranger's hot take. Retrospective
and educational only — never a forward recommendation.

Full reasoning in [`final_review.md`](final_review.md) §3.

### 11.4 Private fields — the genre's proven loop, and the one real gap

**The largest miss the comparative review found.** Two decades of paper-trading competitions —
Investopedia, MarketWatch VSE, Wall Street Survivor, the SIFMA Stock Market Game — run on the same
engine, and it is not the public leaderboard. **It is the private league.** A teacher runs a class
game, an office runs a bragging-rights pool, friends run a season. The public board is furniture;
the game people stay for is the one containing people they know. Season-long fantasy sports is the
same finding at consumer scale — people stay in one league with the same ten humans for a decade.

Every competitive surface in this design faces **strangers**: the open field, auto-matched duels,
house desks. Nothing lets a player say *"join my league."* That sentence is this genre's entire
proven acquisition loop — every private field recruits its own players and then holds them with
social obligation, which is the cheapest retention there is.

> **A private field: any player creates one for a chosen cadence; others join by code.**

It rides the existing field and entry machinery plus one eligibility predicate. It is also the wedge
into classrooms — the SIFMA model — which matters for the AR/MS markets later, and it is config
rather than code.

**Career points are capped in private fields, because alt accounts are free** — the same collusion
reasoning §11.1 applies to challenge-by-handle. But the obvious version of that cap reopens the
§6.5 wound (Amendment D correction 4):

> **Placement points are capped in a private field. The finish stipend still pays.**

The split is principled, not a compromise. **Placement is rank-dependent, so collusion can farm
it** — five alts finishing below you manufactures a `p` of 1.0. **The stipend is rank-independent**:
it requires a real entry, a real trade and a real hold to the close, and no arrangement between
accounts can conjure one. So the mechanic that can be farmed is capped and the mechanic that cannot
is left alone — and a player who lives entirely in private leagues, which is the genre's most
retentive mode, still has a Record that moves.

**Sequenced after duels prove out** (§17.1, Stage 2). A private league of a broken game recruits
nobody.

### 11.5 Reactions — the minimum viable being-seen

§11.3's Phase A pulled forward. A fixed vocabulary tap on a closed run — *clean run · brave · lucky
· disciplined · ouch* — has **no moderation surface at all**, because there is no free text. Strava
kudos is the comparator that proved low-bandwidth social is enough: being seen retains, and
conversation is optional.

It matters early because relatedness is otherwise entirely absent until fields populate, and that is
months (§16.4).

### 11.6 The skill stat — a number volume cannot farm

Chess separates the number that measures *skill* (a rating: it falls, it converges, it means the
same thing for everyone) from grind currencies and permanent titles. **Career points confound the
two.** They accumulate with volume, so a mediocre high-volume player out-points a skilled occasional
one — and the Record never answers the question the product exists to teach: *am I getting better?*

> **A rolling skill stat on the Record: trailing-N-run average alpha** (or Sharpe —
> `sharpe_ratio` already ships). Visible beside career points, and it **never gates anything.**

Layer 3, one-way rule intact. **It can fall, which is exactly what makes it credible** — a number
volume cannot farm, for the veteran to defend and the improver to watch rise. *Numbers > adjectives*
is the brand voice; this is the brand's own stat. It is also the pairing key §11.1 wants.

A windowed query over data slice 3 already stores. Stage 2, with the Record maturing.

### 11.7 The rest

Every other variant is specified in [`games_roadmap.md`](games_roadmap.md): rivals, retrospective
reveal, desks, bracketed fields, and ten more. Two constraints bound the whole space:

- **The standing copy-trading rejection holds.** `rejected_features_register.md` rejects
  copy-trading and follower feeds on brand grounds. The roadmap's **retrospective reveal** (trades
  hidden while a run is open, revealed at close) is the resolution rather than an exception: the
  reveal lands after a position can no longer be copied, so it is education, not signal-following.
- **No free-text chat, ever.** A one-person team has no moderation capacity. Structured interaction
  only.

## 12. Edge cases

The states a game design has to answer before a build team hits them.

| Case | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Field never fills** | `ABANDONED` (§4.3). Entrants roll into the next field of that cadence; no forfeit, no debit, no record. |
| **Field of exactly 1–2** | Scores benchmark-relative, awards no title (§6.6). Never reaches the placement formula. |
| **Ticker halted mid-run** | Last valid mark recorded **and flagged** in the snapshot, never silently carried forward (§14.7). |
| **Ticker delisted mid-run** | Position marked to the last valid price and frozen; the run continues. Flagged on the Close so the result is explicable. |
| **Anonymous → claimed mid-run** | The run must survive account claim. `merge_service.py` does **not** re-key `BadgeRow` or `StreakFreezeRow` today — a known pre-existing gap that would now also strand game entries and career points. **This CR must fix the merge path for its own tables**, or a player loses their record at the exact moment they create an account. |
| **User deletes account mid-run** | Entry is removed from the field; remaining entrants re-rank. Because rank is raw TWR sorted, removal does not change anyone's relative order — only `n`, and therefore `p`. Recompute at close, never retroactively. |
| **Mock-priced days** | The run goes `VOID` — no points, no placing, no title (§14.1). |
| **Container restart mid-settlement** | Scoring is idempotent (§4.3); the tick re-runs safely. |
| **Clock/timezone** | Every boundary is US market time, not device local. A weekly run closes at the US close, wherever the player is. |
| **Stock split mid-run** | **Adjust quantity and cost basis in place, flag the snapshot row.** Not a capital event and **not a TWR break** — economic value is unchanged, so the chain must not split here. The board and the Close state it (CR040). |
| **Dividend paid mid-run** | **Not credited at MVP** — see G1 below. The benchmark is price-only to match (§6.6.3). |
| **Insufficient cash for fee** | A BUY is rejected if cash < notional + fee (§5.3). The ticket shows the cost before confirm, so this should be unreachable from the UI — but the API must enforce it. |

### 12.1 The three engine gaps

Verified absent from the engine today. None were in this CR before Amendment D.

| # | Gap | What it breaks | Behaviour | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | **Dividends are not modelled.** Nothing credits a dividend to any NAV — only display math and halal-purification math exist. | Q/H/Y runs mismeasure total return, and the **Dividend Desk is handicapped by its entire yield** — its whole strategy is invisible to the scorer. | **(a) now:** declare the benchmark **price-only** (§6.6.3) so the yardstick matches what players can earn. **(b) before Q/H/Y:** credit cash dividends on ex-date (yfinance serves the calendar), flagged in the snapshot row. | (a) slice 3 · (b) gates slice 6 |
| **G2** | **Splits are unhandled.** Zero split logic in the engine. | A 4:1 split mid-run craters `held quantity × new price`. Training absorbs it with a free reset; **a scored run corrupts the board.** | Adjust in place on detection, flag the row, state it. See §12 above. | **slice 2** — the first scored runs are exposed |
| **G3** | **Quote freshness is §5.1 one timescale down.** Fills take Yahoo's last price behind a 60s cache. If that price lags real time, a live-price watcher trades on known information. | The overnight time machine, intraday. | **Measure first** on melehost, alongside §16.5's mock-frequency task: sample Yahoo lag against a reference during market hours. If material, a delayed-fill rule (fill at the next snapshot) is the heavy fix. | measure **before any board is live** |

**G3's priority dropped, and the reason is the fee.** An exploit now has to clear ~20 bps per round
trip to be profitable, so it must be *large* to be worth running — and large lags are the easy kind
to detect. The measurement still has to happen; it is due diligence rather than urgent.

#### The landing surface — one screen, three states

**Every revision of this CR specified a lobby and none specified what a player actually lands on.**
Those are not the same screen, and the gap matters: the lobby is a five-cadence menu, which is the
right surface for exactly one of the three states a player can arrive in and the wrong one for the
two that matter most.

| State | Who | What the screen is | The one action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | never entered | **a pitch, not a lobby** — one matchup, one cadence, no menu | start your first run |
| **B** | holds a live run | **the run is the hero** — whichever closes soonest, with its beat | trade it |
| **C** | between runs | **the re-entry moment, gone cold** | enter the next one |

- **State A is F3's first-run duel, made into a landing.** *"Your first run: you versus the market.
  Five trading days. Beat the Index Desk."* A beginner meeting a five-cadence menu is meeting the
  highest-choice-load task in the product before they know what a run is — and choice overload is a
  documented first-session killer. The §7 disclosure renders **full text here**, once, then
  compresses to a chip forever after (§13.3's entry fence).
- **State B sorts by what closes soonest**, because that is where the tension is. Other live runs
  compress to one line each. The daily beat and its attribution (§10.4) belong on this card, not
  behind a tap.
- **State C is worth designing hardest.** The 60 seconds after a Close is handled by the Close
  itself (§10.3) — but a player who left and returns two days later lands *here* instead. It is the
  second-best shot at `close → re-entry`, and **the only one that works without push**, which does
  not exist yet. Everything on it points at one tap: next field, countdown, entrant count, and
  *rebuild last book* (§5.4).

> **The five-cadence lobby still exists — one tap deeper**, reached deliberately by a player adding
> a second book. It is not what anyone lands on. A menu is what you build when you don't know which
> of your users is looking at it, and here the server always does.

#### The Record is the product's second surface

It was listed above as one row — *"does not exist today"* — while the review series quietly attached
eight retention mechanics to it: career points and titles, run history with forfeit counts, duel
W–L, the PR board (§8.4), the skill stat (§11.6), returns-by-style (§10.4), badges and flair, and
intent history. **The season loop's entire home.** It should be designed like one, with its own
information hierarchy:

```text
IDENTITY    title · handle · skill stat
MOVEMENT    career points · PRs · streak of finishes
HISTORY     runs entered / finished / forfeited · returns by style · duel record
```

#### Two fences on the surfaces

> **Entry stays one screen, three taps to entered.** The review's own finding was that first-entry
> choice load kills beginners — and then the series re-loaded the entry screen with the no-rules
> disclosure, the cost disclosure, the intent tag, challenge cards, cadence choice and
> rebuild-last-book. The disclosure renders full-text on **first entry only** and compresses to a
> chip after; the intent tag is one optional row; challenges hide behind a single *"add a
> challenge"* affordance; rebuild-last-book appears only when a previous book exists.

> **The Close obeys the three-beat budget** (§10.2). Everything else goes in the debrief panel.
> Both fences exist because additions accumulate on the two screens that can least afford them.

Reuse `AccentCard`, `HexChip`, `HexButton`, `league_common.dart`. Every string needs `app_en.arb` +
`app_ms.arb` + `app_ar.arb`; Arabic is RTL. **This CR adds a new user-facing string set and carries
`retranslate:[ar,ms]`.**

## 14. Integrity guards

Structural, per CR040 — *prompt instructions are not controls*.

1. **Mock-priced days void the run, loudly.** `price_source` is already tracked end-to-end. A P&L
   game scored on invented prices is a score generator — one of D-060's three objections, still
   live. The board must state which days.
2. **NAV snapshots are append-only, user-scoped, and must NOT foreign-key to `sim_portfolios`.**
   `sim_engine.reset_portfolio()` (`sim_engine.py:394`) hard-deletes the portfolio row and every
   `SimTradeRow`; a FK would cascade the game's history away. Key on `user_id` + `run_id` — the
   reset-immune shape `reputation_events` already proves.
3. **One live run per cadence** (§4.1) — closes the parallel-entry farm.
4. **Thin fields score benchmark-relative and award no title** (§6.6) — closes the beat-nobody farm
   and the `n = 1` divide-by-zero.
5. **Restart → forfeit + career debit + minimum debit** (§6.7).
6. **Entry closes at the bell** — no late entry into a running field.
7. **Fills only during market hours** (§5.1) — closes the stale-price time machine.
8. **Stale or halted marks recorded and flagged**, never silently carried forward.
9. **No board renders another player's absolute AMI Cash** — layer 1 is private (§3.2).
10. **Smurfing** — `user_devices.device_install_id` exists; the title multiplier removes most of the
    payoff.
11. **The training safety floor is untouched.** No-rules applies to the game path only (§7).
12. **House desks pay identical trading costs** (§5.3). Their P&L is computed exactly as a human's;
    a fee-exempt desk would be a tuned result by the back door.
13. **The trading cost is burned, never pooled.** A pool of forfeited stakes is a wagered stake and
    §15's prize leg dies. Asserted as a test, not a convention.
14. **The career ledger clamps at zero — the ledger, not the display** (§6.5). The number on screen
    is always the number in the table.
15. **No paid feature alters a run's inputs, scoring, or ranking** (§3.1.1). Post-close explanation
    is the entire permitted surface for plan tier.
16. **Mirrors are private** (§10.4). Intent, wildness, counterfactuals and heat never render on a
    board or in another player's view.
17. **Challenge completions pay markers, never career points** (§8.4) — otherwise self-imposed
    constraints become farmable.
18. **The finish stipend requires a real run** — entered, ≥ 1 executed trade, not forfeited, held to
    close. Without all four it is a check-in in disguise, which §4 of the dark-pattern register
    refuses.

**App Store declaration.** CR064 bound the zero-P&L rule as *a binding invariant, not a design
note*, because the "simulated gambling: **No**" answer depends on it. That declaration must be
re-validated before this ships. Of D-060's three original objections, the third — *"needs NAV-history

**The three-leg test, and where this design sits:**

| Leg | Status |
|---|---|
| **Consideration** | **Absent by design** — entry is free and can never cost anything (§4.2) |
| **Prize** | **Absent by design** — rewards are cosmetic and non-purchasable (§8, rules 1 and 2) |

**What increases the risk, accepted by decision:** §7's no-rules arena, and the halal-screen bypass.

**What strengthens the skill leg — compile this for the lawyer.** Chance-versus-skill is the only
live leg, and Amendment D added four mechanics that all reward deliberation over impulse:

- **The trading cost** (§5.3) prices churn, so spray-and-pray is measurably worse than a thesis.
- **Queue-first execution** (§5.1) means the primary flow is *plan the book the evening before*, not
  react to a tick.
- **One beat per market close** (§10) caps the arousal loop by design.
- **The lab framing** (§1.1) inverts the objection: the game is an instrument that **falsifies**
  gambling psychology with the player's own data — *the wider you go, the less likely you are to
  make money*, discovered from personal run history rather than asserted in a lesson.

That set is evidence, not just good design: **the game's mechanics systematically price out impulse
play.** It belongs in the §15 legal pass as an argument, not merely as a description.

**One new thing the lawyer must see: the post-mortem is a paid feature** (§3.1.1, §10.2). Entry
stays free and the prize stays cosmetic, so "no purchase necessary" is undisturbed — but a reviewer
will ask whether paying improves the chance of winning. The answer is that it buys *explanation of a
finished run*, never an input to a live one, and that the free Close is complete on its own. Better
to hand them that paragraph than let them find it.

**What must stay true forever:** §8's rules 1 and 2. Awarding credits on a P&L outcome converts the
Record into money and re-introduces *prize* — that is the step that needs the lawyer.

D-064 made `competition_rules.md` standalone *specifically* so scoring changes could re-version it
without a full ToS re-issue. That pays off exactly here.

## 16. Risks

1. **Store rejection or a forced declaration change.** The largest risk, and binary. Mitigated by
   phasing (§17) so no compliance surface ships until the legal work lands.
2. **No arena rules means the weekly board is substantially luck.** Accepted (§7). Cadence prestige
   ordering absorbs part of it; roadmap modes #9 and #10 exist to give skill somewhere to show.
   **Mitigation that changes nothing today:** keep recording per-period mandate compliance on the
   *training* path, so a "Clean Run" mark could switch on later with no backfill.
3. **The halal-screen bypass** (§7) — a brand risk in GCC markets, mitigated only by disclosure.
4. **Thin fields are the near-certain case at alpha.** With a handful of users, **every**
   long-cadence field is below the placement threshold, so §6.6's benchmark path is the *default*
   code path for months and must ship in phase 1.
5. **Mock-price frequency on Alpha is unmeasured.** If the yfinance fallback fires often, §14.1
   voids runs routinely and the unranked state becomes the common case. Worth measuring before any
   board goes live.
6. **Career points that can fall may demoralise** — particularly a new player who blows up twice in
   month one, the cohort most likely to churn. The zero floor caps the damage; §8.4's progress
   markers are what carry retention.
7. **Five concurrent books is real cognitive load** even at one-per-cadence. Weekly-first phasing
   means this is never tested at full width until the loop is proven.
8. **Shell rewrite coupling** (§13.1). CR109 + CR133 landing together is a large simultaneous
   change to navigation *and* a new subsystem — the highest-regression-risk moment in the project.
9. **This CR deletes the app's only daily-return mechanic.** The streak lives in
   `reputation_service.py:271`, which Amendment A removes — and the game's heartbeat is *weekly*.
   The daily challenge content survives (it only stops awarding), but net, there are fewer reasons
   to open the app daily after this ships than before. See
   [`playability_review.md`](playability_review.md) §3; the recommended fix is to re-home a streak
   onto **runs finished / periods entered** rather than check-ins, which is a query over
   `game_entries`, not a subsystem.
10. **The game is the app's *differently* supported mode** — re-worded by Amendment D. Training has
    12 agents and a safety floor; the game deliberately has neither, yet it is scored and permanent.
    The lab framing (§1.1) resolves what this originally called *unsupported*: the support is
    **measurement rather than advice** — the mirrors of §10.4, the attribution on the daily beat,
    the counterfactuals at the Close. A beginner's first action is still "pick a stock", but §11.1
    now bounds it: the first run is a duel against the Index Desk. Gating first entry behind a
    training milestone remains open and remains Saiful's call, since it delays the headline feature.
11. **House desks feed real users' career points.** Saiful chose to let disclosed desks count
    toward scoring (§11.2), which means the desk roster is a lever on everyone's permanent Record.
    Two guards keep it honest: performance is **computed, never fabricated**, so nobody is tuning
    difficulty; and desks are **excluded from titles**, so the top of the ladder stays human. Revisit
    if the roster ever needs hand-weighting to feel right — that would be the moment it became a
    difficulty dial. The undisclosed variant was considered and **reversed by Saiful**: *"if we do
    not disclose that it's a bot… the user is copying what they thought is a person!"* — the record
    of that reasoning is in §11.2 because it is the argument that should stop anyone re-proposing it.
12. **Two currencies plus career points.** `credit_balance` (monetized), AMI Cash (the stake) and
   career points (the score). Amendment A removed `users.reputation`, so three numbers rather than
   four — still worth a naming pass (§18.1).
13. **Paid coaching on a scored board is a perception risk even though it is not pay-to-win**
    (§3.1.1). Two players enter the same field on identical terms and only one is coached
    afterwards. Structurally sound — nothing paid touches a live run — but *"the paying players get
    better"* is a sentence a user can form, and the answer has to be ready before it is asked, not
    after. The mitigation is that the free Close is complete, and that the boast the game is built
    around (*beat the market*) needs no subscription to earn.
14. **The fee and the stipend must ship together** (§6.5). The fee is scoring-neutral under
    placement and scoring-negative under the benchmark, so shipping it alone tilts the median early
    player's Record negative — the exact cohort with the least reason to stay. Neither is a
    standalone slice.
15. **The two crowded screens.** Entry and the Close each accumulated six-plus additions during the
    review series. §13.3's fences hold the line, but every future feature will want one of those two
    screens, and the budget has to be defended rather than assumed.

## 17. Phasing — thin slices

**Full detail, with schema, contracts, fences and per-slice acceptance, is in
[`implementation_plan.md`](implementation_plan.md).** Summary here.

This CR reads as a very large delivery. Most of it is **not on the critical path**, because of what
§6.6 surfaced: at alpha field sizes the entire Contest layer is nearly inert — every field sits
below the placement threshold, so runs score against the benchmark anyway. **Cohorts, demand
gating, the placement curve, titles and the multiplier are scale features, not launch features.**
Building them first means building machinery that cannot run.

| Slice | Ships | Compliance surface |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · The NAV spine** | `portfolio_nav_daily`, the tick, `twr.py`, and the **equity curve on the training portfolio**. Valuable alone — the app cannot draw a portfolio's history today. | **zero** |
| **2 · The run** | Game portfolios, entry, the game trade path with the §5.1 market-hours rule, one-per-cadence, restart, the `merge_service` fix. No score, no board. | **zero** |
| **3 · The close** | Scoring pass, **benchmark-relative scoring**, the career-point ledger, `VOID`, the Close screen, the Record surface. **First playable game.** | yes — first board-like surface |
| **4 · The field** | The open board, placement, cadence weights, titles, ×0.5, minimum forfeit debit, demand-gated rolling starts. **Turns on when fields clear `n ≥ 8`.** | yes |
| **5 · Ceremony + push** | The period arc (§10); push is its own CR. | no new |
| **6 · Other cadences** | Monthly, then Q/H/Y — **config over the same machinery.** | no new |
| **7 · Remove the old league** | §13.4. Separable; safe first or last. | no |
| **8 · Rewards** | Flair, agent treatments, the Room plaque, progress markers. Needs slice 4. | no |

**Slices 1–2 carry zero compliance surface**, so engineering starts immediately while the §15 legal

**The volume is not the risk — the coupling is** (§13.1). CR109 + CR133 can be *developed* together
and *shipped* apart: the game can live behind the Floor card until CR133's destination exists.
Recorded here so it is a choice rather than a discovery.

Child CRs are deliberately **not minted** while this is design stage.

### 17.1 Staged commitment — where the money goes, and the gates

The direct answer to *"before I commit to a HUGE development cost."* The slice table above is the
build order; this is the **funding** order, and the difference matters: **status systems only pay
once there is a crowd to hold status in.** The crowd is built by the week loop, and the week loop is
cheap.

**Stage 1 — the addictive core.** Slices 1, 2, 3 and duels, plus everything Amendment D added to
them: the finish stipend and the alpha→points formula (§6.5, §6.6.1 — the structural pair), the
milestone first rung, the first-run duel, the re-entry CTA and its two metrics, daily-beat
attribution, the trade ticket, the mirrors, the PR board, the streak with its bye week, handle
keep-or-reroll, Phase A reactions, and the share card. Ceremony ships in its slice-3 form — the
Close and Wind-Up screens on the three-beat budget — not the full slice-5 arc.

**This is a complete three-loop game at weekly cadence**, and it is roughly a third of the plan's
surface. It contains every hook that matters.

> **Gate 1 — before funding slice 4** (placement, titles, the multiplier, demand-gated starts):
> `first-run activation` and `close → re-entry` meet targets **Saiful sets before the build**, so
> the gate cannot be argued backwards from the data. If re-entry is weak, no amount of placement
> machinery fixes it — the week loop itself needs work.

**Stage 2.** Slice 4, the monthly cadence, the full ceremony arc, push (its own CR — the
final-stretch beat is inert without it), private fields (§11.4), the skill stat (§11.6), and the
Record's full information hierarchy.

> **Gate 2 — before funding Q/H/Y, cosmetics and roadmap modes:** fields clearing `n ≥ 8`
> organically, and the season loop showing life — title rungs being crossed, duel records accruing.

**Stage 3.** Long cadences, slice 8 rewards, and the roadmap — with **#9 (risk-adjusted) and #8
(blind runs) early**, both XS and both skill-legible.

**The three loops this funds**, so anything that strengthens none of them can be recognised as
decoration:

| Loop | Cycle | Carried by | Keystone metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day** | one US close | the beat card with attribution (§10.4) | beat-card opens/day |
| **Week** | one run | enter → beats → stretch → settle → Close → **re-enter** (§10.3) | **close → re-entry rate** |
| **Season** | the Record | stipend + first rung + titles + duel W–L + streak of finishes | **% of players whose counter moved** |

The week loop is the game. The day loop keeps it warm. The season loop is why the week loop does not
exhaust itself — and it is the loop both of §6.5's structural problems sat in, which is why they were
the two findings that mattered most.

**Naming — not a tunable, and the only one that blocks copy work:**

   repo-wide search returns zero hits, so the string set starts clean. Still unnamed: **the game

   player's counter at every close without making titles farmable by attendance; the fee must price

   with a 1.00 minimum. Neither ships without the other.
3. **`ALPHA_FULL`** (§6.6.1) — the alpha that pays a full win. Chosen so a typical good week
   (~+1–2% excess) pays in the same range as a good placement finish.
4. **`ENTRY_FEE_BPS`** (§6.6.2) — the achievable-benchmark adjustment. Follows directly from the fee.
5. **Thin-field thresholds** (§6.6) — placement `n ≥ 8`, titles `n ≥ 20`, max wait 30 days, min
   entrants 8. Set together against a **measured** alpha entry rate, not guessed.
6. **Title thresholds** (§6.4) — 500 / 2,500 / 10,000 / 30,000 above the milestone rung. Balance
   against the +140 a weekly win pays at analyst.
7. **`MIN_FORFEIT_DEBIT`** (§6.7) — the size of the floor.
8. **Duel career-point deltas** by cadence (§11.1).

**Standalone:**

9. **The benchmark index** (§6.6) — the *basis* is settled (price-only, §6.6.3); which index is not.
   SPY is the obvious default, but a small-cap-heavy run measured against a mega-cap index reads as
   skill or failure that is really style drift. QQQ, IWM and equal-weight each bias differently.
10. **Blowup threshold** for the proactive restart offer (~50%?).
11. **Settlement freeze duration** — ~1h is a guess; anticipation versus annoyance.
12. **Queued-order visibility** (§5.1) — does an evening order show as pending on the board, or stay
    private until it fills? More load-bearing now that queue-first is the primary flow.
13. **Escalator `N` and multiplier** (§5.3.2) — reserve; only sized if Gate 1 says the flat fee is
    too blunt.

**Saiful's calls, not tuning:**

14. **Gate 1 and Gate 2 targets** (§17.1) — activation and re-entry rates. **Must be set before the
    build**, or the gate can be argued backwards from whatever the data turns out to be.
15. **Gating first entry behind a training milestone** (§16.10) — it delays the headline feature.

## 19. Acceptance

**Scoring and the layers**

- TWR chain-links across a restart: a −40% run followed by a fresh stake does **not** read as flat.
- Career points balance: 52 weekly wins ≈ one annual win, same title.
- No board surface renders an absolute AMI Cash figure for any user other than the viewer.
- **No reward, title or threshold alters starting capital, available data, or any trade
  constraint** — the §3.1 one-way rule, asserted as a test.

**Entry and lifecycle**

- Entering a second run of a cadence the player already holds is **refused** (§4.1).
- Entry never costs credits, money or a consumable (§4.2).
- A field that never reaches its minimum goes `ABANDONED` and its entrants are rolled forward with
  no debit (§4.3, §12).
- Scoring at `SETTLING → CLOSED` is idempotent across a container restart.

**Trade rules**

- An order placed outside US market hours does **not** fill at a stale price; it queues to the next
  open, and the UI says so (§5.1).
- A queued order does not appear in a NAV snapshot before it fills.
- Positions open at close are marked, not liquidated (§5.2).

**Thin fields**

- **A field of one scores, does not crash, and does not pay a win.** `n = 1` and `n = 2` never reach
  the placement formula; both route to benchmark-relative scoring and neither awards a title.
- A thin-field run states its basis — field size and benchmark — on the board and in the Close.

**Restart**

- A restart forfeits the run **and** debits career points at the standing held.
- Restarting and riding a run to its close produce the **same** career-point cost, plus the
  minimum-debit floor.
- Forfeiting from mid-field costs the minimum debit, not zero.
- Career points never render below zero, and the signed net plus the forfeit count are both visible.

**Integrity and edges**

- The game submit path never consults a mandate; the **training** path still rejects mandate
  breaches — a regression test on both sides of §7.
- The game entry surface renders the no-rules disclosure, naming the mandate and the screens that do
  not apply.
- A mock-priced day voids the run and the UI states which days.
- NAV snapshots survive `reset_portfolio()`.
- **A claimed account keeps its runs, entries and career points** — the `merge_service` gap in §12.
- Deleting an account mid-run removes the entry and re-ranks the field at close, not retroactively.
- Titles recompute from the career-point total when any run closes — no synchronized roll exists
  anywhere in the scoring path.

**The trading cost** (§5.3)

- A BUY of 1,000 notional deducts **1,001.00**; a 50-notional BUY deducts **51.00** (the minimum
  applies). SELL proceeds arrive net.
- Two runs with identical gross trade sequences, one padded with 40 wash fills: **the padded run's
  TWR is lower by the fee drag** — asserted numerically, not by inspection.
- The ticket shows the cost before confirm; a queued order shows the estimate at placement and the
  actual on the fill.
- **Training-path trades carry zero fee** — regression on both sides of the split, like §7.
- A desk's NAV series reflects the same costs a human's does.
- **The fee does not break the TWR chain** — it is not a capital event.
- Fees are burned: no table, no counter and no field-level total ever accumulates them.

**Scoring corrections** (Amendment D)

- **The ledger clamps at zero, not the display.** A player at 0 who scores +8 shows **8**, never 0 —
  asserted directly against the stored ledger, since this is the failure the original wording caused.
- A finished run pays the stipend; a **forfeited** run, a run with **zero executed trades**, and a
  run not held to close each pay **nothing**.
- **The alpha path is capped**: no benchmark-relative outcome, at any alpha, pays more than winning
  a field outright.
- Alpha is scored against the **achievable** benchmark (net of one entry fee) while the Close
  **displays** the gross comparison — both asserted, because the point is that they differ.
- The first title rung fires on **3 finished runs with no forfeit**, and never on points.
- Placement points are **capped in a private field**; the finish stipend **still pays** there.

**Ceremony and surfaces**

- The Close renders **three beats**; every other block is reachable only from the debrief panel.
- **A free user's Close contains rank, delta, curve and both counterfactual lines.** The paid
  post-mortem is absent, and **nothing else is** — asserted as a test, because this is the fence
  that keeps the paywall out of the peak-end moment.
- No upgrade prompt renders inside the three beats.
- The agent post-mortem is generated from a **fixed template over computed run data**; a test
  asserts no forward-looking field exists in that template (CR038 — not a prompt instruction).
- Near-miss copy never renders on a loss.
- Entry is reachable in **three taps**; the no-rules disclosure renders full-text on first entry and
  as a chip thereafter.
- Intent tag, wildness index, counterfactuals and heat **never appear in any board or opponent-facing
  payload** — asserted at the serializer, not the widget.

**Engine gaps**

- A 4:1 split mid-run leaves NAV **continuous**, adjusts quantity and basis, flags the row, and
  **does not break the TWR chain**.
- A dividend-paying holding accrues **no** cash at MVP, and the benchmark series is **price-only** —
  asserted together, since the pair is the point.

**Build**

- `pytest backend/tests/unit/ -q` green; `flutter test` + `flutter analyze --no-fatal-infos` green.
