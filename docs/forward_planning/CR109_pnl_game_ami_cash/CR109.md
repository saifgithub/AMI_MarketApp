**Amendment B — 2026-07-30, this revision. Saiful rejected round 1 as a game.**

> *I feel there is a weakness here, with the game and the training combined. I am wondering

He is right, and the sharp way to say why: **five concurrent windows over one NAV curve is

| Round 1 | Now |

## 1. Why

Saiful: *"You are my new game designer for the app. This is a game of portfolio success.
think about the game, how to be fair to new players, and to older players, high score, etc
etc."*

Then, setting the frame when asked where the D-060 line sits:

> *"This is a new game. Specifically designed for P&L of your AMI CASH. Measured only by
> the win/loss during the game period. game period could be weekly, monthly, quaterly, 6th
> months, annual cadence."*

**What exists today measures effort, not skill.** The shipped competition —
`reputation_service.py` (519 lines) + `league_service.py` (326 lines) — scores process only:
`challenge_correct: 5`, `lesson_passed: 5`, `agent_unlocked: 10`, `room_verdict: 3`,
`trade_disciplined: 2`. Its own source states the constraint: *"no P&L-linked event exists
or ever will (store declaration + D-060)."* A user can top the weekly league without their
portfolio ever having made money, and nothing in the product tells them whether they are any
good at the thing the product is about.

## 2. Two surfaces, cleanly split

Saiful, on whether the game convenes the analyst Room:

> *"Do we convene rooms for games, I think we do not. **Games is only about trade.**"*

That settles the separate-account question by implication. Training has the Room, the 12
agents and a mandate; the game has none of them. Two rulesets that different cannot share one
portfolio row.

| | Training | The Game |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio | existing `sim_portfolios`, **unchanged** | new, one per run |
| Room / 12 agents | yes | **no** |
| Mandate + safety floor | yes, enforced at submit | **none** (§5) |
| Scored | never | always |
| Reset | free, unlimited | restart = forfeit + career debit |
| Compliance surface | zero | all of it |

**The frame: the Room is the classroom, the game is the exam.** You prove you internalised
what the agents taught, without the agents. This also gives the product's own fantasy — you
are the CEO of a 12-agent analyst desk — something to pay off, which round 1 never used.

### 2.1 Why the split is load-bearing, not cosmetic

Three verified facts, and the middle one is the weakness Saiful was smelling:

1. **`submit()` hard-rejects mandate breaches today.** `sim_engine.py:607-640` calls
   `check_mandate_compliance` and returns `accepted=False` on position-size, sector,
   drawdown, cooldown or over-trading violations. This **corrects round 1's §9.2**: the
   "pure P&L rewards 100% concentration" risk was overstated for the training path — that
   trade cannot be submitted at all.
2. **The mandate is per-*user*, and the user can edit it.** `MandateRow` is keyed on
   `user_id` and `mandate_store.py:6` records that *"Settings → My Mandate can edit a single
   user's mandate."* On a shared account, **loosening your own mandate becomes a
   win-the-game move** — the mandate is the curriculum's core instrument, and the game would
   turn it into a min-max dial. That is "the game and the training combined", concretely.
3. **`resolve_mandate` falls back to a client-supplied `mandate_override`.**
   `mandate_store.py:193` prefers the stored mandate, but onboarding is anonymous-first and
   the row is only created at claim (`api/auth.py:82`), so an anonymous player has no stored
   mandate and **supplies their own rules** via the request body (`api/sim.py:67`). Moot
   under §5's no-rules decision, but it is why the game must not reuse the training submit
   path unexamined.

Compliance also improves: the *training simulator* — which is what the App Store declaration
actually describes — stays P&L-unscored, and the game becomes a separable surface that can be
feature-flagged off without gutting the product.

## 3. A run is an entry, not an account

Entering a cadence opens a **run**: **10,000 AMI Cash**, the same stake for every entrant at
every cadence, with its own open and close, its own board, scored on TWR. AMI Cash is the
game's money — Saiful: *"AMI Cash is the 'money' used in the game. Like Monopoly money."*

**Why this is the fix for Amendment B.** Fresh capital per entry means **everyone starts dead
equal on day one**, so new-vs-veteran fairness is structural rather than tuned — and unlike
five windows over one curve, each entry is a genuinely separate decision with its own strategy
and its own tension. The mid-period-join problem disappears entirely, every run has a natural
ceremony boundary (§7), restart is just re-entry, and alpha can ship **one** cadence without
designing the others away.

**Entry closes at the bell.** You enter before the run opens or wait for the next one. A race
has a start line, and the closing window is itself an anticipation beat.

**Rolling starts for the long cadences.** Weekly and monthly run on fixed calendar starts;
Quarter / Half / Annual open on a rolling basis, so a player arriving in March isn't benched
until January. Rolling fields are **demand-gated, not calendar-gated** — see §4.6, which is
where rolling starts turn out to cost something.

### 3.1 One live run per cadence, maximum

Saiful asked: *"can you enter multiple long term games?"* **No** — and the reason is an
exploit in §4's curve, not a UI limit.

Career points pay `+100` for a win and only `−40` for a loss, so **parallel entries are
strictly +EV.** With rolling monthly Annual starts and no entry limit, the optimal play is to
hold 12 concurrent Annual runs, play each maximally aggressive, and let the best one place
high while the losers cost 40% of what the winner pays. That is the same shape as the
restart-on-red exploit the career debit already closes.

So a player may hold at most one Weekly + one Monthly + one Quarterly + one Half + one Annual
— five books, **never two of a kind.** Rolling starts still do their job: you never wait more
than a month to begin a long run, you just can't stack them.

### 3.2 The loop

```text
ENTER  →  TRADE  →  DAILY BEAT  →  FINAL STRETCH  →  SETTLE  →  CLOSE  →  RE-ENTER
  ↑                                                                            │
  └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Daily decision, weekly resolution at the shortest cadence, seasonal prestige at the longest.
§7 is this loop's ritual layer.

## 4. Scoring — P&L to points

Two numbers doing two different jobs. Keeping them separate is most of the design.

### 4.1 Board rank — no formula

Raw run TWR, sorted. P&L becomes a percentage; the percentage is the rank. Nothing else.

```text
per sub-period between capital events:  rᵢ  = (NAV_end / NAV_start) − 1
run return:                             TWR = Π(1 + rᵢ) − 1
```

Chain-linked sub-period returns split at capital events — the GIPS standard. It earns its
place three times:

- **Restart becomes mathematically clean** — a restart is just a capital event.
- **Stake size stops mattering.** [`tiers_and_pricing.md`](../../initial_specs/06_monetization/tiers_and_pricing.md)
  promises $10K / $100K / $1M sim capital by plan (unbuilt — `_STARTING_CAPITAL = 10_000.0`
  is hardcoded at `sim_engine.py:195`). Ranking on % TWR makes larger capital a realism
  feature, never a rank advantage.
- Any future top-up is neutralised automatically.

> **Invariant, to be written down and tested: boards rank on % TWR only, never absolute AMI
> Cash.** The moment a board ranks money, capital tier becomes pay-to-win and the "no
> purchase necessary" position in [`competition_rules.md`](../../../legal/policies/competition_rules.md)
> collapses with it.

### 4.2 Career points — placement, never raw return

**This is the load-bearing choice.** If points came off the percentage directly, a bull
quarter would hand everyone points and a bear quarter would debit everyone — career points
would record *when a player played* rather than *how well*, and a mediocre player who joined
during a rally would outscore a strong one who joined during a crash. That breaks precisely
the fairness §3 exists to protect. Placement cancels the market out for free: everyone in a
field faced the same one.

```text
career_points = base(p) × cadence_weight × title_multiplier
p = (n − rank) / (n − 1)          over the WHOLE open field · 1.0 = first, 0.0 = last

p ≥ 0.5   base = +100 × ((p − 0.5) × 2) ^ 1.5      convex — the very top is worth a lot
p < 0.5   base = − 40 × ((0.5 − p) × 2)            linear and shallow
```

Winning is worth 2.5× what losing costs; the damping is in the curve, not bolted on after.

| Rank of 30 | 1 | 3 | 6 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | +100 | +80 | +53 | +23 | ~0 | −12 | −26 | −40 |

Placement over a large open field stays well-behaved — 400th of 5000 gives `p ≈ 0.92`.

### 4.3 Cadence weight is asymmetric

Gains scale with committed time; losses with its **square root**. A long *bad* run is mostly
market conditions, a long *good* run is hard to fake, and a player should not be crushed for
having been invested through a bear half-year.

| | Week | Month | Quarter | Half | Year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gain × | 1 | 4 | 13 | 26 | 52 |
| Loss × | 1 | 2 | 3.6 | 5.1 | 7.2 |

The loss row is √cadence: winning the year is worth 52× winning a week, but losing the year
costs only 7.2×, because √52 ≈ 7.2. It is one number and it is retunable from real data
without touching anything else in the design.

**Balance check:** win the year at Analyst = `100 × 52 × 1.4` = **+7,280**. Win 52 weeks =
`100 × 1 × 1.4 × 52` = **+7,280**. Neither cadence is a farm.

### 4.4 Titles are thresholds, not a promotion event

Saiful chose to inherit the five shipped names but spotted the consequence of rolling starts:
*"since we have rolling starts, we need to consider how this is done."* With staggered run
closes there is no synchronized boundary to promote on, which is exactly what
`league_service.weekly_roll()` relied upon.

So titles are driven off the **running career-point total**, recomputed whenever any run
closes. No roll, no cohort assembly, no relegation event — relegation is just the total
falling back through a threshold. Simpler than the league it replaces, and it fits rolling
starts exactly.

| Title | `title_multiplier` | Threshold (first cut) |
|---|---|---|
| apprentice | 1.0 | 0 |
| analyst | 1.4 | 500 |
| trader | 1.6 | 2,500 |
| senior | 1.8 | 10,000 |
| floor_veteran | 2.0 | 30,000 |

The multiplier is **fixed at run open** so it cannot drift mid-run. Thresholds are a first cut
to be balanced against the +140 a weekly win pays at analyst (§12.3).

### 4.5 Decided values

- **×0.5 partial credit while absolute TWR is negative.** Top of the field in a bear run still
  scores, at half. Progression never stalls through a downturn — which would punish anyone who
  joined during one — and losing money never pays like winning.
- **Career points floor at zero.** The ledger is signed (restarts debit it) but the headline
  cannot go below 0. Consequence handled in §4.6: a player sitting at 0 has nothing left to
  lose, so the restart debit stops deterring exactly the newest players.

### 4.6 Thin fields — the cost of rolling starts

Saiful, on §3's rolling starts:

> *"we need to think the cohorts for the longer game, since we allow rolling start. on a
> popular platform, this is great. but on a new platform, we may find a month with a single
> player!"*

This is the most serious hole in the design and it is three problems, not one:

1. **`p = (n − rank)/(n − 1)` is undefined at `n = 1`** and degenerate at `n = 2`, where it is
   either exactly 1.0 or exactly 0.0 — maximum payout or maximum debit on a coin flip.
2. **A field of one pays the largest prize in the game for beating nobody.** First of one at the
   annual cadence is `+100 × 52 × title_multiplier`. On a thin platform the rational play becomes
   *find the rolling start month nobody entered and win it by default.*
3. **Rolling starts break the property that justifies placement scoring in the first place.**
   §4.2 chose placement precisely because *everyone in a field faced the same market*, which
   cancels market beta for free. Different start months face different markets — so thin fields
   **cannot** be fixed by merging cohorts across start dates, which is the obvious first idea.

Note the problem is confined to the long cadences. Weekly and monthly are calendar-driven, so
every player is in the same field and thinness only means a small field, never a stale one.

**The fix, three parts:**

- **Demand-gated starts for Quarter / Half / Annual.** A rolling field opens when it reaches a
  minimum entrant count **or** at a maximum wait, whichever comes first — not on a fixed monthly
  calendar. This self-scales: monthly or faster on a busy platform, quarterly on a new one, with
  no tuning. It preserves the same-market property because everyone in a field still starts on
  the same day. The lobby must show the queue honestly ("7 of 8 entered — opens when full, or in
  11 days").
- **Benchmark-relative career points when the field is below the placement threshold.** Score
  `alpha = TWR − benchmark TWR` over the same window instead of placement. This is not a
  fallback hack: it preserves the *exact* property placement was chosen for — market
  cancellation — while working at any field size including `n = 1`, and it kills the
  beat-nobody farm because you are measured against the index rather than against an empty
  field. It is also how real funds are judged. **No new infrastructure:**
  `sim_engine.current_history(ticker, period)` serves any ticker including SPY and already
  returns a source flag for the §10.1 mock-price guard, and `trading_math/portfolio_stats.py`
  already ships `beta()`.
- **Titles require a real field.** A thin field can still *score*, but "2026 Annual Champion"
  requires a minimum field size. Prestige needs witnesses.

**Degrade loudly** (CR040): a thin-field run states its scoring basis on the board and in the
Close — *"Field of 3. Scored against the S&P 500, not against the field."* Silently switching
scoring basis is exactly the failure this rule exists to prevent.

First-cut thresholds, to be balanced (§14): placement needs `n ≥ 8`; titles need `n ≥ 20`;
maximum lobby wait 30 days.

### 4.7 Restart — rescues capital, never the record

Always available; nobody is ever trapped. On restart: the run is **FORFEIT** — removed from
the board, so the player neither holds a rank nor takes one from anyone else — and a fresh run
can be entered.

**The loss still debits career points**, scored at the standing held when they walked. This is
Saiful's rule (*"when you re-start, the points you've lost will still hit your career
points"*) and it closes the biggest asymmetry in the design: without it, restart is free when
losing, so the rational strategy is *restart on red, ride on green*, and every result anyone
records is a survivor. With it, **restarting and riding it out cost the same**, so the hit
cannot be dodged and restarting can never improve an outcome. What the player actually chooses
is *crystallise now and get fresh capital* versus *hold and keep the option of recovering*.

**Re-entry is immediate, not exile.** An earlier draft benched the player until the next
period; with a debit applied on top that punishes the same act twice, and for a Half or Year
it means months on the bench. The debit *is* the price.

**The entry model opens a churn hole that round 1 did not have.** The debit is
placement-based, so a player sitting mid-field (`p ≈ 0.5`) is debited ≈ **0** — forfeiting
from mid-field is free. With rolling starts there is always another field to join, so the play
becomes: enter, and the moment it isn't going well, forfeit at par and re-enter. The zero
floor makes this worse for the players most likely to do it — at 0 there is nothing to debit.
Two additive fixes, neither of which is an arena rule:

- **A minimum forfeit debit** — forfeiting costs at least a fixed floor per cadence regardless
  of standing, so churn is never free. Sized so one forfeit stays cheaper than a genuinely bad
  finish; the mercy rule must remain merciful.
- **Forfeits counted visibly on the career record** — runs entered, finished, forfeited. A
  forfeit-heavy record reads as what it is with no mechanical penalty at all.

The existing 24h reset cooldown stays and blunts weekly churn; it does nothing for annual
churn, which is what the minimum debit is for. Below a blowup threshold (~NAV ≤ 50% of stake)
the app *offers* the restart rather than making the user hunt for it.

## 5. No arena rules — and how that is implemented honestly

Saiful, asked whether the game should run a locked competition mandate:

> *"do games have mandate? I think games do not have mandate. It's up to you if you want to
> follow your mandates. There should not be any mandate. Games is about showing your
> capabilities. your mandates is not in play. convince me otherwise"*

He was shown the counter-argument and reaffirmed the decision. **The game submit path does not
call `check_mandate_compliance`.** No position cap, no sector cap, no drawdown cap, no
cooldown, no over-trading limit, **and no halal / locale / long_only screen.**

The counter-argument, recorded because it was made and rejected rather than never raised:

1. **The halal and locale screens are not risk rules, and they live inside that same
   function.** `safety_floor.py:289` is `if c.halal:` — driven by a user-set mandate flag —
   and the locale/market-availability screen sits at `:339`. Bypassing the compliance check
   bypasses both.
2. **The store declaration.** CR064 bound the zero-P&L rule as a binding invariant because
   "simulated gambling: **No**" depends on it. A P&L contest with no position limits, no risk
   constraints and a restart button after a blowup is the most gambling-shaped version of
   this. One uniform position cap would have been the cheapest artefact to put in front of a
   reviewer.
3. **Placement stops measuring skill at short cadences.** With no position cap, optimal weekly
   play is 100% of stake into the highest-volatility name available; if everyone plays
   optimally the weekly board is decided by which coin landed heads, and career points then
   record luck — the one thing §4.2 exists to prevent. It washes out by the annual.

**Accepted risk, explicit (Saiful, 2026-07-30).** A user who declared halal-only in their
mandate trades **unscreened** on a scored path, and locale restrictions do not apply there.
Recorded here the way D-060 was recorded, not glossed.

**Two obligations follow, neither of which is a gate:**

- **Disclose it.** The entry surface states plainly that the arena has no constraints and that
  your mandate and screens do not apply here. A screen that silently stops existing is exactly
  the CR040 failure mode (*"if this fires constantly and silently, what does the user end up
  believing?"*); saying so out loud is not a rule.
- **Never let the training path drift.** `check_mandate_compliance` stays mandatory on the
  training submit path — the uncoachable safety floor is untouched by this CR.

The position cap is not lost, it is **re-homed as an opt-in game mode** — survival runs, mode
#10 in [`games_roadmap.md`](games_roadmap.md). An opt-in mode is a different product from a
global rule.

## 6. Fairness

**New players**

1. **Fresh capital per entry** — everyone starts dead equal on day one of every run. This is
   the core mechanism; everything else is support. It is strictly stronger than round 1's
   "every window opens at 0%", because there is no carried book either.
2. TWR — capital size is irrelevant to rank.
3. No mid-period join problem: entry closes at the bell, so every entrant runs the same clock.
4. Weekly ships first, so a day-one player has a real, scored, ceremonied result in **5
   trading days**.
5. No global all-time ranked ladder. Career points are personal — Saiful's *"for personal
   satisfactions"* — never a lifetime leaderboard, which is structurally unwinnable for anyone
   who joined later.

**Veterans**

1. Career points persist forever as a signed net record (floored at zero).
2. Long cadences are the prestige events; the annual title is the one that means something.
3. `title_multiplier` rewards winning while carrying a higher title, which also removes the
   incentive to smurf.
4. Permanent titles — "2026 Annual Champion" never expires.
5. Multi-category Annual Awards (§7) mean depth of play is recognised in more than one way.

**MVP field structure: one open field per cadence per period.** Saiful: *"what we deliver in
MVP is only open games, meaning all players all at the same time, no duals."* This drops round
1's cohorts of ≤30 and the `_assemble_week` shuffle-and-chunk reuse. Bracketed fields split by
title are laned as roadmap mode #5 for when fields grow large enough to need them.

**The fairness argument has a floor, and it is §4.6.** An open field is only fair while it is a
field; at `n = 1` or `n = 2` placement is undefined or a coin flip, and on a young platform
rolling long-cadence starts produce exactly that. §4.6 is therefore load-bearing for this
section, not an edge case appended to it.

## 7. The period arc — the end-game ceremony

Round 1 rolled periods silently. There was no close-out moment, no reveal, no trophy, no
shareable result — one of Amendment B's three named gaps.

| Beat | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Entries close** | countdown; the last-call moment |
| **The bell** | field revealed — size, stake, who's in |
| **Live** | one rank-movement beat per US market close. **Not per tick** — per-tick movement is a slot machine, and this is a training product. |
| **Final stretch** | "3 days left. You're 4th. 2nd is 1.1% ahead." The anticipation engine. |
| **Settlement freeze** | positions lock at period end, results withheld ~1h. Anticipation manufactured by delay — the cheapest ceremony in games. |
| **The Close** | full-screen: your curve replayed, final rank counted up, career-point delta, the field revealed |
| **The Record** | permanent career entry + share card |
| **The Wind-Up** | the *loss* ceremony. A blowup gets a dignified post-mortem with real numbers and immediate re-entry — never a fail screen. Losing must be a chapter, not an ending. |
| **Annual Awards** | multi-category: Best Return, Best Risk-Adjusted, Best Drawdown Control, Most Improved. **More categories = more winners**, which serves "fair to new and old" at the ceremony layer and not only in the scoring. |

**Reuse.** `Celebrate.micro/.meso/.major` ships at `mobile/lib/services/celebration.dart`,
though `major` is hardcoded to `AgentUnlockedScreen` and needs generalising. `ShareCardData`
is a sealed class with four variants at `mobile/lib/widgets/share/share_card.dart:21` — the
run result is a fifth. Annual Awards are nearly free because
`backend/app/trading_math/returns.py` already ships `sharpe_ratio` and `max_drawdown_pct`
operating on exactly the value series a NAV table produces.

**Push notifications do not exist and are a prerequisite.** The only trace in the repo is
`note="TODO B1: fire APNs push notification here"` at `api/room.py:134` — no tokens, no APNs,
no FCM. Saiful chose **in-app ceremony now, push next**. Stated rather than glossed: without
push, the final-stretch beat only reaches players who already opened the app, so the
anticipation layer is incomplete until the push CR lands.

## 8. Customer-to-customer interaction

Amendment B's second named gap. MVP has **none** by decision — *"only open games… no duals."*

Every variant is specified in [`games_roadmap.md`](games_roadmap.md): duels, rivals,
retrospective reveal, desks, bracketed fields, and nine more modes. Two constraints bound that
whole space and belong here:

- **The standing copy-trading rejection holds.** `rejected_features_register.md` rejects
  copy-trading and follower feeds on brand grounds. The roadmap's **retrospective reveal**
  (trades hidden while a run is open, revealed at close) is the resolution rather than an
  exception: the reveal is retrospective, so it is education, not signal-following.
- **No free-text chat, ever.** A one-person team has no moderation capacity. Structured
  interaction only — fixed-vocabulary reactions, numbers, records.

## 9. What gets built

### 9.0 Where it lives in the app

The app ships five bottom-nav destinations — `FLOOR · PORTFOLIO · JOURNAL · LESSONS · SETTINGS`
(`home_shell.dart:29`) — and `hex_bottom_nav.dart` is built for exactly five, with DEF043 having
already fixed a label that wrapped because `labelMono`'s 1.8 tracking overflowed its cell. So a
sixth tab is not available, and burying the whole game behind a Floor card undersells it.

**Saiful's call: move Settings off the bottom nav.** *"consider moving settings to a less
prominent real estate."* Settings is a low-frequency destination occupying prime real estate,
which makes it the right thing to demote.

| | Today | With the game |
|---|---|---|
| Slot 1 | FLOOR | FLOOR |
| Slot 2 | PORTFOLIO | PORTFOLIO |
| Slot 3 | JOURNAL | **GAME** |
| Slot 4 | LESSONS | JOURNAL |
| Slot 5 | SETTINGS | LESSONS |
| — | — | Settings → **gear in the app bar** |

- **`GAME` takes a real slot.** Four characters, so DEF043's label-width problem doesn't recur.
- **Settings moves to a gear/avatar affordance in the app bar**, reachable in one tap from
  anywhere. The one item that must not become hard to find is **Settings → My Mandate**, since
  the curriculum tells users to revisit it — verify that path is still one tap after the move.
- **`_LeagueSection` in `settings_screen.dart:606` is deleted** rather than moved (Amendment A).

## 10. Integrity guards

Structural, per CR040 — *prompt instructions are not controls*.

1. **Mock-priced days void ranking for the run, loudly.** `USE_REAL_MARKET_DATA` falls back to
   a deterministic mock walk and `price_source` is already tracked end-to-end (the LIVE/MOCK
   pill in `portfolio_screen.dart::_QuoteSourcePill` renders it). A P&L game scored on invented
   prices is a score generator — this was one of D-060's three stated objections and it remains
   live today. The board must state *which days* voided it.
2. **NAV snapshots are append-only, user-scoped, and must NOT foreign-key to
   `sim_portfolios`.** `sim_engine.reset_portfolio()` (`sim_engine.py:394`) hard-deletes the
   portfolio row and every `SimTradeRow`; a FK would cascade the game's entire history away.
   Key on `user_id` + `run_id` — the same disjoint, reset-immune shape that already makes
   `reputation_events` survive resets.
3. **One live run per cadence** (§3.1) — closes the parallel-entry farm.
4. **Thin fields switch to benchmark-relative scoring and cannot award a title** (§4.6) — closes
   the beat-nobody farm and the `n = 1` divide-by-zero.
5. **Restart → forfeit + career debit + minimum debit** (§4.7) — closes restart-on-red and the
   mid-field churn hole.
6. **Entry closes at the bell** — no late entry into a field already in progress.
7. **Smurfing** — `user_devices.device_install_id` exists; the title multiplier removes most of
   the payoff.
8. **Stale or halted marks recorded in the snapshot**, never silently carried forward as last
   price.
9. **The training safety floor is untouched.** No-rules applies to the game path only (§5).

## 11. Compliance — what this contradicts

**This is the highest-risk section of the CR and the reason it is not laned.** Four governing
documents currently forbid what this builds. Saiful was shown each and set the frame anyway
(*"This is a new game"*); that is his call to make, and it is recorded here rather than glossed.

| Where | What it says today | Action needed |
|---|---|---|
| [`roadmap.md:138`](../../initial_specs/10_delivery/roadmap.md) | "P&L-based leaderboards — Encourages gambling psychology" (decisive cut) | Strike or amend |
| [`decision_log.md:302`](../../initial_specs/11_decisions/decision_log.md) **D-060** | The "Paper Cup" P&L-adjacent variant **rejected**; *"raw-returns ranking crosses the anti-gambling guardrail… risks the store 'simulated gambling: No' declaration… needs NAV-history infra that doesn't exist"* | Superseding decision required |
| [`daily_and_streaks.md`](../../initial_specs/04_education/daily_and_streaks.md) | "Sim P&L — 0, explicitly not counted"; P&L leaderboards listed as an anti-pattern | Amend |
| [`competition_rules.md`](../../../legal/policies/competition_rules.md) v1.0 | §1 "what the Competition is — and is not", §8 "Not gambling; skill versus chance" | Re-version v1.0 → v2.0 |

**App Store declaration.** CR064 bound the zero-P&L rule as *a binding invariant, not a design
note*, because the "simulated gambling: **No**" answer depends on it. That declaration must be
re-validated before this ships. Of D-060's three original objections, the third — *"needs
NAV-history infra that doesn't exist"* — is **still literally true**: there is no NAV,
equity-curve or portfolio-snapshot table anywhere in `models.py`. §9 builds it.

**What reduces the risk materially:** Saiful's payout answer — *"this may change, but likely
credit will come into play at some point, but for now just points"*. No entry fee removes
*consideration*; no prize of monetary value removes *prize*; what remains is skill-versus-chance,
which is where §8 of the rules already sits. **Awarding credits — a purchasable currency, packs
at $4.99–$49.99 — on a P&L outcome is the step that needs the lawyer**, so the schema should
carry a payout hook from day one while paying only points.

**What increases it, and is now accepted by decision:** §5's no-rules arena. A contest with no
position limits and a restart button is the most gambling-shaped configuration of this design,
and the halal screen does not apply on the scored path.

D-064 made `competition_rules.md` standalone *specifically* so scoring changes could re-version
it without a full ToS re-issue. That decision pays off exactly here.

Also stale and now doubly wrong:
[`rejected_features_register.md`](../../initial_specs/11_decisions/rejected_features_register.md)'s
"copy-trading / social leaderboards" row, never reconciled with D-060.

## 12. Risks

1. **Store rejection or a forced declaration change.** The largest risk, and it is binary.
   Mitigated by phasing (§13) so no compliance surface ships until the legal work lands.
2. **No arena rules means the weekly board is substantially luck.** Accepted by decision (§5).
   Cadence prestige ordering absorbs part of it — variance falls with horizon on its own — and
   roadmap modes #9 (Sharpe-scored) and #10 (survival) exist to give skill somewhere to show if
   the open boards do degenerate. **Mitigation that changes nothing today:** keep recording
   per-period mandate compliance on the *training* path, so a "Clean Run" mark could switch on
   later with no backfill.
3. **The halal-screen bypass** (§5). A brand risk in GCC markets specifically, mitigated only
   by disclosure.
4. **Thin fields are the near-certain case at alpha, not the edge case.** Saiful raised it
   directly: *"on a new platform, we may find a month with a single player!"* With a handful of
   alpha users, **every** long-cadence field will be below the placement threshold, so §4.6's
   benchmark-relative path is the *default* code path for months — which means it must be built
   in phase 1 alongside placement, not deferred as a fallback. The demand gate also means the
   first Annual field may not open for its full 30-day wait.
5. **Mock-price frequency on Alpha is unmeasured.** If the yfinance fallback fires often the
   §10.1 guard will void runs routinely and the unranked state becomes the common case. Worth
   measuring before any board goes live. Compounds with the thin-field problem: a thin field
   scored against a mock-priced benchmark is doubly meaningless.
6. **Career points that can fall may demoralise** — particularly a new player who blows up
   twice in month one, the cohort most likely to churn. The zero floor caps the damage; the
   visible forfeit count (§4.7) is what carries the honesty instead.
7. **Five concurrent books is real cognitive load** even with one-per-cadence. Weekly-first
   phasing means this is never tested at full width until the loop is proven.
8. **Two coexisting currencies plus career points.** `credit_balance` (monetized), AMI Cash
   (the stake) and career points (the score). Amendment A removed `users.reputation`, so this
   is three numbers rather than four — still worth a naming pass (§14.1).

## 13. Phasing

Ordered so engineering proceeds in parallel with the legal work:

1. **NAV snapshots + equity curve** — valuable standalone, unblocks everything, **zero
   compliance surface.**
2. **Game portfolios + the run/entry machinery** — no board yet, no scoring.
3. **The weekly open board** — Saiful's first cadence: proves the entire loop in 5 trading
   days rather than 90. **Includes §4.6's benchmark-relative path from day one**, not as a later
   fallback — at alpha field sizes it is the default code path, so shipping placement alone would
   ship a divide-by-zero.
4. **The period arc + ceremony, in-app** (§7).
5. **Push infrastructure** — its own CR; completes the anticipation layer.
6. **Career points + career record screen** — the high score; also finally renders the dark
   badges.
7. **Monthly, then Quarter / Half / Annual** — config over the same machinery.
8. **Roadmap modes** — [`games_roadmap.md`](games_roadmap.md), in the order set there.
9. **Credits payout** — only after lawyer review.

Child CRs are deliberately **not minted yet** — this CR is still design stage and not approved
for build. Lane them when it is.

## 14. Not decided yet

1. **Naming.** AMI Cash is settled as the game's money (*"Like Monopoly money"*) and is net-new
   in the repo — a repo-wide search returns zero hits, so the string set starts clean. Still
   unnamed: **the game itself**, a **run**, and the **boards**.
2. **The minimum forfeit debit** (§4.7) — the size of the floor. New in Amendment B; it exists
   because the entry model plus a zero career-point floor makes mid-field forfeiting free.
3. **Title thresholds** (§4.4) — first cut 0 / 500 / 2,500 / 10,000 / 30,000, to be balanced
   against the +140 a weekly win pays at analyst.
4. **Thin-field thresholds** (§4.6) — first cut: placement needs `n ≥ 8`, titles need `n ≥ 20`,
   maximum lobby wait 30 days, minimum entrants to open a rolling field 8. All four interact and
   should be set together, ideally against a measured alpha entry rate rather than guessed.
5. **The benchmark itself** (§4.6) — SPY is the obvious default for US equities, but it is a
   choice with consequences: a small-cap-heavy run measured against a mega-cap index will read as
   skill or failure that is really style drift. QQQ, IWM or an equal-weight index would each bias
   differently. Worth one deliberate decision rather than defaulting.
6. **Blowup threshold** for the proactive restart offer (~50% of stake?).
7. **Settlement freeze duration** — ~1h is a guess; it trades anticipation against annoyance.

## 15. Acceptance

- TWR chain-links across a restart: a −40% run followed by a fresh stake does **not** read as
  flat.
- Entering a second run of a cadence the player already holds is **refused** (§3.1).
- A restart forfeits the run **and** debits career points at the standing held; forfeiting from
  mid-field costs the **minimum debit**, not zero.
- Restarting and riding a run to its close produce the **same** career-point cost, plus the
  minimum-debit floor.
- Career points never render below zero, and the true signed net plus the forfeit count are
  both visible on the career record.
- The game submit path never consults a mandate; the **training** submit path still rejects
  mandate breaches (a regression test on both sides of §5).
- The game entry surface renders the no-rules disclosure, naming the mandate and the screens
  that do not apply.
- Titles recompute from the career-point total when any run closes — no synchronized roll
  exists anywhere in the scoring path.
- **A field of one scores, does not crash, and does not pay a win.** `n = 1` and `n = 2` never
  reach the placement formula; both route to benchmark-relative scoring (§4.6), and neither
  awards a title.
- A thin-field run states its scoring basis — the field size and the benchmark — on the board
  and in the Close, rather than switching basis silently.
- A rolling long-cadence field does not open below its minimum entrant count until the maximum
  wait elapses, and the lobby shows the queue count and the deadline.
- A mock-priced day voids ranking for the run and the UI states which days.
- NAV snapshots survive `reset_portfolio()` — the regression a FK to `sim_portfolios` would
  cause.
- The snapshot tick is idempotent across a container restart.
- Career points balance: 52 weekly wins ≈ one annual win, same title.
- No board surface renders an absolute AMI Cash figure for any user other than the viewer.
- **Nav (§9.0):** five bottom-nav labels still render on one line at the largest supported text
  scale (the DEF043 regression), and **Settings → My Mandate is reachable in one tap** from the
  app bar.
- `pytest backend/tests/unit/ -q` green; `flutter test` + `flutter analyze --no-fatal-infos`
  green.
