## 2. What the game is

**One portfolio. Five concurrent measurement windows. One daily NAV series feeding all of them.**

The user keeps the single sim portfolio they have now (`SimPortfolioRow.user_id` is `unique=True`). Games are not separate accounts — they are *windows* over the same NAV curve. Week, Month, Quarter, Half and Year all score the same trading activity simultaneously.

This is what makes new-vs-veteran fairness structural rather than tuned: **every window opens at 0% for everyone.** A player who joined this morning and one who joined last year both start Monday's board at zero, because nothing carries across a window boundary. It also means one piece of infrastructure buys the entire game.

### 2.1 Score is time-weighted return, never absolute AMI Cash

```text
per sub-period between capital events:  rᵢ  = (NAV_end / NAV_start) − 1
period return:                          TWR = Π(1 + rᵢ) − 1
```

Chain-linked sub-period returns split at capital events — the GIPS standard. It earns its place three times:

- **Restart becomes mathematically clean** — a restart is just a capital event.
- **Stake size stops mattering.** [`tiers_and_pricing.md`](../initial_specs/06_monetization/tiers_and_pricing.md) promises $10K / $100K / $1M sim capital by plan (unbuilt — `_STARTING_CAPITAL = 10_000.0` is hardcoded at `sim_engine.py:195` for everyone). Ranking on % TWR makes larger capital a realism feature, never a rank advantage.
- Any future top-up is neutralised automatically.

> **Invariant, to be written down and tested: boards rank on % TWR only, never absolute AMI Cash.** The moment a board ranks dollars, capital tier becomes pay-to-win and the "no purchase necessary" position in [`competition_rules.md`](../../legal/policies/competition_rules.md) collapses with it.

## 3. Scoring — P&L to points

Two numbers doing two different jobs. Keeping them separate is most of the design.

### 3.1 Board rank — no formula

Raw period TWR, sorted. P&L becomes a percentage; the percentage is the rank. Nothing else.

### 3.2 Career points — placement, never raw return

**This is the load-bearing choice.** If points came off the percentage directly, a bull quarter would hand everyone points and a bear quarter would debit everyone — career points would then record *when a player played* rather than *how well*, and a mediocre player who joined during a rally would outscore a strong one who joined during a crash. That breaks precisely the new-vs-veteran fairness §2 exists to protect. Placement cancels the market out for free: everyone in a cohort faced the same one.

```text
career_points = base(p) × cadence_weight × tier_multiplier
p = (n − rank) / (n − 1)          1.0 = first, 0.0 = last

p ≥ 0.5   base = +100 × ((p − 0.5) × 2) ^ 1.5      convex — the very top is worth a lot
p < 0.5   base = − 40 × ((0.5 − p) × 2)            linear and shallow
```

Winning is worth 2.5× what losing costs; the damping is in the curve, not bolted on after.

| Rank of 30 | 1 | 3 | 6 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | +100 | +80 | +53 | +23 | ~0 | −12 | −26 | −40 |

**Cadence weight is asymmetric** — gains scale with committed time, losses with its square root. The justification is not softening: a long *bad* run is mostly market conditions, a long *good* run is hard to fake, and a player should not be crushed for having been invested through a bear half-year.

| | Week | Month | Quarter | Half | Year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gain × | 1 | 4 | 13 | 26 | 52 |
| Loss × | 1 | 2 | 3.6 | 5.1 | 7.2 |

`tier_multiplier`: apprentice 1.0 · analyst 1.4 · trader 1.6 · senior 1.8 · floor veteran 2.0 — reusing the shipped `league_service.TIERS` ladder, which also removes the incentive to smurf.

**Balance check:** win the year at Analyst = `100 × 52 × 1.4` = **+7,280**. Win 52 weeks = `100 × 1 × 1.4 × 52` = **+7,280**. Neither cadence is a farm.

## 4. Restart — rescues capital, never the record

Always available; nobody is ever trapped. On restart: fresh AMI Cash, a new `run_id`, and every currently-open entry across all five windows is marked **FORFEIT** — removed from standings, so the player neither holds a rank nor takes one from anyone else.

**The loss still debits career points**, scored at the standing held when they walked. This is Saiful's rule and it closes the last exploitable asymmetry in the design: without it, restart is free when losing, so the rational strategy is *restart on red, ride on green*, and every result anyone ever records is a survivor.

With it, **restarting and riding it out cost the same career damage.** There is no way to dodge the hit and restarting can never improve an outcome. What the player actually chooses is *crystallise now and get fresh capital* versus *hold and keep the option of recovering* — a real tradeoff, not a free button.

Two consequences follow:

- **Career points are a signed ledger.** They can fall. The career surface must therefore show earned and given-back separately, or the headline number is unreadable.
- **Re-entry is immediate, not exile.** An earlier draft sat the player out until each cadence's next period; with a career debit applied on top that punishes the same act twice — and for a Half or Year restart it means months on the bench. The debit *is* the price, so a new run re-enters every window at once, scored on TWR from its start, subject to the participation threshold in §5.

The existing 24h reset cooldown stays. Below a blowup threshold (~NAV ≤ 50% of stake) the app *offers* the restart rather than making the user hunt for it.

## 5. Fairness

**New players**

1. Every window opens at 0% for everyone — join today, compete on today's board. This is the core mechanism; everything else is support.
2. TWR — capital size is irrelevant to rank.
3. Tier-matched cohorts of ≤30, reusing `league_service._assemble_week`'s shuffle-and-chunk.
4. **Mid-period joins** are scored on TWR from join date but ranked only with ≥60% of the period served. Someone joining in November competes on week/month/quarter immediately, sits out the annual title, and enters it in January.
5. No global all-time ranked ladder. Career points are personal — Saiful's *"for personal satisfactions"* — never a lifetime leaderboard, which is structurally unwinnable for anyone who joined later.

**Veterans**

1. Career points persist forever as a signed net record.
2. Long cadences are the prestige events; the annual title is the one that means something.
3. Tier multiplier rewards winning against harder cohorts.
4. Permanent titles — "2026 Annual Champion" never expires.

## 6. Integrity guards

Structural, per CR040 — *prompt instructions are not controls*.

1. **Mock-priced days void ranking for that period, loudly.** `USE_REAL_MARKET_DATA` falls back to a deterministic mock walk and `price_source` is already tracked end-to-end (the LIVE/MOCK pill in `portfolio_screen.dart::_QuoteSourcePill` renders it). A P&L game scored on invented prices is a score generator — this was one of D-060's three stated objections and it remains live today. The period must state *which days* and *which other windows* it took down.
2. **NAV snapshots are append-only, user-scoped, and must NOT foreign-key to `sim_portfolios`.** `sim_engine.reset_portfolio()` hard-deletes the portfolio row and every `SimTradeRow`; a FK would cascade the game's entire history away on the first restart. Key on `user_id` + `run_id` — the same disjoint, reset-immune shape that already makes `reputation_events` survive resets.
3. **Restart → forfeit + career debit** (§4).
4. **Cohorts assembled at period open**, shuffled within tier.
5. **Smurfing** — `user_devices.device_install_id` exists; the tier multiplier removes most of the payoff.
6. **Stale or halted marks recorded in the snapshot**, never silently carried forward as last price.

## 9. Risks

1. **Store rejection or a forced declaration change.** The largest risk, and it is binary. Mitigated by phasing (§10) so no compliance surface ships until the legal work lands.
2. **Pure P&L means the short cadences reward concentration.** Saiful chose *"Pure P&L, no gate"* over a mandate-compliance eligibility bar; consequence recorded once: at weekly horizons the optimal play is 100% of AMI Cash in the most volatile name available, which is the opposite of what the 292-lesson curriculum teaches. Cadence prestige ordering absorbs part of it — variance falls with horizon on its own. **Mitigation that changes nothing today:** keep recording per-period mandate compliance (the safety floor already computes it at trade time, so this stores an existing number rather than building a feature). If the boards degenerate into a lottery, a "Clean Run" mark or an eligibility gate switches on later with no redesign and no backfill.
3. ~~**Four coexisting numbers.** `users.reputation` (process), career points (new), `credit_balance` (monetized) and AMI Cash (the stake) would all compete for the same screens — and **CR093** is already `proposed` for a *lifetime reputation tier ladder*, which would make three ladders. A naming pass is a prerequisite, not a polish step.~~ **Reduced by §0's amendment:** `users.reputation` is being removed and CR093 closed as stale, so only career points, `credit_balance` and AMI Cash remain — still worth a naming pass, but two ladders, not three.
4. **Mock-price frequency on Alpha is unmeasured.** If the yfinance fallback fires often, the §6.1 guard will void periods routinely and the unranked state becomes the common case rather than the exception. Worth measuring before any board goes live.
5. **Career points that can fall may demoralise.** Particularly a new player who blows up twice in month one — the cohort most likely to churn.

## 10. Phasing

Ordered so engineering proceeds in parallel with the legal work:

1. **NAV snapshots + equity curve** — valuable standalone, unblocks everything, **zero compliance surface**.
2. **Weekly + monthly boards** — the loop, smallest cadence set.
3. **Career points + career record screen** — the high score; also renders the dark badges.
4. **Quarter / Half / Annual** — config over the same machinery.
5. **Credits payout** — only after lawyer review.

## 11. Acceptance

- TWR chain-links across a restart: a −40% run followed by a fresh stake does **not** read as flat.
- A restart forfeits every open entry across all five windows **and** debits career points at the standing held.
- Restarting and riding a run to period end produce the **same** career-point cost.
- A mock-priced day voids ranking for every window containing it, and the UI states which days and which windows.
- NAV snapshots survive `reset_portfolio()` — the regression a FK to `sim_portfolios` would cause.
- The snapshot tick is idempotent across a container restart.
- Career points balance: 52 weekly wins ≈ one annual win, same tier.
- No board surface renders an absolute AMI Cash figure for any user other than the viewer.
- `pytest backend/tests/unit/ -q` green; `flutter test` + `flutter analyze --no-fatal-infos` green.

## 12. Not decided yet

1. **Partial credit when top of cohort but negative in absolute terms.** A bear quarter, down 8% while the cohort is down 20%. Full placement points is principled but reads as "I lost money and won big"; zero is intuitive but freezes progression for everyone through a downturn, punishing anyone who joins during one. **Recommended: placement × 0.5 while absolute TWR is negative.** Needs Saiful's call — every other value above is tunable without changing shape.
2. **Floor career points at zero, or allow negative?** Lean: floor the headline, show the true net beneath it.
3. **Naming.** "AMI Cash" is net-new — a repo-wide search returns zero hits. The game, the stake ("a Run"?) and the boards all need names, resolved together with risk §3.
4. **Cadence phasing** — all five at once, or weekly + monthly first.
5. **Blowup threshold** for the proactive restart offer (50%?).
6. **Loss-damping shape** — the √cadence weight in §3.2 is a proposal, not a measured value.
