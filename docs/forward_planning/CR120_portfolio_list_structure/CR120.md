## 1. Why

Saiful, 2026-07-29:

> *"The portfolio screen has watchlist, holdings and trade history. They will all grow.
> We need a better way than simple scrolling one after another. We also need to limit
> the trades to either a number of items — maybe last 10 trades — or of a period, trades
> in the past month, or a combination of both. Users can still check trades in the
> Journal instead."*

### 1.1 Measured

[`portfolio_screen.dart:137`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/sim/portfolio_screen.dart#L137) is a
**`ListView(children:)`, not `.builder`** — every row widget is constructed eagerly on every
rebuild. `TradeRow` and `_WatchlistRow` are both `ConsumerWidget`s, and the screen
`ref.watch`es `simNotifierProvider`, so a price refresh rebuilds all of them.

Measured in the prototype at a 390 × 844 device, read out of the DOM (`scrollHeight /
clientHeight`), not estimated:

| Account | Scroll | Screens | Row widgets built at once |
|---|---|---|---|
| modest — 8 watch, 3 held, 2 open + 18 closed | 2,815 pt | **3.6** | 31 |
| six months — 18 watch, 6 held, 3 open + 57 closed | 6,892 pt | **8.9** | 84 |
| heavy — 40 watch, 12 held, 5 open + 195 closed | 19,772 pt | **25.4** | 252 |

Growth is linear in account size with no upper bound, because **nothing on the server is
capped either**: `sim.list_trades`
([`sim_engine.py:447`](../../../backend/app/services/sim_engine.py#L447)) selects every trade the
user has ever made, `opened_at DESC`, with no `limit` and no `offset`.

### 1.2 Three structural facts that shape the fix

1. **Open and closed trades are interleaved by open date.** There is no status split anywhere
   in the render path — `for (final t in state.trades)`
   ([`:170`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/sim/portfolio_screen.dart#L170)) walks one list ordered
   by `opened_at`. A live position opened in April sits below forty closed trades from May.
   **Open trades are money at risk, not history, and must never be capped.**
2. **The watchlist is not user-curated — it auto-grows.**
   [`sim.py:302`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L302) silently adds every traded ticker, and
   `watchlist_store.add` has no cap. So it accumulates without the user ever touching it, and
   the same ticker can appear in all three sections at once.
3. **The metric rows only render `if (isApprove)`** — unrelated to this CR but the same
   screen; noted in CR106.

## 2. The Journal is a lossy archive for free users — the premise needs correcting

Saiful's *"users can still check trades in the Journal instead"* is **half true, and the half
that is false decides the design.**

**True:** every submitted trade writes a journal entry with the full trade JSON in the payload
([`sim.py:314`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L314)), closes write another
([`:393`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L393),
[`:446`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L446)), and the Journal already ships a **TRADE filter
chip** ([`journal_screen.dart:39`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/journal/journal_screen.dart#L39))
plus search. The destination exists and is one tap away.

**False:** **Floor Pass gets 30-day retention.**
[`journal_store.py:42-47`](../../../backend/app/services/journal_store.py#L42-L47) —
`_retention_days_for_plan` returns `FLOOR_PASS_RETENTION_DAYS` for the free plan and `None`
(unlimited) for paid. Meanwhile `sim_trades` has **no retention at all**.

So for a free user the relationship is inverted: **the Portfolio is the durable record of their
trades and the Journal is the lossy one.** Capping the Portfolio as a *fetch* limit and pointing
at the Journal would silently delete a free user's own trade history from every surface in the
app. That is the CR040 class — degrade loudly, never silently — with the user's own data as the
thing lost.

**Consequences, both binding:**

- The cap is a **view default with an in-place escape hatch**, never a fetch limit. `SHOW ALL 57`
  stays on the Portfolio's own durable data.
- The Journal pointer is **plan-aware**. On Floor Pass, with entries older than the window, the
  Portfolio states what the Journal will not have — *"Journal keeps 30 days on Floor Pass — 47 of
  these 57 are older. They stay here."* Never a bare link that quietly loses rows.

Two further gaps found while checking this, both flagged in §6: the Journal's own list endpoint
is `limit=100` with no pagination or cursor, and the trade → journal write is wrapped in
`except Exception: pass`.

**That one line is the *entire* plan-dependent surface of this screen** — verified, not assumed:
there is not a single `plan` reference in `sim.py` or `watchlist.py`, and no plan-gated limit
anywhere in the sim path (`credit_service` and `reputation_service` hold the only plan gates, and
neither touches the Portfolio). So the difference is one caveat appearing or not appearing,
beneath the action it qualifies. That placement is deliberate — a user does not need to know about
Journal retention until they are about to rely on the Journal — but it does mean the difference is
invisible from the landing tab, which is why the prototype's plan control now states what it
changes and navigates to it.

### 2.1 The client must not branch on plan — it branches on `retention_days`

Saiful, seeing the toggle: *"So a slightly different UI depending on plan."* Yes, but **not
implemented as one**, and the distinction is load-bearing.

The Journal list response already carries `retention_days`, the client already parses it into
`JournalState.retentionDays` ([`journal.dart:141`](../../../mobile/lib/models/journal.dart#L141),
[`journal_providers.dart:23`](../../../mobile/lib/state/journal_providers.dart#L23)), and the
Journal screen already renders an amber notice from it
([`journal_screen.dart:113-120`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/journal/journal_screen.dart#L113-L120)).

Two consequences:

1. **This is not a new pattern.** CR120 extends a disclosure that already ships to the screen that
   links to it — same fact, same amber, same tone. Reinventing it here would be DEF098's
   two-renderer class on a monetisation-adjacent string.
2. **The UI never needs to know the plan.** It branches on *"is there a retention window?"* — an
   integer, or `null` for unlimited, computed server-side. No plan state to sync, no wrong-branch
   risk if the client's idea of the plan is stale, and if the retention policy ever changes the app
   follows without a release. **Never hardcode 30 in the app.**

**Fail-safe rule.** `retentionDays` reaches the client today only on the *journal* list response,
which the Portfolio does not call. If it is unknown, the Portfolio **shows the caveat without a
number** — *"Your Journal may not show all of these. Nothing is deleted; they stay here."* — and
never the reassuring branch. A spurious warning is recoverable; a missing one is the thing this
whole section exists to prevent (CR040). B3 below removes the guess.

### 2.2 Nothing is deleted — and the shipped string says otherwise

Writing the honest version of this line exposed a defect in the one already shipping:

> `journalRetentionWarning` — **"Floor Pass: last {days} days only. Upgrade to keep everything."**

**Retention is a read-time filter, not a deletion.**
[`journal_store.py:134-136`](../../../backend/app/services/journal_store.py#L134-L136) adds
`where created_at >= cutoff` to the *select*; the only `delete(JournalEntryRow)` in the store is
`clear()`, a test helper. An entry outside the window is hidden, not destroyed, and reappears the
moment the plan changes. For sim trades it is doubly wrong: `sim_trades` has no retention at all.

So *"Upgrade to **keep** everything"* asserts a data loss that is not happening, to drive a
purchase. The honest verb is **see**. Filed as defect #7 — the correction is one word, but it sits
on a paid-conversion surface, so it is not mine to edit unilaterally.

CR120's own line therefore says what is true: *"Your Journal shows the last 30 days — 47 of these
57 are older. **Nothing is deleted; they stay here.**"*

## 3. The cap on closed trades

**Decided (Saiful, 2026-07-29): last 25, with the span they cover stated.**

```
LAST 25 CLOSED   17 MAY – 28 JUL
```

The period is **described, never used to filter.** That is the whole rule, and it is what makes
it safe:

- A **pure period** filter ("past 30 days") shows an empty history to a user who took a break,
  and shows all 200 to a day-trader who did not.
- A **combination** has to pick union (unbounded) or intersection (empty at one end). Both
  failure modes, one rule.
- **Last N with the span described** is never empty while trades exist, never unbounded, and the
  dates answer the question the period filter was reaching for.

**Open trades are never capped, in any structure**, and get their own section above closed —
fixing §1.2's interleave at the same time.

Above the capped list sits a **summary**, because the win/loss split and the net are the answer
most users actually want and neither requires reading a row:

```
▐████████████████████▌▐███████▌        41 W / 16 L
 WON 41   LOST 16   HIT RATE 72%   NET +$39,330
```

The summary is computed over **all** closed trades, not the visible 25 — a hit rate over an
arbitrary display window would be a wrong number wearing the authority of a statistic.

## 4. Structure — DECIDED: segmented tabs

**Saiful, 2026-07-29, after reviewing all four in the prototype: segmented tabs.**

All four were built and measured on identical data before the call. Landing state = what the
user sees on arriving at the tab; worst state = the longest scroll they can reach.

| Structure | modest | six months | heavy | worst reachable (heavy) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today (shipped) | 3.6 | 8.9 | 25.4 | 25.4 — no other state exists |
| **Segmented tabs — chosen** | 1.0–2.8 | 1.7–3.7 | 2.9–4.2 | 4.2 (Watchlist) |
| Bounded dashboard | 2.5 | 3.0 | 3.2 | 3.2 |
| Collapsible sections | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.4 | 9.7 (all expanded) |

Screens of scroll, read from the DOM in the prototype at 390 × 844.

### 4.1 The design

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PORTFOLIO                              +   │  64pt, unchanged
├────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ TOTAL VALUE                        ● LIVE  │  pinned, compact —
│ $255,614.88                                │  never scrolls away
│ +$13,203.12 (+5.45%)      CASH $31,050.00  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ⬡POSITIONS 12⬡ ⬡WATCHLIST 40⬡ ⬡HISTORY 195⬡│  44pt tab bar
├────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                            │
│   the selected list, lazily built,         │
│   with the full viewport to itself         │
│                                            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Tab bar.** Three segments, `FlatTopHexagonBarClipper(cornerCut: 8)` matching
`ticker_chart.dart:185` and CR106's BOARD|TRANSCRIPT toggle, `labelMono` 10pt, active =
solid `hexBlue` with `textHigh`. **Fill flip only, 200ms `AmiMotion.easeOut` — no sliding
indicator, no swipe-between-tabs** (the DS rules out motion on text, and a horizontal swipe
gesture inside a bottom-nav tab fights the OS back gesture). Each label carries its
**count**, which is what tells a user the other two tabs are not empty — the mitigation for
the one real cost of this structure, a horizontal switcher nested inside the bottom-nav tab
they are already in.

**Tab state is `TabController` state, never a persisted preference.** Saiful asked whether the
Portfolio should land on Positions or resume the last tab; the answer is *both, at different
scopes*, and the shipped shell already gives all three for free —
[`home_shell.dart:42`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/home_shell.dart#L42) hosts the five nav
destinations in an **`IndexedStack`**, so `PortfolioScreen`'s State lives for the whole app
session:

| Scope | Behaviour | Code |
|---|---|---|
| Tap a holding → detail → back | tab preserved | none — State survives the push |
| Portfolio → Floor → Portfolio | **tab preserved** | none — `IndexedStack` keeps the State |
| App killed and relaunched | **lands on Positions** | none — fresh State |

**Persisting across launches is rejected, and for the same reason open trades moved onto the
landing tab:** a user whose last visit ended on History would open the app the next day with an
armed stop behind a tab they cannot see. The pinned value card means total value, P&L and cash
are visible on every tab regardless, so the *only* thing landing on History costs is sight of
the live positions — which is precisely the thing that must not be optional.

Also rejected: a staleness rule ("reset if resumed after N minutes"). A tab that sometimes
remembers and sometimes does not is worse than either rule consistently applied.

Doing nothing is therefore the whole implementation: no `SharedPreferences` key, no stale-
preference migration, no CR106-style `setMode`, and none of CR106's **T-MODESIDE** trap surface.

**The Positions tab carries a live pip when an open trade exists.** Its count is holdings, so
the tab under-reports its own contents; a second number would conflate two different things and
tab labels have no width to spare. A 6pt flat-top hex in family cyan — **not amber: an open
position is a state, not a warning** — sits after the count, turns `textHigh` when the tab is
active, and is absent when there are no open trades. It also means that if the persistence rule
above is ever revisited, nothing live is silently hidden.

**POSITIONS carries holdings *and* open trades — History is closed trades only.** This is the
one thing this structure could get wrong. An open trade is money at risk with a stop that can
fire; behind a tab the user does not land on, it is invisible. So the landing tab holds the
sector donut, the holdings list, then an `OPEN TRADES` section, and the History tab's count
reads `195`, not `200`. It also fixes §1.2's interleave for free — there is no longer a list
that contains both.

**The sector donut moves inside Positions**, where it describes the list beneath it. Today it
renders above the watchlist and reads as unrelated to anything.

**HISTORY** = the §3 cap: `LAST 25 CLOSED · 17 MAY – 28 JUL`, the summary strip captioned
`ACROSS ALL 195 CLOSED`, 25 rows, then `SHOW ALL 195` (a pushed route over the Portfolio's own
durable data) and the plan-aware `REVIEW IN JOURNAL ›`.

**A list over 12 items gets search + sort inside its own tab** — a full viewport per list is
exactly what buys room for them, and it is what the rejected dashboard could not offer without
a navigation. Defaults are per list, not one generic sort: **holdings by value** (the biggest
position is the one that matters), **watchlist A–Z** (a watchlist is scanned to *find* a name,
and a movers-first order moves the name every day), **closed newest first**.

### 4.2 Measured, per tab

| Tab | modest | six months | heavy | heavy @ 1.15 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Positions | 1.1 | 1.7 | 2.9 | 3.2 |
| Watchlist | 1.0 | 2.0 | **4.2** | 4.6 |
| History | 2.8 | 3.7 | **3.7** | 4.1 |

**History stops growing at six months** — 3.7 screens whether the user has 57 closed trades or
195. That is the 25-cap doing its job.

**Watchlist is the one unbounded tab**, and it is unbounded for a reason nobody chose:
`sim.py:302` auto-adds every traded ticker (§1.2, and defect #5). Search and sort make 40 rows
navigable; they do not make 200 acceptable. If the auto-add stays, this tab needs its own cap
conversation later — flagged, not solved here.

### 4.3 Why the other two lost

- **Bounded dashboard** is the flattest (3.2 at heavy, bounded by construction) and nothing is
  ever behind a mode. Its cost is that no list ever gets a full viewport, so search and sort
  always live one navigation away, and every list is a 5-row taste of itself. Kept in the
  prototype so the comparison stays auditable.
- **Collapsible sections** are the cheapest change and the weakest guarantee: 2.4 screens is a
  state the user can undo with two taps, and at 9.7 the original problem is back. Anything
  collapsed by default is also invisible to a user who never learns headings are tappable.

### 4.4 Not a rendering-only fix

Each tab becomes a `ListView.builder` / `CustomScrollView`. A tab makes the *other* lists
absent; it does not make the visible one lazy, and the Watchlist tab at 40+ rows and the pushed
all-closed-trades route both need laziness on their own terms.

## 5. Backend

Phase 1 needs **nothing** from the backend — every cap is a view concern over data already
fetched. Two follow-ons worth filing separately once the structure lands:

| # | Gap | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | `GET /v1/sim/trades/{user_id}` takes `limit` + `offset` (or a cursor) and returns `total` | The pushed "all closed trades" route is the one place that genuinely wants pagination. Today the client fetches every trade on every portfolio refresh — at 195 trades that is the whole ledger over the wire to render a 25-row tab. |
| **B2** | `status=open` / `status=closed` split honoured client-side | The parameter already exists server-side (`list_trades(..., status=...)`) and is never used by the app; the sections need it if paging lands. |
| **B3** | `journal_retention_days` on the sim portfolio/trades response | Today the number only arrives on the *journal* list response, which the Portfolio never calls — so the History footer either reads another screen's provider (possibly unloaded) or guesses. Neither is acceptable for a disclosure; §2.1's fail-safe covers the gap until this lands. |

## 6. Defects spotted — flagged to the Architect to mint, not filed here

1. **Open trades are buried in trade history.** No status split in the render path
   ([`portfolio_screen.dart:170`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/sim/portfolio_screen.dart#L170));
   ordering is `opened_at DESC` across open and closed together, so a live position with money at
   risk sits below every trade opened after it. Live today, independent of this CR.
2. **The whole Portfolio is an eager `ListView(children:)`** with `ConsumerWidget` rows and a
   provider watch above them — 252 row widgets rebuilt on every price tick at heavy use.
3. **The trade → journal write is `except Exception: pass`** ([`sim.py:328`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L328),
   and the same at `:393`/`:446`). A swallowed failure means a trade exists with no journal entry
   and nothing anywhere says so — CR040. This gets materially more expensive the moment the
   Portfolio points at the Journal as the archive.
4. **The Journal list endpoint is `limit=100` with no pagination**
   ([`journal.py:59`](../../../backend/app/api/journal.py#L59)). It returns `total`, so the client
   *could* say "showing 100 of 340", and does not. A paid user with a long history silently loses
   the tail — the same silence, one plan up.
5. **The watchlist auto-grows with no cap and no disclosure**
   ([`sim.py:302`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L302)). The user never added those tickers and
   is never told they were added.
6. **`_WatchlistRow` sets the price at `statMid` (24px) and the ticker at `labelMono` (12px)** —
   the price shouts and the identity whispers, which is backwards for a list you scan by ticker.
   Cosmetic, but it is why the watchlist reads as the loudest block on the screen.
7. **`journalRetentionWarning` claims a data loss that does not happen.** *"Floor Pass: last
   {days} days only. Upgrade to keep everything."* — but retention is a read-time filter
   (`journal_store.py:134-136`), the only `delete(JournalEntryRow)` is a test-only `clear()`, and
   `sim_trades` has no retention at all. Nothing is deleted; entries reappear on upgrade. The
   string applies purchase pressure on a false premise, on a paid-conversion surface, in shipped
   user-facing copy — CR038/DEF059's confident-wrongness family. Fix is one verb (*keep* → *see*)
   plus a clause saying nothing is lost. **`retranslate:[ar,ms]` for `journalRetentionWarning`.**

## 9. Acceptance

1. Landing scroll (Positions tab) is **≤ 3.0 screens at the heavy profile** (40 watch, 12 held,
   5 open + 195 closed) at 1.0 text scale, and the History tab does not grow once the cap binds
   — measured, not asserted.
2. **No open trade is ever hidden**, capped, collapsed by default, or ordered below a closed one.
   Open trades render on the **landing** tab; the History count excludes them.
2b. Tab selection survives in-app navigation **and bottom-nav switches**, and **resets to
   Positions on cold start**. It is never written to `SharedPreferences`. If an open trade
   exists, the Positions tab shows the live pip whether or not it is the selected tab.
3. Closed trades show at most **25** by default, with the covered span stated as dates.
4. The win/loss/hit-rate/net summary is computed over **all** closed trades, and says so if the
   visible list is capped.
5. `SHOW ALL` reaches every closed trade **from the Portfolio's own data**, without depending on
   the Journal.
6. When closed trades older than the retention window exist, the Journal pointer states how many
   the Journal will not show, **and that nothing is deleted**. With `retention_days == null` the
   line is absent. The branch is on `retention_days`, never on a plan name, and **never on a
   hardcoded 30**.
6b. With `retention_days` unknown, the number-less caveat shows — asserted by a test that starts
   the Portfolio with no journal state loaded.
7. No tab renders through `ListView(children:)`; each is lazy, as is the pushed route.
7b. All three tab labels with their counts fit on one line, unshrunk, at 1.0 and 1.15 in EN —
   and the check is repeated in AR and MS before those locales ship.
8. Every numeric run survives `dir: rtl` unreordered — asserted on `+$1,234.56 (+7.89%)`
   specifically.
9. Nothing clips at 1.15 text scale in either theme.
10. The closed-trade row renders through **one** widget shared between the History tab and its
    pushed route — DEF098's two-renderer class, which this screen is already close to
    reproducing.
