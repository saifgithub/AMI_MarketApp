# CR120 — Portfolio: structure for three growing lists, and a cap on trade history

**Status:** proposed · design round (spec + prototype), no code
**Track:** UI Designer · **Raised:** 2026-07-29

**Prototype:** https://claude.ai/code/artifact/c61aef31-8385-4637-9d92-a3bdef402c5e
Source: [`prototype/`](prototype/) — `template.html` is the source, `portfolio.html` is generated.

---

## 1. Why

Saiful, 2026-07-29:

> *"The portfolio screen has watchlist, holdings and trade history. They will all grow.
> We need a better way than simple scrolling one after another. We also need to limit
> the trades to either a number of items — maybe last 10 trades — or of a period, trades
> in the past month, or a combination of both. Users can still check trades in the
> Journal instead."*

### 1.1 Measured

[`portfolio_screen.dart:137`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/sim/portfolio_screen.dart#L137) is a
**`ListView(children:)`, not `.builder`** — every row widget is constructed eagerly on every
rebuild. `TradeRow` and `_WatchlistRow` are both `ConsumerWidget`s, and the screen
`ref.watch`es `simNotifierProvider`, so a price refresh rebuilds all of them.

Measured in the prototype at a 390 × 844 device, read out of the DOM (`scrollHeight /
clientHeight`), not estimated:

| Account | Scroll | Screens | Row widgets built at once |
|---|---|---|---|
| modest — 8 watch, 3 held, 2 open + 18 closed | 2,815 pt | **3.6** | 31 |
| six months — 18 watch, 6 held, 3 open + 57 closed | 6,892 pt | **8.9** | 84 |
| heavy — 40 watch, 12 held, 5 open + 195 closed | 19,772 pt | **25.4** | 252 |

Growth is linear in account size with no upper bound, because **nothing on the server is
capped either**: `sim.list_trades`
([`sim_engine.py:447`](../../../backend/app/services/sim_engine.py#L447)) selects every trade the
user has ever made, `opened_at DESC`, with no `limit` and no `offset`.

### 1.2 Three structural facts that shape the fix

1. **Open and closed trades are interleaved by open date.** There is no status split anywhere
   in the render path — `for (final t in state.trades)`
   ([`:170`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/sim/portfolio_screen.dart#L170)) walks one list ordered
   by `opened_at`. A live position opened in April sits below forty closed trades from May.
   **Open trades are money at risk, not history, and must never be capped.**
2. **The watchlist is not user-curated — it auto-grows.**
   [`sim.py:302`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L302) silently adds every traded ticker, and
   `watchlist_store.add` has no cap. So it accumulates without the user ever touching it, and
   the same ticker can appear in all three sections at once.
3. **The metric rows only render `if (isApprove)`** — unrelated to this CR but the same
   screen; noted in CR106.

## 2. The Journal is a lossy archive for free users — the premise needs correcting

Saiful's *"users can still check trades in the Journal instead"* is **half true, and the half
that is false decides the design.**

**True:** every submitted trade writes a journal entry with the full trade JSON in the payload
([`sim.py:314`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L314)), closes write another
([`:393`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L393),
[`:446`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L446)), and the Journal already ships a **TRADE filter
chip** ([`journal_screen.dart:39`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/journal/journal_screen.dart#L39))
plus search. The destination exists and is one tap away.

**False:** **Floor Pass gets 30-day retention.**
[`journal_store.py:42-47`](../../../backend/app/services/journal_store.py#L42-L47) —
`_retention_days_for_plan` returns `FLOOR_PASS_RETENTION_DAYS` for the free plan and `None`
(unlimited) for paid. Meanwhile `sim_trades` has **no retention at all**.

So for a free user the relationship is inverted: **the Portfolio is the durable record of their
trades and the Journal is the lossy one.** Capping the Portfolio as a *fetch* limit and pointing
at the Journal would silently delete a free user's own trade history from every surface in the
app. That is the CR040 class — degrade loudly, never silently — with the user's own data as the
thing lost.

**Consequences, both binding:**

- The cap is a **view default with an in-place escape hatch**, never a fetch limit. `SHOW ALL 57`
  stays on the Portfolio's own durable data.
- The Journal pointer is **plan-aware**. On Floor Pass, with entries older than the window, the
  Portfolio states what the Journal will not have — *"Journal keeps 30 days on Floor Pass — 47 of
  these 57 are older. They stay here."* Never a bare link that quietly loses rows.

Two further gaps found while checking this, both flagged in §6: the Journal's own list endpoint
is `limit=100` with no pagination or cursor, and the trade → journal write is wrapped in
`except Exception: pass`.

## 3. The cap on closed trades

**Decided (Saiful, 2026-07-29): last 25, with the span they cover stated.**

```
LAST 25 CLOSED   17 MAY – 28 JUL
```

The period is **described, never used to filter.** That is the whole rule, and it is what makes
it safe:

- A **pure period** filter ("past 30 days") shows an empty history to a user who took a break,
  and shows all 200 to a day-trader who did not.
- A **combination** has to pick union (unbounded) or intersection (empty at one end). Both
  failure modes, one rule.
- **Last N with the span described** is never empty while trades exist, never unbounded, and the
  dates answer the question the period filter was reaching for.

**Open trades are never capped, in any structure**, and get their own section above closed —
fixing §1.2's interleave at the same time.

Above the capped list sits a **summary**, because the win/loss split and the net are the answer
most users actually want and neither requires reading a row:

```
▐████████████████████▌▐███████▌        41 W / 16 L
 WON 41   LOST 16   HIT RATE 72%   NET +$39,330
```

The summary is computed over **all** closed trades, not the visible 25 — a hit rate over an
arbitrary display window would be a wrong number wearing the authority of a statistic.

## 4. The four structures, measured

All four were built and measured on identical data. Landing state = what the user sees on
arriving at the tab.

| Structure | modest | six months | heavy | worst reachable state (heavy) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Today (shipped)** | 3.6 | 8.9 | 25.4 | 25.4 — there is no other state |
| **Bounded dashboard** | 2.5 | 3.0 | **3.2** | 3.2 — bounded by construction |
| **Segmented tabs** | 1.0 | 1.4 | 2.3 | 4.2 (History tab) |
| **Collapsible sections** | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.4 | 9.7 (everything expanded) |

Figures are screens of scroll, measured from the DOM in the prototype.

### 4.1 Bounded dashboard — recommended

Every list previews **5 rows** and carries a real destination. Order is **money-at-risk first**:

```
TOTAL VALUE  $107,172.49                    ● LIVE     ← pinned, compact
+$5,423.69 (+5.33%)              CASH $12,400.00
───────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOLDINGS  6                            SEE ALL ›
  ◕ sector donut + legend
  NVDA · AMZN · JPM · XOM · JNJ
  1 MORE · $4,496 ›
OPEN TRADES  3                         (never capped)
  HON · LLY · MCD
WATCHLIST  18                          SEE ALL ›
  5 rows · 13 MORE ›
CLOSED  57
  win/loss bar · WON 41 · LOST 16 · 72% · +$39,330
  5 rows
  SHOW ALL 57              REVIEW IN JOURNAL ›
  ⬢ Journal keeps 30 days on Floor Pass — 47 of these
    57 are older. They stay here.
```

**Nothing expands in place.** Every `SEE ALL` / `SHOW ALL` **pushes a route** with its own header,
search and sort. That is not a detail — inline expansion is exactly what un-bounds the dashboard,
and the pushed screen is what makes the cap cost the user nothing: the destination offers search
and sort, which a preview of any depth cannot.

The **sector donut moves inside Holdings**, where it belongs. Today it renders above the
watchlist and reads as unrelated to anything.

### 4.2 The other three, and why they lose

- **Tabs** land smaller (1.0–2.3) and make lazy building trivial, but they nest a horizontal
  switcher inside the bottom-nav tab the user is already in, and turn two-thirds of the screen
  into a mode that must be remembered. **No tab pattern exists in the app** outside
  `alpaca_connect_screen.dart`. Kept in the prototype so the comparison stays auditable.
- **Collapsible sections** are the cheapest change and the weakest guarantee: 2.4 screens is a
  state the user can undo with two taps, and at 9.7 screens the original problem is back.
  Anything collapsed by default is invisible to a user who never learns headings are tappable.
- **Today** is the baseline.

### 4.3 Not a rendering-only fix

Whatever structure is chosen, `ListView(children:)` becomes `ListView.builder` /
`CustomScrollView` + slivers on the pushed routes. A capped preview makes the eager list *small*;
it does not make it *lazy*, and the pushed "all 195 closed trades" route needs laziness on its own
terms.

## 5. Backend

Phase 1 needs **nothing** from the backend — every cap is a view concern over data already
fetched. Two follow-ons worth filing separately once the structure lands:

| # | Gap | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | `GET /v1/sim/trades/{user_id}` takes `limit` + `offset` (or a cursor) and returns `total` | The pushed "all closed trades" route is the one place that genuinely wants pagination. Today the client fetches every trade on every portfolio refresh — at 195 trades that is the whole ledger over the wire to render 5 rows. |
| **B2** | `status=open` / `status=closed` split honoured client-side | The parameter already exists server-side (`list_trades(..., status=...)`) and is never used by the app; the sections need it if paging lands. |

## 6. Defects spotted — flagged to the Architect to mint, not filed here

1. **Open trades are buried in trade history.** No status split in the render path
   ([`portfolio_screen.dart:170`](../../../mobile/lib/screens/sim/portfolio_screen.dart#L170));
   ordering is `opened_at DESC` across open and closed together, so a live position with money at
   risk sits below every trade opened after it. Live today, independent of this CR.
2. **The whole Portfolio is an eager `ListView(children:)`** with `ConsumerWidget` rows and a
   provider watch above them — 252 row widgets rebuilt on every price tick at heavy use.
3. **The trade → journal write is `except Exception: pass`** ([`sim.py:328`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L328),
   and the same at `:393`/`:446`). A swallowed failure means a trade exists with no journal entry
   and nothing anywhere says so — CR040. This gets materially more expensive the moment the
   Portfolio points at the Journal as the archive.
4. **The Journal list endpoint is `limit=100` with no pagination**
   ([`journal.py:59`](../../../backend/app/api/journal.py#L59)). It returns `total`, so the client
   *could* say "showing 100 of 340", and does not. A paid user with a long history silently loses
   the tail — the same silence, one plan up.
5. **The watchlist auto-grows with no cap and no disclosure**
   ([`sim.py:302`](../../../backend/app/api/sim.py#L302)). The user never added those tickers and
   is never told they were added.
6. **`_WatchlistRow` sets the price at `statMid` (24px) and the ticker at `labelMono` (12px)** —
   the price shouts and the identity whispers, which is backwards for a list you scan by ticker.
   Cosmetic, but it is why the watchlist reads as the loudest block on the screen.

| `portfolioSortNewest` / `SortValue` | `NEWEST` / `VALUE` |
| `portfolioNoOpenPositions` | `No open positions` |

**RTL:** verified in the prototype. Every numeric run is bidi-isolated — this caught a live
instance of CR106's **T-BIDI** trap, where `+$5,423.69 (+5.33%)` rendered as `(5.33%+` under
RTL reordering. Any run mixing sign, digits and parentheses needs the isolate; the layout mirrors,
the numbers do not.

## 9. Acceptance

1. Landing scroll on the Portfolio tab is **≤ 3.5 screens at the heavy profile** (40 watch,
   12 held, 5 open + 195 closed) at 1.0 text scale, and does not grow super-linearly with account
   size — measured, not asserted.
2. **No open trade is ever hidden**, capped, collapsed by default, or ordered below a closed one.
3. Closed trades show at most **25** by default, with the covered span stated as dates.
4. The win/loss/hit-rate/net summary is computed over **all** closed trades, and says so if the
   visible list is capped.
5. `SHOW ALL` reaches every closed trade **from the Portfolio's own data**, without depending on
   the Journal.
6. On Floor Pass, when closed trades older than the retention window exist, the Journal pointer
   states how many the Journal will not have. On a paid plan that line is absent.
7. No section renders through `ListView(children:)`; the pushed routes are lazy.
8. Every numeric run survives `dir: rtl` unreordered — asserted on `+$1,234.56 (+7.89%)`
   specifically.
9. Nothing clips at 1.15 text scale in either theme.
10. Holdings, watchlist and closed trades each render through **one** widget shared between the
    preview and its pushed route — DEF098's two-renderer class, which this screen is already
    close to reproducing.
