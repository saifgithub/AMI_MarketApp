## Headline

**Residual flip rate at n=5 is 5/60 votes (8.3%) — above the ~5% WP07 threshold — and
every single flip was caused by a lost draw, not by the PM changing its mind.**

On XOM, 15 of 20 votes returned APPROVE and 5 returned PASS. All 5 PASS outcomes came
from votes where at least one draw failed to parse; **all 7 votes that got a full 5
readable draws returned APPROVE, unanimously in outcome.** The parse loss shrinks the
denominator to an even number, the vote ties, and `_vote_pm_samples` breaks ties to PASS.
The verdict flipped because a read was thrown away, not because the desk disagreed.

That reframes the answer to WP07's question. Raising n is not the lever. **Fixing the
parse loss is** — and that is [R43's](../../dev_instructions/R43_DEF_draft.md) DEF, now
with a 300-draw corpus behind it instead of a 5-draw smoke run.

## The question, stated precisely

Production already votes `pm_self_consistency_samples=5` (CR214, `config.py:751`). R47
asks how often the shipped procedure still lands somewhere other than its own centre.

**The ~19.7% baseline is not the comparison it looks like.** Read the provenance
(`config.py:737-741`, `_vote_pm_samples` docstring, CR197): replaying 136 committed
convenes three times each on byte-identical prompts, **26 of 132 (19.7%) did not return a
*unanimous* verdict** — a *non-unanimity* rate across 3 draws, not a flip rate of a
verdict. The comparable per-verdict figure in that same comment is the other one: **a
single draw disagrees with the 3-vote majority 6.6% of the time.**

So two numbers are reported here, kept apart:

- **vote-level flip rate** — the n=5 analogue of the 6.6%: how often a full 5-draw
  production vote lands off the modal outcome.
- **draw-level dissent + parse loss** — the n=5 analogue of the 19.7%: how much the
  individual reads disagree, and how many are lost entirely.

Anyone quoting "19.7% → X%" without that distinction is comparing two different things.

## Method

Three cached profiles, one full 12-agent convene each to **fix the transcript**, then the
**PM stage alone** replayed k=20 times per profile — each repetition a complete
production-mirroring vote (5 draws, production `_parse_pm_verdict`, production
`_vote_pm_samples`). The eleven upstream agents are never rerun: if the transcript moved
between repetitions, a verdict difference could be the transcript's rather than the PM's
own decode variance. `pm_replay.py` reads the `system_prompt` / `user_message` bytes back
out of the banked `run.json` verbatim.

Requests strictly sequential — the serve is a shared LAN resource. 300 draws, ~1.5 h.

`flip_rate` is measured against the **modal** outcome, not the first vote: the first vote
is one sample of the same distribution, and anchoring on it would make the number depend
on run order.

| Profile | Mandate | LIVE fields | PM prompt tokens | Convene verdict (the fixed transcript's own PM run) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CAT | `long` | 71/72 | 8,039 | PASS 4/4 |
| MSFT | `long` | 90/92 | 9,363 | PASS 2/4 (a genuine split) |
| XOM | `long` | 90/92 | 9,036 | APPROVE 4/4 |

The three span the outcome range — a stable PASS, a contested name, a stable APPROVE.

### The flip mechanism, isolated

Outcome broken down by how many of the five draws actually parsed:

| Draws parsed | XOM outcomes | CAT | MSFT |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | **APPROVE ×7, PASS ×0** | PASS ×19 | APPROVE ×15 |
| 4 | APPROVE ×5, **PASS ×4** | PASS ×1 | APPROVE ×5 |
| 3 | APPROVE ×3, **PASS ×1** | — | — |

Every XOM flip sits in a degraded vote. The five PASS votes read:

```
vote  2  parsed 4  agree 2/4  ['APPROVE', None, 'PASS', 'APPROVE', 'PASS']
vote  9  parsed 3  agree 2/3  ['APPROVE', None,  None,  'PASS',    'PASS']
vote 11  parsed 4  agree 2/4  ['APPROVE', 'PASS', None, 'APPROVE', 'PASS']
vote 12  parsed 4  agree 2/4  ['APPROVE', None, 'APPROVE', 'PASS',  'PASS']
vote 17  parsed 4  agree 2/4  [None, 'PASS', 'PASS', 'APPROVE', 'APPROVE']
```

Four are 2-2 ties on four survivors; one is 2-1 on three. `_vote_pm_samples` breaks ties
to PASS (the safe side), which is correct behaviour given the input it was handed — the
input is what is wrong. **An odd sample count chosen precisely so a majority always
exists is silently turned even by the parse loss.**

### What the lost draws actually were (all 300 draws, 23 losses)

| Failure mode | Count | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Fabricated tool-use narration | **11** | *"Let me pull the fact sheet first, then convene the room."* The PM roleplays orchestrating the Room instead of returning a verdict. `finish_reason=stop`, 34–60 output tokens. |
| **Correct verdict in the wrong format** | **9** | A complete decision — action, sizing, entry/stop/target, mandate compliance — written as labelled prose instead of the JSON envelope. The parser discards it. |
| Truncated at the 1700 ceiling | **3** | `finish_reason=length`, clipped mid-JSON. Thinking was **OFF**. |

The second row is the expensive one. Verbatim, one discarded draw:

```
Verdict: APPROVE
Reasoning: Execution's 3% sizing with a tight R:R is too aggressive for a Risk-3
mandate given the 183% capital return ratio against FCF … Cutting size to the
Conservative/Risk-3 balanced midpoint of 2.75% and moving the stop to $147.74 …
Final trade: XOM | BUY | 2.75% | $164.15 | $169.68 | $147.74 | 180d
Mandate compliance: PASS
```

That is a good analyst turn thrown away on a formatting technicality, and it is one of
the votes that tipped a verdict to PASS.

## Conclusion

1. **The residual flip rate at n=5 is 8.3% (5/60), above WP07's ~5% threshold** — but it
   is not the PM being indecisive. It is a parse bug expressed as a verdict flip.
2. **Raising n is the wrong fix and would partly mask the problem.** n=7 would survive one
   lost draw more often, at linear decode cost (the latency ceilings at `config.py:123/:139`
   are real), while still discarding good verdicts and still tying when two are lost. The
   defect stays; only its visibility drops. **Recommendation: leave `pm_self_consistency_samples`
   at 5** and fix the parse path.
3. **R43's DEF is the load-bearing item here, and this run strengthens it considerably** —
   7.7% loss over 300 draws against DEF067's <2% acceptance bar, with a demonstrated
   causal chain to flipped verdicts. Its two candidate fixes both bite: CR210's
   `pm_verdict_schema()` grammar (`room_json_constraints_enabled`, currently OFF) makes
   both prose modes unrepresentable; the cheaper `raw_decode` fallback in
   `extract_json_object` recovers the trailing-prose class. **This note files nothing** —
   minting the DEF is the Architect's job.
4. **Denominator honesty.** A vote that discards draws reports agreement over the
   survivors: XOM vote 9 reads `2/3` and CAT vote 4 reads `4/4` — "unanimous" — with a
   read thrown away. The string is not wrong, it is under-specified. Whatever the DEF
   does, `agreement` should carry the requested count too.

## Caveats

- **Not decision quality.** Nothing here says whether APPROVE or PASS was *right* on any
  of the three names. A stable wrong verdict scores identically to a stable right one.
- **Three transcripts, one mandate (`long`).** The mandate was held fixed so the PM's
  input varied only by ticker. Flip rates under other mandates are unmeasured.
- **Ticker-dependent, strongly.** 0%, 0%, 25% is not a distribution you can average with a
  straight face; the pooled 8.3% is reported because WP07 asked for a rate, but XOM is the
  finding and CAT/MSFT are the control. Why XOM's PM prompt provokes 3× the parse loss of
  the others on a near-identical sheet is **not** established here.
- **The CAT pickle is older than the other two.** Cached 2026-09-02 at 71/72 LIVE fields;
  MSFT and XOM were fetched 2026-09-03 at 90/92 and include the earnings-revision /
  surprise-history fields from the R21 data work. Within-profile flip rates are unaffected
  (every repetition on a profile saw byte-identical bytes), but CAT's numbers are not
  strictly comparable to the other two.
- **One serve, one model build.** Not transferable to another model (CR217's business).
- **A brief LAN outage** hit the first XOM convene (`Host is down`; `.74` unreachable with
  100% ping loss while the gateway and melehost answered normally). It recovered within
  ~15 s of polling; that convene was discarded and rerun clean. No R47 replay was
  affected. Recorded because an unexplained gap in a run log is worse than a noted one.
