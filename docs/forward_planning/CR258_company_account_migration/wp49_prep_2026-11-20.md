## 1. Decision: new IDs for Beta, not the existing ones

**Decision: new bundle ID and new Play package name under the company accounts. Keep the personal ones for Alpha, untouched.**

Proposed values (the founder may choose others; every script reads them from `mobile/beta_accounts.env`):

1. iOS bundle ID: `ai.agenticmarketintel.trade`
2. Android applicationId / Play package: `ai.agenticmarketintel.trade`
3. Kotlin `namespace` and `MainActivity` stay `ai.agenticmarketintel.ami_trade` (the namespace is independent of the applicationId; the Android doc recommends them equal but supports the split, and changing it is a code move with no benefit).

Why these shapes: the Beta app becomes the public app (CR267: TestFlight external, then Play closed track, same store records), and a Play package name is permanent, so no `.beta` suffix. Not `ai.agenticmarketintel.amitrade`: Apple bundle IDs are case-insensitive, so it is a case-variant of the personal `...amiTrade` and could collide with the personal App ID.

Why not keep the existing IDs:

1. **Apple.** An app can only be transferred if "the app must have at least one version that was released to the App Store" ([App transfer criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/app-transfer-criteria/)). Our personal app is TestFlight-only, so it cannot be transferred. Without a transfer the bundle ID stays registered to the personal team, so the company team cannot hold it. (App Store Connect says a transferred app "maintains its Bundle ID"; that applies to transfers only.) Apple also requires TestFlight to be switched off before any transfer ([Overview of app transfer](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/overview-of-app-transfer/)).
2. **Google.** "Package names can't be deleted or re-used" and are "unique and permanent" ([Play Console Help, create and set up your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152)). `ai.agenticmarketintel.ami_trade` already exists in the personal Play account (fastlane has pushed to its internal track), so the company account cannot create it. The only route is Play's app transfer between two active accounts ([Transfer app ownership](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247)): it moves users, stats and ratings and offers a new upload key / signing key, but it drags the personal app's history into the company account and needs Play support (the page quotes two business days). Not worth it for an app with no public users.
3. Android doc: "Once you publish your app, you should never change the application ID. If you change the application ID, Google Play Store treats the upload as a completely different app." A new package is a new app by definition, which is what we want.

CR258's open decision 1 ("keep or new IDs") is therefore closed as **new IDs**. Nothing about this needs the personal accounts to release anything.

### What this means for existing Alpha testers

1. Alpha is unchanged: personal TestFlight + Play internal, `api-alpha`, bundle `ai.agenticmarketintel.amiTrade`, package `ai.agenticmarketintel.ami_trade`. D-073 keeps personal as the testing-only account.
2. The Beta app is a **different app** on the device (different bundle ID / package): it installs next to the Alpha app, shares no keychain, local data or anonymous account, and talks to `api-beta` with a separate database. Alpha testers who should join Beta are **invited fresh** to the company TestFlight / Play track; nothing migrates.
3. No Sign in with Apple `sub` migration is needed: the identifier is team-scoped, so the same Apple ID gets a different `sub` under the company team, but Beta starts with its own empty database. (Apple's "transfer identifier" process exists only for app transfers, which we are not doing.)
4. Personal Alpha builds keep working until their TestFlight builds expire (90 days after upload). Retiring the personal artefacts is CR258 phase E, after Beta is stable, not part of this prep.

## 4. Play App Signing and the upload key

1. **App signing key**: let Google generate it (the default for new apps, and required for AAB). Do not upload the personal one.
2. **Upload key**: generate a **new** company upload keystore; do not reuse the personal `~/.android-keys/ami-trade-upload.keystore`. Reuse would tie the company app's release path to a personal file, and on a later account transfer Google offers to replace it anyway.
3. Local generation (run by the founder, any time; **PREP**, touches no console):
   `keytool -genkeypair -v -keystore ~/.android-keys/company/ami-trade-beta-upload.jks -alias upload -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000`
   then create `~/.android-keys/company/keystore.properties` with `storeFile=`, `storePassword=`, `keyAlias=upload`, `keyPassword=` and point `AMI_BETA_ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PROPERTIES` at it. Back both files up in the company password manager; neither goes in the repo.
4. Per `fastlane/Fastfile`, the very first upload of a new app is manual in the Play Console web UI (enrolls Play App Signing); `supply` works from release 2 on.
5. After the first upload, copy the **app signing certificate SHA-1** (Play Console, Test and release, App signing) and the upload key's SHA-1 (`keytool -list -v -keystore …`) into the company GCP Android OAuth client, or Google Sign-In fails on Play-installed builds.
6. Org-account note: the closed-test "12 testers for 14 days" rule applies to personal developer accounts; S7 says the company Play account is an Organisation account, so it is a soft target (CR267 WP-54 note). Confirm in the Console when the production-access page first shows.
