`backtest_cutoff_probe.py` established empirically — not from a model card —

event recall reaches **2025-01** (it dates DeepSeek R1 correctly) and fails

the two boundaries plus an 8-week margin. Earlier as-of dates are refused in

## 3. Results (n=125 runs, 13 approvals — directional only)

**Name selection**, 4-week return vs SPY:

| | mean | median | positive |
|---|---|---|---|
| APPROVE (13) | +4.83% | +2.19% | 8/13 (62%) |
| PASS (97) | +0.72% | −0.06% | 47/97 (48%) |

The +4.11-point spread has a date-clustered 95% interval of **−5.1% to +14.6%**
(effective n = 68 dates, not 125 runs). **Removing one name (STX +48.8%) drops
the APPROVE mean to +1.17%** — level with PASS. At 1 week APPROVE is *behind*
PASS. Verdict: directionally positive, statistically nothing, which is what
n=13 should look like.

**Followed-the-trade P&L** (`trade_pnl_pit-pilot-2.md`) — the question a user
actually asks. Walking each approval's stated entry/stop/target/horizon over
daily bars: **+$1,478 on a $100,000 book (+1.48%), win rate 4/12 (33%)**, one
entry never filled. Market-entry policy gives +1.26%, 5/13 — so it is not an
entry-policy artifact. **Strip the single best trade (M, +70%) and the book
loses $622.**

### Method notes that must survive
- The random-pick null in `report_pit-pilot-2.md` reports p=0.025. **Ignore it.**
  The pilot spread 130 pairs over 71 Fridays, so most dates convened 1–2
  tickers; drawing 1 name from a pool of 1 is not a control. **The full sweep
  must use fewer dates with more tickers each** for that test to mean anything.
- Trade simulation is deliberately pessimistic: same-bar stop+target resolves as
  the stop, gaps fill at the open not the level, positions whose horizon outruns
  the data are marked OPEN and never counted as wins.

## 4. Defects the harness found

**DEF255 (filed, open) — the horizon/stop contradiction.** The PM states
multi-year holding periods that are the *mandate label* restated, not a thesis
window: `Horizon.LONG` renders as "3–10 years" into every prompt and the PM
emits `horizon_days: 1095` (exactly 3 years) in 4 of 13 approvals. Nothing in
the fact sheet supports a 3-year view — technicals are a 3-month window,
fundamentals are TTM, the only forward datum is an FOMC countdown in days. And
the stops contradict it: **4 of the 5 three-year theses were stopped out**, CRM
on a 14.3% stop. The field is unspecified in the PM contract (`horizon_days:
<integer, required if APPROVE>` — no definition, no range, no consistency
requirement), and the code's own fallback (42 days) disagrees with the model's
modal answer (1095) by 26×. It reaches users via the mobile verdict board.
**This is prompt/PM-contract territory — CR156 — and today's work should absorb it.**

**Two harness defects, found and fixed in-session:**

1. **EDGAR YTD differencing.** Filers report *cumulative* year-to-date durations
   (91/183/273/364 days off one fiscal-year start), not discrete quarters.
   Keeping only quarter-length spans found exactly ONE quarter per fiscal year,
   so every TTM failed and **P/E, P/S, FCF yield, EV/EBITDA and dividend yield
   all resolved at 0%** across 25 tickers. Fixed by differencing consecutive YTD
   facts (commit `ee21c8dc`), keeping the FY−(Q1+Q2+Q3) path for filers who do
   report discrete quarters, plus a diluted-share fallback for market cap and 3
   more D&A tag variants. Coverage after: rev_growth 78%, profit_margin 71%,
   net_cash 71%, pe 57%, P/S 64%, fcf_yield 53%, dividend_yield 53%,
   ev_to_ebitda 35% — **75% of pairs carry ≥4 of 8 fields**, and 6 of the
   remaining P/E gaps are genuine loss-makers where P/E should not exist.
2. **402 → orphan index row.** `start_run` wrote the idempotency row before
   `spend()`, so a credit top-up mid-sweep left the pair indexed-but-never-run
   and the retry 409'd against its own orphan. Fixed in the same commit.
3. **Prompt-scanner mis-attribution.** The leakage scanner correlated
   `llm_audit` rows by user + a ±60s window; a sweep uses one batch user and
   spaces runs 15s apart, so the tail swallowed the next run's rows and produced
   **447 phantom hard fails**. Now attributes by prompt content (each Room
   prompt names its own ticker in the fact-sheet header).

## 5. Leakage verdict: CLEAN

- **0 hard fails** across 1,520 audit rows after the scanner fix.
- Independently verified by a different method: all **126 distinct (ticker,
  fact-sheet anchor) pairs in the prompts matched their own batch pair exactly —
  zero mismatches.** No prompt was ever anchored past its own as-of date.
- 156 price "flags" are the expected coincidence class — spot-checked WMT
  @2026-06-19, where the flagged 117.18 is simply the as-of close that had not
  moved by the following Monday.
- 1 correlation failure: the single run in flight when the sweep was stopped
  (`status=running`, so no wall-clock window). Benign.
- Zero `AsOfLeakageError` raised in the API logs for the whole batch.

## 6. What the pilot proves about the tested Room

**APPROVE rate is 13/125 ≈ 10.4%**, against the live Room's measured 24–37%.
The gap is the ablations: no news, no social, no analyst consensus. That is a
finding in itself — the live Room's willingness to approve leans on the soft
inputs the backtest cannot reconstruct — and it means the APPROVE arm will stay
small, so the full sweep needs its ~800 convenes concentrated on fewer dates to
get both statistical power and a working random-pick control.

## 7. Standing-instrument value (the reason the design rule mattered)

Because as-of mode lives in the production services rather than a fork, the
harness re-tests the real Room after any change. The pinned regression recipe:
same universe file, `--n-pairs 26 --repeat-pairs 0 --seed 164`, fresh batch-id,
~1.1 h. Any prompt or data-feed CR landing today can be verified against it, and
`backtest_trade_pnl.py` grades the trade instruction, not just the name.
