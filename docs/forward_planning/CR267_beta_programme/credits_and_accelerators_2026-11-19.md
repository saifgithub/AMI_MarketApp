# Startup credits and accelerators for a Malaysia-based company

CR267 WP-40, for gate S5. Every fact below was read on **2026-11-19** from the vendor's or programme's own page (URL beside each row). "Not stated" means the page loaded and does not say; "can't verify" means the page did not load or only a third-party source had it (listed in section 4, and not used elsewhere). Amounts and terms change; re-read the page before applying. Companion file: `hosting_comparison_2026-11-18.md`.

## 1. Summary: top 3 actions this week

1. **Apply to Cloudflare for Startups, Tier 3: up to $10,000, about 30 minutes.** Bootstrapped or self-funded with under $1M raised needs no backer. Review takes up to 48 hours. Credits last one year or until used. They cover Workers, R2 (up to $10,000), D1, Queues, Pages and similar; they do not cover registrar services. Needs a live public website, a business email on that domain, a company under 10 years old, and a first-time application (one per startup).
2. **Apply the same afternoon to the three no-backer hyperscaler tiers: Microsoft for Startups (up to $5,000 after business verification), Google for Startups Start tier ($2,000), AWS Activate Founders ($1,000 to $5,000). About 1 hour each, about $8,000 to $12,000 combined if all are approved.** Approval is discretionary on all three. Only worth the time if production or Beta may run on that cloud. Azure credits state they cover Virtual Machines. Google and AWS pages say less. Google requires the company website domain to match the email domain and the billing account. AWS requires an account on the Paid Tier Plan.
3. **Apply to Alibaba Cloud AI Catalyst (up to $120,000 in cloud credits, plus up to 2B free Model Studio tokens, about 1 hour) and Huawei Cloud Startup Program (minimum $5,000 voucher, more on application).** Alibaba's page states no funding or backer requirement, only: unlisted company, founded within 10 years, accessible website, AI as core technology, a plan for using Alibaba Cloud. Campaign period ends 2027-03-31. Whether a Malaysian company qualifies is not stated on either page; the Alibaba form is hosted on Alibaba's Singapore survey site. This is the only route found to a six-figure credit without a backer, and it also corrects the planning table, which has Alibaba as "needs funding".

Not this week: every accelerator route (Table B) is slower and most take equity or need an incorporated Sdn Bhd with an operating history. The DeepInfra grant is likely out of reach (needs $250K to $10M raised).
