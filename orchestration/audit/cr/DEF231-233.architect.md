# Round 2 — the MAJOR is fixed

Branch: `main` @ **`f2f24bb5`** (pushed to origin). depends-on: none.

- `f2f24bb5` fix(room): DEF231 audit r1 MAJOR — the level must be the verb's
  own object, not merely near it

**The finding was right and the diagnosis was exact.** The round-1 gap
`[^\n$]{0,45}?` asks only that the price sit NEAR the verb. Proximity is not
grammar, and "reclaim" taking a non-price direct object is ordinary PM prose.
Both of your reproductions annotated a level the sentence made no directional
claim about — a false `[AMI …]` note in the same voice as the genuine SNDK
correction, which is precisely the cost my own comment names.

**Fix.** The gap is now a **whitelist of the tokens a level reference is built
from** — articles/determiners, the prepositions, the level nouns (`level`,
`support`, `low`, `high`, `range`, `floor`, …) and N-day/week/month spans —
rather than a distance:

```python
_LEVEL_CONNECTIVE = (
    r"(?:the|a|an|its|their|this|that|prior|previous|former|recent|old|key|"
    r"to|toward|towards|above|below|back|of|at|around|near|over|under|"
    r"level|levels|support|resistance|line|low|lows|high|highs|price|mark|"
    r"zone|area|floor|ceiling|band|base|range|session|close|day|week|month|"
    r"moving|average|sma|ema|\d+(?:-(?:day|week|month))?)"
)
_LEVEL_GAP = rf"(?:[\s,–—-]+{_LEVEL_CONNECTIVE}\b)*[\s,]*"
```

One unrecognised noun ends the match. So the failure mode is a **miss**, by
construction, which is the bias this check was supposed to have and did not.
The whitelist also subsumes the old "no second `$` in the gap" rule — `$` is
not in the vocabulary — so that ad-hoc constraint is gone rather than layered.

**Both reproductions, against the real function:**

```
>>> f("The company must reclaim its margin story before we'd pay $52.30 for it.", 60.00)

>>> f("Sentiment could pull back toward caution before the print, last quoted at $52.30.", 45.00)

```

# Round 3 — the corpus sweep, and what it found (DEF234)

- `3f4d33d2` fix(room): DEF234 — the level parser stopped at the thousands

**Context: this round exists because Saiful asked how the round-1 MAJOR got in

**DEF234 (severe, was live ~25 min).** `(\d+(?:\.\d+)?)` stops at a comma:

```
>>> _annotate_direction_against_price(
...   "we'd want to see it break above $1,073.46 with volume expansion", 1080.00)
"[AMI checked this instruction against the price: the last close $1080.00 is
 already 107900.0% ABOVE $1.00, ...]"
```

One occurrence in the corpus, but the exposure is every four-figure level a PM
writes with a separator, and the failure is maximally visible rather than
subtle. **This was present in round 1 and round 2 and neither of us caught it** —
worth noting on your side too: the round-1 audit reproduced a false positive
from *invented* prose, which is a different distribution again from the real
corpus. Inventing adversarial inputs found the grammar bug; only the corpus
found the numeric one.

**Second, milder hole — introduced BY the round-2 fix.** The connective
whitelist rejected markdown emphasis, so `break above **$27.65**` (the PM
writes markdown; it is in the corpus) silently stopped matching. A coverage
loss invisible to every test, because every authored test case was written
without markdown. Found only by **diffing** the round-1 and round-2 extraction
sets over the corpus (126 → 119), not by looking at either alone.

**Fix, three parts:**

1. `_LEVEL_NUMBER` accepts grouped thousands; the parse strips separators.
2. `_LEVEL_GAP` tolerates `*`, `_`, `(` immediately before the `$`.
3. `_DIRECTION_MAX_PLAUSIBLE_GAP_PCT = 400.0` — a backstop for the NEXT parse
   defect in this class rather than for this one. A PM does not tell a user to
   wait for a level five times away from the price, so a gap that large is
   evidence the extraction failed. Refuse to annotate, log
   `room_pm_direction_implausible_gap`. A miss is this check's designed failure
   mode; a confident "107900.0% ABOVE" is the one it must never have.

**Corpus after the fix:** 121 extractions over 946 reasons, zero truncated
mid-number, zero markdown-mangled, and the two round-1 false-positive shapes
still silent.
