## BLOCKER — Lesson 368's risk-adjusted return-gap figures do not match the
## cited paper, under any of the paper's three reported measures

§2 of the submission named this exact claim as the one to re-check
independently: *"it still looks wrong at a glance and that is exactly the
kind of claim that survives by looking familiar."* It is wrong — not
transposed-but-defensible, actually wrong against the paper the frontmatter
cites (`sources:` names *Quarterly Journal of Economics 116(1), 2001* — the
published version, not a working paper).

**What I did.** Fetched the actual PDF from the cited author's own faculty
page (`faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/gender/boyswillbeboys.pdf`,
confirmed as QJE Feb. 2001, pp. 261–292) and read it directly — abstract,
Section III.A (all households), III.B (single households), and Table IV (the
two-factor risk-adjusted regression). Not relayed, not summarized by a
search snippet — read the primary source myself.

**The lesson's claim** (368, body text and Quiz 1, marked as the correct
answer):

> men traded 45% more than women and earned annual risk-adjusted net returns
> **1.4** percentage points lower … single men traded 67% more than single
> women and earned **2.3** points less per year

**What the paper actually reports** — three distinct measures, none of
which produces `1.4` (full sample) / `2.3` (singles):

| Measure | Full sample (women vs. men) | Singles (women vs. men) |
|---|---|---|
| Net return *reduced by trading* (abstract: 2.65% men / 1.72% women) | **0.94** pp/yr | **1.44** pp/yr |
| Net own-benchmark abnormal return (§III.A/III.B, the paper's primary measure) | **0.94**%/yr (0.078pp/mo × 12, p. [278]) | **1.4**%/yr (0.120pp/mo × 12, explicitly stated: *"annually 1.4 percent"*, p. [278–279]) |
| Two-factor (market+size) risk-adjusted alpha — the paper's own literal "risk-adjusted" measure, Table IV | **1.1**%/yr (9bp/mo, stated directly in-text) | **~1.72**%/yr (0.143pp/mo × 12, Table IV Panel B) |

The number **`1.4`** genuinely appears in the paper — but as the **singles**
annualized own-benchmark gap, not the full-sample gap the lesson attaches it
to. The number **`2.3`** does not appear anywhere in the paper under any of
the three measures; the closest real figures for the singles comparison are
1.4%, 1.44pp, or 1.72%. On top of that, the paper is explicit that **raw net
returns between men and women were not statistically significantly
different** (p. [278]: *"For none of these returns are the differences
between men and women statistically significant"* — annualized net returns
were actually 16.3% men vs. 16.9% women, i.e. reversed in sign from "men
earned … lower"), so reading the lesson's phrasing as a raw-return
comparison makes it wrong in direction as well as magnitude.

This is graded content — **Quiz 1 marks the wrong pairing as the correct
answer** — carrying a citation to a real, checkable, high-profile paper that
says something else. This is precisely the CR060 failure mode CR132 exists
to prevent, and precisely the thing the architect flagged as needing an
independent check rather than a self-check. It is not a stylistic
quibble — a reader who verifies the citation finds a paper that contradicts
the lesson.

**Suggested fix** (not prescriptive — the architect owns the edit): the
cleanest correction is to use the paper's own headline own-benchmark figures
and its own words, which the lesson is already 90% of the way to doing
correctly for the trading-frequency side: *"men traded 45% more … and their
trading cost them 0.94 percentage points more per year than it cost women
(own-benchmark net return); narrow to singles and the gap widens to 1.4
points."* That keeps the "gap widens for singles" narrative the lesson is
built around — which the real numbers still support (0.94 → 1.4, or 1.1 →
1.72 under the risk-adjusted framing) — without the invented magnitude.

### MINOR (new) — Lesson 369 slightly overstates the Huang & Chan institutional-null result

"The institutions on the other side of those same trades showed no such
pattern" reads stronger than the paper's own abstract, which reports futures
proprietary firms DO show a break-even effect after large morning losses
specifically — it just doesn't move market volatility/liquidity the way
individual traders' does (which is the lesson's actual, correct point).
Recommend softening to "didn't move the market the way individual traders
did." Not blocking on its own.

### Refinement on the BLOCKER above — the full-sample "1.4" is independently defensible, narrowing the fix

Re-reading *Boys Will Be Boys* footnote 21 (the three-factor Fama–French
extension of Table IV, adding book-to-market as HML): *"The intercepts from
these regressions indicate that after accounting for the market, size, and
book-to-market tilts of their portfolios, women outperformed men by twelve
basis points a month (1.4 percent annually)."* This sentence directly
follows and extends the paragraph's own two-factor "All households" result
(9bp/mo, 1.1%/yr) with no marital-status qualifier — i.e. it's the
All-households figure, under the paper's own definition of "risk-adjusted
return" (the regression intercept, stated explicitly a few lines earlier:
*"the intercept, αi, is an estimate of risk-adjusted return"*). So the
lesson's **full-sample** "1.4 percentage points, risk-adjusted" claim is
independently confirmable, not just plausible-sounding — it genuinely is in
the paper, under the three-factor model, for the full sample. No parallel
three-factor breakdown by marital status is published anywhere (footnote 21
is narrative, not a table), so there is no equivalent published
"risk-adjusted, singles" figure at all — meaning `2.3` isn't just
transposed or under-cited, it has no home anywhere in this paper under any
of its three measures (own-benchmark, two-factor, three-factor), full
sample or singles. This narrows the architect's fix: the full-sample "1.4"
sentence can likely stand as written (it's correct under the three-factor
reading); only the singles "2.3" needs to change, to the genuinely-published
two-factor singles figure (**1.7%**, Table IV Panel B, 0.143pp/mo × 12) or
the own-benchmark singles figure explicitly stated in text as **"1.4 percent
annually"** — but if own-benchmark 1.4 is reused for singles, the full-sample
sentence needs to switch measures too so the two numbers in the same
sentence are drawn from the same yardstick (own-benchmark: 0.94/1.44, or
risk-adjusted: 1.4/1.7 — not one from each). Either resolution keeps the
"gap widens for singles" narrative intact, which the real numbers support
under every measure checked (0.94→1.44, 1.1→1.7, or 1.4→[no published
figure]).

Also cross-checked the WebSearch tool (not a primary source, used only as a
