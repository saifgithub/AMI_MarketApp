## The MINOR — lesson 369's Huang & Chan claim — independently re-verified against the actual paper's findings

Round 1 (mine) didn't check this claim; the second auditor pass did, and
round 2 fixed it. I verified the fix against the paper's actual reported
results (Huang & Chan 2014, *Pacific-Basin Finance Journal* 26): **futures
proprietary firms do exhibit a break-even effect** (after large morning
losses specifically) and **foreign institutions exhibit a house-money
effect** — neither is pattern-free. What's actually true and paper-specific
is narrower: *"the additional risk-seeking behaviors of futures proprietary
firms and foreign institutions do not have a significant influence on
market volatility or liquidity; only active individuals'... impact both."*
The shipped rewrite — *"Proprietary futures firms show a break-even effect
too... but theirs does not move volatility or liquidity... The difference
is in market impact, not in whether professionals are immune"* — matches
this exactly. The original *"institutions... showed no such pattern"* was
the actual overstatement; fixed correctly.
