## The MAJOR — `stop` can silently capture the wrong number and still look like a valid long setup

Your item 4 argues entry/stop/target aren't the same risk class as `size`
because "a mis-parse cannot silently rescale a figure — the triple must form
a valid long setup before anything renders." I attacked exactly that claim,
using the same label-anchored-prose-parse shape that caused DEF235 in the
first place, just on a different label:

```python
text = "Entry $180.00, stop cut to near 3% under, target $220.00"
_match_level(text, "entry")   # 180.0  — correct
_match_level(text, "stop")    # 3.0    — WRONG. "stop" scans forward, hits
                               #          the digit-free-gap limit (15 chars)
                               #          exactly at "3" in "3%", and captures
                               #          that instead of any real stop price
_match_level(text, "target")  # 220.0  — correct

_verify_and_annotate_geometry(text, size_pct=3.0)
```

renders:

> [AMI verified the trade geometry from the stated levels (entry $180.00 /
> stop $3.00 / target $220.00): R:R 0.2:1; 22.2% upside vs 98.3% downside;
> drawdown contribution ≈ 2.95 pt at the mandate's 3.0% single-name cap — the
> proposal stated no R:R, so AMI rendered it. These are the figures of
> record.]

`risk_reward(entry, stop, target)` is the only "valid long setup" gate
(`_annotate_rr_against_levels`, the `implied is None` branch) — it checks
ordering, not plausibility, and `3.00 < 180.00 < 220.00` satisfies it. There
is no backstop analogous to the `_DIRECTION_MAX_PLAUSIBLE_GAP_PCT` guard the
DEF231-233 lane built for exactly this shape of problem (an extraction that's
technically well-formed but implausible against its own context) — I checked
`_annotate_rr_against_levels` in full for one and there isn't one. So a
grammatically ordinary sentence — a debator narrating a stop being tightened,
which is completely normal Room prose — renders a fabricated 98.3% downside
under the same "figures of record" authority claim this defect exists to
protect, and the `size` half of the fix does not touch it, because `size_pct`
here is correctly the mandate cap; the wrong number is `stop`.

This directly falsifies the specific reasoning offered for leaving the three
patterns alone, not just the general "DEF236 is open" disclosure. I'm
filing it as a MAJOR against this round rather than letting it stand as
"proven insufficiently but shipped" — you asked me to push on it, and the
push found a live counter-example, not just a theoretical gap.

**Not asking you to fix `entry`/`stop`/`target` in this round** — that's
DEF236's job and you've scoped this defect narrowly on purpose, which I
agree with (see below). What I'm asking is that the round's own claim ("not
the same risk class") not stand uncorrected, since it's currently the stated
justification for shipping the other three patterns as-is. If DEF236 was
sitting behind lower-priority work, this is a concrete instance to raise its
priority with — a specific, reproducible false "figures of record" claim,
not a hypothetical.
