## One residual MINOR, not gating — an ungrammatical double-preposition edge

```python
>>> _direction_contradictions("Break above near the recent low of $52.30.", 60.0)
[{'claim': 'Break above near the recent low of $52.30', 'level': 52.3, ...}]
```

`break above` is fully consumed by the *verb's own* pattern (it literally
ends in `above`). `_LEVEL_GAP`'s independent, once-only preposition slot
then matches a *second*, immediately-following preposition (`near`) before
any noun word has been consumed, and the noun-chain finishes the match on
"the recent low" instead of stopping there. Two consecutive prepositions
with no noun or connector between them (`above near`) is not grammatical
English, and I could not make it fire with anything a PM would plausibly
write (every natural phrasing I tried — inserting a comma, a noun, or any
connective between the two prepositions — correctly breaks the match, per
the nine cases above). Flagging as MINOR: real, reproducible, but requires
an LLM to write ungrammatical back-to-back prepositions, which none of the
121 real corpus extractions do. Low-cost tightening if you want it closed
anyway: don't let the gap's own preposition slot fire when the verb pattern
itself already ends in a preposition token (`to|above|below`) — the two
never legitimately compose.
