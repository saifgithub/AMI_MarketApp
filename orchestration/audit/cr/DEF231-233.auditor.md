## The MAJOR — `stock clears $X` fires on a non-price dollar figure exactly the way bare `clear` did

Your fix's theory: what separates the earnings sense from the price sense is
*who* is doing the clearing (an agent — company/management/trader/position —
vs. a price-related subject — price/price action/stock/breakout). That's
true for the corpus you built it against, but it's not true of the word
"stock" in general financial English. "Stock" is exactly as reliable a
metonym for "the company/the security" as "management" or "the position"
are, and it composes just as naturally with a non-price dollar figure:

```python
_direction_contradictions(
    "The stock clears $52.30 billion in market capitalization this quarter.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# FIRES: {'claim': 'stock clears $52.30', ..., 'price_is': 'above', ...}

_direction_contradictions(
    "The stock clears $52.30 billion in daily trading volume today.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# FIRES

_direction_contradictions(
    "The stock clears $52.30 million in short interest outstanding.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# FIRES

_direction_contradictions(
    "The stock clears $52.30 per share in cumulative dividends paid since IPO.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# FIRES
```

4/4, using the exact same `close=60.00, level=52.30` setup your own
`test_clear_still_fires_on_the_price_sense` uses. "XYZ stock clears $50
billion in market cap" is not a contrived sentence — it's a standard
financial-headline construction, arguably more common than several of the
round-6 earnings-sense counter-examples were. It renders the identical
fabricated directional note the round-6 MAJOR did, on a number that was
never a price claim.

**Why the corpus-attestation guard doesn't catch this — and can't, as
designed.** `test_every_clear_subject_is_attested_by_the_corpus` proves each
whitelisted subject *co-occurs with "clear(s/ing)" somewhere in the corpus*
— it's a check that the word is real, not a check that the word is
unambiguous. "Stock" genuinely is attested for the price sense (your own
"capital preserved until the stock clears the $52.30 breakout level"
fixture row), so the guard passes; it has no way to see that the same word
also heads a market-cap/volume/short-interest/dividend claim, because no row
in the 811-row corpus happens to write that sentence. This is DEF234's own
diagnosis repeating one level down: *"a false-positive class is invisible to
mutation testing by construction, because no test asserts on it, so no
mutant dies"* — here it's invisible to the corpus-attestation guard by the
same construction, because no row asserts the ambiguity either.

**I checked whether `price` / `price action` / `breakout` share this hole
and don't think they do, at least not naturally.** I could only produce
awkward, non-idiomatic constructions for those three ("price action clears
$52.30 million in trading commissions") — they technically fire too, but
nothing in ordinary financial writing would produce them, unlike "stock
clears $X in market cap," which is closer to a stock headline cliché than an
edge case. Flagging the technical fact for completeness, not gating on it.

**Not filing a fix.** Two shapes occur to me and I haven't tried to break
either, so take them as leads, not a decision already made: (1) drop `stock`
from the subject whitelist and accept the coverage loss (you already
accepted a similar loss on the elided-subject miss this same round, on the
same "a miss costs nothing" principle); (2) keep the subject gate but add a
scale-noun exclusion *for the object side* — your own "why not a suffix
gate" section shows a suffix-only approach fails on the agent-subject cases
because the disqualifying information isn't always in the suffix, but it
didn't test combining subject-gate AND scale-exclusion, which is a different
proposal than the one you rejected.
