## Attack #1 (the one you asked me to attack hardest) — DEF231's regex fires on a plausible PM sentence it shouldn't

You named this yourself: *"attack the regex: find a sentence where it fires and
should not... If you can produce a plausible PM sentence that annotates
wrongly, that is a real finding, not a nit."* Found one, reproduced directly
against the real function, not a hand-trace:

```python
>>> from app.services.room_runner import _direction_contradictions
>>> text = "The company must reclaim its margin story before we'd pay $52.30 for it."
>>> _direction_contradictions(text, 60.00)
[{'claim': "reclaim its margin story before we'd pay $52.30", 'level': 52.3,
  'close': 60.0, 'gap_pct': 14.7, 'price_is': 'above'}]
>>> _annotate_direction_against_price(text, 60.00)
("The company must reclaim its margin story before we'd pay $52.30 for it.\n\n"
 "[AMI checked this instruction against the price: the last close $60.00 is "
 "already 14.7% ABOVE $52.30, so that level is not something the price has "
 "yet to win back...]", [...])
```

**Root cause.** `reclaim` is the one verb in `_DIRECTIONAL_CLAIMS` with no
required preposition — `reclaim(?:s|ing|ed)?` matches bare, then the 45-char
gap-with-no-second-`$` rule does the rest of the work. Every other verb
anchors tighter (`recover... to/above`, `rise... to/above`, `break... above`,
all requiring the preposition to sit directly after the verb). "Reclaim" is
loose because its legitimate shape (*"reclaim the 50-day range low of
$998.19"*) takes the level as a **direct object with no preposition** — but
that same looseness admits *any* nearby, unrelated `$` figure once "reclaim"
takes a **non-price** direct object ("reclaim its margin story", "reclaim
credibility", "reclaim leadership"), which is completely ordinary PM prose.
The sentence above never made a directional claim about $52.30 at all — it's
a valuation reference ("what we'd pay"), not a level to win back — and the
check cannot tell the difference, because nothing about the match requires
the `$` figure to be the verb's own object.

**Not a one-off — the same class fires on a second, differently-anchored verb:**

```python
>>> _direction_contradictions(
...   "Sentiment could pull back toward caution before the print, "
...   "last quoted at $52.30.", 45.00)
[{'claim': 'pull back toward caution before the print, last quoted at $52.30',
  ...}]
```

`pull back to/toward/towards` has the same shape: the preposition is anchored
to the verb, but nothing after the preposition is required to *be* the
verb's object, so "toward caution... last quoted at $X" fires exactly like
"toward $X" would. (I could not get `climb back above` or bare `reclaim ...
to/above` shapes to false-fire the same way — those require the literal
token immediately after the preposition-adjacent word to be part of an
unbroken "back above" span, which real intervening prose breaks.)

**Why this matters more than a stray false positive.** Your own comment in
the code names the exact cost: *"a miss costs nothing; a false annotation on
the Verdict Board would cost the surface its credibility."* This ships a
`[AMI checked this instruction against the price: ...]` note — in the same
voice as the genuine SNDK correction — onto a verdict where the PM made no
directional price claim at all. It is reachable on every real run with no
precondition beyond ordinary PM narration (no withheld agents, no synthetic
data needed — confirmed the insertion point at `room_runner.py:2916` is the
unconditional shared point, not gated behind `ctx.withheld`, so this is not a
theoretical corner). Not covered by any existing test: every "coherent"
case in `test_a_coherent_instruction_is_left_alone` is verb-immediately-
followed-by-its-own-`$`-level; none exercise verb-with-non-price-object
followed by an unrelated nearby figure, which is the shape that breaks.

Filing **MAJOR**, not BLOCKER — it's additive-only (doesn't touch action,
size, entry/stop/target, or the safety floor; confirmed `.model_copy` only
ever updates `reason`), and the fix is narrow: require the `reclaim`/`pull
back` shapes to be followed by their own preposition+level with no
non-price noun phrase in between (e.g. tighten the gap pattern to require
"the"/"a"/digits-only text before the `$`, or require `of $` / a bare `$`
immediately after the gap starts), or drop `reclaim` to requiring an
explicit "the ... of $X" shape the way the row's own worked example is
phrased. This is exactly the failure mode you asked me to go find, not
speculative — reproduced against the real function, not invented.
