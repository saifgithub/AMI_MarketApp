## The MAJOR — `per share` gates out genuine price levels, not just non-price quantities

Your reasoning: *"No price is ever `$52.30 billion`, `$52.30 per share`, or
`$52.30 in short interest`."* The first and third are true. The middle one
isn't — a stock price **is** inherently a per-share dollar figure, and PM
prose routinely says so explicitly, especially when a level needs
disambiguating from a market-cap or aggregate number in the same paragraph:

```python
_direction_contradictions(
    "Price breaks above $52.30 per share resistance.", 60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# MISS — []

_direction_contradictions(
    "Shares reclaim $52.30 per share support in the final hour.", 60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# MISS — []

_direction_contradictions(
    "The stock clears $52.30 per share, confirming the breakout.", 60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# MISS — []
```

All three are unremarkable financial writing — "breaks above $X per share" is
if anything more natural than several of your own accepted fires. Unlike the
`in\s+\w+` clause, which you flagged yourself and invited an attack on, this
one shipped with full confidence and no caveat.

**I checked whether `per share` is even earning its keep before calling this
a MAJOR** — dropped only that alternative from `_NOT_A_PRICE_AFTER`, kept the
scale words and `in\s+\w+`, reran the round's own test file: 2 failures, not
0 —`"...$52.30 per share in cumulative dividends..."` and `"...$52.30 per
share in option premium."` both re-fire. So it isn't dead weight; it's doing
real work excluding two of your own constructed non-price cases. **The clause
is necessary and insufficient at the same time** — it can't currently tell
"per share" attached to a non-price quantity from "per share" attached to an
actual price, because nothing about the token "per share" itself carries that
information. The two dividend/premium cases are disambiguated by "cumulative
dividends" / "option premium" — not by "per share" — and the gate is reading
the wrong word.

**Not filing a fix, same as last round's convention.** One shape occurs to me:
route the disambiguation through the same "quantity noun" list "per share"
is currently doing alone — something like requiring "per share" to be
followed by (or preceded near enough by) a quantity noun (dividends, premium,
earnings, proceeds) rather than treating "per share" as sufficient on its
own. I haven't tried to break it and it may have its own gaps the way the
subject gate and the suffix-only gate each did.
