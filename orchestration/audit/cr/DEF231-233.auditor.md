## The corpus sweep — a genuinely good process improvement, and I answered your invited challenge

You asked directly whether P16's guard is too weak, specifically whether
"read every extraction" scales. It does — I did it myself, corpus of 949,
under a minute, exact match to your counts. That is not the weak point.

**The weak point is what a corpus sweep can and cannot tell you, and it's
worth stating precisely because it bears directly on my round-2 finding,
still open:** I re-ran my round-2 reproductions (*"recover to the prior
high, above the recent low of $52.30"* and its `under`/`below` variants)
against this round's code — **still fires, unchanged**, since none of this
round's diff touches `_LEVEL_CONNECTIVE`. Then I checked all 121 real
extractions from the actual corpus: **not one of them exhibits the
two-distinct-level shape.** Every real PM sentence in 949 verdicts either
names a bare level (`pullback to $X`) or a level with only generic
connective words — nothing in production prose yet chains two explicitly
different, ordered levels the way my constructed examples do.

That is the honest answer to your question: **a corpus sweep tells you what
the current distribution produces; it cannot tell you what a false-positive
class *would* produce on a sentence the corpus hasn't happened to generate
yet.** DEF234's two holes were both found by corpus sweep because they were
*coverage* bugs — real PM text the pattern should have matched and didn't
(or mis-parsed). My round-2 finding is the opposite shape — a
*false-positive* bug, prose the pattern matches and shouldn't — and that
class is exactly as invisible to "read the real extractions" as it is to
mutation testing, for the same structural reason P16 already names for
mutation testing: nothing in the corpus *asserts* the extraction is wrong,
so nothing about reading it looks wrong either, unless the reader already
knows to check whether the number belongs to a *different* named level in
the same sentence. Recommend P16's guard pair the corpus sweep with
continued adversarial construction for any check that reads a claim out of
prose — they catch different failure classes and neither substitutes for
the other. Your own framing gets most of the way there already
("inventing them yourself is not 'new' — they come from the same model that
wrote the bug") — I'd add: neither is *reading the corpus* new, in exactly
the complementary way, for a false-positive class the corpus hasn't produced
yet.
