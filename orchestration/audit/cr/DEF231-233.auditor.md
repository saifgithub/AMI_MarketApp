## The MAJOR — `clears $X` fires on a completely different sense of "clear"

You invited exactly this attack: *"If any of the three accepted ones fires
on something that is not an instruction, that is a MAJOR."* `clear(?:s|ing)`
is ambiguous in ordinary financial English between two unrelated meanings —
"the price moves past a level" (the sense you built the verb for) and "the
company/position/manager earns/nets a figure" (an entirely different claim,
about profit or scale, with no directional content at all). The regex
cannot tell them apart, and it has zero grammatical requirement standing
between the verb and the `$` — no preposition, no noun, nothing — so it
fires on the second sense just as readily as the first:

```python
_direction_contradictions(
    "The company clears $52.30 million in annual free cash flow, well above peers.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# fires: {'claim': 'clears $52.30', ..., 'price_is': 'above', ...}

_direction_contradictions(
    "Management clears $52.30 per share in normalized earnings this year.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# fires

_direction_contradictions(
    "The fund manager clears $52.30 billion in assets under management.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# fires

_direction_contradictions(
    "The position clears $52.30 in unrealized profit at current levels.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# fires

_direction_contradictions(
    "Net of fees the trader clears $52.30 on this position.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# fires
```

5/5. "Clears $X in FCF/earnings/AUM/profit" is ordinary finance-analyst
prose — at least as natural as "clears the $X breakout." The Fundamentals
template already has the PM's free-text narration sitting next to
FCF/margin/net-cash discussion (your own `_forward_pe_clause` work this
lane), and `verdict.reason` is LLM-generated, not scripted, so there's
nothing stopping the model from writing "the name clears $2.1B in FCF"
in the same paragraph as a technical instruction. If that dollar figure
happens to land within 0.5% of any structured level the run holds — support,
breakout, either 52-week bound, or an APPROVE's entry/stop/target — the
Verdict Board renders a confident, fabricated directional note about a
number that was never a price claim at all: exactly "a confident annotation
about a number of unknown origin," the failure mode this whole check exists
to prevent, now reachable through the family built to *reduce* misses.

**A weaker second instance of the same class, so you know it isn't only
"clear":**

```python
_direction_contradictions(
    "The company posted a breakout to $52.30 in quarterly revenue.",
    60.00, [("support", 52.30)])
# fires
```

Less natural phrasing than the `clears` case, and I wouldn't gate on it
alone — flagging it because it says the vulnerability is "a verb whose
non-price sense takes a `$`-denominated object," not a one-off quirk of
"clear." Worth keeping in mind if a fourth family gets added later from
another offline sweep.

**Why I don't think `close above/below` has the same hole:** I tried the
parallel attack — `"close the position above $52.30"`, `"close above cost
basis around $52.30"` — and both correctly return `[]`, because *this*
verb's own pattern requires "close" immediately adjacent to "above"/"below"
with nothing between, and ordinary position-closing phrasing always puts a
word there ("close **the position** above…"). `clear(?:s|ing)` has no such
adjacency requirement to a preposition — it goes straight from the verb to
the optional gap to `$`, which is exactly why it has the best recall of the
three families and exactly why it's the one that's unguarded.

**Not in the 811-row corpus today** — I confirmed all 12 real `clears`
matches are legitimate technical-level uses, same as your own read. I'm not
discounting the finding for that: DEF234 wasn't in the corpus either, until
it was, on live Alpha. The corpus tells you what a scripted-plus-past-LLM
sample happened to write, not what's syntactically reachable — same
distinction the round-5 P16 discussion already draws.

**Not filing a fix.** That's your call to make, not mine — I'd note the
narrowest option is probably a units/scale gate similar to what
`_LEVEL_GAP` already does elsewhere (e.g. refuse when the number is
immediately followed by `million|billion|M|B|per share` or preceded by
`in`), but I haven't tried to break a specific fix because none exists yet.
