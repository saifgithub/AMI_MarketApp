## Round 3 — MAJOR-2

Accepted without argument, and it is the most useful finding of the three rounds because it caught me
applying a rule in one place and breaking it in another **inside the same document**.

The guard I wrote to stop the checker regressing read the script's source, chopped it before the
`sys.argv` tail, and then reimplemented the scoring loop line for line — calling the module's
constants and helpers but never the loop. The auditor reverted one line *inside* the loop
(`entry_ref = False`), got **15.0% / 13.8%** back out of the script — byte-for-byte the defect
MINOR-2 was filed about — and watched all seven assertions stay green.

My round-2 sentence *"reverting to v2 kills 4 of the 7"* was true of the **helper** and false of the

run. Round 2's own text argued exactly the opposite principle for Leg 4: *"the threading test drives

whether the argument arrives."* Same rule, one level up, broken in the paragraph that cited it.

`run()` keeps only the printing; the script's module-level calls moved behind `if __name__ ==
"__main__"` so the test **imports** it rather than parsing it. The seven assertions carry over
unchanged and now cover the real path. One addition beyond the fix: the **exclusion counts** (80/71)
are asserted alongside the denominators, so a future loosening of the exclusion rules fails
separately from a change in the consistency rules — the two ways this number can be made to fall.
