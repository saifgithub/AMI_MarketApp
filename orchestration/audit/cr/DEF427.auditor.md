# DEF427 — auditor verdicts (Journal empty-state overflow)

## Round 1 — auditor u66

**SHA audited:** `f544bc6b`. Diff reviewed as `b5de455c..f544bc6b` for `journal_screen.dart`
and the new test file. The DEF426 half of the same commit (`qa/appium/**`) was not in scope and

**Tier A.** The submission said a MAJOR would raise it, and there is one.

### The question

Does the Journal empty state stop overflowing on the devices we ship to? And would the new tests
fail if the overflow came back?

### What holds

The fix itself is correct and it clears E5-U2. I pumped the Journal with the house ad **allowed
to render** (see MAJOR-1) at 393 wide and swept the height:

```
                          h= 852  700  600  560  520  480  440  400  360  320  280
pre-fix  (b5de455c) 1.0     -    -   27   67  107  147  187    - 2exc 2exc 2exc
pre-fix  (b5de455c) 1.3     -   72  172  212  252    - 2exc 2exc 2exc 2exc 2exc
fixed    (f544bc6b) 1.0     -    -    -    -    -    -    -    -   11   51   91
fixed    (f544bc6b) 1.3     -    -    -    -    -    -   13   53   93  133  173
```
(px overflowed; `-` = none; `2exc` = two exceptions, not decomposed. Pre-fix `-` at the smallest heights sits among exceptions and is not read as "fits".)

The pre-fix code overflows by 27px at a 600pt Journal region with the ad showing. That is
E5-U2's 29px, give or take the device's exact inset. The fix removes that overflow at every
height down to 400pt at 1.0×. Five of the lane's twelve tests fail when the fix is reverted, so
the guard does work. The rows marked `2exc` show more than one exception, and I didn't
break them down.

On the architect's questions:
1. `Expanded` always bounds `maxHeight` in today's tree, and the clamp to 0 is the right degrade.
3. With slack, the `minHeight` plus `Center` layout is equivalent to the old one.
4. The app is portrait-locked (`main.dart:69`), so landscape overflow is moot today.

### MAJOR-1 — the tests never render the element that caused the defect

The lane's own root cause says the empty state overflowed because the `AdSlot` sibling took
height from the same `Column`. The test harness never lets that ad render:
- `_pumpEmptyState` pumps twice with no elapsed time.
- `AdSlot` resolves its fill in a `scheduleMicrotask` plus `await gate.request(...)`, so it is
  still `SizedBox.shrink()` when the assertions run.
- After a 2s pump, the same harness renders a `HouseAdCard` 235pt tall (317pt at 1.3×). The
  mandate the harness pins is `floor_pass`, the ad-served plan.
- The harness also gives the *embedded* Journal the whole screen height (`SizedBox(height:
  size.height)`). In the real `YouScreen`, the Journal sits under a header, a segment control,
  a nav bar and the safe-area insets.

Consequences, all measured:
- **Both "iPhone 17 (iOS Simulator, E5-U2)" cases pass on the unfixed code.** I reverted
  `journal_screen.dart` to `b5de455c` and ran the lane's file: 1.0× and 1.3× at 393×852 are
  both green. The test named for the reported defect doesn't reproduce it.
- **With the ad rendered, the fixed layout still overflows** once the Journal region is ≤360pt
  at 1.0×, or ≤440pt at 1.3× (table above). The ad is a fixed 235–317pt sibling *outside* the
  new scroll view, so the scroll can't absorb it. I did not measure how tall the Journal
  region is on an SE-class phone, or at text scales above 1.3×, so I'm not naming a device.
  The numbers above are measured. What happens between them isn't.

**Fix direction:**
- Make the harness render the ad: pump past the fill, or override `adGateProvider` with a
  fixed `HouseAdFill`.
- Give the embedded Journal a realistic height, or pump it inside `YouScreen`.
- Add one case that fails on `b5de455c` at the iPhone 17 condition.
- For the remaining overflow, either put the ad inside the scroll view (CR122's placement is
  "empty state", which that still honours), or let the slot collapse when there's no room.
  Which one is the architect's call.

### Recorded, not scored

- **Below-the-fold impressions.** On a short viewport, the ad can now render below the fold.

  AdMob counts its own impressions by visibility.
