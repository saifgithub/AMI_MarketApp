## Not graded — the ratio is shape-blind, and CR139 should know it

`window_days / n_observations` is a single scalar over the whole window, so it
cannot tell *how* the missing days are arranged. They matter enormously. Twelve
seeds, same 75 missing days, same resulting ratio, two arrangements:

```text
ratio 1.6439, 424 observations
  contiguous hole (one 75-session halt)   mean bias  +0.2%   [-0.0%, +1.1%]
  even thinning  (75 scattered days)      mean bias  +9.1%   [-0.4%, +17.8%]
```

A ~45× difference in the harm at an identical guard reading. The reason is that
variance is additive: a single return spanning k days carries the variance of
all k, so a contiguous gap loses almost nothing, while scattered gaps convert
many multi-day moves into "daily" ones and √252 over-scales every one.

Sweeping the contiguous case, the guard does not fire until L=90 — and at L=75
the σ it is on the verge of refusing is overstated by 0.2%:

```text
hole L=20  ratio 1.4551  sufficient  bias +0.0%
hole L=60  ratio 1.5877  sufficient  bias +0.1%
hole L=75  ratio 1.6439  sufficient  bias +0.7%
hole L=90  ratio 1.7042  REFUSED  sparse_grid
```

I am **not** grading this. The guard errs in the safe direction on the
contiguous side (refusing a number that would have been fine is the conservative
error, and a 90-session halt is a genuinely broken feed), and the diffuse case
below the line is the explicitly deferred CR139 territory with Saiful's
"guard now, correct later" on it.

It is here because CR139's scope says the guard "must be re-derived or retired"
once annualisation fits the grid — and this is the argument for *retired*.
Re-annualising by realised period length handles both shapes correctly and by
construction, whereas no single value of a shape-blind ratio can separate a
+0.2% book from a +9.1% one. The measurement is preserved in the run report.
