## MAJOR M1 — the `insufficient_cause` enum widened 4 → 6 with no parity guard, and a benchmark feed outage silently deletes the beta tile

Executed in a Flutter widget test against the real card, not read from source. Beta
insufficient, every other block sufficient so the card stays **populated**:

```
cause=benchmark_misaligned   betaTile=no   benchmarkNote=YES
cause=feed_unavailable       betaTile=no   benchmarkNote=no
```

`health_card.dart:376` renders the beta tile only when `sufficient == true`, and
`:430-434` gates the only explanation on `insufficientCause == 'benchmark_misaligned'`.
So on a SPY feed outage the tile vanishes and **nothing takes its place** — no note, no
placeholder, no change in the card's chrome. The reader cannot tell "beta was not
measured" from "beta does not apply to this book", and the card still looks like a
complete measurement. That is CR040's question answered the wrong way: it degrades, and
it degrades invisibly.

### I narrowed your claim before grading it

I first forced all five causes onto the beta block and got four silent ones. Then I
traced which the engine can actually emit on a **populated** card
(`portfolio_health.py:326-330, 355, 429-430`):

- `short_window`, `dropped_weight_exceeded`, `zero_variance` all set `estimator_cause`,
  which makes `portfolio_volatility`, `beta`, `effective_bets` **and**
  `risk_contribution` insufficient together. `_allInsufficient` (`health_card.dart:141-149`)
  is then true and the card switches to `_InsufficientState`, which explains itself —
  including a documented `default` branch for the two causes with no pinned copy
  (`:250-256`). That path is **correct** and I am not grading it.
- Only `feed_unavailable` and `benchmark_misaligned` fire with `estimator_ok == true`,
  i.e. with the card populated. Of those two, one has a note.

So the live gap is exactly one cause — the one you predicted. `effective_bets` is
clean for the same reason: its only populated-card insufficiency is `t_over_n`, and
that note exists (`:421-422`), which I also confirmed by execution.

### Why MAJOR

- It is **live**, not latent. `bench_feed_failed` comes from M01's real feed-failure
  set, and a Yahoo rate-limit on SPY is the ordinary case — it is why this module's
  mock-marks refusal exists at all.
- The enum has **no guard in either direction**. `feed_unavailable` and `zero_variance`
  have no `app_en.arb` key and no parity test. M09's `test_cr136_dart_parity.py` covers
  `METRIC_VALUE_UNIT` and the three refusal codes and not this.
- This is the **second occurrence** of the DEF210 shape — a backend enum consumed
  across the language boundary with nothing holding it — and CLAUDE.md's standing rule
  is that a second occurrence gets an entry *with a guard*. M09's audit produced the
  guard for the first one; this enum was minted here and left out of it.

### Fix

Two halves, and the second is the one that lasts:

1. Give `feed_unavailable` a note in the beta branch, or invert the branch so any
   insufficient beta gets *some* explanation and `benchmark_misaligned` merely gets a
   more specific one. Inverting is better — it is closed by construction, so cause
   number seven cannot reopen it.
2. Extend `test_cr136_dart_parity.py` to a third contract over the six
   `INSUFFICIENT_*` constants, as you proposed. Compare as a **set**, so a widening
   fails on the backend side the day it happens rather than the day someone reads the
   card. The fix shape you named is right; it just needs to be the set, not a list of
   the ones that currently have copy.

Fix lands in `health_card.dart` (M09's file) and `test_cr136_dart_parity.py` (M09's
guard), but the finding is M04's: this lane minted the values and owns the contract.
