## 1. What was broken, and what changed

### DEF227 — the trend field could not say "up"

`technicals.py` computed `aligned_up` / `aligned_down` and collapsed both into
`trend = "trading" if (aligned_up or aligned_down) else "consolidating"` on the
next line. Two reachable values, neither directional. Live, same call the Room
makes: `NVDA` $222.90 near its 50-day high and `GME` $19.14 on its 52-week low
both rendered `Trend: trading`. And `trading` is not a trend read in English —
it is a null token under a label promising one, so in a risk-framed prompt the
model guesses, and it guessed "no confirmation".

Now reports the direction it already computed: `uptrend` / `downtrend` /
`consolidating`.

**The line this does not cross:** DEF052 (AT:R58) deleted a *fabricated*
direction claim the computation never backed. This one is the computation's own
output. Nothing new is asserted — the same two booleans, no longer discarded.

### DEF228 (part 1) — position-in-range was inferred, not stated

The block stated support and breakout and never the current price. Live run
`8da2d287` (GRAB, 2026-08-07 15:48Z): support $3.18, breakout $4.06, price
$3.67 — inside the range. The Market Analyst wrote *"price sits below the
recent range floor"*, and `bull_researcher` cited it as *"correctly notes a
recent breakdown"*, `research_manager` and `trader` called it *confirmed*, and
the PM passed on it — then in the same sentence directed the Room to await *"a
retest of the $3.18 support level"*, which concedes price was above $3.18.

`Technicals` now carries `price`; both renderers state `last close $P (N% of
that range)`. `range_position_pct` is arithmetic on two numbers already on the
sheet — it asserts nothing new — and returns `None` on a zero-width range so a
made-up position is never printed (CR040).

**Part 2 is carved to DEF231, not dropped.** It needs to read directional
claims out of free prose; a regex that silently passes reads as coverage while
providing none. The row states the open design question rather than pretending
it was answered.

### DEF229 — provenance, and a label that was a trigger

**(a)** The stack registers as `fallback(cache(yfinance)->mock_walk)`.
`Quote.source` has always carried its leaf so the LIVE/MOCK pill cannot lie.
History carried no such marker, so a yfinance failure returned a synthetic walk
*as a successful history call* — the block still rendered, still said "Real
yfinance OHLCV for {sym}, computed this call", still carried `(LIVE)`. That is
DEF052's class arriving through a different door, defeating the CR104/DEF123
provenance machinery from underneath.

`market_data.history_with_source(provider, ticker, period)` returns
`(bars, leaf_name)`; `FallbackProvider` reports whichever leg served,
`CachingProvider` reports its inner leaf rather than its own `cache(...)` stack
name, and a `getattr` path means any provider without the method reports its
own `name` — so a test fake is never mistaken for the walk.
`compute_technicals` refuses `SYNTHETIC_HISTORY_SOURCES` and logs
`technicals_synthetic_feed_rejected`. Callers already render "not available
this call" for `None`; the honest answer was built, what was missing was the
ability to tell.

Scoped to the technicals path only — quotes keep their never-fail floor,
because there the pill already tells the user which leg fired.

**(b)** `breakout = max(highs[-50:])` is the 50-day high, rendered as
`breakout level: $Y`. Naming resistance "the breakout level" tells the model
the only valid long entry is above the 50-day high; paired with (227) the
predicate is unsatisfiable and every chart position maps to *wait* — below →
"no breakout confirmation" (AMD, XOM, CELZ); at it → "overbought,
mean-reversion risk" (HPQ, RSI 70). Renamed in **five** places: the 1-on-1
block, `_format_profile`'s range line, `_format_profile`'s disclosure header,
`room_runner.py`'s scripted template, and `content/agents/market_analyst.md`.
**The row named four. The fifth was found by a guard, not by reading** — see
§4.

## 5. DEF230 — re-measurement, and why it is NOT closed here

DEF230 is the zero-APPROVE regression itself. **It stays `open`.** Its cause is
not proven and cannot be from stored data alone. What this batch does is fix
the three inputs it correctly identifies as *not* the cause but *are* what
leaves the PM with no bullish technical read, and correct three of its figures.

Reproduce (melehost, `ami_trade`):

```sql
WITH genuine AS (
  SELECT r.*, r.verdict->>'action' act
  FROM room_runs r JOIN users u ON u.id = r.user_id
  WHERE coalesce(r.verdict->>'overridden_from_llm','false') = 'false'
    AND coalesce(u.last_app_version,'') <> 'room-benchmark'
    AND r.verdict IS NOT NULL)
SELECT 'July -> 07-30' w, model_tier, count(*) n,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE act='APPROVE') appr
FROM genuine WHERE triggered_at >= '2026-07-01' AND triggered_at < '2026-07-31'
GROUP BY 1,2
UNION ALL
SELECT 'since 07-31', model_tier, count(*), count(*) FILTER (WHERE act='APPROVE')
FROM genuine WHERE triggered_at >= '2026-07-31' GROUP BY 1,2 ORDER BY 1,2;
```

```
July -> 07-30 | mid     | 39 | 15
July -> 07-30 | premium | 13 |  2      -> 52 runs, 17 APPROVE = 32.7%
since 07-31   | mid     |  3 |  0
since 07-31   | premium | 12 |  0      -> 15 runs,  0 APPROVE
```

1. **The filed 18.7% baseline is contaminated by the benchmark it claims to
   exclude.** 471 of its 498 runs are 2026-07-16/17/18 — 100/147/216 premium
   runs a day. Only 07-20 was excluded. Corrected human-only baseline is
   **32.7%**, so 0/15 is p≈0.003 (~1 in 380), not ~1 in 18. **The correction
   makes the case stronger, not weaker** — which is the point of making it.
2. **Last human APPROVE is 07-29 20:56 (BAC), not 07-30.** The 07-30 08:21
   APPROVE is a room-benchmark user; 07-30 06:04–07:10 is 20 consecutive
   `overridden_from_llm=true` stubs.
3. **The `room_runner.py` mtime argument is void.** melehost reads 07-30 16:43
   because `rsync -a` preserves the Mac's mtime (07-30 19:43, +3h) — last
   *edit*, not last deploy; every promotion rsyncs the whole tree.
4. **Uncontrolled confound — tier.** 12 of 15 recent runs are `premium`; human
   July premium is 2/13 = 15% vs mid 15/39 = 38%. Tier-stratified the drought
   is p≈0.03, an order of magnitude weaker. The 13-run premium baseline is thin
   and partly inside the window — it bounds the claim, it does not settle it.
5. **Uncontrolled confound — the dominant user's own mandate.** 11 of 15 recent
   runs are `8f1e288a`. His mandate v1 has **no risk-limit fields at all**; v2,
   created **08-06 06:49**, is where `sector_cap_pct` / `single_name_cap_pct` /
   `max_open_risk_pct` / `max_trades_per_day` first appear. CR101's constraint
   set entered his prompt **six days after the drought started**.
6. Provider genuinely exonerated — `llm_audit` is `vllm` throughout.

**The closing measurement**, for whoever picks DEF230 up: a tier-stratified,

Specifying it without stratifying on tier measures the mix shift in (4).
