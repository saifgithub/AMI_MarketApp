## MAJORS

### M1 — R2b's weight floor is applied twice, on two different denominators; the rule silently does not fire on a pair the payload carries

§8 attack 4 asked: *"confirm the correlation-pair list M04 emits applies the same
floor, or the rule sees pairs the payload never mentions."* It does not, and the
answer is the other direction — M05 sees FEWER pairs than the payload mentions.

- M04 emits a pair only if both legs clear the floor on the **covered** sleeve:
  `portfolio_health.py:612-614`, `floor = RULE_R2B_MIN_PAIR_WEIGHT_PCT / 100.0`
  against `v_weights`, and `v_weights[i] = p.value / covered_invested`
  (`portfolio_health.py:367`, survivors only).
- M05 then re-filters the same list on the **full invested** sleeve:
  `portfolio_rules.py:316-318`, `weight_of[a] >= RULE_R2B_MIN_PAIR_WEIGHT_PCT`,
  where `weight_of` is `invested_weight_pct = 100.0 * p.value / full_invested`
  (`portfolio_health.py:643`, every risky holding, dropped ones included).

`covered_invested <= full_invested`, so the second filter is strictly tighter and
can only discard. Observed on a book with one dropped holding at 20% of invested
value (exactly `DROPPED_WEIGHT_MAX`, the most the engine still calls sufficient):

```
AAA: M04 covered-basis=5.6250pp (emitted=True) | M05 full-basis=4.5000pp (kept=False)
BBB: M04 covered-basis=5.6250pp (emitted=True) | M05 full-basis=4.5000pp (kept=False)
rho=0.97 vs fire line 0.90 -> R2b fired=False
```

A ρ=0.97 pair, far above the 0.90 fire line, present in `correlation_pairs`, and
R2b is silent — for a reason unconnected to correlation or to either documented
basis. The band is not narrow-by-luck: with drops up to 20%, any pair between 4.0%
and 5.0% of full invested value is emitted and then discarded.

**The existing test cannot catch this.** `test_r2b_boundaries_and_weight_floor`
(test file :306-322) builds every holding with `included=True` and no drops, so
the two denominators coincide in the fixture — it exercises M05's filter in
isolation, never against the basis M04 actually used.

Fix is a decision, not just a patch: filter once. Either M05 trusts M04's list (it
is already filtered) or both use one stated basis — M05's own doc pins R2b to
"invested sleeve", and `HoldingInput`'s docstring pins `invested_weight_pct` to
FULL invested, so M04's covered-basis filter is the one that disagrees with the
written contract. Whichever way it goes, a regression test needs a dropped holding
in the fixture or it re-opens silently.

### M2 — R0's boundary rounding renders a breach sentence whose own two numbers say "compliant"

The breach test is on the unrounded weight (correct, strict `>`, matching the
gate), but the slot is `round(_total_value_pct(holding), 1)`
(`portfolio_rules.py:232`) and `_f5` renders it at 1 dp beside the cap
(`portfolio_finding.py:538-543`). At a true weight of 35.04% against a 35.0% cap,
the rendered §F5 line is, verbatim:

```
- Your mandate caps a single holding at 35.0%. AAA is at 35.0% of your total portfolio value today.
```

R0 fires (correctly — 35.04 > 35.0), and the sentence the user reads asserts a
breach while displaying a weight equal to the cap. That is the shown-vs-enforced
contradiction R0 exists to remove, in the product surface, produced by R0 itself.
Narrow (true weight within 0.05pp above the cap) but not rare over a live user
base, and the CR's own hostile-reader standard is the one this fails.

Graded MAJOR rather than MINOR deliberately: severity was genuinely arguable to me
— every financial surface rounds — so per PROTOCOL "doubt resolves toward MAJOR".
Cheap fixes exist (render the breach weight at enough precision to exceed the cap,
or ceil it at 1 dp, or drop the restated number in favour of "above your 35% cap").
