## Findings

### MAJOR-1 — PGR/PLR are dollar-weighted; Odean 1998's definition — the one the block cites beside them — is count-weighted

`_disposition_ratio` accumulates DOLLARS (`realised_gains += ev.realised_pnl`,
`paper_gains += paper_pnl`, `behaviour_diagnostics.py:491-515`) and computes
`pgr = realised_gains / (realised_gains + paper_gains)` over those dollar sums
(`:517-524`). The counts it also accumulates (`realised_gains_count`,
`paper_gains_count`, …) are never used in the ratio.

Odean (1998)'s PGR/PLR are COUNT proportions — number of realised gains over number of
gain opportunities (realised + paper). Odean's own paper states it directly: "PGR is the
number of realized gains …" ([Odean 1998, Haas PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/areinvestorsreluctant.pdf)),
and the published baseline figures the block itself renders — `0.148` / `0.098`
(`_NEW_BASELINES`, `behaviour_diagnostics.py:147-155`, rendered by `_f3_behaviour` at
`portfolio_finding.py:754-760`) — are the paper's count-based Table I values.

The two bases diverge materially. One $900 realised gain with nine $100 paper gains:
dollar-PGR = 0.50, count-PGR = 0.10 — the user would read "realised 50.0% of their
available gains" against "the measured account population realised 14.8%", a ~3.4x
overstatement of the comparable quantity. The lane's own pinned fixture proves the basis
divergence without any fixture of mine: `test_disposition_ratio_hand_built_fixture_matches_odean_by_hand`
(`test_cr222_behaviour_diagnostics.py:392`) asserts `plr == 200/300 ≈ 0.667`; Odean's
count definition on the same fixture gives 1/2 = 0.5. The test's docstring calls the
dollar derivation "Odean's definition" — it is not; this is a correlated builder+fixture
error, and the module docstring repeats the claim ("Odean's own definition",
`behaviour_diagnostics.py:46-49`).

Why MAJOR, not MINOR: the CR's own acceptance criterion is "PGR/PLR on a hand-built lot
fixture matches Odean's definition" (CR222 doc, Acceptance §Diagnostics) — unmet; the
rendered block presents user figure and published baseline as the same measure when they
are not; and measurement fidelity against cited literature is this CR's entire reason to
exist. No cash path, no gate, so not a BLOCKER.

Fix is cheap: the counts are already computed — `pgr = realised_gains_count /
(realised_gains_count + paper_gains_count)` (None when the denominator is 0, same as
now). The hand-built test's expected values change (0.667 → 0.5 on that fixture), and the
test's docstring derivation needs correcting, not just its constants.

### Findings

#### MAJOR-1 — count basis now in place, but the counting UNIT is not Odean's. The lane's own acceptance fixture still does not match Odean

Round 2 moved the numerator and denominator from dollars to counts (`behaviour_diagnostics.py:521-528`).
That part is correct and pinned (M1–M4 killed). But the counts are tallied per **lot per closing
event**, and Odean tallies per **stock per sale day**, only in **portfolios of 2 or more stocks**,
never counting a stock sold that day as paper, and measuring against **average purchase price**:

- Realised side: every FIFO-matched lot a sell draws down emits its own `_ClosingEvent`
  (`:391-400`). Each one is a realised count (`:494-500`). Odean counts one per stock sold that day.
- Paper side: at each closing event, every other open **lot** of every ticker is one paper count
  (`:505-517`). That includes the unsold remainder of the ticker just sold, and tickers sold later
  the same day. Odean counts one per stock not sold that day.
- No filter for 2 or more stocks. Odean counts nothing on a sale day in a 1-stock portfolio.

Under the round-1 dollar basis, lot granularity summed out. Under a count basis each lot is a vote,
so round 2's fix newly exposes this. Part of this is correlated on the audit side: round 1
(U69) prescribed the ratio change but not the unit, and the architect implemented what was
prescribed.

Hand computation on the lane's own fixture (`test_disposition_ratio_hand_built_fixture_matches_odean_by_hand`, `test_cr222_behaviour_diagnostics.py:392-477`):
- Day 10, holdings {AAA, BBB}, 2 stocks: AAA sold at 120 vs 100 = 1 realised gain. BBB unsold at 90
  vs 100 = 1 paper loss.
- Day 20, holdings {BBB}, 1 stock: Odean counts nothing.
- CCC days: holdings {CCC}, 1 stock (lots of one ticker): Odean counts nothing.
- **Odean: PGR = 1/(1+0) = 1.0, PLR = 0/(0+1) = 0.0.** The implementation, as asserted by the test
  at `:476-477`: PGR 1.0, **PLR 0.5**. The CR acceptance criterion ("PGR/PLR on a hand-built lot
  fixture matches Odean's definition") is still unmet, on the fixture written to prove it.

New count-basis test (`:480-544`), hand computation: day 10, holdings {BIG, SM0..SM8}, 10 stocks:
1 realised gain, 9 paper gains, **PGR 0.10. This matches Odean**, but only because every SM ticker
is a single lot. PAD sale days are 1-stock days, so Odean's PLR is undefined. The implementation
gives realised_losses_count 9, which makes PLR 1.0. The test asserts no PLR.

Probes (measured, `pytest -s` output):

|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 1 sale at a gain; other holding built in 4 buys (DCA), all at a gain | PGR 0.2 (1 / (1+4)) | 0.5 (1 stock, 1 paper gain) |

P5 rendered P4 through `_f3_behaviour`: *"Disposition ratio (Odean 1998): this user realised 100.0%
of their available gains and 100.0% of their available losses."* This sits directly above *"the
measured account population realised 14.8% … and 9.8%"*. Round 1 called this degenerate case
"algebraically forced under either weighting" (OUT-OF-SCOPE). Under Odean's own rule it is **not
forced**: it is not measured. It is reclassified here as part of this MAJOR.

The module docstring misstates the source it approximates (`behaviour_diagnostics.py:55-57`): *"a
full Odean replication marks every open lot to market on every trading day"*. Odean counts only on
sale days (quote above). So the documented "approximation" is Odean's own method, and the real
deviations (lot vs stock, event vs day, same-day-sold counted as paper, no 2-stock filter) are
undocumented. `:49-50` also says "every position", while the code counts lots.

Why MAJOR: this is the same acceptance criterion as round 1's MAJOR, unmet on the lane's own
fixture. The figure is rendered beside the Odean baseline as the same measure. DCA buys, partial
sells, same-day bracket closes and one-at-a-time trading are ordinary patterns in this app.
Fix shape (architect's call): group by (sale day, ticker). Count one realised event per ticker sold
that day, and one paper event per ticker held at the start of the day and not sold that day,
against its average purchase price. Skip days with fewer than 2 tickers held. Re-derive the
hand-built test by hand. PLR on that fixture becomes 0.0.

#### MAJOR-2 — the rendered baseline is the POOLED all-account figure, shown beside ONE account's ratio; Odean's per-account average is 0.57 / 0.36

`_f3_behaviour` renders one user's PGR/PLR, then *"Published baseline (Odean 1998): the measured
account population realised 14.8% … 9.8%"* (`portfolio_finding.py:760-767`). 0.148 / 0.098 is

implying a per-user count ratio is).

Why it matters to the user: a user whose account behaves exactly like Odean's average account
(PGR 57%) reads "57.0%" against "14.8%", and comes away believing they realise gains nearly 4x as
readily as typical investors. That is the round-1 overstatement again, through a different
mechanism. The copy is literally true, so severity is in doubt. Per PROTOCOL "doubt bounces", this
is MAJOR. Tier A: published-methodology copy asserting a sourced figure. Fix is data/copy level:
render the per-account averages (0.57 / 0.36) as the comparable baseline, or keep the pooled figure
but label it as pooled. Register any new literals in the allow-list.
