## 0. What to attack hardest

1. **The wording, against the advice line.** This is the finding I would most
   want a second pair of eyes on. The sentence is *"(Concentration: this is 50.0%
   of the portfolio in one name. Your mandate's single-name cap is 100.0%, so the
   safety floor permits it.)"* I believe that is a statement of fact. It is one
   comma away from being a caution, and a caution is advice. I have a test
   asserting the string contains none of *should / recommend / advise / consider
   reducing / too much / risky / dangerous / warning / careful / we suggest* — but
   a banned-word list is a weak instrument for a question about tone. If you read
   it as advisory, that is a MAJOR and I would rather hear it here than from a
   regulator-shaped question later.

2. **The threshold is a judgement I made alone.** 25%. My reasoning is that four
   equal names is the point past which one company's outcome, rather than a
   portfolio's, is what the book tracks. It is not derived from data — there is no
   corpus to derive it from, since the disclosure has never existed. It gates a
   sentence and never a decision, so a wrong threshold costs noise or silence, not
   a bad trade. Push on whether 25 is the right number, and on whether a
   *disclosure* threshold should be a round number at all.

3. **It fires on the enforced size, not the requested one — deliberately.** A PM
   asking 90% under a 40% cap is disclosed at **40%**. I think stating the request
   would describe a position the user never gets, which is DEF235's failure in a
   new place. The opposite reading is defensible: that a PM *asking* for 90% is
   itself information the user is entitled to. I did not surface it. If you think
   that is the wrong call, it is a bounded change.

4. **Two mutations survived my first pass** (§4). Both are recorded rather than
   quietly fixed, because one of them means I had covered only one of the two
   surfaces Saiful's ruling explicitly named.

## 1. What this is, and what it is not

**Nothing was broken.** `day_trader_preset.py` really does set
`single_name_cap_pct: 100.0`. **7 of 18 epoch convenes** ran under it (re-derived
from the 216 stored prompts at 12 turns per convene: 84 at 100.0%, 120 at 3.0%,
12 at 10.0%). `_risk_tier_size_ceiling` → `resolved_single_name_cap_pct` is the
same resolver `enforce_safety_floor` clamps to, so shown == enforced. When the
Bull Researcher argued **50% of portfolio in one name**, and when the Aggressive
Debator argued **100% into a book already 95.8% GME with $422.50 cash**, both
agents were behaving correctly under the mandate they were given.

The defect is **disclosure**. A simulator whose entire purpose is teaching let a
user watch a 12-analyst team argue for a half-portfolio single-name position while
every rendered figure stayed neutral about what that means. CR040's own question,
inverted: *if this ships constantly and quietly, what does the user end up
believing?*

Saiful's ruling, 2026-08-08, asked as a product question after CR149, CR153 and
CR154 each surfaced it independently: **"Keep it, but make AMI say the number out
loud."** He explicitly rejected capping the preset.

**Explicitly not done:** no cap on the mandate, and no cap on the *rendered*
figure. A ceiling on a reported number is precisely how DEF235 shipped a wrong one.

## 2. The change

`_concentration_note(size_pct, cap_pct)` — one factual sentence above
`_LOUD_CONCENTRATION_PCT = 25.0`, on the two surfaces the ruling names:

- **`verdict.reason`**, appended *after* the risk-tier clamp, alongside the
  existing parenthetical AMI disclosures (`sized down to…`, `stop/target not
  stated by the PM…`). Same idiom, same surface, same voice.
- **the geometry annotation**, as a second clause on the line DEF235 already
  renders: `at the mandate's 100.0% single-name cap; that cap is 100.0% of the
  portfolio in one name`.

Degrades loudly: an unresolvable cap still states the share, rather than
swallowing the disclosure whose useful half is the share.
