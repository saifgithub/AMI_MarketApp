### MINOR — the invariant only recognises ISO dates

The Architect flagged this for the auditor as a question rather than asserting it was a gap. Measured:

**M4** — a non-ISO unanchored date (`Last 10-K filed March 14, 2026`) rendered into the header →
**154 passed, GREEN**. `_ISO_DATE_RE` is `\b\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2}\b`, so `March 14, 2026`, `July 30` and
`14/03/2026` all pass through unseen.

**Graded MINOR, not MAJOR**, and the reasoning matters because I scored the analogous CR104 gap the
same way: the polarity is right and the invariant **is** load-bearing (M5), there is **no live
instance** — I measured 0 unanchored dates of any format across all 12 prompts and both message
roles — and every date this codebase renders goes through `isoformat()` or `strftime("%Y-%m-%d")`.
This is a **detection-completeness limit in a test**, not a hole in production behaviour. Cheap to
close: add a month-name alternation to `_ISO_DATE_RE` (~3 lines). Not holding the lane for it.

### Recorded, not scored

- **The earnings line's structural refusal is a silent omission, not a loud one.** A profile carrying
  a live earnings date without a computed interval renders **no earnings line at all** rather than
  `Next earnings: not available`, which is the idiom every other field in this renderer uses. The
  direction is right (better nothing than an unanchored date, and CR040 prefers refusing) and the
  path is unreachable through `_profile_for_ticker`, which always computes the interval alongside the
  date — so this is an observation about idiom consistency, not a defect.
- **UTC vs US-market date.** D4 asked the lane to *pick a basis and state it*; it picked UTC and
  states it in the rendered string, which satisfies D4 as written. The residual is that an earnings
  date is a US-market date, so for part of the day a non-US user's "in 3 days" is one off a
  market-day count. Disclosed rather than hidden, and the model now has a stated basis instead of
  none.
