## MAJOR-3 — two cells of the input-side table are mid-run counts

The table is headed **"post-fix (468 turns)"**. Two of its four cells are not
counts over 468 turns.

Measured on the committed corpus, `Reference position` lines and
`Primary trend` lines matched by their exact rendered forms:

| | claimed | measured at `3be47b81` |
|---|---|---|
| RefPos lines, bare `X% below entry` — pre | 156 / 156 | **156** ✓ |
| RefPos lines, bare — post | 0 | **0** ✓ |
| RefPos lines, pair-bound form — pre | 0 | **0** ✓ |
| RefPos lines, pair-bound form — post | **53 / 53** | **158** (line-level; 156 turn-level) |
| `Primary trend`, one end only — pre | 351 / 351 | **351** ✓ |
| `Primary trend`, one end only — post | 0 | **0** ✓ |
| `Primary trend`, explicit inverse — pre | 0 | **0** ✓ |
| `Primary trend`, explicit inverse — post | **125 / 125** | **351** |

Every pre-fix figure is exact. Both wrong figures are post-fix, and both are
about a third of the true value. Sorting the corpus by `created_at` locates
them precisely:

```
count reaches  53 (pair-bound)  at turn 165/468 — 2026-08-14T11:51:00Z
count reaches 125 (inverse)     at turn 167/468 — 2026-08-14T11:51:41Z
corpus window: 10:59:09Z → 13:32:04Z
```

Two independent metrics landing at the same wall-clock instant, **41 seconds
apart, ~35% through the run**, is not a definitional difference. These were
read off a run still in flight — round 3 said as much at the time ("26 of 39
convenes complete") — and not re-taken when the corpus finished.

**Why this is MAJOR and not a MINOR like round 1's.** The substantive claim
is *true*: 100% conversion, verified on the full corpus in both directions.
But the denominators are what make this a measurement rather than an
assertion, and as printed they say the post-fix epoch has **a third as many**
reference-position and trend lines as the pre-fix one. It does not — 351 vs
351, and 156 vs 156 at turn level. A reader comparing the columns is invited
to infer a change in the *input* that did not happen, in the one table whose
job is to characterise the input. That is the deliverable's headline table
carrying a false statement about the corpus it names.

Mitigating, and worth saying plainly: **the error runs against your own
interest.** The true result is three times stronger than the one reported. It
reads as an oversight in refreshing a figure, not as anything else.

**Fix:** re-take both counts on the completed corpus (158 / 351) and, while
in the header, add `3be47b81` to the SHA table — it still says "**two**" and
now carries a third SHA holding the corpus, the sweep and the new pin. That
is the same class as MAJOR-1, in a much weaker form, since the addendum names
its SHA inline immediately.

what the paragraph is actually for. But a movement spread over three domains
is a slightly *less* benign shape than one repeated lexical pattern — so the
mischaracterisation runs in the flattering direction, which is the direction
your own closing sentence says the discipline has to cut against.
