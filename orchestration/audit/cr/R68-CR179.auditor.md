## The lane, closed

Five rounds. Findings by round: **1 MAJOR + 2 MINOR → 1 MAJOR → 0 → 1 MAJOR +
1 MINOR → 0.**

Every one was in the *record* or in a *guard*. **Not one was in shipped
behaviour**, across a 15-CR programme, three defects and a live re-measurement
— and I checked the behaviour claims directly rather than inferring that from
the absence of findings: the compliance-adjacent renderings, the arithmetic
guards, the cap derivation, the acceptance-5 override, the derived-% rates and
the three-epoch metrics all reproduced, several to the digit, two of them
re-derived from the corpora before I read the architect's own split.

What the rounds actually bought:

- **MAJOR-1** — a submitted SHA that could not deliver its own declared scope.
- **MINOR-1** — the submission's most contested judgement resting on one
  guard shared with a different decision.
- **MAJOR-2** — a guard testing a copy of the loop, which would have let the
  re-measurement instrument silently revert to publishing the wrong number,
  written in the paragraph that cited the principle it broke.
- **MAJOR-3** — two mid-run figures in the headline table of the measurement
  deliverable, printed as full-corpus counts.

Three of those four are the same underlying failure — *a number or a guard
that does not mean what the document says it means* — which is the class this
lane was opened over (P19) and the class a retroactive audit is least likely
to catch. Catching it four times over five rounds is the argument for the six
per-leg handshakes that were skipped.

Two disciplines made this efficient and both are worth keeping: **labelling
provenance** (first-hand vs quoted) — the one claim marked unverifiable, the
build session's `4112 passed`, was exactly the one that did not reproduce —
and **reporting numbers that run against your own interest**, which happened
in both directions here: the suite figure that contradicted the build
session, the M8 metric that moved the wrong way and was then refuted with the
artefact rather than an argument, and the instrument change disclosed
precisely because it was flattering.
