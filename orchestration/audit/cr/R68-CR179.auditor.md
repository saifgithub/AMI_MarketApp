## MAJOR-1 — DEF303 is declared in scope but is not in the submitted SHA

Two bolded statements in the submission contradict each other:

- Header: **SHA `86d9af99` … "Everything below is contained in it."**
- Known-open: **"done this session as `DEF303` (`5279c721`), and it is in
  scope for this audit."**

`git merge-base --is-ancestor 5279c721 86d9af99` → **false**. `5279c721` is
ten commits later and on a different track tag (AT:R69, not AT:R68).
Measured at the submitted SHA:

```

grep -c "the_conservative_debator_is_the_one_agent_whose_cap_was_binding"
  tests/unit/test_cr179_token_budget_derivation.py               →  0     (the guard does not exist)
```

So the submitted SHA contains **neither the fix nor its guards** for an item
the submission explicitly asks the auditor to attack ("Attack this: I claim
the token measurement is the ground truth…").

Why this is MAJOR rather than a note: bindings §"Auditing the live tree"
makes the committed SHA the **mandatory** audit substrate ("the auditor MUST
check out the architect's committed SHA into a scratch worktree … never trust
the shared working tree as-is"). A declared scope the declared SHA cannot
deliver breaks that binding — an auditor following it finds no DEF303 and
must either skip the item or improvise a second checkout, which is what I
did. Left as-is, the durable record would read "R68-CR179 COMPLETE at
`86d9af99`" while a third of the DEF303 verification happened somewhere else;
a future reader checking out `86d9af99` would find the Conservative still at
800 and reasonably conclude either that DEF303 went unaudited or that the
auditor certified something absent.

I weighed closing this as MINOR on the reading that "Known open, not claimed
as done" is explicitly a loose-ends section outside the programme scope the
header describes. That reading softens it but does not clear it, because
"it is in scope for this audit" is unambiguous. Genuinely borderline ⇒
PROTOCOL.md "doubt resolves toward MAJOR, not COMPLETE".

**Fix is cheap:** name both SHAs in the header (or resubmit at a SHA
containing both) and say which claims live at which. No re-work.
