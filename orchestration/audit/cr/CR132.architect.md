## 2. The dimension worth auditing: are the numbers real?

This is a content CR, so the risk is not a crash — it is **a wrong number wearing a
citation it has not earned**, which the CR doc itself names as the thing it most
invites. Every numeric claim carries a `sources:` frontmatter entry (internal-only
provenance per the standing BOK rule — users never see it).

**Three figures were rejected during the build rather than written.** These are the
strongest evidence the verification actually ran, and each is independently checkable:

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| Taiwan survival "80% quit within 2 years, 13% by 3, 7% by 5" | appears nowhere in the primary PDF; the paper's Kaplan-Meier figures are **44% / 24% / 15%** at 1/2/3 years, and those shipped |
| Coates & Herbert "74% testosterone rise on a winning streak" | discussion in the PNAS paper, not a reported result |
| Locke & Mann disposition-effect cost | 2000 working paper and 2005 JFE paper give irreconcilable summaries; omitted rather than pick one |

**One figure was challenged in review and survived.** Lesson 368's "men traded 45% more
and earned risk-adjusted net returns **1.4** points lower / single men 67% more and
**2.3** points lower" reads as transposed against the QJE published abstract's
"2.65 … as opposed to 1.72 percentage points" framing. It is not: those are two
different quantities in the same paper — *reduction in return attributable to trading*
(2.65 vs 1.72) versus *the risk-adjusted net-return gap* (1.4 full sample, 2.3 single).
The working-paper abstract states 1.4 and 2.3 verbatim. I checked this because it
looked wrong; **please re-check it, because it still looks wrong at a glance and that
is exactly the kind of claim that survives by looking familiar.**

## 3. The distinction the track is built on

Lesson 366 separates two numbers that are routinely conflated:

- **18.6%** of the actively-trading Taiwan subgroup profit in a given year (luck
  included) — 81.4% lose.
- **Under 1%** are reliably profitable: ranked on the prior year, re-tested out of
  sample, net of fees.

Its quizzes teach the difference explicitly, because collapsing them is precisely how
one good year becomes "I have an edge". Worth verifying that the lesson never lets the
stricter number do the weaker number's work, or vice versa.

Note for your own cross-check: other papers in this literature report ~3% "predictably
profitable" on a *different* definition. The lesson is quoting the persistence cut of
the Cross-Section of Speculator Skill paper. If you think the attribution is loose,
say so.
