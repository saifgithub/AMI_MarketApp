## ⚠️ D1 — THE SPEC IS STALE ON ONE POINT. Read this before you build the tab bar.

CR120 §4.1 says the tab segments use:

> `FlatTopHexagonClipper(cornerCut: 8)` matching `ticker_chart.dart:185` and CR106's BOARD|TRANSCRIPT toggle

**Every clause of that sentence is now wrong.** The spec was written on 2026-07-29 morning; CR117
landed that afternoon and is what this instruction would have collided with.

1. **`FlatTopHexagonClipper` no longer exists.** It emitted an **eight**-point path while its own
   docstring called it a cut-corner octagon. CR117 renamed it `CutCornerOctagonClipper` across 12
   call sites and deliberately did **not** let anything else inherit the vacated name — so a stale
   reference is a **compile error, not a wrong shape**. If you type the old name you will find out
   immediately. That is by design; it is why you are safe to build here at all.
2. **`ticker_chart.dart:185` is the octagon** — one of the 12 renamed sites. Copying it gives you
   angled top and bottom, which is not the shape the design calls for.
3. **CR106's BOARD|TRANSCRIPT toggle no longer looks like that either.** DEF146 (same commit)
   rebuilt it, because copying the per-chip octagon is precisely what produced the reported bug.

**Use `FlatTopHexagonBarClipper(endInset: 8)`** — angled ends, flat top and bottom, six sides, holds
its angle at any aspect ratio.

**Clip the WHOLE BAR ONCE — not each segment.** This is the load-bearing half and it is DEF146
verbatim: two independently-clipped segments are *two chips with a gap between them*, whatever their
geometry, and the user reported that as *"the buttons are not hex design"* — they were describing a
control that had come apart. Each segment's own border is also wrong: its outer edges get clipped
away and only the inner run survives, which reads as a stray divider.

**`mobile/lib/widgets/room/room_view_mode_toggle.dart` is the worked example.** One `ClipPath`
around a `Row`, a 1pt `AmiColors.slate700` `Container` as the internal divider (a *gap* re-creates
two objects — that is the defect in disguise), and segments that carry fill only. Copy that
structure; it is three days old and audited. Your bar has three segments instead of two, so it needs
two dividers.

## Architect decisions — settled, do not re-litigate

### D2 — `SHOW ALL` ships in Phase 1, as an in-place expansion, NOT the pushed route

CR120 §8 puts *"the pushed all-closed-trades route"* in Phase 2, and §9 acceptance 5 requires
`SHOW ALL` to reach every closed trade. Taken literally that ships a Phase 1 with a hard 25-cap and
**no way to see trade 26** — which is the exact silent loss §2 spends 60 lines preventing. For a
Floor Pass user the Portfolio is the *durable* record and the Journal is the lossy one; a cap with
no hatch deletes their own trade history from every surface in the app.

**Ruling:** Phase 1 ships the escape hatch as an **in-place expansion** — `SHOW ALL 195` flips the
History tab's own list from 25 rows to all of them, inside the tab, still lazy. No new route, no
navigation, no second render path. Acceptance 5 is therefore **in scope**; the *pushed route* stays
in Phase 2. This also makes acceptance 10 (one shared row widget) nearly free — but **still extract
the closed-trade row into its own widget**, because Phase 2's route is the second renderer and
DEF098's class is two renderers of one rule with neither a superset.

### D3 — retention is THREE states, not `int?`. This is the trap in this lane.

`JournalState.retentionDays` is `int?` (`journal_providers.dart:24`), and
`journal_screen.dart:113` renders its warning `if (state.retentionDays != null)`. That works **on
the Journal screen**, which always loads its own state first.

**On the Portfolio it inverts.** The Portfolio never calls the journal endpoint, so `retentionDays`
is `null`-because-never-loaded — indistinguishable from `null`-because-unlimited. A naive
`if (retentionDays != null)` therefore renders **nothing** for a Floor Pass user: the reassuring
branch, silently, on the exact disclosure that exists to stop a user losing data they think is
archived. That is CR040 with the user's own record as the thing lost, and it will look correct in
every manual test where you visited the Journal first.

**Required:** model it as three states — **unknown** / **unlimited** / **N days** — and derive
*unknown* from something that is not `retentionDays` itself. Per §2.1's fail-safe: unknown ⇒ show
the caveat **without a number** (`portfolioJournalRetentionUnknown`), never the reassuring branch.
A spurious warning is recoverable; a missing one is not.

### D4 — DEF155 folds into this lane: the shipped retention string claims a loss that does not happen

`journalRetentionWarning` — *"Floor Pass: last {days} days only. Upgrade to **keep** everything."*
Retention is a **read-time filter** (`journal_store.py:134-136` adds `where created_at >= cutoff` to
the *select*); the only `delete(JournalEntryRow)` is a test-only `clear()`, and `sim_trades` has no
retention at all. Nothing is deleted; entries reappear on upgrade.

CR120 §6 flagged it as *"not mine to edit unilaterally."* **As Architect I am ruling it in.** You are
writing the honest twin of this sentence (`portfolioJournalRetention`) on the screen that *links to*
the one telling the lie — leaving both shipping is DEF098's two-renderer class on a monetisation
string, which §2.1 itself warns against. It is one verb plus one clause, and it is under independent
audit here.

**Minted `DEF155`.** Fix the EN string (*keep* → *see*, plus a clause that nothing is deleted), carry
`retranslate:[ar,ms]`, and **write `docs/defect/_registry/DEF155.row.md` yourself** — the domain
owner writes the row, not the Architect (CR081). Then regenerate. Do not touch `def_list.md` by hand.

### D5 — tab state is `TabController` state and nothing else. Doing nothing is the implementation.

No `SharedPreferences` key, no stale-preference migration, no CR106-style `setMode`, and therefore
none of CR106's **T-MODESIDE** trap surface. `home_shell.dart:42` hosts the five nav destinations in
an **`IndexedStack`** (verified), so `PortfolioScreen`'s State survives a bottom-nav switch for free
and dies on cold start — which is exactly the three behaviours §4.1's table wants. Persisting across
launches is **rejected**: a user whose last visit ended on History would open the app next day with
an armed stop behind a tab they cannot see. A staleness rule ("reset after N minutes") is also
rejected — a tab that sometimes remembers is worse than either rule applied consistently.

## AMENDMENT 1 — CR118 folded in (Saiful, 2026-07-29, minutes after dispatch)

**If you have already passed the acceptance table, read this anyway — it adds one criterion.**

**CR118: cap the sector-allocation legend's height and scroll inside the card.** You are moving that
donut into the Positions tab (§4.1), so you are the one holding its new home and this belongs in the
same pass rather than as a follow-up against a layout that no longer exists.

The legend renders **every** sector inline, so the card's height is a function of how diversified the
portfolio is — the one thing the product actively encourages the user to increase. The shipped
example has 4 rows (Cash 89.3%, Communication Services 5.3%, Technology 3.3%, Other 2.2%) and fits
fine; at the GICS level the backend classifies to, a well-spread portfolio reaches **11 sectors plus
Cash plus Other**, which pushes holdings and everything below it off the first screen. Forward-
looking, not a present failure — that is why it is a CR.

**Ruled:** cap the legend to roughly the donut's height and scroll **within it**, so the card's
footprint is constant regardless of diversification. Rejected: top-N with a "show all" expander —
the whole point of the card is seeing your concentration at a glance, and that hides it behind a tap.

**This is the part that goes wrong, so it is spelled out:**

- An inner scrollable needs a **bounded height and its own `ScrollController`**. An unbounded nested
  `ListView` inside a scrollable **throws**; a `shrinkWrap: true` one **silently re-expands to full
  content height** — which looks perfectly fixed in a 4-sector test and regresses the moment someone
  diversifies. That failure mode is why this note exists.
- **Test with a synthetic 13-row allocation, not the reporter's 4.** A 4-row fixture cannot
  distinguish a working cap from a broken one.
- Give the capped list a **visible affordance that it scrolls** — a fade or a partial row at the cut.
  Clipped content with no cue reads as a rendering bug; that is exactly how this pattern generated
  the clipped-CTA reports under **DEF075**.
- Your container is now the Positions tab's lazy list, **not** the old top-level
  `ListView(children:)` at `:137` that CR118's note was written against. Re-read the nesting before
  you bound the height.

**Acceptance 11 (new):** the sector card's height is constant at 4 sectors and at 13, the legend
scrolls within its own bounds, and the cut carries a visible affordance — asserted with a 13-row
fixture.

**CR116 and CR119 are CLOSED against you, not added.** CR116 ("holdings above the watchlist") is
**superseded** — tabs remove the ordering question entirely, the two lists are no longer in one
column. CR119 was the design commission that **produced** CR120; delivering this lane closes it.
Do not implement either as a separate change.
